The Bute Lordship Courts at Llandough

Evidence, reported venues, archival leads and legal context. This page separates what contemporary reports record from what remains a catalogue lead or an interpretation requiring the original manorial records.

Version 1.0.0 · 30 October 2026 · Research synthesis; not a title opinion or a finding about ownership.

Scope control. Four questions must not be collapsed: (1) who held the lordship or manorial jurisdiction; (2) whether a court was held and what type the report called it; (3) where a particular sitting took place; and (4) who owned or occupied a particular freehold parcel. A court report, a juror’s name, a venue or a manorial catalogue entry does not, by itself, answer the other questions.

PRESS · READ		 The report describes the annual court leet and court baron of the lordship of Bute at the Merry Harriers Hotel, Llandough. It records a vote of sympathy for the family of the late John Williams of Great House, Llandough, and describes him as a long-serving juror. CATALOGUE ONLY · SOURCE PENDING		 The catalogue describes Llandough Manor rentals, with Cogan and other manors, for 1808–1881 (R 6/27–45), and Llandough Manor presentments, with other manors, for 1793–1934 (M 17/110–314). Venue: Great House and the Merry Harrier(s) Hotel

The existing Bute Estate page cites Historic Environment Record GGAT02038s for the historical association between Great House and manorial courts. That heritage-record statement must be read in its own date and site context; it is not a conveyance or proof of a continuous court venue.

The contemporary press reports for 1904–1917 and the 1908 report identify a hotel venue in Llandough: the spelling appears as “Merry Harriers Hotel” in EV-0243 and “Merry Harrier Hotel” in the EV-0283 record description. The newspaper evidence therefore supports that reported venue for those particular sittings. It does not prove that Great House was never used for a court, nor that the hotel was the venue at every date in the manor’s history.

Safe reading: treat the Great House heritage-record reference and the hotel venue reports as distinct statements. Reconcile them only after the underlying HER entry and any earlier court books or steward’s papers have been inspected.

Names, jurors and officers

  • John Williams of Great House: EV-0243 connects the late John Williams of Great House with many years of jury service. This is evidence of participation in the court’s public proceedings, not proof that he owned the farm or held a particular parcel as freeholder.
  • John Williams on the 1910 jury: EV-0283 gives the name without a residence or property description. A connection with the Great House family is possible but not established by the name alone. Do not identify him as John Williams junior without a further record.
  • Jenkin M. Williams / Jenkin Williams: EV-0283 lists the name among Llandough constables in 1913 and 1917. The record does not establish identity with Jenkin Morgan Williams in the separate Llandough Farm material (EV-0238).
  • Presentments: the 1910 report records no presentments at that particular sitting concerning deaths of freeholders, encroachments or other matters relating to manorial rights. That is a report about one sitting; it is not evidence that no such presentments existed in other years.

Court Leet and Court Baron: terminology and limits

The 1908 report uses both terms for the annual court of the lordship of Bute. EV-0283 also describes the proceedings as a combined court leet and court baron. The labels identify the form in which the newspaper reported the proceedings; they do not, without the court’s own records, establish the precise jurisdiction exercised at each sitting, the rules applied to every manor named, or the legal effect of a particular entry.

For this reason, this page does not attribute unlocated functions to the Llandough court. No assertion is made here that it held a particular assize, conducted a regular audit at Great House, issued a specific closing proclamation, or exercised a power merely because a court of another manor used that procedure.

Archive references that must not be conflated

  • NLW Bute Estate Records, D 219: the catalogue material discussed on the Bute Estate page concerns leases and agreements, including Cydfin / Tŷ Mawr Farm and the separately named Llandough Farm. It is not itself a set of court presentments. Keep the farm names, acreages and deed groups distinct.
  • EV-0278: the NLW / Manorial Documents Register entries for Llandough Manor rentals and presentments are relevant archival leads, but the volumes remain to be inspected.
  • Glamorgan Archives DRA 22: this is the Manor of Colwinstone, not Llandough. DRA 22/2 (Court Leet and Court Baron book, 1841–1867) and DRA 22/4 (steward’s fees, 1889) must not be cited as Llandough court records.
  • Mountjoy chronology: the May 1922 formation of Mountjoy Ltd, the 1926 catalogue entry (EV-0614), and the 1932 press report of a court-related dinner are separate items. Company formation does not prove that the company held the lordship. A catalogue entry is not the underlying record. A reported dinner does not, by itself, establish a formal court sitting, the legal identity of the lord, or the continuation of jurisdiction after the last reports in EV-0283.

Legal distinction: lordship is not parcel title

A manorial lordship and the right to hold a manorial court are not interchangeable with ownership of each freehold within the manor. A person’s jury service, appointment as a constable, or appearance in a presentment does not by itself prove that person’s title to Great House Farm. Equally, a court’s reported venue does not identify the owner of the building in which it met.

The catalogue end-date of 1934 in EV-0278 is not proof that a court sat every year to 1934, or that every function described in earlier proceedings continued unchanged. The original presentments, rentals, steward’s papers and any relevant transfer or appointment instruments are needed before a more specific legal conclusion can be made.

Outstanding record checks

  1. Inspect NLW M 17/110–314 and identify whether any 1908, 1910 or later presentment names Great House, John Williams, a tenant, a freeholder, an encroachment or a boundary.
  2. Inspect NLW R 6/27–45 and distinguish manorial rentals from farm or estate rentals; test whether any entry concerns Great House and the 1877 change.
  3. Obtain and inspect the underlying HER GGAT02038s entry and record the exact passage, date, source and building to which the court-venue statement refers.
  4. Locate the full 1932 newspaper report and distinguish a dinner or social event from a formal court sitting, appointment or legal instrument.
  5. Keep DRA 22 out of the Llandough source chain; identify the correct Llandough manorial series before assigning any archive reference.
