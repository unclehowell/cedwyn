## 1. Bottom line

1. **The licence did one of the two things, not both.** BP's 31 October 1974 letters were held to stop Mary (Nancy) Williams's adverse possession of the farmhouse and garden (A2) against BP: *established by primary record*. Nothing in the record shows BP using her occupation as *its own* possession against a different true owner, and in law it could not have done so with an unaccepted, unilateral licence (§5). BP never needed to: its case at trial and on appeal rested on a paper title that nobody contested.
2. **The licence arrived 41 days before BP's last enforcement right ran out.** The 1962 possession order could be enforced only until 11 December 1974. The letters are dated 31 October 1974; the warrant was withdrawn before 25 November. A lapsing enforcement right was replaced by a licence that the Court of Appeal treated as permanent. *Established* (dates); the reading that this was the purpose is *strong inference*.
3. **The whole pattern rests on one untested premise: that WGR, and through it BP, held the paper title to Parcel A.** The Court of Appeal wrote "The plaintiffs, BP Properties Ltd, have the paper title to the farm" as an uncontested starting point. No court examined the 25 December 1938 conveyance, the 31 December 1969 conveyance or the 1971 abstract. *Established* (the judgment's own words and scope).
4. **The registration timeline the court relied on is wrong for the farmhouse.** The judgment states that BP had been registered proprietor of the farm "since November 1982". The farmhouse title WA240304 was applied for on 23 February 1983, 31 days before Mary died, and merged into WA231076 on 2 February 1987, 179 days before the appeal judgment. *Contradicted* (the court's sentence against HMLR's history).
5. **The 1939 tenancy, which covers Parcel B only, is absent from the 1987 judgment.** The court went straight from the 1916 "whole of the farm" tenancy to the 1949 surrender. If the 1939 letting replaced the 1916 tenancy, the farmhouse was outside any WGR tenancy from 18 May 1939, and the 1949 unwritten re-grant brought it back 9 years, 8 months and 15 days later, before a 12-year period could complete (18 May 1951). *Strong inference* as a question; *unresolved* as a fact (WGR rental books D696 would settle it).
6. **There was no 1961 registration of the woodland.** The 29 March 1961 deed appears on the woodland register as a burden on land ("subject to the rights contained in"), and that land stayed with WGR, passed to BP in 1969/1982 and to Forest of Cardiff in 1994. *Contradicted*: any account of a 1961 registration in Cardiff's name.
7. **VG41 is not a twelve-year event.** The Commons Registration Act 1965 green test is allotment, custom or **20 years'** use as of right, and the 16 June 1970 date falls inside the national 1967–70 registration window. *Contradicted* as a limitation step; who claimed ownership of the green in 1970 is *unresolved* and important.
8. **The 1928 documentary route and the 1940 limitation route both meet the same obstacle: the 1916 Bute tenancy of "the whole of the farm".** If the house was in that letting and rent was paid to Bute and then WGR, both routes fail for A2 unless the 1916 plan or the rent books show otherwise (§6).
9. **For A1 (woodland) the decisive date is 4 July 1967**, twelve years after WGR took possession on 4 July 1955. Even a valid Williams freehold of A1 would, on the ordinary rules, have been barred then unless an action was brought or the right of action was concealed (§6.4).
10. **The coordinated-playbook hypothesis is partly supported and partly contradicted** (§9): the land, actor and dependency links are real from 1955 to 1987; the claimed twelve-year rhythm is not.

## 5. Could BP use Mary's licence to count her possession as its own against the true owner of Parcel A?

### 5.1 What the court actually did (established by primary record)

Dillon LJ held that the 31 October 1974 letters "unilaterally granted her a licence"; that it would be "artificial to say that Mrs Buckler 'accepted'" them; but that "from the time of her receipt of the letters, Mrs Buckler was in possession of the farmhouse and garden by the licence of BP Properties Ltd, and her possession was no longer adverse within the meaning of section 10 of the 1939 Act." The reasoning has two legs, both explicit:

- Slade J in *Powell v McFarlane*: time "can never run in favour of a person who occupies or uses land by licence of **the owner with the paper title**".
- The position must be judged "from the standpoint of **the person with the paper title**. What could that person have done?"

So use (1), neutralising her own limitation case against BP, is on the record.

### 5.2 Use (2): attributing her occupation to BP against someone else

Use (2) would need three things, and none is in place.

1. **A real holding under BP.** Possession through a licensee (or tenant) counts as the licensor's possession because the occupier holds *under* the licensor: by agreement, attornment or written acknowledgment (compare Real Property Limitation Act 1833 s.14; LA 1939 s.24). The court found no agreement, no acceptance and no consideration. Its "licence" is a construct of limitation law between the occupier and the paper owner, built on what the paper owner could or could not do. It does not make BP a possessor in fact.
2. **A paper owner who is BP.** Both legs of the reasoning are framed around "the owner with the paper title". If the true owner of A2 was someone other than BP (on the family case, the Williams freeholder from 1928, whose estate Mary represented in fact if not by grant), the licence was granted by a stranger. A stranger cannot license the true owner's land, and the *Hughes v Griffin* rule that "possession is never adverse if it can be referred to a lawful title" then points the other way: Mary's occupation would be referable to her own family's title.
3. **Possession adverse to the true owner.** If the true owner was the Williams family, the person in occupation *was* the true owner's family. Occupation by the owner's own family cannot be someone else's adverse possession against that owner.

**Conclusion (legal analysis, strong inference):** BP could not lawfully have counted Mary's occupation as its own possession against a different true owner of Parcel A. The court's construct protected BP only on the footing that BP was the paper owner.

### 5.3 Did BP rely on it anyway? Where it would show

- **In court:** no. BP sued on paper title and the licence; Mr Buckler junior pleaded possessory title only. *Established.*
- **At first registration:** this is the one place use (2) could have operated in practice. Under LRA 1925 s.70(1)(g), the rights of every person in actual occupation bind a first registered proprietor unless enquiry was made and the rights not disclosed. Mary was in actual occupation of A2 on 23 February 1983. If the form 1C declaration or the April 1983 answers described her as BP's licensee, the registrar would have seen no adverse occupier and no live dispute, though BP's own 1974 action, in which she had pleaded limitation, "has never been finally heard". The solicitors certified "no question or doubt affecting the title" for WA231076; the WA240304 declaration has not been read. *Unresolved.* Settled by WA240304 file items 4, 5, 7, 10 and 11 (§11).
- **In conveyancing:** the 23 May 1975 conveyance shows the licence technique for easements ("temporary and revocable licence only of the Vendors") in standard drafting. That is ordinary boilerplate and is **not** evidence of use (2). *Established; neutral.*

### 5.4 Mary's position, and the true owner's

- **Mary as against BP:** her limitation case failed because she did not repudiate the letters. Her solicitors advised her "to do nothing while the 12-year period from the date of the possession order of December 11 1962 expired." The court said the result "may" have been different if she had "plainly told BP Properties Ltd that she did not accept the letters". *Established.* (Mr Wolf MW-11 / reopen R2 is exactly this.)
- **Mary as the true owner (family case):** if the 1928 route is proved, her occupation after 1974 was the owner's own occupation. The licence would bind neither her title nor her estate, and her rights were overriding at first registration. The court never reached this because nobody pleaded it.
- **A third-party true owner** (for example the Thomas heirs, if Bute had sold the freehold to Thomas in 1877 and nothing passed later): BP had no paper title to A2 against that owner, and its 1975 and 1983 registrations rest on a chain going back to Bute. Mary's occupation would be adverse to that owner from 1928 (*strong inference* if the Thomas sale is proved), and that is the 1940 route in §6.

## 6. The 1928 title and the 1940 adverse-possession case, kept separate

### 6.1 Route 1: documentary title from 1928

- **Basis:** Mary's c.1974 statement (GHF-E011-02): the freehold of the farmhouse, buildings and about 10 acres "was sold by the Bute Estate to Daniel Thomas", with rent paid by the Williams family until 1928. *Supported by testimony.*
- **What the statement itself shows:** the freehold went to **Thomas**, not to Williams. For a Williams freehold in 1928 there must have been a further instrument or a binding contract from Thomas (or his estate) to Williams. None is held; the family papers that might have held it were removed in 1950–52 (GHF-E123, *supported by testimony*). *Strong inference* from her own words.
- **If it was only a contract, not a conveyance:** after 1925 an unregistered estate contract is void against a purchaser of the legal estate for money's worth (Land Charges Act 1925 s.13(2)), **but only against a purchaser from the person who held the legal estate.** If Bute had already sold to Thomas in 1877, WGR bought nothing of Parcel A from Bute in 1938, and this defence never arises. *Legal analysis.*
- **Against route 1:** (a) the Bute 1916 tenancy of "the whole of the farm" (GHF-E118). Bute letting the house in 1916 is hard to square with a Thomas freehold, unless the 1877 deal was a quarry *lease*; the Cedwyn record notes the family accepted that the 1916 tenancy covered both areas. (b) The 1876–1912 Bute catalogue entry for 33 acres "formerly part of Great House Farm, for the Llandough Limeworks" (NLW D153/4-8) suggests a lease structure, with Bute keeping the freehold. (c) Quarrying ended by November 1925, not 1928.
- **Verdict:** *Unresolved*, and it turns on GHF-E115/E116/E117 (the 1877 instrument and Bute trustees' receipts: capital, meaning sale, or rent, meaning lease) and the 1916 plan.

### 6.2 Route 2: limitation maturing by 1940

- **Law then in force:** RPLA 1833 ss.2, 3, 8 and 34 as amended by the 1874 Act (12 years); LA 1939 from 1 July 1940 (s.4(3), s.9(2) periodic tenancies, s.10 adverse possession, s.16 extinction).
- **Against Thomas (or his estate):** if the Williams family held under Thomas from year to year without a written lease, Thomas's right accrued at the last rent (RPLA 1833 s.8). Twelve years from a 1928 payment means 1940 (the exact day depends on the receipt), and Thomas's title would then be extinguished (s.34). *Strong inference, conditional* on a Thomas freehold and an unwritten tenancy.
- **Against Bute and WGR:** route 2 fails for any land within the 1916 Bute tenancy while rent under it was paid. The court records arrears "in the mid-1930s and in the late 1940s", which implies a continuing tenancy with payments. Possession referable to that tenancy is not adverse to Bute/WGR (*Hughes v Griffin*). *Established* that the court saw a continuing tenancy; *unresolved* whether it covered A2.
- **The 1939 hinge (new):** if the 18 May 1939 written tenancy of 5.30 acres *replaced* the 1916 yearly tenancy (implied surrender by re-grant), the farmhouse was outside any WGR letting from that date, and without rent for the house a 12-year period would complete on 18 May 1951. The 2 February 1949 unwritten re-grant to Frederick, which the court treated as covering the farmhouse, came before that. The 1952 decision "not to proceed with the letting" left no written terms or plan. *Strong inference as a question; unresolved as a fact.* Test: WGR rental books and plan book (Glamorgan Archives D696, c.1938–44). If rent fell to 8s a month from 1939, the 1939 letting was a replacement.
- **Do not confuse the routes:** route 1 is a title by grant; route 2 is a title by extinction of someone else's. Proving route 2 against Thomas does nothing against Bute/WGR if the house was in the 1916 letting.

### 6.3 What the 1955 seizure does to each route

- **A2:** retained by Mary. The court held joint adverse possession from 2 Feb 1955, cut off by the 1962 action and then by the licence. *Established.*
- **A1 and C:** taken on 4 July 1955. Whoever owned them, WGR was in possession from that date.

### 6.4 The true owner of A1 and C after 1955

On the ordinary rules, an owner dispossessed on 4 July 1955 had until 4 July 1967 to sue (LA 1939 s.4(3)). After that the owner's title to unregistered land was extinguished (s.16). Postponement needs an action based on dishonesty by the defendant, or a right of action concealed by such dishonesty (LA 1939 s.26, whose statutory word we do not repeat here). The removal of title papers in 1950–52 does not conceal a dispossession the family saw happen in 1955, so s.26 is unlikely to help for A1/C unless the 1955 papers misdescribed the land to the court in a way the family could not discover. *Legal analysis; strong inference.* This is the hardest point for any present claim to the woodland, and the reason the 1955 order papers (GHF-E125/E126) matter.

## 7. What the 1987 courts decided, and what they did not

**Decided (established by primary record, GHF-E141):**
- Mr Buckler junior's possessory title by adverse possession failed: for the rest of the farm on the trial judge's findings (acts "too tenuous and equivocal", not appealed); for A2 on appeal.
- Frederick and Mary were "jointly in adverse possession of the farmhouse and garden from the expiration on February 2 1955 of his previous tenancy" (reversing the trial judge's animus finding).
- The 11 December 1962 order was enforceable for 12 years; leave on 19 September 1974 was within jurisdiction; WGR's/BP's title was not extinguished before 31 October 1974.
- The unaccepted letters made her possession licensed from receipt, so time stopped (s.10(2) LA 1939).
- The "wrong company" point (BP Properties licensed before its May 1975 conveyance) failed because the letters showed it "had agreed to buy" (reopen R1 tests this).
- If Mary had acquired a possessory title before registration, it "would have been an overriding interest" and her personal representatives could seek rectification. No grant to her estate had been obtained.

**Assumed, not decided:**
- BP's paper title to the farm ("have the paper title"); the scope of the 1938 and 1969 conveyances; that the 1916 tenancy, the 1949 tenancy and the 1955 order covered "the whole of the farm" including A2.

**Misstated:**
- "since November 1982 they have been the registered proprietors". True of WA231076; not of the farmhouse (WA240304, applied 23 Feb 1983, merged 2 Feb 1987). *Contradicted.*

**Not before the court:**
- The 1877/1928 documentary title (never pleaded; "No such documents were, however, ever produced").
- The 1939 tenancy and its plan (not mentioned).
- The 1974 action's ownership plea on its merits (adjourned, "never been finally heard").
- What HMLR was told in 1982–83; the 2 February 1987 amalgamation.
- A1, B and C on appeal (only A2 was appealed).

The court was competent to decide possession and limitation between BP and the occupier, and it did. It was not a trial of the 1928 root title, and it decided nothing for or against a Williams documentary freehold. Equally, its possession order and its licence holding stand as binding between the parties unless set aside.

## 9. The coordinated-playbook hypothesis

**The method tested:** characterise the family's occupation as tenancy or permission, then use that characterisation in possession orders, deeds and registration to build or consolidate another party's title.

### Strongest evidence for
1. **Unbroken actor chain and dependency.** WGR's 1949 tenancy (court: whole farm) → 1955 order and execution (all but A2) → 1959 and 1965 tenancy offers on A2 → 1962 order on A2 → benefit of that order carried by the 1969 conveyance → revived by BPPT in 1974 → licence letters → 1975 split → 1982–83 registrations → 1987 amalgamation → 1987 judgment resting on paper title and licence. Each step depended on the one before. *Established.*
2. **Every step was a characterisation of occupation**, never a test of title: tenancy (1949), a reported tenancy attempt (1954, GHF-E182), tenant's order (1955), tenancy offers (1959, 1965), licence (1974). The only title documents in the chain (1938, 1969, 1971) never reached a court. *Established.*
3. **Knowledge.** WGR knew of Mary's ownership assertion by 1959 at the latest (court); the county council by November 1958 (press); BPPT by 3 July 1974 (her defence). The registration in 1983 came while that defence was unheard, with a "no question or doubt" certificate on the main title. *Established; disclosure in 1983 unresolved.*
4. **Timing at the moments that mattered:** leave on two days' notice with 83 days left; letters with 41 days left; a farmhouse registration 31 days before her death; amalgamation 179 days before the appeal judgment. *Established dates.*
5. **The 1975 structure** kept development value away from the house carrying the licence. *Established (register); purpose: strong inference.*

### Strongest evidence against
1. **The twelve-year rhythm fails on the dates.** 1949→1961 is 12 y 1 m 27 d with no legal period behind it; 1958→1970 is 11 y 6 m to 12 y 1 m and VG41's test is 20 years; 1974→1983 is 8 y 3 m 23 d; 1974→1987 is 12 y 3 m 2 d with nothing ending then; 1988→2000 has no recorded event. *Contradicted* as a mechanism.
2. **There is an ordinary explanation for every corporate step:** a landlord recovering from a non-paying tenant (1953–55); a purchaser enforcing a judgment it bought with the land (1974); a group moving an awkward asset between companies after a press campaign (1974–75); compulsory registration (1982) followed by tidying the second title (1983, 1987). *Plausible alternative.*
3. **The 1916 tenancy** (court record) is a pre-WGR, pre-dispute document describing the whole farm as let. It is the single strongest record against the parcel case, and nothing in it was manufactured by the later actors. *Established (recital).*
4. **VG41 and the 1961 deed** involve different actors (a county council; a brick company and Cardiff City) and different legal mechanisms; neither is a step in BP's title. *Established.*
5. **No document yet shows a false statement to a court or the Registry.** The gaps (1938 plan, 1969 deed, 1983 declaration) are gaps, not proof. *Unresolved.*

**What would prove a connection:** a form 1C declaration silent about Mary's occupation or her plea; a 1938 plan excluding the house; a 1969 plan including land the 1938 deed did not; a WGR or BP file note linking the licence to the 11 December 1974 deadline; a link between the Penarth agent and WGR.
**What would disprove it:** a 1938 plan including A1/A2; a 1983 declaration disclosing her occupation and the unheard action; 1877 trustees' accounts showing rent only (a quarry lease).

**Assessment:** a connected course of conduct by one landlord line (WGR → BPPT → BP Properties) that repeatedly re-characterised occupation and never tested title is *established*. That it was planned as a single scheme to take Parcel A by registration is *unresolved*: the documents that would show intent (1983 declaration, 1938 and 1969 plans) are the ones not yet read. The twelve-year cadence is *contradicted*, and should be dropped from public pages in favour of the dated dependency chain above.
