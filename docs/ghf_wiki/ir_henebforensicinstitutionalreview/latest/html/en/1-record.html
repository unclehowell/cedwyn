Documented involvement and implications#

Expand the documented findings and reparations relevance

What the record shows, without reservation:

  • Held material was not released until after formal pressure. The full HER source file, including the Suggett December 1988 note, existed in Heneb’s custody. It was digitised and uploaded only after the EIR request of 8 September 2026 and further correspondence. The first substantive reply (6 October) did not supply it. That is incomplete first response under the Environmental Information Regulations.

  • 38-year delay in public access. A primary source note written in December 1988 about a site demolished amid public controversy remained undigitised and offline until October 2026. For a contested heritage site this is inadequate archival practice.

  • No explanation of process or gaps. The 14 October email states the file “has now been digitised and uploaded” but provides no account of earlier search methodology, why digitisation was delayed, or whether any material was lost, weeded or never catalogued.

  • Classification remains narrower than the source material itself. GGAT02038s continues to be headed and classified “POST MEDIEVAL – Farm” even though the now-released Suggett note records possible medieval fabric, reported medieval ironwork, and the historic character of the site next to the parish church. The public record does not accurately surface the content of its own source file.

  • Release under challenge. Disclosure of the source file occurred only after sustained formal requests and after a complaint about HER identifiers had already been lodged. That sequence is a matter of record.

  • 16 October schedule response leaves a practical gap. Generic “publicly available on Archwilio” and collective “not held” answers do not identify the specific record, field or document that answers each numbered item in the 19 September schedule. The former GGAT paper archive at Baglan is confirmed but no catalogue, index or inspection procedure is supplied. Sections 194–195 of the Historic Environment (Wales) Act 2023 and Heneb’s EIR status are unanswered.

Liability-relevant note for reparations purposes: The documented failures are process failures under EIR (incomplete first response; delayed release of held environmental information) and archival practice. They establish that held material was not made available promptly. They do not, on the present record, establish that the Suggett note itself was withheld in order to influence the 1988 listing or demolition decisions (the note post-dates the demolition). The ongoing complaint on HER classification remains open (five-week target from 29 September 2026).

1974 survey, Suggett note and delayed source-file access: relevance to the 1988–89 proceedings#

The distinction is between the HER entry’s public citation of R. F. Suggett’s December 1988 note and access to the underlying source-file material. Heneb’s October 2026 correspondence reportedly stated that the source file had been digitised and uploaded to Archwilio. This supports a documented question about access and timing; it does not, by itself, prove that the existence of the note was concealed or that any custodian acted unlawfully.

The HER entry also refers to H. J. Thomas’s 1974 survey, which reportedly identified historic architectural features and considered a possible medieval core, while noting access limitations. The original survey and the Suggett note are distinct records and must not be conflated. The evidential question for the 1988 demolition and the family’s Strasbourg application (14464/88, inadmissibility decision dated 14 April 1989) is whether the survey or its findings were known to the family and their representatives, Cadw, and relevant decision-makers; what was submitted to the Commission; and whether any missing material could have affected a relevant argument.

Limit: historic significance alone did not automatically confer Grade II listing or establish that demolition was unlawful. The surviving material currently identified does not establish what the Commission received or that it would have reached a different decision. Compare the original 1974 survey, the Suggett note and its source-file metadata, the Cadw listing/spot-listing record, the ECHR application and surviving decision, and the planning records. Related records: GHF-E137 — 1974 H. J. Thomas survey; GHF-E183 — Heneb source-file request and replies; GHF-E184 — Heneb schedule of records and response; GHF-E145 — Strasbourg application and decision.

Research update — 1974 survey, HER source-file access and Strasbourg (17 October 2026)#

The HER entry GGAT02038s already cited R. F. Suggett’s December 1988 note and referred to H. J. Thomas’s 1974 survey. Heneb reportedly said on 14 October 2026 that the underlying source file had been digitised and uploaded to Archwilio; its 16 October response addressed the schedule of records. The key distinction is between the public HER citation and the later availability of the underlying source-file material. The present evidence does not establish that the existence of the Suggett note was concealed.

The original 1974 survey predates demolition on 6 December 1988; Suggett’s note postdates the demolition. The research question is what the original survey and note actually record, and when those records were available to Cadw, the planning authorities, the family and their representatives. Historic significance does not by itself prove that statutory listing was required or that demolition was unlawful.

Evidence links: GHF-E137 — 1974 survey; GHF-E183 — source-file request and replies; GHF-E184 — schedule and response.
