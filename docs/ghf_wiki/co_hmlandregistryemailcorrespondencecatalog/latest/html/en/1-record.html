Dear HM Land Registry Customer Support,

I am writing to raise a formal concern that the registered title to land formerly occupied by my family, first registered in the name of BP Properties Ltd in or around November 1982, may not accurately reflect the legal and factual position that existed at the time of first registration.

I respectfully request guidance on the appropriate procedure to apply for the entry of a notice or restriction, and/or for rectification or alteration of the register, on the basis that an occupied and disputed interest was not recorded.

*Summary of relevant facts (supported by court records and contemporaneous newspaper reporting):*

*Long-term occupation and asserted ownership*
My grandmother and family before her, occupied the farmhouse and surrounding land openly and continuously for centuries, but certainly decades prior to registration; without payment of rent and while asserting ownership. The property was maintained as our family home and farm.
2.
*No conclusive determination of title in 1974*
Proceedings in July 1974 relating to possession and title were adjourned and never substantively determined. Further proceedings in October 1974 were interrupted by correspondence from BP entities offering a purported licence to occupy. As a result, there was no final judicial determination in 1974 establishing BP’s title on the merits.
3.
*Express rejection of the purported licence*
My grandmother expressly rejected the licence, stating that the land was hers. This rejection was acknowledged in later proceedings, but as having been communicated to BP Pension Trust Limited, not the claimant in BP Vs Buckler 1987 i.e. BP Properties Ltd.
4.
*Inconsistent treatment of BP corporate identity*
Subsequently the rejection was discounted on the basis that it was addressed to BP Pension Trust Limited rather than BP Properties Ltd. However, in dismissing my father’s appeal to that case almost immidiately afterwards, the court and same judge, treated BP Pension Trust Limited and BP Properties Ltd as “effectively the same entity for substantive purposes”. This inconsistency resulted in the rejection being acknowledged but disregarded when it favoured BP, while corporate equivalence was relied upon to defeat the appeal.
5.
*Forced removal and restraint (not abandonment)*
My family did not abandon the property or leave by our own accord, nor did we fully understand what or why this was happening. In 1987, my father and all of us, his dependants, were forcibly removed. Contemporary newspaper reports state that he was also taken from a hospital bed, detained without criminal charge, and made subject to a restraining order preventing him from returning to the farm. He was not even permitted to retrieve personal belongings.
6.
*Demolition following enforced exclusion*
While my father was later restrained from returning to occupy or defend our home, the farmhouse was demolished. This enforced exclusion and demolition rendered continued occupation impossible and removed the physical evidence of long-term residence.
7.

*Registration despite occupation and unresolved dispute*

BP Properties Ltd was registered as proprietor in 1982, at a time when:

– the land was occupied by my family,
– ownership was actively disputed,
– no court had conclusively determined title, and
– cessation of occupation occurred only later through enforcement and restraint, not voluntary departure.

No notice, restriction, or record of the disputed and occupied interest appears to have been entered on the register at first registration.

*Purpose of this correspondence*

I am not seeking to relitigate historic proceedings at this stage. I am seeking to ensure that the land register accurately reflects interests and disputes that existed at the time of first registration, and that the absence of abandonment and the circumstances of enforced removal are properly understood.

I respectfully request advice on:

– the appropriate procedure to apply for a notice or restriction reflecting an omitted interest; and/or
– the process for seeking rectification or alteration of the register under the Land Registration legislation applicable at the time.

Please advise on the relevant forms, evidential requirements, and next steps.

Regards,

Requester

Dear HM Land Registry – Fraud Review Team,

Sirs/Ma’am,I am writing to request a review of the integrity of a land registration made in or around *1982*, which later underpinned the proceedings commonly referred to as *BP v Buckler (1987)*.

My concern is to raise reasonable grounds of belief that the 1982 registration *may have been obtained or maintained through
misrepresentation, non-disclosure, or procedural irregularity*, resulting in a register entry that did not reflect a title definitively determined by a court of law.

In summary:

*No definitive judicial determination of ownership existed at the time of registration*
Prior to the 1982 registration, the courts had not conclusively determined ownership of the land.
–
Proceedings in *July 1974* were adjourned.
–
An appeal pursued by my grandmother on *31 October 1974* was effectively neutralised following two letters sent by BP-associated entities, rather than by a substantive judicial ruling on title.
–
Ownership was *asserted*, not adjudicated.
2.
*Competing assertions of ownership were unresolved*
BP asserted ownership, but so did my grandmother, who maintained possession and occupation until her death in *1983*, after the registry entry had already been filed.
At no point prior to her death was there a final court decision extinguishing hers (or our families) claim or possession.
3.
*Inconsistent treatment of BP entities in later proceedings* In the 1987 proceedings, the judge indicated that correspondence had not been replied to because it was sent by a different BP entity (BP Properties rather than BP Trust).
However, in my father’s related appeal, the same judge treated *BP Trust and BP Properties as effectively the same entity*.
This inconsistency meant that:
–
We were said to have lost because of non-reply, while
–
The judgments simultaneously acknowledge that a reply was in fact made and that the entities were treated as one.
This contradiction is material, as it goes directly to whether BP’s asserted title was properly established.
4.
*Possession and long occupation were not displaced by lawful process* My family had occupied the land openly and continuously for generations without challenge. We consistently asserted ownership and, in any event, possession sufficient to ground squatter’s or possessory rights. Our removal was *not voluntary*; it was effected by force and exclusion, not by a final determination of title.
5.
*Subsequent events reinforce the concern*
Following the appeal, my father’s application to the European Court of Human Rights was blocked, the farm was demolished preventing re-entry, and he also died thereafter in *1991*.
Shortly after his death in 1994, the land was excavated and items of archaeological value were removed, despite prior knowledge of Roman remains on the property (including a Roman soldier previously uncovered within the living structure).

Taken together, these matters raise serious questions as to whether the 1982 registration accurately reflected the legal position at the time, or whether it relied on assertions and correspondence that had not been validated by a court and that were later treated inconsistently.

I respectfully request that HM Land Registry review whether:

–

The statutory requirements for registration were properly satisfied in 1982; and
–

The register may be inaccurate or was obtained by mistake, misrepresentation, or material non-disclosure.

I am able to supply supporting documents, title numbers, correspondence, and a concise timeline if required. But everything already on public record indicates and supports everything I’ve highlighted in this email.

Regards,

Requester
