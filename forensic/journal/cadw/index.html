6 November 2026Journal entryNot held (date of destruction); five internal documents releasedref ATISN 26-0009 (1988 listing file: disposal or transfer record)

Letter of 6 November 2026 from Cadw's Head of Historic Environment Inspectorate (email 12:53 UTC) answering ATISN 26-0009, received 10 October 2026. The request asked for any record of what happened to the file on the 6 December 1988 decision not to list Great House Farm (inventory, closure note, transfer list, disposal authority, retention schedule, destruction record or note of the search), the date and responsible unit for any disposal or transfer decision, and, if neither exists, a statement distinguishing a contemporaneous disposal decision from a present-day inability to locate the file, with the repositories and systems searched. The reply states that, after a search of paper and electronic records, no information is held 'that indicates when the file ... was destroyed', and releases five documents: Records Management emails of 28 May to 2 June 2026 (Doc 1), 12 to 20 August 2026 (Doc 2) and 27 to 29 October 2026 (Doc 3), references for six files of potential interest identified under ATISN 27021 (Doc 4), and a 2007 extract from Cadw's database of old resurvey file numbers (Doc 5). It refers to the ATISN 27021 response and internal review for the extent of searches and gives the Information Commissioner as the next route.

Assessment (our reading): An on-time answer that discloses more than most 'not held' replies: the internal emails show the file was reported destroyed on 1 June 2026, and that post-merger practice kept unsuccessful listing files for about three years. The gap is the record behind that report: no log, authority or date of destruction is produced, item 3 is answered by cross-reference, the six related files have no stated status, and the 1993 date attached to CL 72/381 in the resurvey database is not explained.

What they did

  • Answered on 6 November 2026, the twentieth working day after receipt on 10 October.
  • Released internal emails showing how the position was reached, with officials' names redacted. In Doc 1 (1 June 2026) Records Management answers a request for file CL72/381 parts 1 and 2 with: 'Unfortunately, the file has been destroyed.'
  • Released Records Management's August 2026 view (Doc 2): post-merger retention schedules kept unsuccessful listing or spot-listing cases for about three years before disposal; the retention arrangements before Cadw's merger with Welsh Government cannot be stated exactly.
  • Released the six file references of potential interest (Doc 4) and a resurvey-database extract (Doc 5, dated 12 April 2007) listing file CL 72/381 for the community of Penarth with the date 21/01/1993 in its 'signed' column.

What they did not do

  • No destruction record, disposal authority, schedule entry or date is given for CL72/381; Doc 1 records the file as destroyed without citing a log or record of the destruction.
  • The reply frames the answer around when the file was destroyed; the request asked whether the file remained held, was transferred or was destroyed, and for the record of whichever applied.
  • Item 3 (distinguishing a contemporaneous disposal decision from a present-day inability to locate the file, with the repositories, indices and systems searched for this request) is not answered separately; the reply points to the ATISN 27021 internal review.
  • The current status (retained, transferred, destroyed or not traced) of the six files listed in Doc 4 is not stated.
  • No internal review is offered for this request; the reply gives the Information Commissioner as the route on the ground that ATISN 27021 has already been reviewed.

Fair note

  • Section 1(1)(a) of the Freedom of Information Act 2000 is met by a statement that information is not held; where no item-level destruction log was kept, a 'not held' answer on the date of destruction is the expected answer, and short-retention casework files from the late 1980s and 1990s were often disposed of without one.
  • Releasing the internal search correspondence goes beyond a bare 'not held' and is the kind of advice and assistance section 16 and the section 45 Code of Practice encourage.
  • The section 45 Code of Practice (2018, part 5) recommends an internal review procedure for any request; the Information Commissioner may accept a complaint without one where the same matter has already been reviewed. ATISN 26-0009 is a new request with a distinct scope.
  • The 'signed' date in a resurvey database may record when a resurvey list was signed rather than the opening or closing date of the file; what the 1993 date refers to is not checked.

Open questions

  • What record supports the 1 June 2026 statement that file CL72/381 parts 1 and 2 has been destroyed?
  • Does file CL 72/381, dated 21/01/1993 in the resurvey database, relate to the 1988 decision, and how does that date sit with an approximate three-year retention period?
  • What is the current status of each of the six files listed in Doc 4?
  • Will Welsh Government offer an internal review of ATISN 26-0009?

5 November 2026Journal entryRequest logged from 29 October; the 2 July email not treated as a requestref ATISN 26-0141 (and ATISN 26-0143)

Email of 5 November 2026, 15:15 UTC, from the Chief Inspector of Cadw acknowledging ATISN 26-0141: the requester's 29 October request for recorded information about furniture, furnishings, antiques, fixtures, heirlooms and other movable property originating from Great House Farm, to be searched under the alternative names supplied the same day. The acknowledgement records the request as received on 29 October with a response due by 26 November 2026, and states that the requester's earlier email of 2 July 2026 asking for the same information 'was only copied to Welsh Government's Freedom of Information Officer and was not therefore treated as an information request to Welsh Government at that time'. A second acknowledgement from the same official at 15:53 UTC (ATISN 26-0143) logs a separate 29 October request about 1926 and 1938 conveyances, also due 26 November.

Assessment (our reading): The 29 October request is logged and its scope restated closely. The open point is the start date: Welsh Government treats 29 October, not 2 July, as the date of receipt, on the ground that its FOI address was a copy recipient; the Act's definition of a request does not on its face turn on that distinction, while the email's wording and distribution leave room for the reading Welsh Government has taken.

What they did

  • Logged the furniture request as ATISN 26-0141, received 29 October, due 26 November 2026.
  • Restated the scope in terms that track the 29 October email, including both 16:52 emails listing alternative names.
  • Stated that the 2 July email had been a copy to the Freedom of Information Officer and was not treated as a request.

What they did not do

  • The 2 July email was headed 'Freedom of Information Act 2000 Request', was addressed to Cardiff Council with the Welsh Government FOI address among eight copy recipients, gave a name and address for correspondence, described the information, and asked any authority more likely to hold it to transfer the request. No acknowledgement or reply from Welsh Government between 2 July and 29 October is found in the requester's mailbox.
  • The restatement does not repeat the site identifiers or the exclusions (Llandough Farm opposite the hospital, Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge, Llandow, Aberthin) given in the 29 October email.
  • The 2 July categories of internal reports and legal advice, family-named correspondence, and correspondence with the Bute Estate, museums and national bodies are not in the restatement; the 29 October email itself described the request as limited to inventories, transfer records, provenance, photographs and correspondence.

Fair note

  • Section 8(1) of the Freedom of Information Act 2000 describes a request as one in writing, stating the applicant's name and an address for correspondence, and describing the information requested; the section does not distinguish an addressee from a copy recipient. Whether a copied email is a request to each recipient is a question the Act's text does not settle on its face.
  • The 2 July email named Cardiff Council and Cardiff Castle in its specific items and was sent to eight bodies at once, so a recipient could read its copy as for information. Another copy recipient, Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales, logged it as a request (FOI 2026-020) and replied.
  • The restatement of the 29 October scope is close and accurate to that email.

Open questions

  • On what basis was a written request received by the Freedom of Information Officer on 2 July not treated as a request, and was any record made of that decision?
  • Will the search apply the 29 October site identifiers and exclusions?
