Email of 9 November 2026 (12:40 UTC) from the Council's scrutiny officer answering the requester's email of 2 November 2026, which asked which Topic Suggestion Form is current and where it should go, whether the earlier wording could be used as the submission, and when topic suggestions are next considered. Assessment (our reading): A helpful, procedural reply: it corrects an out-of-date route, accepts the existing wording and gives a dated window (screening within 5 working days, agenda 1 December 2026). Nothing is decided yet; the outcome depends on the submission and the Chair's screening.

What they did

  • Explained that the Word form found on the Council's website carried a predecessor's contact and has been replaced by an online web form on the Council's customer portal, and gave the link.
  • Confirmed that the wording of the requester's earlier email can be used as the submission.
  • Set out the timing: requests are screened with the Committee Chair within 5 working days of receipt, so that an agreed item can go on the next available agenda, which in this case would be 1 December 2026.
  • Asked where on the Council website the older form was found, so that it can be traced.
  • Apologised for the delay.

What they did not do

  • Did not say in terms whether the online form routes to the Place Scrutiny Committee recommended on 26 October 2026 (the old Word form's list of committees did not include it).
  • Gave no view on whether the topic is likely to be accepted; the screening with the Chair is still to come.

Fair note

  • A scrutiny topic suggestion is not a request under FOIA or the EIR, so no statutory clock applies; a seven-day turnaround that falls on the officer's first day back is prompt.
  • Asking where an out-of-date form was found is ordinary housekeeping and helps the Council remove a stale page; it puts nothing new on the requester.
  • The reply moves the matter forward: a named route, the existing wording accepted, and a dated committee window.

Open questions
