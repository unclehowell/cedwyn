4 November 2026Journal entryOne point answered; alteration and indemnity declined; court named as forumref HMLR Wales Office Z084NQV (title WA231076)

Letter of 4 November 2026 from HM Land Registry, Wales Office (ref Z084NQV, signed by an Assistant Land Registrar), headed for title WA231076, land on the north side of Leckwith Road, Llandough, with Persimmon Homes (Wales) Limited shown as proprietor. It answers, point by point, the requester's letter of 12 August 2026 about the surviving first-registration documents, and the further correspondence of 15 October 2026 asking for the register to be altered or an indemnity considered. The letter was placed in HMLR's online portal; the email of 4 November 2026, 11:07 UTC, carried only a link to it.

Assessment (our reading): A short final position. HMLR gives one concrete answer, that the 'no question or doubt' statement is in the 1982 first-registration application for the fields title, and closes the administrative route on alteration and indemnity, naming the court as the forum. The exact certified words, and whether the farmhouse application carries the same statement, remain unseen; official copies of the two applications are the documents that would show them.

What they did

  • Answered point 2, which asked where the solicitors' certificate that they were not aware of any question or doubt affecting the title appears: "The statement is contained in the application for first registration (1E)". The surviving Form 1E for WA231076 is dated 30 November 1982 (item 13 on HMLR's list of retained documents).
  • Answered points 1 and 3 to 7 by referring to the list of retained documents that HMLR has already supplied as available for inspection.
  • On the 15 October correspondence, restated that no application for alteration or indemnity may be considered, referring to its letters of 19 April and 25 May 2026, and to its letter of 23 March 2026 for the history of the title and its first registration.
  • Stated that the Registrar cannot interpret or explain the deeds or give legal advice, that there is nothing further it can usefully add, and that "The appropriate forum for any further action is the Court and not HM Land Registry."

What they did not do

  • Did not set out the wording of the certificate as it appears in the 1982 Form 1E; only an official copy of that form shows the exact words certified.
  • Is headed for WA231076 only and does not say whether a similar statement appears in the application for the farmhouse title WA240304 (Form 1C, February 1983).
  • Did not give a fresh decision on the 15 October application; it relies on the reasons in the April and May 2026 letters.
  • Replied about 12 weeks after the letter of 12 August 2026, which had been chased on 16 September 2026.

Fair note

  • This is registry correspondence, not a request under FOIA or the EIR, so no statutory reply period applies to it.
  • Under the Land Registration Act 2002 (Schedules 4 and 8) the court can order alteration of the register and decide questions of indemnity. Once the registry has declined, directing a disputed matter to the court is an established position, and the registry does not give legal advice to any party.
  • Answering point 2 directly, and naming the document that holds the statement, is a concrete and checkable answer that the earlier correspondence did not give.
  • HMLR had set out its reasons on alteration and indemnity earlier in the year, so a short reference back is consistent with its earlier letters.

Open questions

  • What exact words of the certificate appear in the 30 November 1982 Form 1E (item 13)?
  • Does the application for the farmhouse title WA240304 (Form 1C, February 1983) carry a similar statement?
  • When can the listed documents be inspected, and can official copies of items 13 and 11 be supplied?

4 November 2026Journal entryClock paused for identity; new date 6 November 2026ref HMLR subject access request D260928

Email of 4 November 2026, 15:20 UTC, from HM Land Registry's Information Rights Team (ref D260928), replying to the requester's emails of 2 and 3 November 2026 about the subject access request of 28 September 2026 (titles WA231076 and WA240304). HMLR had asked for identity evidence on 27 October 2026, one day before the original date of 28 October 2026.

Assessment (our reading): A procedural step, handled courteously. The clock pause is lawful, but the identity request came late in the month, which moved the date from 28 October to 6 November 2026. The substantive response is due tomorrow.

What they did

  • Confirmed that the identity evidence supplied has now been destroyed.
  • Gave a new statutory date for the reply of 6 November 2026, "taking into account the time paused until we received your ID evidence".

What they did not do

  • Did not yet provide any personal data or say what searches are under way.
  • Did not state the date on which the identity evidence was received, from which the new date is calculated.
  • Did not explain why identity evidence was first asked for on 27 October 2026, a month after the request.

Fair note

  • Under UK GDPR Article 12(3) a controller has one month to respond, and under Article 12(6) it may ask for information needed to confirm identity; ICO guidance treats the month as running from receipt of that information. Pausing the clock for identity is lawful.
  • ICO guidance expects identity checks to be raised promptly after a request arrives; raising them near the end of the month lengthens the overall wait.
  • Confirming destruction of the identity evidence, and stating a clear new date, is good data-protection practice.

Open questions

  • Will the response arrive by 6 November 2026?
  • Will it cover both titles and all the records described in the 28 September request?
