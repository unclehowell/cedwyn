This page examines HM Land Registry's handling of historic registration records, the current information-rights routes and the researcher's ability to pursue ordinary public-record enquiries. It separates what the record states from the researcher's concerns and from matters still requiring proof.

1. Purpose, independence and public-interest basis

The researcher states that the historical work began decades before he suspected dishonesty or concealment. Its original purpose was to reconstruct and retell the family's history at a planned home and museum in Argentina, using Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr as the subject of a heritage reconstruction. He states his suspicion of dishonesty or concealment developed later and that he publicly announced it in June 2025.

The research has therefore two distinct phases and purposes: (1) long-running historical, genealogical, architectural and archival research undertaken for a heritage and museum project; and (2) a later documentary investigation into possible irregularities in title registration, litigation and the treatment of records. The second phase must not be projected backwards as the motive for the first.

Source qualification: the researcher's account of the decades-long origin and June 2025 announcement is recorded as his statement. A dated copy of the June 2025 public announcement has not yet been located in the sources inspected for this review. Recovery task: locate and preserve the original post, email, article or archived page so the date and wording can be independently cited.

The researcher wishes to act in his ordinary capacity as a member of the public conducting historical research and making FOI/EIR and other records requests. The standard sought is equal, ordinary treatment: no preferential access, no disadvantage, no prejudgment and no different treatment because of his family connection to people involved in the historical dispute. This assessment does not request special treatment or privileged access. It asks whether requests are correctly routed, accurately classified, acknowledged and answered under the same rules applied to comparable members of the public.

The researcher's family relationship to historic parties is relevant biographical context, but it is not itself proof of discriminatory treatment. Any legal discrimination assertion would require a legally relevant protected ground and evidence of causation; no such finding is made here.

2. Assessment method and evidence language

Each proposition is assigned one of these states: documented (a source records it); officially stated (an institution states it); researcher-reported (the user's account, not independently verified); inference / working hypothesis (a reasoned possibility requiring tests); unresolved (the record is incomplete or inconsistent); or not established (the available material does not prove it).

A missing document, an unanswered question, a mistaken administrative route, a title-plan anomaly or a refusal to determine ownership is not, by itself, proof of dishonesty or concealment. Equally, the fact that HMLR has completed a complaint stage does not answer every separate factual question about the content of its surviving file.

This is an institution-specific assessment. It does not assess BP, the courts, the Welsh Government, Cadw/Heneb, GGAT, police, local authorities or professional firms as actors. They appear only where necessary to understand the HMLR record. Their conduct must be reviewed separately against their own records and responsibilities.

7. Dishonesty, concealment and government involvement — evidential gate

The researcher reports that suspected dishonesty/concealment is the prevailing working suspicion among collaborators. No formal consensus document or independently verified consensus record has been located. This is therefore recorded as a reported working view, not an established institutional or expert finding.

At the date of this assessment, the material reviewed has not established dishonesty, concealment or government involvement. It has identified questions about the 1969 root deed, 1971 abstract, first-registration examination, exact parcel extent, identity evidence, first-registration class and 1987 amalgamation. Those questions merit source-led investigation. They do not, without more, prove that anyone acted dishonestly or concealed information.

Dishonesty Act 2006 — examples of relevant legal elements

  • Section 2, false representation: a representation must be false or misleading, the person must know it is or might be false/misleading, and act dishonestly with the prescribed intention to make a gain or cause loss/risk of loss.
  • Section 3, failure to disclose: there must be a legal duty to disclose, dishonest failure to disclose, and the prescribed intent.
  • Section 4, abuse of position: a relevant position with an expectation to safeguard another's financial interests, dishonesty, and the prescribed intention must be evidenced.

See Dishonesty Act 2006, sections 2–4. Other civil, property, public-law or criminal concealment routes may have different elements and must be assessed by a qualified lawyer against the facts; there is no single free-standing “suspicion threshold” which turns every unresolved anomaly into a legal trigger.

Government involvement is a separate proposition

Government shareholding in BP, policy oversight, public funding, an institutional relationship, an official's awareness or a public body's possession of records would not by themselves establish government participation in wrongdoing. A defensible proposition would need to identify: the precise official or body; the role and legal authority exercised; a specific act, instruction, decision or omission; what the actor knew at the relevant time; the record proving it; and a causal connection to the stated conduct or result.

Project / operation trigger rule — CLOSED unless evidence passes the gate.
No project, operation, escalation or formal assertions trigger is to activate solely because of suspicion, a reported consensus, a missing record, an unanswered FOI, an irregularity, a complaint rejection or the fact that a historic state relationship existed. Trigger only after (1) a precisely framed proposition; (2) authenticated primary evidence with provenance; (3) the relevant legal elements individually mapped to that evidence; (4) material contrary evidence and reasonable alternative explanations assessed; and (5) independent written legal review concludes that the applicable threshold is met. Until then, retain the matter as a research hypothesis and keep operational triggers inactive.
