Forensic update — 10 November 2026

Current institutional assessment · correspondence reviewed 10 May 2025–10 November 2026

Current assessment: The earlier “nothing disclosed” label was inaccurate and is withdrawn. The National Archives has provided meaningful assistance and partial disclosure: a specialist archivist inspected the original Llandough manorial return in HMC9/311; TNA identified the House of Lords Judicial Papers A–C 1–63 and supplied transfer/release paperwork; its expanded FOI search identified National Farm Survey and Agricultural Executive Committee research routes; and its FOI team identified the relevant 1910 Valuation Office map as IR 131/4/96. The latest response, on 10 November, is a separate refusal under FOIA section 14(2) of a repeat request about the still-unidentified IR 58 field book. That refusal is not a “not held” finding and does not establish that the field book is absent.

1. What TNA has done and how it has helped

  1. Initial records guidance and search limits — 6–7 February 2026. Remote Enquiries (Robert Fleming, CAS-313954-Y6C0H3) explained that Discovery keyword searches cover catalogue titles and descriptions, not the contents of the records, and directed the requester to land-ownership, Chancery and appeal-case guides, the Land Registry and local archives. TNA welcomed an in-person search at Kew and described its paid-search option. Principal Records Specialist Charlotte Smith then searched the limited judicial series J 157, J 83 and J 84 and reported no relevant records in those series. She also explained that TNA does not generally annotate or review records because of unresolved disputes, and gave advice on modern records selection, retention schedules and the interpretation of archival silences. These were limited-series searches and general policy guidance, not a search of every possible government or court record.
  2. Specialist manorial-record investigation — 24 February–3 March 2026. Philip Gale, Head of the Collections and Cultural Property Team, explained that the Manorial Documents Register describes the content, extent and location of manorial records; it is not a register of land ownership or title descent. He identified the principal repositories for Llandough manorial records as the National Library of Wales, Glamorgan Archives and West Glamorgan Archives. He then personally inspected the original Manorial Documents Committee returns in HMC9/311 and sent an image of the single return for the Manor of Llandough. He reported that this original return contained less information than the online MDR entry and suggested that some surviving manorial records may not have been in the NLW's custody when the original survey was made. On 3 March he further confirmed that, apart from the pre-war survey returns, TNA held only the online MDR information; it held no other old index cards. He identified D/D B M/3, an 1884–1937 court book held at Glamorgan Archives, and NRA 581, a 370-page paper catalogue of Stuart/Bute estate records deposited at the NLW in July 1950, which can be consulted at TNA by prior appointment. He also explained that Bute estate records were dispersed and that bundle-level catalogue descriptions may conceal individual documents. His suggestion that the farm may have been leasehold rather than copyhold by 1900 was a research possibility, not a finding about this property.
  3. Broadened FOI catalogue search and partial disclosure — 20 August–23 September 2026. After seeking clarification under FOIA section 1(3), TNA said its 12 August search had initially used four property/location terms within the PRO and RW catalogue series and produced no results. In its 23 September response to CAS-347296-M0V4Y1 (CRM:007302479), TNA said it had then searched those terms plus twelve further terms in a wider catalogue search. It expressly confirmed that it held some relevant information and disclosed some of it. It identified Judicial Papers A–C 1–63, including material relating to BP Properties Ltd and BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2, under the House of Lords as Court of Appeal. It confirmed that the record was open to the public at Kew, with four working days’ preparation needed for production. It also supplied three redacted files: an “On Discovery” release schedule, a high-level delivery schedule, and a Collect 27 Series 11 manifest received from the Parliamentary Archives. TNA identified the associated reference as YHL/PO/JO/10/11/2536. The schedules record the catalogue release date as 20 May 2026; that is a Discovery release date, not proof of the original transfer or accession date.
  4. Further archival routes identified in the same FOI response. TNA said National Farm Survey material for the 1940s was likely to exist, although it could not all be searched online by farm name; it directed the requester to the 1941–43 National Farm Survey research guide. It also identified Agricultural Executive Committee minutes and papers for Glamorgan under Discovery browse reference C42711, but said those records could not be searched online by farm name and would require paid research by TNA staff. These are promising search routes, not confirmation that an individual Great House Farm record has been located.
  5. Valuation Office map and field-book guidance — 12–14 October 2026. In response to a location-only enquiry, TNA identified the map it believed covered the property as IR 131/4/96, Discovery reference C1667398. It explained that it could not identify the corresponding IR 58 field book without the income-tax parish and the farm's assessment number, which it expected the map to help establish. It checked the Genealogist digitised-record site and said its coverage appeared not to include Glamorgan. TNA said Penarth might be the income-tax parish, but expressly described that as a guess. It offered three practical routes: inspect the map at Kew, commission paid research, or engage an independent researcher. On 14 October its Remote Enquiries reply also supplied catalogue search links for Glamorgan XLVII and Penarth field books and reiterated that catalogue searches do not search record contents.

2. What the replies do not establish

  • IR 58 field book: no individual field-book piece/item, accession, box, shelf or store location has been identified. The 12 October response did not confirm that a member of staff physically retrieved or checked the relevant field book. It said TNA could not identify it without the assessment number. The 10 November response refused a repeat request for location/physical-check information under section 14(2); it did not say the field book is not held.
  • Judicial Papers transfer history: TNA disclosed release/delivery schedules and a manifest, but the recorded Discovery release date of 20 May 2026 should not be conflated with the physical transfer date, accession date, or a complete chain-of-custody history. The internal review CAS-356384-B2F5L9 remains the route by which the handling of the earlier request is being challenged.
  • Negative catalogue results: “Relevant information not found” is a report of a search outcome, not proof that a record never existed, was destroyed, or was never transferred. The 23 September result described an expanded catalogue search; TNA also warned that Discovery searches descriptions rather than the contents of the underlying records.
  • Central-government records: TNA's view that local private land disputes and private-company demolition would not ordinarily be central-government business is an explanation of its expectations about likely record creation/transfer. It is not a record-by-record transfer audit and does not rule out records catalogued under a department, statutory function, company, road scheme, court or other creator.
  • HMC9/311: the original manorial survey return was inspected, but it is a short manorial-document survey return, not the original title deeds, estate rental, land valuation book or a comprehensive inventory of all surviving Llandough records.
  • ST1673 Object Name Book: the “Missing at Transfer” reference cited by Ordnance Survey concerns the Object Name Book for map area ST1673. It must not be conflated with the separate IR 58 Valuation Office field book. The catalogue label alone does not identify who was responsible, why it was missing, or the contents of the missing book.

Forensic Assessment — The National Archives

Scope: TNA as archival discovery/custody authority for transferred government and corporate records.

Role

TNA is relevant where government or historical corporate records may have been transferred from departments or deposited collections. It is not a title adjudicator.

Forensic control test

Identify the exact collection/classmark, transfer status, closure/restriction and catalogue provenance. Where a public-record route is exhausted, ask whether an earlier transfer or departmental retention record exists rather than treating catalogue absence as destruction.

Current finding

As at 10-10-2026, the material reviewed does not establish wrongdoing by The National Archives. This is not a finding that the archive had no involvement; its particular custody and search functions are addressed here.

Human action

Run targeted searches for Wales Office/Welsh Office, land-registry/leasehold and relevant corporate records, recording classmarks and transfer/disposal information. Where an expected record remains with a department, identify the department and preservation route.

Acceptance: each TNA search produces classmarks/copies or a documented negative-search outcome with custody explanation.
