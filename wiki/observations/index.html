Observations are derived research records. Each one links back to its source artifacts and records its limits and test questions. They are not themselves original evidence.

OBS-0001 · working observation

The twelve-year period recurs across the land ownership and possession dispute

Twelve years recurs as a legally significant period and as a useful interval to test against the dispute chronology. The recurrence is a pattern for investigation, not a conclusion that every twelve-year interval had the same legal cause or effect.

OBS-0002 · working observation

Repeated treatment of Mary Williams’s occupation as permissive

Several catalogue entries concern the legal characterization of Mary Williams’s occupation as permissive, including accounts of earlier proceedings and later registration. The pattern warrants comparing the wording, date, source and independence of each occurrence.

Read observation and its references

OBS-0003 · working observation

Name variants and dispersed records complicate the chronology

The property and estate appear under multiple names and spellings, while relevant records are spread across different registers, archive catalogues, maps and web themes. That creates a genuine difficulty in linking records and retelling the chronology consistently.

Read observation and its references

OBS-0004 · working observation

Alfred Thomas’s 1927 death conflicts with Mary Williams’s remembered 1928 rent payment

Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 statement places the last rent payment, about £4 per annum, in 1928 and names “Alfred Thomas the son of Daniel Thomas” as recipient. The Dictionary of Welsh Biography records Alfred Thomas, 1st Baron Pontypridd, as dying unmarried on 14 December 1927. A separate, secondary transcription of a 27 December 1927 newspaper report says an unofficial report had the bulk of his property going to Cardiff Corporation and citizens. The conflict is material, but neither the actual 1928 payee nor any Llandough land passing under Alfred’s estate is established.

Read observation and its references
