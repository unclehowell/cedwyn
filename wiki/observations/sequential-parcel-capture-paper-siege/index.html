Sequential parcel capture and permission-framing: the Paper Siege working hypothesis

OBS-0004 · Leading working hypothesis; unproven

Summary

A parcel-by-parcel model tests whether separate conveyances, public-body takings, possession proceedings, licences, registrations and title consolidation progressively reduced the Williams family’s independent standing on paper while the family continued to occupy or claim land. It includes the legal outcome, alternative explanations, source gaps and falsifiers. It does not establish a coordinated scheme or wrongdoing.

Analysis

The model distinguishes John Williams’s c.1841 occupation of 36 tithe plots from proof of ownership; provisional Parcel B (fields under the 1939 agreement) from Parcel A (farmhouse, garden and woodland); and the southern green as provisional Parcel C. It tests the disputed 1939 signature, Mary’s 1958 Crichel Down complaint, the 1974 letters, 1975 BP transfer, 1982–83 first registration and notice, 1987 amalgamation, and subsequent planning and archaeology.

Death, suppression and hidden-archaeology scenarios are kept separate and labelled unsupported or fiction-only. The 1987 Court of Appeal result remains the operative legal outcome.

Tests

  • Obtain and compare the full 1938, 1969 and 1975 deeds and plans; create one georeferenced parcel overlay.
  • Obtain the complete 1939 agreement, first page / schedule and original plan; verify the source and provenance of the signature using independent genuine exemplars.
  • Complete the WA231076 and WA240304 first-registration files, including every form, requisition, reply, notice, proof of service and occupier statement.
  • Read the original 1955 and 1962 possession orders, execution returns and the complete 1974 pleadings / correspondence.
  • Build a parcel-specific inventory of each public-body taking, including the 1958 Crichel Down complaint and the relevant compulsory-purchase instruments and plans.
  • Verify the chronology and causes of death of John Williams, Frederick Buckler, Mary Williams and William Buckler through reliable civil, probate and coronial records.
  • Inspect the full 1989–94 planning and archaeological files, including the excavation report, osteology, site dating and condition 18 file.
  • Identify the Neath fire precisely: institution, building, date, incident report, affected records, inventory and recovery / insurance documentation.

Limits

The complete conveyances and plans are not all inspected; the 1939 signature and provenance are not authenticated; the complete first-registration files and proof of service remain material. The reported Neath fire and family-paper loss have not been linked to a common cause. Deaths near legal or planning milestones do not establish homicide.
