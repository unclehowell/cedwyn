Three-part Parcel A: chronology, order scope and later title transfers

Provisional model. Parcel A and Parcel B on this page are provisional analytical polygons, built from a family statement of c.1974 and later maps. They are not surveyed or conveyance-proven boundaries. They stay provisional until the 1876–78 Bute, Daniel Thomas and Williams instruments and the later deed and court plans are found and overlaid.

Executive finding

The most useful new approach is to stop treating Parcel A as one undifferentiated area. The working model separates (1) northern woodland/forest, (2) the central occupied farmhouse/buildings/garden, and (3) south-eastern ground associated with the 1970 VG41 village-green record. Parcel B remains a separate eastern-fields control area. The boundaries must be reconstructed from original plans before these labels can support a legal claim.

The key question is whether the land claimed, the land covered by each court order, the land actually entered or delivered under the execution process, the land conveyed by WGR, and the land later registered are the same polygons. If they diverge, the precise mismatch—not the mere existence of different later owners—is the evidential lead.

Chronology that needs to be reconciled

Critical legal qualification: non-occupation does not automatically defeat an order

The published report of B.P. Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2 says the appeal was limited to the farmhouse and garden and that the rest of the 1955 possession order was not challenged [EV-0080]. It also recounts the 1955 order as concerning the whole farm. This is a substantial adverse point and must be addressed, not omitted.

Absence of habitation alone does not establish that an order cannot apply. The relevant tests are the precise land described, the parties and persons legally bound, the right to possession established or assumed, the execution authorised and carried out, and whether any independent third-party title was pleaded and determined. A possession order is not itself a conveyance to BP, but neither does non-occupation automatically disapply it.

The careful question is therefore: did the BP litigation decide title to the separate woodland and VG41-associated ground against independent title-holders who were not parties, or did it decide the parties’ possession rights within the scope of the mapped proceedings? Answer only from pleadings, sealed orders, plans and applicable law.

Source: published report of B.P. Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2. The original court file and sealed orders should be checked before asserting precise legal effect.

Why the 1961 deed is a high-value bridge

GHF-E129 is indexed as a deed dated 29 March 1961 involving Western Ground Rents, South Western Brick & Tile Company and Cardiff City [record pending: EV-0040]. Its current evidence page says a verbatim transcription is not available and its exact land/purpose remains unresolved. The parties and date alone do not prove a conveyance, lease, reservation or encumbrance over any proposed A component.

Three outcomes remain open: (1) a lawful, parcel-specific dealing supported by the root title; (2) an overlap whose authority is unclear and requires the full chain; or (3) a material plan/title mismatch that warrants a focused misdescription or misrepresentation inquiry. The deed, schedules, plan, recitals, operative clauses, consideration, signatures and underlying deeds must be examined before choosing between these outcomes.

VG41 as an independent provenance checkpoint

The 1970 VG41 file may provide an independent chronology and boundary control for A-South-East. Identify the applicant, ownership entry, map, notices, objections and stated title basis. A register entry is not automatically proof of fee-simple ownership; the Council’s role as register custodian or registering authority does not itself make it the owner.

Potential indicators and innocent alternatives

Public-authority involvement: keep the evidential ladder clear

  1. Documented act: a body signs an authenticated deed, makes a recorded decision or holds a register.
  2. Documented inconsistency: a specific reply conflicts with a primary record, or a defined search fails to locate an expected record.
  3. Unresolved irregularity: title, boundary, notice or authority remains unexplained after primary records are compared.
  4. Possible wrongdoing: evidence indicates a specific material misstatement, unlawful act or knowing concealment.
  5. Coordinated involvement: requires evidence of instructions, agreement, communications or a pattern that cannot reasonably be explained by separate lawful functions.

The current leads justify document testing, not a conclusion of fraud or government coordination. Allegations should identify the act, responsible person/body, source, applicable duty or legal element, materiality and effect. Publish adverse evidence and innocent explanations alongside the family case.

Repeated pattern of occupancy characterisation → challenge → later registration (family reconstruction, 18 October 2026)

The reconstruction (SOURCE PENDING where no record is cited) shows the same sequence three times on Parcel A:

  • Northern forest: 1949 attempt to characterise occupation as permissive; Mary (Nancy) Williams asserted ownership; approximately twelve years later (1961) a registration / synthetic estate was created (lead document: the 29 March 1961 deed).
  • Southern green (later VG41): 1958/59 attempt to characterise occupation as adverse; Mary (Nancy) Williams again asserted ownership; about 11 years later (16 June 1970) the land was registered as village green [record pending: EV-0044].
  • House and garden: the same method was used by BP — occupation framed as permissive (licence letters, 31 October 1974); first registration of the home/garden about 13 years after the 31 December 1969 WGR–BP conveyance (WA240304 application, 23 February 1983), then amalgamation [record pending: EV-0050; PENDING: EV-0066; PENDING: EV-0078]. The later two intervals are approximately 11 years (1958/59 to 1970) and 13 years (1969 to 1983). The separate 1949–61 sequence is around 12 years by calendar-year subtraction, but the exact 1949 date and the land affected by the 1961 deed still need primary-record confirmation. These are distinct leads, not one repeating twelve-year scheme.

Evidence label: SOURCE PENDING / reconstruction. The 1949 and 1958/59 characterisations and Mary’s responses remain high-priority primary-record targets. The parallel with the BP method is analytical, not a judicial finding of fraud or racketeering. The 1987 Court of Appeal decision determined possession of the house and garden; it did not adjudicate the northern forest or the southern green against independent title-holders who were not parties. Ownership of those two parcels is still asserted.

1958 public allegation — “It’s a Crichel Down” (Western Mail, 20 November 1958)

Documented public allegation (family lead, 18 October 2026): The Western Mail of 20 November 1958, p.7, under the headline “It’s a Crichel Down, says woman farmer”, reported that Mrs Buckler told the county planning committee that Glamorgan County Council, Penarth Urban Council, Cardiff Rural Council and Western Ground Rents had “walked in and taken” the family’s land [record pending: EV-0037]. This is a public allegation against the councils and WGR made 29 years before the 1987 Court of Appeal decision. Crichel Down was the famous 1954 scandal in which land compulsorily acquired for wartime use was not returned to the original owners despite a promise, leading to a public inquiry and the resignation of the Minister of Agriculture. Mrs Buckler’s use of the phrase placed the family’s experience in that contemporary frame of official taking without return.

Evidence label: Reported contemporary press account — primary clipping still to be obtained and catalogued. The allegation itself is contemporaneous public knowledge of the family’s claim against the councils and WGR in 1958, strengthening the 1958/59 strand of the repeated-pattern chronology. It does not by itself prove title or fraud; it is high-value evidence that the challenge was made openly and on the record long before the later registrations and the 1987 litigation.

Related lead — 1900 Bute succession: Contemporary papers reported that the 3rd Marquess of Bute’s will left his Llandough land to his son Lord Colum [record pending: EV-0012]. This may interrupt the usual chain assumed to run cleanly to the 1938 conveyance to Western Ground Rents. Priority document: the 23 February 1905 conveyance listed on the WA231076 file should be ordered from HMLR and compared with the 1877 / 1928 material and the 1938 plan [record pending: EV-0013].

Parcel identity decision rule: what exactly was included in the 1987 amalgamation? (18 October 2026)

Answer on the presently catalogued evidence: unresolved. WA231076 and WA240304 are title identifiers, not parcel descriptions that by themselves identify all three proposed parts of Parcel A. The 1987 amalgamation tells us which registered title continued, not whether the combined title polygon equalled the entire historic Parcel A, only segments of it, or land described differently in the operative deeds. Each of A-North (forest), A-Centre (occupied house/buildings/garden) and A-South-East (later associated with VG41) must be tested separately against the pre- and post-amalgamation plans.

Required map-to-map comparison

  • Start with the original 1877 agreement/conveyance and plan, read with the 1924 and 1928 records that are said to define when the family’s claimed Parcel-A interest took effect.
  • Plot the 1938 conveyance and 1939 tenancy plan. The wiki’s current reconstruction treats the 1939 plan as depicting the eastern agricultural fields (Parcel B); this helps define the claimed control area but does not alone prove the separate freehold title.
  • Overlay the 1955 claim/order/warrant and execution return or plan. Keep the extent legally ordered distinct from the land physically entered or delivered on 4 July 1955.
  • Inspect the full 29 March 1961 WGR / South Western Brick & Tile / Cardiff City deed and plan. The current catalogue says it affects land now within the woodland title, but its actual boundary and legal effect remain unknown.
  • Overlay the 1962 house-and-garden possession-order plan, the 1969 WGR conveyance to BP Pension Trust, and the 23 May 1975 conveyance said to support WA240304 [record pending: EV-0052].
  • Obtain the original VG41 register entry and map dated 16 June 1970. Determine whether it covers A-South-East, a portion of it, a neighbouring area, or something else; do not infer ownership from the green designation.
  • Compare the filed plans for WA231076 and WA240304 at first registration with the title plan immediately before and after the February 1987 amalgamation, then follow every relevant part into current titles, including the woodland title associated with WA735527.

The three possible outcomes are materially different

  • Whole Parcel A within the amalgamated title: the same title polygon demonstrably covers A-North, A-Centre and A-South-East, and the root documents are said to support that extent. Even then, whether the transferor held good title to each polygon remains a separate legal question.
  • Only segments of Parcel A included: the 1961 deed, VG41 file, other transfers or registration plans show that only part of one or more components entered a different chain. The wiki must then describe the precise overlap rather than claiming that the whole component was either lost or merged.
  • No proved Parcel-A overlap with the eastern registration: the relevant polygons do not match or the deeds describe a different holding. The historic ownership theory and current successors must then be traced separately rather than treating amalgamation as a unifying event.

Registered title plans ordinarily depict general boundaries. Where the dispute is about strips or edges, the deed plan, written parcel description, orientation, scale, adjoining ownership and any surveyor notes may be essential. A coloured overlay prepared from uncertain or unscaled images must be labelled illustrative, not conclusive.

The proposed 1949/1961, 1959/1970 and 1971/1982–83 timing links may guide which records to check first, but do not identify the land. In particular, the currently listed 1959 offer relates to the occupied farmhouse and garden; the 1961 deed’s woodland overlap is still unproved; and the 1959-to-1970 interval is about 11 years, not 12 [record pending: EV-0039].
