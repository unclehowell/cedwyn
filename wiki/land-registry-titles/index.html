Land Registry Titles

The land of Great House Farm was first… … read more

The land of Great House Farm was first registered at HM Land Registry by BP Properties Ltd in 1982–83. All documents cited here are in the Evidence Library.

Central title-chain test — 15 October 2026. For WA240304, the live question is what primary evidence established WGR’s title to the farmhouse-and-garden parcel before the 1969 transfer and the 1975 conveyance to BP Properties. The 1939 tenancy plan is evidence about tenancy extent, not a substitute for the 1938 conveyance. The 1969 deed is absent from the disclosed schedule, not proven never to have been examined. Read the 1983 First Registration: Parcel A Title Basis and Alleged HMLR Mistake alongside the title chronology and evidence catalogue.
1900 will / 1905 conveyance lead — unresolved. A research note reports that 1900 newspaper coverage said Lord Bute’s will left Llandough land to his son Lord Colum. The particular article and the clause identifying the land have not been inspected. Inheritance to Lord Colum would be an intermediate succession event, not by itself a break in the title chain. The priority test is the 23 February 1905 conveyance and any schedule/plan recorded on WA231076 (GHF-E026-03), compared with the will/probate and the 1938 Bute–WGR conveyance. The deed and plan remain outstanding; no parcel conclusion follows until they are read. See New Archival Leads, section 8.2.

Dependency control: this page records the register/title chronology; it does not by itself determine whether every parcel within a later registered title was conveyed by the root instruments. Change control, 5 October 2026. (1) DOCUMENTED FACT: the woodland register WA735527 (charges entry 2) describes the 15 May 1924 record on the WA231076 file as “an Agreement dated 15 May 1924 made between (1) John Marquis of Bute and Earl of Dumfries and (2) James Pelham Stuart Corbett” that “relates to the construction and maintenance of a drain”. It is recorded as an agreement, not a conveyance. FAMILY EVIDENCE: Mary recalls 1928. UNRESOLVED: whether the 1924 record bears on Mary’s 1928 is unknown until the agreement and its plan are read. (2) DOCUMENTED FACT: the 1987 judgment ties the May 1975 conveyance to the farmhouse and garden, and WA240304 was registered on that conveyance, so WA240304 was the farmhouse-and-garden title. Its file holds Linklaters/HMLR correspondence of 8 and 12 April 1983, after Mary’s death on 26 March 1983. (3) The woodland title holds land from two strands (blue: 1975 conveyance; pink: 1982 conveyance subject to Bute’s 1905 and WGR’s 1961 deeds). Full analysis: Case Analysis — Connections, Patterns and Questions and the Legal Review (land, section 2).

The 1982/83 first registration merged both parcels into one estate and the 1987 amalgamation completed it. Parcel B had a paper root through Bute’s retained land and WGR; for Parcel A the first-registration material does not identify any historic conveyance supplying the root, and the later possession, tenancy-offer and licence records are context, not substitutes for it. The underlying deeds, plans, application papers and requisitions will show how the registered extent was established. WA240304 was registered on the conveyance the 1987 judgment ties to the farmhouse and garden; its exact extent will be confirmed by its old title plan.

UnresolvedWhat did HMLR ask in April 1983 about occupation or adverse claims, and what did Linklaters answer? Mary had pleaded ownership and the Limitation Act in the 1974 county court action, which was never decided. Whether that live plea was disclosed when the “no question or doubt” certificate was given is the key question for the family’s concealment case (Case Analysis §3.1; Legal Review, F3). 🕒

Research note. The family’s case is that this registration wrongly included the house parcel. The certificate was given while Mary (Nancy) Williams was living in the farmhouse and asserting ownership. The registration merged both parcels on the pre-1928 description (1928) and did not disclose her claim. The amalgamation request was made during the Court of Appeal proceedings.
