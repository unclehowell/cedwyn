iOutside records
How we treat outside records. Tŷ Mawr is first named in the record we have found in David Stewart’s 1824 survey (EV-0211). The Williams family acquired Tŷ Mawr in 1667 (sources: Mary Williams’ statement in the 1974 newspapers; ITV News, 1988). Earlier documents on this page that come from the Bute estate or other third parties, such as the Cydwin lease or a Bute catalogue heading, are not records of Tŷ Mawr: their application to it is under forensic examination, and we test them rather than repeat them. This page records the family’s requests for records about Great House Farm from November 2025 to September 2026, and how each public body responded. It is drawn from the family’s own email correspondence. Officials are identified by role, not by name. Dates are when each email was sent or received.

Machine-readable research index: AI Agent & Machine-Readable Index — canonical links, identifiers and public retrieval endpoints.

It shows what it has taken to recover the evidence now in the Evidence Library: requests refused as “not held”, records found but withheld as “out of scope”, requests renumbered or proposed for closure, official email addresses that bounce, and files said to be “most likely destroyed” with no record of destruction. 🕒

Discovery date — important distinction. TNA’s email states that parliamentary records were loaded into Discovery in phases and that the series containing this record was released on Discovery on 20 May 2026. That is the catalogue/Discovery release date. The catalogue entry does not say beside the BP v Buckler case that it was “discovered” in May 2026, and the May date is not the creation date of the 1987–88 papers.

Next steps: obtain the bundle at Kew; preserve the 23 September email; request/record the catalogue release and transfer history; compare the search terms and scope used in the earlier and later TNA searches; and incorporate the judicial papers into the BP v Buckler reconstruction.

MOD FOI2026/09190 — entry added 10-10-2026

Evidence: GHF-E169 — MOD internal review dated 10 October 2026.

The MOD states that its searches across MOD archives and targeted searches in the Army Secretariat and Counterterrorism and United Kingdom branches did not locate information matching the request. Separately, it neither confirms nor denies whether it holds Special Forces-related information under FOIA 2000, section 23(5). The review acknowledges delay and concludes that timeliness requirements were not met, without clearly identifying in its concluding paragraph which stage caused that finding. It says personal-information elements were referred for a separate subject access process.

Interpretive control: this is a dated record of the MOD’s statement, not proof that relevant historical records never existed or that military personnel were or were not involved. Keep FOI2026/09190 distinct from FOI2026/09019 unless the MOD confirms a relationship. Do not mark the subject access strand complete until its reference and substantive outcome are recorded.
