Response Tactics & Response Friction Score (RFS)

This material now lives at https://fcg.bucklerfamily.estate/case/response-tactics-response-friction-score.

Page ID: 3714 · Title: Response Tactics & Response Friction Score (RFS) · Evidence status: Corrected 18 Oct 2026; evidence labels on the edited lines · Version: 1.0.1 · Editing Protocol

Purpose. This register tests the “three-hat” model (procedural holding response; defensive or scope-shifting response; high-friction use of delay, cost, confidentiality or remit) against the wording of actual public correspondence about Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr, Llandough and the Williams/Buckler family. It scores individual messages, not whole institutions or people.

Label: the published term is Response Friction Score (RFS). It measures observable difficulty in getting a clear, relevant and usable answer. It is not a rating of a person’s character, a finding of misconduct, or proof of concealment or unlawful behaviour. A lawful refusal can still be high-friction; an honest “not held” response can be low-friction.

Scoring rule: five dimensions, each 0–2

  • Answer completeness: 0 = directly answers the question; 1 = partly answers or needs follow-up; 2 = substitutes a previous answer, closes the exchange without addressing the material question, or leaves a central point unanswered.
  • Scope discipline: 0 = tracks the request; 1 = an arguable mismatch or unclear interpretation; 2 = a substantial reframing or wider search basis without showing why it is necessary.
  • Search, estimate or record-method transparency: 0 = gives a usable explanation; 1 = some relevant method is missing; 2 = important method is unexplained, internally unclear, or cannot be tested from the reply.
  • Withholding / legal-basis clarity: 0 = explains whether material is held, withheld or absent and identifies the basis; 1 = broad or partly ambiguous explanation; 2 = unclear whether information is held or withheld, or a legal basis is asserted without enough particulars to understand its application.
  • Timeliness, assistance and next steps: 0 = on time (or a clearly explained holding update with a usable route); 1 = a missed or vague commitment or a partial next step; 2 = a verified missed deadline or promised date with no firm revised date or adequate explanation. Score delay only after checking the correct statutory clock, clarification pauses, extensions and source chronology.

Interpretation: 0–2 low friction; 3–4 limited friction; 5–6 material friction; 7–8 high friction; 9–10 very high friction. A component should be scored only where the source supports it. The total is a reproducible editorial aid, not a legal test or probability that wrongdoing occurred. Confidence is recorded separately.

Force, Secrecy and Licence: tagging lens

  • Force: tag only if the wording shows an express threat, compulsion, sanction or actual use of coercive power. A statutory citation alone is not “force”.
  • Secrecy: tag when the message relies on withholding, neither-confirm-nor-deny treatment, privacy/confidentiality, restricted access or a missing decision trail. The tag describes the information effect, not motive.
  • Licence: tag when the response relies on a statutory threshold, exemption, cost limit, procedural rule, discretionary remit or institutional power. This can be entirely legitimate; the relevant question is whether its application is explained and fits the request.

Each assessment also records mitigating features, source quality and the next document needed. Where a reply or attachment has not been inspected, it is listed as unscored, not assigned a hostile score by assumption.

Initial source-reviewed scores — 18 October 2026

1. Llandough Community Council — quoted wording attributed to 20 September 2026

RFS 7/10 — high friction; low confidence. The quoted wording says the Clerk had already directed the requester to information held and “We have nothing more to add.” This appears to close the exchange rather than respond to the later itemised question about records created after the 2002 archive deposit, photographs and what records transferred in 1982. The original 20 September email has not been separately recovered: the wording is preserved as a quote in later correspondence, so score only the quotation, not the missing message’s full context.

Tags: closure without a fresh answer; reliance on a prior response. Power lens: no Force evidence; the quote has a closure effect, but without the original message it is not enough to infer deliberate secrecy. Next step: retain the 28 September email containing the quote and obtain the original 20 September message if available.

Source-status note: quoted reply, not recovered original · Open Council correspondence thread.

2. Llandough Community Council Chair — 28 September 2026 forwarding reply

RFS 1/10 — low friction; medium confidence. The published transcription states that the 21 September email had been passed to the Clerk for consideration. That is a holding/forwarding message, not a substantive answer or refusal. The transcription is not the original .eml and does not include the original headers. It should not be treated as evidence of evasion on its own.

Tags: routing / holding reply. Power lens: none clearly evidenced in this message. Next step: assess against the later substantive response, not in isolation.

Source: Council reply transcription · Open Council correspondence thread.

3. Llandough Community Council Clerk — section 12 refusal, 11 October 2026

RFS 5/10 — material friction; medium confidence. The 16 October response is available as a Wayback transcription, but the current GHF-E184 wiki page explicitly says the original thread, complete headers and attachments have not yet been captured there. The letter invokes section 12, gives the £450 / 18-hour limit, estimates 224 sets of minutes for 2002–2016, says “about 20 minutes to read each set” and “about 10 minutes to assess each set”, then states a total of over 2,240 minutes (about 37 hours). The calculation appears to use 10 minutes per set; it does not explain whether the 20- and 10-minute figures are alternatives or cumulative. This is a precise, testable question about the stated method, not proof the refusal is unlawful.

The letter’s mitigating features must remain on the record: it identifies section 12, states the £450 threshold and staff-time rate, proposes narrowing the date range (for example 2002–2006), and gives an internal-review route and the right to complain to the ICO. The proper challenge is to ask how the estimate was constructed, what activities were counted and why the narrow confirm/deny questions require review of all 224 sets. Do not claim that section 12 automatically fails because an estimate is not perfectly itemised; the ICO says no precise calculation is always required, but estimates should be sensible and realistic and advice/assistance should be considered.

Tags: statutory cost threshold; estimate method needs clarification; possible scope-fit issue. Power lens: Licence (statutory cost power); no evidence of Force. Next step: ask for the calculation and search tasks that produce the estimate, then test the response against ICO section 12 and section 16 guidance.

Source: 11 October refusal transcription · Open Council correspondence thread · ICO section 12 guidance · ICO section 16 guidance.

4. Heneb HER Team — schedule response, 16 October 2026

RFS 6/10 — material friction; high confidence in the transcribed email. The reply addresses a 48-point schedule, uses public-availability pointers for some items, gives many itemised “not held” answers, explains that the HER change log does not store snapshots of table contents, and says predecessor HER paper records are held at Baglan. Items 3–5 remain unclear: the HER says its change log does not store snapshots, then says it cannot release “this information” because it contains personal data. The wording does not distinguish cleanly between no snapshot existing and information being held but withheld. Score vector [1,0,1,2,1] = 5/10. GHF-E184 states that the source page remains transcript-pending, so this is a medium-confidence score, not a claim that the original email was recovered.

This is not a finding that the data-protection position is invalid. The follow-up should ask Heneb to distinguish “not held / no snapshot exists” from “held but withheld”, identify the categories of personal data and legal basis relied on for items 3–5, and say whether a redacted or non-personal extract is possible. The 48 numbered replies and the stated archive location are mitigating features.

Tags: held-versus-withheld ambiguity; broad data-protection explanation; public pointer not direct copy. Power lens: Secrecy (data-protection / snapshot limitation) and Licence (claimed legal basis); no evidence of Force.

Source: Heneb HER email, 16 October · Open referenced correspondence thread.

5. Amgueddfa Cymru — Museum Wales — 7 October 2026

RFS 1/10 — low friction; medium confidence. The public text archive preserves a transcription, not the original .eml. The Museum said it did not hold a separate item-by-item audit schedule of excluded search results, explained that staff reviewed results for relevance and that the decision outcome was reflected in the 5 October response, then answered the MS 3360/3 and waterwheel-location points. The absence of an audit schedule matters as a record-keeping limitation, but the reply openly acknowledges it and gives a usable explanation rather than hiding it.

Tags: no separate contemporaneous search schedule (record gap, not proof of evasion). Power lens: none clearly indicated by this email. Assessment: the disclosure that a log does not exist reduces response friction even though it leaves a gap in the evidence trail.

Source: Museum Wales email, 7 October · Open Museum Wales correspondence thread.

7. Amgueddfa Cymru — Museum Wales — 12 October 2026

RFS 1/10 — low friction; medium confidence. The public text archive preserves a transcription, not the original .eml. The Museum directly states that it does not hold a separate search log, file note, internal note or other recorded document explaining the FOI 2026-024 scope assessment. It gives a retrospective explanation of the searches and why other finds were treated as outside the narrowly described discovery, says Church Farm records have since been supplied, and confirms “No records remain withheld.” The absent contemporaneous rationale is a record gap, but the reply plainly admits that gap and describes its reasoning.

Tags: no contemporaneous decision record; retrospective explanation. Power lens: none clearly indicated by this message. Low RFS does not mean the missing rationale is immaterial; it means the reply is direct about it.

Source: Museum Wales email, 12 October · Open Museum Wales review thread.

How to keep the register fair and useful

  • Score a specific message and, where needed, a linked thread; do not give every email from one institution the same rating.
  • Mark the source as original, full transcription, quotation, summary or automatic acknowledgement. Lower confidence when the original or attachments are missing.
  • Keep two separate findings: response friction and record gap. A candid admission that no search log exists may identify an important record gap while still earning a low friction score.
  • Use time-based delay flags only after the actual due date, including extensions or clarification pauses where relevant, is verified.
  • Re-score when the institution clarifies the calculation, supplies records, corrects an error, or an original message is recovered. Keep the old score and reason for change in the audit trail.
  • Do not infer collusion, fraud, concealment or hostile intent from the score alone. Those are separate allegations requiring independent evidence.

Official guidance used for the legal comparison

  • ICO: section 12 — cost of compliance. The ICO says estimates need to be sensible and realistic; the £450/18-hour limit applies to most public authorities, with staff time estimated at £25/hour, and advice/refocusing should be considered.
  • ICO: section 16 — advice and assistance.
  • ICO: section 17 — refusal notices. The ICO notes that there is no statutory requirement for a section 12 notice to include the estimate, but a clear explanation and breakdown can help the requester understand how the limit is said to apply.
