University reading lists (Talis Aspire / Leganto) as an academic route for Cedwyn's open records and e-books

Harvested 2026-10-31 16:15Z (skills harvest). Topic not in INDEX before ('talis' 0, 'leganto' 0, 'reading list' only in a harvest note). Distinct from the Alan Ball award, review copies to journals, WorldCat/Open Library catalogue records and library e-lending (OverDrive/BorrowBox/D2D partners), which are already covered.

What the sources show (read 31 Oct 2026 unless marked)

  • Talis, "Talis Aspire in 2024" (talis.com, read): over 1.65 million resource lists created worldwide; in 2024 institutions published nearly 340,000 lists, viewed by students nearly 40 million times, over 50 million student clicks; one customer case study: 75% of courses have a list and 85% of lists are 'all-digital' for prescribed items. A list item is a click straight to the resource.
  • Welsh universities run public Talis lists, readable without login: University of South Wales (southwales.rl.talis.com, read, bilingual interface), Bangor (bangor.rl.talis.com, read: a Welsh Linguistics list cites John Davies, A History of Wales, 2007, marked 'Argymhellir' / recommended), Aberystwyth (aber.rl.talis.com, read: Lifelong Learning department lists modules such as XS21810 'The Geological History of Wales'). Reading (reading.rl.talis.com) and Durham are public too. Leganto (Ex Libris) is used at King's, Imperial, Manchester (search snippets).
  • How items get onto lists: academics add items with the bookmarking browser extension; the URL is sent to the list and the metadata is read from the page (Talis Support 'Bookmarking - Nuts and Bolts', snippet; page behind a bot check from here). Lists are then sent to the library 'for review and purchase' (ProQuest/Talis integration note, snippet). Open web resources can be added directly; nothing has to be bought.
  • Holley, 'Self-Publishing and Academic Libraries', ACRL 2015 (PDF read): academic libraries mostly overlook self-published titles (no CIP, weak metadata, few reviews, jobbers do not supply them), BUT the two realistic doors are (1) a faculty member who wants the book for teaching or research, and (2) special-collections librarians collecting 'books of local interest' comprehensively; local-history self-publishing is singled out as primary-source value. Tenure committees give self-published books little weight, so academics cite them as sources, not as authorities.
  • Ithaka S+R, 'The Current State of Academic E-Book Business Models' (read): libraries struggle to acquire e-monographs through non-standard routes; budgets and staff are the main constraints. An item that is free, stable and well described costs the library nothing to list.

The skill: make records list-ready, then let lists find them

  1. List-ready page: one stable URL per record/e-book, never moved (301 if ever moved); visible title, author/creator (organisation name, not a person), date, publisher, and an abstract on the page itself; embedded citation metadata the bookmarker can read (COinS/Highwire 'citation_*' meta + schema.org Book/CreativeWork; Cedwyn already has a cite-this-record skill - reuse it). A DOI (Zenodo skill) makes the item survive URL changes.
  2. Free or sample route: a free first edition or a free chapter PDF is what a lecturer can put on a list without a purchase request; the paid edition stays one click away on /ebooks/.
  3. Find the fit, read-only: search the public Talis instances (USW, Bangor, Aberystwyth, Cardiff if public) for modules on Welsh history, landed estates, Glamorgan, heritage/public history, archives practice, property/land law history. Record module code, list URL, what is listed now, and the gap our records fill. Do not log in, do not use 'Feedback' forms.
  4. Library door: most university libraries have a public 'suggest a book/e-book' form (UEA example, snippet), but it is for members; we do not use it. The legitimate door for a non-member is a lecturer or a special-collections librarian - that is outreach, so it waits for the project lead's GO (draft only, HubSpot).
  5. Measure: referrers from *.rl.talis.com and leganto hosts in Cloudflare/beacon logs; any hit = a list somewhere links us. Check monthly; expect zero for months.

Pitfalls

  • Never ask a student or member to submit a purchase request on our behalf (misuse of a member service).
  • No outbound links from the public site to list pages; this is an inbound route only.
  • A list entry does not mean endorsement; never write 'used at X University' publicly unless the live list shows it, and then only factually with the date checked.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • This run (zero spend, read-only): add to the next website task the list-ready checklist for /ebooks/ and the evidence catalogue: citation meta present on each record page, organisation as creator (never a family member's name), free first-edition PDF linked from each e-book page.
  • Research task (online gap finder or bizdev): a one-off sweep of USW, Bangor and Aberystwyth public lists for Welsh local/estate history modules; save as the project workspace (module, list URL, current items, our matching record). No contact.
  • Outreach (only on the project lead's GO): one HubSpot draft to a named module convenor or a special-collections librarian offering the free edition and the catalogue as a teaching source; companies/institutions only, never private individuals' personal addresses.
