Kickstarter for a special/print edition: when it pays, and the readiness gate

Harvested 26 Oct 2026 (skills harvest 20:07). Status for Cedwyn: PROPOSAL / owner decision. Parked behind an audience gate (below).

What the practitioners report (read 26 Oct 2026)

  • Kickstarter publishing category: 69,000 projects launched, 3.2 million unique backers, over $380 million pledged since 2009. Average pledge in publishing last year: $72. Source: Oriana Leckert, Kickstarter Head of Publishing, interviewed on The Creative Penn (Apr 2026) and in Indie Author Magazine (10 Jul 2026).
  • For an early-career publishing campaign, "maybe 20 to 30 percent of your audience will come from the larger Kickstarter ecosystem". So 70-80% of backers are people the author already reaches (Leckert, Indie Author Magazine).
  • Joanna Penn: six campaigns, over $140K combined (The Creative Penn, 20 Apr 2026). Her launches were "the culmination of years of build-up" with an audience that followed her interests and photos first.
  • Set up the Kickstarter pre-launch page as early as possible, once the project is approved, and keep promoting it. It doesn't need finished rewards to go live; it collects "notify me on launch" followers (Creative Penn).
  • What sells: special editions, hardbacks, signed copies, behind-the-scenes extras, tiers that include the e-book. Backers pay for the object and the involvement, not a 99p e-book.
  • Fees: Kickstarter's 5% platform fee plus payment processing (the processing rate was not checked this run; read kickstarter.com before any budget).

How to run one (the playbook)

  1. Audience first. Estimate backers = (people you reach who'd buy) / 0.75. With a $72 average, a £1,500 goal needs roughly 25 backers, about 19 of them from your own list or channels.
  2. Pre-launch page live 4-8 weeks ahead. Every post, email and talk points to "Notify me on launch". Track the follower count weekly. That count is your demand test.
  3. Tiers: e-book only (low), e-book + exclusive extra (PDF map pack, annotated transcription), physical edition, top tier with a personal or local element (a named thank-you in the book, a guided walk or talk). Keep postage simple: UK-first, digital-only tiers for overseas.
  4. Goal = the real minimum print run plus fees plus postage, not the hoped-for total. Use stretch goals for extras.
  5. Launch day is the spike. Email the list, post on every channel and ask partners in the first 48 h. Day-one momentum gets campaigns surfaced inside Kickstarter (the 20-30% ecosystem share).
  6. Fulfil fast, and keep backers informed with updates. Afterwards, add backers to the mailing list (with consent) for the next book.

Readiness gate (our rule, derived from the figures above)

Don't launch until both of these hold for 2 weeks: - 50 or more pre-launch followers, or 100 or more engaged email subscribers; and - one month with 3+ organic e-book sales (proof people pay). Below that, the 70-80% own-audience share means the campaign will very likely miss its goal and be a public failure.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Today: mailing list 1, Instagram about 5 followers, 0 sales. The gate fails by a wide margin, so do NOT launch. Logged as a later option.
  • The asset fits Kickstarter well. A printed Great House Farm / Ty Mawr history with the 1824 David Stewart survey map, period records and a parish map pack is exactly the "beautiful edition" that gets the $72-type pledge, where a £9.99 e-book does not.
  • Prep that needs no GO: keep a running list in the project workspace of possible reward items (map reproductions, record transcriptions, a talk). Count, in the Sunday report, how many people would plausibly back.
  • Needs the owner's GO: creating a Kickstarter account or project, any print quote, any public page, any price. Zero spend until then. The project would be in the owner's name, and that is the owner's decision.
  • Wording: no banned words on the page; never name the owner publicly; follow the Bute-dates rule.
