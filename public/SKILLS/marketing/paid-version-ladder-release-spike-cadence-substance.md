# Paid-version ladders live on release spikes: cadence and substance rules

Read 1 Nov 2026. This is a warning skill for any model where buyers pay again for each newer version.

## Practitioner testimony
- Dan Counsell (RapidWeaver, quoted on mjtsai.com 14 Aug 2015, page read 1 Nov 2026) argued for paid upgrades: "Software is the outlier... I can't think of any other business where the customer pays just once and receives free updates and improvements for life."
- Curtis Herbert replied in "Paid Upgrades Won't Fix Your Business" (blog.curtisherbert.com, 14 Aug 2015, page read 1 Nov 2026). Looking at Counsell's sales chart, he writes: "That's a nice spike in upgrade sales, followed by an long-tail of increased sales... You need to keep releasing paid updates to see these spikes, otherwise your sales will diminish back down to unsustainable levels." And: "A sales model based on upgrade pricing relies on convincing your users that the new version of your app is worth more of their money... You need to create a big enough splash with each major update."
- The chart in question came after four years of RapidWeaver 5 sales. The spikes are real, and between releases sales decay.

## Rules that follow
1. **Every paid step must be a visible splash.** A minor bump that costs £4.99 more needs a changelog row a reader can feel: new chapter, new record, new map, new scene. Never a typo fix. Typo fixes are PATCH releases and stay free.
2. **Set a cadence you can keep.** Spikes come only from releases. Plan the next paid step's content before announcing the current one.
3. **Announce each release as an event:** email the list, post, and put a "new in vX.Y" line in the changelog modal. A silent release gives no spike.
4. **Measure per release:** checkouts in the 7 days after against the 7 days before. If a step brings no spike, the step was too small or wasn't announced.
5. **Don't hold content back just to make a step.** Herbert's warning is that upgrade pricing pushes sellers to "hold features back". Our reading: bundle real work into fewer, bigger steps instead.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- The version-standard flywheel can check that each MINOR/MAJOR bump's changelog row names reader-visible content (pages added, new record IDs). A bump whose row is only "fixes" should be a PATCH, so the price must not rise.
- Release announcements go through existing routes: the Sunday growth report and the auto-social posts. There is no new channel.
- Track "7 days before vs after" per version in the project workspace With zero checkouts so far, the first measurable spike is the baseline.
