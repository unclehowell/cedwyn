# T. H. Thomas 1903 "Some Folk-lore of South Wales": the primary text behind the Leckwith boatman tale

Date: 2026-10-30. Read from the NLW scan, page by page, of Reports and Transactions of the Cardiff Naturalists' Society vol. XXXVI (1903), pp. 52-64. The article PDF is AWJBW005007; the scan has no text layer.

## What this is
- Marie Trevelyan (1909, pp. 336-337) cites the Canton-Leckwith boatman's ghost to "T. H. Thomas, Old Folks' Tales, Trans. Cardiff Naturalists' Soc. xxxvi 1903 p. 52". The article that sits at that place is titled **"Some Folk-lore of South Wales"**. It runs pp. 52-64 with 4 unnumbered plates. NLW's search finds no "Old Folks' Tales" title anywhere, so p. 52 is just where the article starts.
- **The tale itself is on pp. 63-64.** A boatman worked the Ely between Canton and Leckwith, and his ghost was seen at the river bend. This is the 1903 primary text, six years older than Trevelyan's retelling.
- On the same pages there are other Glamorgan items we can use: the **Ci-bal** ghost-hound (p. 63) and the children's game **"Ladi Witch yn y ffynnon"** (around p. 58).
- **What is not there:** no tale about Llandough (Penarth), Cogan, Penarth Head or Tŷ Mawr anywhere in pp. 52-64. The Penarth Head fish and the Llanblethian items stay Trevelyan-only (see trevelyan-1909-glamorgan-folklore-public-domain-local-stories.md).
- **Rights:** T. H. Thomas ("Arlunydd Penygarn") died in 1915, so the text is public domain in the UK (70 years after death). The NLW scan notice states "copyright restrictions and license terms may apply" to the digital version. So we quote and paraphrase the words and do not re-host NLW's page images.

## How to use it (marketing craft)
1. **Cite the oldest source.** Practitioners on #FolkloreThursday ask for real folklore with its source and the link last (folklorethursday.com). "A 1903 Cardiff Naturalists' Society paper records…" is stronger than citing the 1909 book alone, and it is checkable.
2. **One tale per post.** Use 2-3 lines of paraphrase plus one short quotation, then the source line (journal, volume, year, page). The link to our own page goes last, with no outbound link (house rule).
3. **Seasonal fit.** A riverside ghost fits Calan Gaeaf and the November Thursdays (5/12/19/26 Nov, per the folklorethursday skill). Ci-bal suits winter. The game suits a children's or family angle.
4. **Correct the citation trail in public.** "Trevelyan's 'Old Folks' Tales' is the 1903 paper 'Some Folk-lore of South Wales'" is a small, citable correction. Archivists and folklorists share that kind of thing, and it builds credibility for free.
