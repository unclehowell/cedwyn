Goodreads Giveaways in 2026: rules, cost, real numbers, and our call

Skill harvested 2026-10-31. Complements goodreads-storygraph-listings-ebooks.md (listing; that file only noted "paid, skip"). This file is the decision sheet if a paid test is ever proposed.

Current rules (Goodreads pages read 31 Oct 2026)

  • Formats: Kindle e-book (Goodreads delivers, up to 100 copies, no book cost or shipping) or print (you post copies within two weeks). Kindle giveaways are only for books published through KDP, with the author's Goodreads connected to the KDP account; the book must already be in the Kindle Store and exist on Goodreads. (goodreads.com)
  • Price: Standard $119, Premium $599 per giveaway (package page, read). Standard: entrants must add the book to Want to Read; the giveaway shows in entrants' friends' feeds; email to the author's followers and to readers who already shelved the book; Goodreads reminds winners to review. Premium adds a premium listing in the Giveaways section and a personal email to everyone who didn't win.
  • Refund: full refund until the day the giveaway starts. Approval within about 2 business days.
  • Who can enter: legal residents of the 50 US states, DC and Canada only, with a Goodreads and an Amazon.com or Amazon.ca account. One entry per person and household. (goodreads.com, read). Print giveaways now offer US and Canada targeting. No UK entrants.
  • Still running in 2026: an author's FB post shows a US-only giveaway of 100 e-books 1–28 Mar 2026 (snippet, Kate White).

Real testimony with numbers

  • Vania Margene Rheault (self-published romance; post 4 Nov 2024, read): Kindle giveaway one week after release, 30 days, maximum 100 e-books, paid $99. 2,240 entrants. Lessons she wrote down: (1) the giveaway is tied to the name on the KDP bank account, not a pen name; (2) Goodreads email verification was a hassle; (3) the 100 copies appeared on her KDP dashboard as processed orders with no royalty, which confused her for days; (4) it is allowed while the book is in Kindle Unlimited; (5) she could not measure ROI: entries became Want-to-Read adds, reviews uncertain; (6) verdict: "Not if you don't have the money to burn"; for measurable ROI she would rather pay for a deal newsletter (Freebooksy, BargainBooksy, Fussy Librarian, Robin Reads, ENT).
  • r/selfpublish thread "Goodreads giveaway program... is it worth it?" (snippet): Standard is "probably only useful if you've already" got an audience there.
  • Goodreads forum "Goodreads Giveaways Algorithm" (151 posts, snippet) shows authors suspicious that winners are random entry-farmers rather than genre readers. Treat review yield as low (assume 0–5 reviews from 100 copies until we measure our own).
  • Goodreads' own 2018 launch post: Standard was $119 with an introductory half price; Premium $599 (introductory $299).

What one test would buy (our arithmetic, not a measurement)

  • $119 / ~2,000 entrants ≈ $0.06 per Want-to-Read add, all US/Canada. That is cheap reach into the North American Welsh diaspora, but a Want-to-Read is not a sale and costs the 100 copies' royalties (they show as zero-royalty orders).

Playbook if the project lead ever states GO

  1. Only for a KDP edition that is live and listed on Goodreads with the correct author name (fix via the Librarians group first).
  2. Standard package, 100 Kindle copies, 28–30 days, starting within 1–2 weeks of a launch or a price promotion.
  3. Before it starts: author profile complete, the book's description ends with the /ebooks/ link text (Goodreads may strip links), a pinned Goodreads blog/post about the book's records.
  4. During: one post per channel on day 1 and the last 3 days only (Rheault posted often and could not tell if it helped).
  5. Measure: entrants, Want-to-Read count before/after, ratings/reviews at +30 and +90 days, KDP orders (minus the 100), US/CA sessions on /ebooks/ with utm_source=goodreads-giveaway.
  6. Stop rule: under 5 ratings at +90 days -> never again.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Default: do not run it. Zero-spend rule; the free equivalents are already in the index (LibraryThing Early Reviewers, BookBub New Release Alerts, Goodreads listing). Record this as a PROPOSAL only in the project workspace if a KDP launch is coming: "Goodreads Standard giveaway, $119, US/CA only, 100 Kindle copies; the project lead's GO required."
  • Check first whether the Cedwyn e-book is on KDP at all (Kindle giveaways need KDP; Gumroad/Stripe-only books cannot run a Kindle giveaway; a print giveaway means posting books to North America ourselves).
  • The US is the second-largest audience after the UK for Vale of Glamorgan tourism content (Vale of Glamorgan Council scrutiny report 16 Jul 2024), which is the only argument for a US/CA-only tool.
  • Never imply reviews are expected in return; Goodreads sends the review reminder itself.
