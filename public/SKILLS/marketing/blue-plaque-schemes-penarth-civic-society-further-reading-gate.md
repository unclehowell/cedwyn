# Local blue-plaque schemes (Penarth Civic Society and Welsh council schemes): gate and the useful route

Harvest 2026-10-29 22:15Z. Internal working note, not public text.

## What the sources say (read 29 Oct)
- **Penarth Civic Society (PCS), Blue Plaques page** (penarthsociety.org.uk, last site update 18 Feb 2026): part of the **Penarth Town Heritage Trail** project (launched late 2021: information boards, blue plaques, QR codes, an interactive guided tour). Penarth "only has one blue plaque" plus two non-blue ones, "one of which is on the wrong house". Criteria, verbatim: they must "be Dead", "be Interesting", and "have lived or worked in the town at a site that still exists (so we can mount the plaque)". They want "ordinary" people too: dockers, midwives, coal trimmers, a seamstress, a boarding-house keeper. The page lists about 50 named or role candidates (e.g. Jack Vincent, "Penarth's last Hobbler"; Kathleen Thomas, swimmer; Joseph Parry, composer). Locations are kept back because owner consent has to be negotiated.
- **How to suggest someone (PCS):** by email to the society. Needed: name and profession, a brief history of why they are suitable, the location they are prominently linked with (public or private) where a plaque could go, and dates (birth, death, dates at the location). A recipient template is planned: ref no., dates, reason, location map link, installation date, news links, **"Links to any further online reading (3rd party sites)"**.
- **PCS content is copyright** ("No use in other media or reproduction allowed without prior consent"), so we must not copy the candidate list or their texts.
- **Penarth Times** (read): the 23 Jul 2026 talk by committee member David Noble gave a project update; members free, non-members £3. The PCS event page lists another date: **Thu 19 Nov 2026, 18:45**, St Augustine's Church Hall, Albert Road.
- **Neath Port Talbot Council scheme** (npt.gov.uk, read), an example of a Welsh council scheme: council-funded plaques can be applied for **1 April to 30 September** each year, self-funded at any time, decided by an advisory panel with guidance/policy documents. **Swansea** (snippet; the page blocks this box): nominee dead at least 20 years, of national or international significance. **Historic England national scheme** (snippet): dead at least 20 years; England only, so not relevant to the Vale.
- Whether the Vale of Glamorgan Council runs its own plaque scheme: **not checked.**

## The gate
- **A plaque for Great House Farm or the Williams family through PCS: NO.** Llandough is its own community outside Penarth town, and PCS requires "lived or worked in the town". The farmhouse is private and its ownership is the subject of the case: any plaque would need owner consent, and nothing about family holdings or addresses may go public (editorial rules). Don't propose it.
- **The useful route is being the "further online reading".** The PCS template links each plaque to third-party reading. If Cedwyn has a properly sourced page on a Penarth-town person, *or on a trade from their list* (coal trimmer, hobbler, dock worker) drawn from free records, it is a natural candidate for that link. This gives a high-trust local backlink and puts us in front of the people most likely to buy local-history e-books.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
1. **Check fit first (research, no contact):** look for overlap between our evidence/research pages and the PCS candidate roles or names, e.g. Penarth dock trades in the Llandough census households, or Williams family members who worked in Penarth town. Record any overlap in growth-report notes with the exact sources. If there is none, stop here: this skill is a gate, not a to-do.
2. **If there is overlap, make the page first:** one short, sourced page on Cedwyn in plain words (free records only, exact refs per archive-catalogue-discovery-exact-refs-publication-note.md, no banned words, no family holdings). No sales pitch on the page beyond the normal /ebooks/ strip.
3. **Then a suggestion email to PCS = PROPOSAL, on the project lead's GO, as a HubSpot draft only.** Follow their format exactly: name, profession, brief history, location, dates, plus one line offering our page as further reading. Never ask for a link in return; offer the research. If the person does not fit "lived or worked in the town at a site that still exists", don't send.
4. **19 Nov 2026 PCS evening** (£3 non-member): whether the project lead or someone local goes is his decision; the talk-pitching route is already in civic-local-history-society-talks-penarth.md. Don't duplicate it.
5. **Measure / kill:** a PCS page linking to Cedwyn (check their site when the recipient pages go live, and our referrer/`/r/pcs` count). If there is no overlap found, or no reply within 10 weeks of one suggestion, close it. Never chase more than once.
