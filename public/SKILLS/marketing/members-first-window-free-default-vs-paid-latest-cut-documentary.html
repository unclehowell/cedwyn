Which cut is free: "members-first" windows for a versioned documentary (free default vs paid latest)

Skill harvested 2026-10-31 ~21:28Z. Topic: e-book/video pricing, community monetisation, YouTube growth. Neighbours: versioned-release-rising-price-ladder.md (price rises per version), release-notes-changelog-versioned-ebooks-art.md, instagram-early-access-reels-follow-gate-door-drops.md (follow gate, not pay gate).

The evidence (31 Oct 2026)

  • YouTube's own creator page (youtube.com, read): the standard perk list names "members-first videos" beside badges and emoji; creators keep 70% after taxes and fees. Third-party guides (sirency.com, youtubetoolkit.com; snippets, not opened) describe the type as "members first, public later": the paid window is time-limited and the video becomes public.
  • Patreon Help "How do I set up an early access post?" (snippet; page blocked by a bot check): early access posts go to members first and become public later, set per post.
  • Patreon, Jack Conte roadmap post (snippet, not opened): fans discover a creator through a free post, enjoy more work, then join. Amplitude case study (snippet): Patreon's "blurred posts" (locked teasers) were built to lift pledge-flow conversion; no figure in the snippet.
  • Creator testimony on reach (all snippets, not opened, no numbers): r/PartneredYoutube threads (2024) ask whether members-first hurts reach, one creator using "a few days" early and another "a week early"; a widely copied Facebook post: "when the video goes public to your subscribers you get a lot less views" — the poster's own guess about the algorithm, not checked anecdote. A LinusTechTips reader complained members-only tiles crowding the feed made them leave.
  • No controlled study with numbers was found this run. Treat the pattern as practitioner convention, not a measured lift.

What the convention states

  1. Free is the newest version after a short window; the window is what's sold. Paid buys "first" (days to a week), not "only". Permanent paywalls on the best cut starve the free funnel, which is where buyers come from (Patreon's own discover-free-then-join model).
  2. The free default must be the best thing a newcomer can see. If the free cut is visibly older or weaker, the first impression is the weak one; the reach testimony above states the public release after a window already gets less push, so don't make it worse by keeping it permanently behind.
  3. Date the window in public. "Members see it first; free for everyone on " on the page converts the wait into a reason, and avoids the "why is the good one locked" complaint.
  4. Lock teasers, not blanks. A visible locked tile with a still and the date (Patreon's blurred-post pattern) works better than hiding it.
  5. One rule for every door. Mixed rules per door confuse; write the rule once and apply it to every version.

How we apply it to Cedwyn (proposal only — a pricing call for the project lead)

  • Tonight's state: Door 2's newest English native cut sits behind the £4.99 "latest" chip; the free default is the older Welsh v6 cut. The root flagged this as the project lead's pricing call.
  • Proposed rule for the /movie/ version system: newest cut is £4.99 for 7 days ("members first"), then becomes the free default automatically; the previous paid window closes when the next cut lands. Show "Free for everyone from " on the chip. Keeps a reason to pay (first look, supports the film) without hiding the best cut from new visitors and YouTube/Shorts traffic.
  • If the project lead prefers a permanent paid "latest", at least make the free default the strongest finished cut and say on the page what the paid one adds (language, lip-sync), so newcomers aren't judging the film by the weaker version.
  • Measure: plays of the free default vs £4.99 unlocks per door for 2 weeks after any change (cf_traffic + Stripe), before generalising.
