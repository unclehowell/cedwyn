Sales pitch / narrative structure: insight, alternatives, perfect world, then proof (Dunford "Sales Pitch", Raskin strategic narrative)

Written 31 Oct 2026 (SKILLS HARVEST). Builds on positioning-competitive-alternatives-dunford-method.md (positioning first, then the pitch).

What the practitioners say

  • April Dunford (guest post on Lenny's Newsletter, 17 Oct 2023, read in part; full post is paid): citing Dixon and McKenna's The JOLT Effect (analysis of over 2 million sales calls), 40% to 60% of B2B purchase processes end in "no decision", mostly not because the buyer preferred the status quo but because they could not decide confidently. "Buyers are more worried about messing up than they are about missing out."
  • Her fix is a two-part structure:
  • Setup (a few minutes, asked as questions: "does this resonate? what do you use today?"):
    1. Insight: what the buyer must know to see why our differentiated value matters. "The most crucial step and also the hardest."
    2. Alternatives: the honest trade-offs of each way of doing the job (including doing nothing).
    3. The perfect world: what a buyer would want if they could have it all, which happens to line up with our strengths.
  • Follow-through: our differentiated value and how we deliver it (demo), proof for each claim (case examples), objection handling, then a clear next step.
  • Rule she took from IBM: never talk about features outside the value they deliver; the pitch helps the buyer decide confidently rather than pushing.
  • Andy Raskin (strategic narrative; podcast summary read, his Medium post blocked here, snippet only): open with a big relevant change in the world that is not about you, show there will be winners and losers, tease the "promised land", then position the product as the way there, with evidence. His standard example is Zuora's "subscription economy" shift, which created urgency without being about Zuora's features.

The template (fill in this order)

  1. Insight / shift (one sentence, true and checkable, not about us).
  2. Alternatives (3-4 rows: approach | good at | falls short).
  3. Perfect world (3 bullets the buyer would agree with).
  4. Our differentiated value (max 3), each with:
  5. Proof (a page, an image, a record reference, a quote with consent).
  6. Objections (top 3 from comments or emails, each answered in one line).
  7. Next step (one call to action, one link). Use it for: the /ebooks/ sales page order of sections, a 20-min talk outline, a webinar, the first lines of an institutional email, a short video script.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

Draft (internal; public copy needs the owner's go and the editorial rules: never name the project lead; banned words states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken; our reading of records is our case, not a court finding): 1. Insight: much of the record of small Welsh estates now sits online in scattered catalogues and newspaper scans; the story only appears when the references are put side by side in date order. 2. Alternatives: the free Cedwyn pages (good for browsing, scattered for reading start to finish); county histories (wide, but skip farm-level detail); a day at Glamorgan Archives (primary, but you must know what to ask for); doing nothing. 3. Perfect world: one readable narrative; every statement with its reference; something you can keep, cite and take to the archive. 4-5. Value + proof: show one real spread (a page with its catalogue references), the contents list, and the evidence catalogue IDs it draws on (internal links only). 6. Objections: "why pay when the site is free?" (the book is the ordered, offline, cited version; the site stays free); "is this a campaign?" (sources shown for every page, our interpretation marked as such); "what format?" (EPUB/PDF, instant download). 7. Next step: one buy button on /ebooks/ plus one free sample link. Agent steps: - Write the full draft into the project workspace next to the positioning sheet; the BIZDEV run turns it into a PROPOSED /ebooks/ section order and a talk outline for civic-local-history-society-talks-penarth.md. - For institutional emails (drafts only, HubSpot hive format), use steps 1-3 in one short paragraph before the ask; never pitch the e-books in research/disclosure correspondence. - Measure: once live, compare buy-button click rate before/after the section reorder (utm-discipline-ga4-funnel.md); keep the version that wins over at least 2 weeks.
