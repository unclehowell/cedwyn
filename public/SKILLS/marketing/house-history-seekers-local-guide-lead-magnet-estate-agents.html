"History of my house" seekers: a local house-history guide as a lead magnet (and why estate agents care)

Topic: people researching the history of their own home are a large, searchable, local audience. A neutral "How to research the history of a house in Llandough / CF64" guide, built from records we already know, earns search traffic, sign-ups and links from people who are not yet interested in the case or the farm. Different from family-history society outreach (people tracing ancestors) and from the e-book funnel pages: here the entry point is the reader's own address. Harvested 2026-10-31 ~23:15Z. "house history", "house historian", "estate agent", "Rightmove", "conveyancing", "property history" had 0 hits in INDEX.md before this.

The evidence (read, with sources)

  • Findmypast house-history survey (blog, 6 Sep 2021, read; survey of 2,000+ UK homebuyers planning to buy within a year; commercial survey, methodology not published):
  • 70,000+ address searches in the 48 hours after Findmypast launched its address search (census 1841-1921 + 1939 Register): demand is real and immediate.
  • 20% of buyers look at a house's history before buying (31% of those buying within six months); 25% would research their home's history more.
  • 22% of sellers actively research their property's history to raise its value; Cardiff is the highest city at 28%.
  • 58% of prospective buyers are more likely to buy a property with a fascinating story; over a quarter would pay ~GBP 16,000 more on average.
  • Younger buyers care most: 39% of 25-34s vs 10% of 55+ willing to pay more; 50% of 25-34s say knowing past occupants would change how they see their home.
  • The National Archives research guide "Houses" (read): the best place to start is the local archive (maps, electoral registers, family and estate papers, building plans from the mid-19th century); TNA itself mainly helps for census years 1841-1921, the Valuation Office Survey 1910-15 and the 1939 Register; title deeds need only be kept 30 years (Law of Property Act 1925), so older deeds may not survive; ask HM Land Registry, the solicitor, the mortgage lender, or a repository holding a solicitor's collection.
  • Supporting sources for a Welsh guide (search results, pages not read in full this run): National Library of Wales Welsh tithe maps and schedules (library.wales digital exhibition); Glamorgan Archives research guides (glamarchives.gov.uk); TNA "Tithes" guide. Commercial house historians (e.g. househistoryresearch.uk) sell bespoke reports, which shows people pay for this (their prices/volumes: not checked).

How to do it (the playbook)

  1. Write one practical guide for one place, not a generic one: the actual record series for that parish in order (tithe map + apportionment, OS 25-inch maps, census 1841-1921, 1910 Valuation Office field books, 1939 Register, electoral registers, building plans, Land Registry, newspapers), where each is held, and which are free online.
  2. Add a worked example from a building whose records are already public and uncontroversial, showing each step with a screenshot of the source and its reference. A worked example is what makes the guide linkable.
  3. Title for the search: "How to find the history of your house in Llandough (CF64)" plus Welsh equivalent; HowTo structured data; one image per step.
  4. Offer the next step: a printable checklist (email sign-up) and "Send us what you find", which grows the local archive.
  5. Channel to estate agents (outward, needs GO): Findmypast's own numbers say sellers use history to raise value (Cardiff 28%). A local agent can link the guide in listings of older CF64 homes; this is a partnership pitch, so draft only.
  6. Measure: guide sessions from Search Console queries containing "history of my house", "who lived in my house", "house history" + place; checklist sign-ups; submissions; onward clicks to /ebooks/ and /research/.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Guide page (PROPOSAL, owner's GO for publishing): "Researching the history of a house in Llandough and Leckwith" under /research/ in cy/en/es, using the record series the evidence catalogue already cites (tithe, census, OS maps, Valuation Office, Land Registry, Glamorgan Archives, NLW). The worked example uses Ty Mawr / Great House Farm records that are already public on the site, written neutrally and linked to evidence IDs, so the guide doubles as an entry point into the case chronology without arguing it.
  • Editorial rules: never name the owner; no banned words (states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken); nothing about family holdings or the addresses of living people; neighbours' houses never used as examples without consent; no outbound links if the theme forbids them (cite archives by name and reference instead).
  • Sign-up: the printable checklist sits behind the existing Mailchimp form (tag: house-history), with a soft link to the e-books as "how one Llandough house's records were assembled".
  • Estate agents / conveyancers in CF64: a one-paragraph pitch offering the free guide for listings of pre-1950 homes, drafted as a [BIZDEV] HubSpot task (companies only, never private individuals); nothing sent without GO.
  • Fits Search Console work: track the queries above separately from the farm-name queries in the CTR-baseline skill; judge first on impressions for "house history" + Llandough/Penarth/CF64, then clicks.
