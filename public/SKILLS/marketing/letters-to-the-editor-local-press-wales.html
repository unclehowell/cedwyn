Letters pages: the free, editor-selected slot in local and national papers

What it is: a short reader's letter (about 150-300 words) replying to something the paper ran. No pitch, no press release, no ad spend. Editors choose letters, and readers and local decision-makers read the letters column closely.

What the sources show (27 Oct 2026; most items are search snippets, marked)

  • How widely letters are read:
  • Cooper et al. 2009, "Letters to the Editor and the People Who Write Them" (JSTOR 20452387, abstract snippet): 52% of editors ranked letters as the best-read items on the editorial page.
  • Perrin, Civic Hope, ch. 5 "People Who Read Letters" (Cambridge, snippet): 64% of regular newspaper readers read the letters column every day.
  • These are US studies. No UK readership figure was found.
  • How selective it is:
  • NYT Learning Network, 23 Apr 2021 (snippet; the page was blocked to our fetch): The Times gets "up to 1,000 letters a day" and publishes "a handful".
  • NYT Insider, 28 Mar 2017 (snippet): "between 10 and 15 letters appear daily".
  • That is about 1-1.5% at a national paper. Local weeklies get far fewer letters; their acceptance rate isn't measured here.
  • Format, consistent across published guidelines:
  • Washington Post help centre (snippet): "fewer than 300 words", starting from an article the paper ran, with full name, home address and email.
  • Most other guidance sits at 150-250 words, includes name, address and daytime phone (not for publication), and pastes the text into the email body with no attachments.
  • Letters "may be edited and shortened" (NYT help, snippet).

How to do it

  1. Hook: reply within 1-3 days to a specific piece the paper ran (a story, a column, another letter). Name its headline and date in your first line. Letters with no hook are the ones that get spiked.
  2. One point, 150-250 words. Add one fact readers can check, and one line saying where to see more, using a typed address rather than a link (print can't click, and many papers strip links).
  3. Sign in a real name with your town. Put your address and phone below the sign-off, marked "not for publication". Never anonymous, never a brand.
  4. Send to one paper at a time. Paste the text into the body with the subject "Letter: re , ". Don't send an attachment, and don't send it to several papers at once. Exclusivity is the usual expectation (not checked for each Welsh title).
  5. Tone: describe concerns and records, not people's motives. No accusations, no names of private individuals. The paper carries the legal risk and will cut or drop anything risky.
  6. Measure honestly: most local letters go out in print plus a web copy. Watch for a referral spike to the typed address with the tool below, and count it as print/word-of-mouth. Expect it to be small.
  7. Cadence: at most one letter per paper a month. Repeat writers become recognisable, and some editors cap how often they print the same person.

  • Status: PROPOSED. Press only goes out on the project lead's GO, and a letter is published in his own name, so he writes or approves every word and signs it. Tab can draft. Tab never sends.
  • Best hooks:
  • Penarth Times or Barry & District News coverage of Llandough heritage, the council or local history.
  • Western Mail / WalesOnline features on lost Welsh houses or archives.
  • Any piece on the 28 July Glaniad (Patagonia) anniversary.
  • Each letter adds one checkable record from the public catalogue, then: "The documents are online at cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate/research".
  • Keep away from the open council matters: the draft minutes of 22 Oct and the FOI review. A letter on those mid-process could harden positions. Wait until the minutes are confirmed and the review answers are in.
  • Editorial rules still apply to anything Cedwyn republishes about a letter: never name the project lead on Cedwyn pages; avoid the banned words; no accusations about named people.
  • Tracking: give each letter its own short typed path, e.g. cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate/letter (a /q/<code> style redirect per qr-codes-print-placement-own-redirects-honest-counts.md), so visits from print are countable.
