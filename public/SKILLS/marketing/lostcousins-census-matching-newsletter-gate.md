## Evidence (pages read today unless marked)
- **How it works (lostcousins.com home page, read):** members enter the relatives on their tree "who were recorded in selected US, Canadian, British, or Irish Censuses", then press Search. Matching is automatic and confidential: "nobody else needs to see your information", and a match on the same census entry means you are related.
- **Censuses supported (FAQ, read):** England & Wales 1841, 1881 and 1911 (incl. Isle of Man and Channel Islands); Scotland 1881; Ireland 1911; US 1880 and 1940; Canada 1881; Newfoundland 1921. The FAQ warns that 1841 shows no relationships, so household members must be inferred with care.
- **Size (site news page, read):** the 100,000th member joined on 30 Sep 2015 (latest public count found; nothing newer published).

## Why it fits a family-history book
- Our readers are the descendants and in-laws of the people in the book. LostCousins finds exactly those people, privately, and only when the census entry is identical, so false matches are rare.
- A matched cousin is more than a reader: cousins hold photographs, letters, leases and family papers that never reached an archive (the home page makes the same point). For Cedwyn that is an evidence route as much as a sales route.
- The newsletter's readers are experienced UK researchers who act on tips; member-written articles get printed.

## How to do it (all steps need a member account = the project lead's GO; nothing here has been done)
1. **Rights and privacy first.** Enter only deceased relatives from the census (living people are never entered or published; see family-tree-page-living-privacy-proof-standard.md).
2. **Build the entry list offline** from our own transcriptions: every Williams/Buckler household member at Great House / Tŷ Mawr, Llandough in E&W 1841, 1881 and 1911 (piece/folio/page refs from our census notes). Keep it in work/ (private), not on the site.
3. **Member enters them** (an account in the family member's own name; Tab never creates it). Search. Each match yields a private contact route inside LostCousins.
4. **Contact script for matches** (drafted as a HubSpot draft, sent only by the member): one line on the shared household, one question ("does your branch hold any papers or photos from Great House?"), one soft line that the family's history is written up at the site. No sales pitch in the first message.
5. **Newsletter route (later):** once the 2027 site is live and the new editor is known, propose a member article: a practical piece, e.g. "Using the 1841 and 1881 censuses to fix a Welsh farm household", worked from our own records, with one mention of the e-book in the author line. Pitch idea first; follow the newsletter's own tone (tips, not adverts).
6. **Measure:** matches found, matched cousins who reply, items of family material offered, and visits on ?src=lostcousins (only if the newsletter prints a link). 

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Now, no GO needed:** Tab prepares the private census entry list (step 2) from what is already transcribed in work/, flags gaps (missing 1911 refs, 1841 relationships inferred), and stores it at the project workspace when the next bizdev run picks this up.
- **On the project lead's GO:** a family member joins (free) and enters the list; Tab drafts reply messages as HubSpot drafts only.
- **Do not:** create any account ourselves, post the list publicly, mention the court case or ownership dispute in cousin messages, or send anything. Never name the project lead in public text.
- **Kill test:** fewer than 2 matches after all Williams/Buckler households are entered, or no reply from any match in 3 months: stop, keep the entry list for the other census-match routes.
