Postcard collectors (deltiology): a sourcing and community route for village views

Written 31 Oct 2026 by the skills harvest. Status: proposal plus a free watch we can run ourselves. "postcard", "deltiology", "ephemera" and "ebay" had 0 skill files in the index before; the only mention was the "ideas left" line.

Why it matters

  • Edwardian picture postcards (roughly 1900-1918, the "golden age" collectors and libraries name) are often the only dated photographs of small villages, farms, lanes and chapels. Postmarks and written messages add a date and a voice.
  • The people who hold them are collectors and dealers, not archives. They meet monthly, trade at fairs, and many enjoy seeing their cards put in context.
  • For Cedwyn, that means two things: new period images for the evidence and timeline pages (with permission), and a warm, non-political audience that buys local-history e-books.

What the sources show

  • Postcard Traders Association (postcard.co.uk, read 31 Oct 2026): formed 1975, vetted UK dealers, auctioneers and fair organisers. It keeps a UK club list and a fairs diary. Its two-day shows are Woking (2027: 14-15 May and 17-18 Sept) and the Festival of Cards in Somerset (2027: 11-12 March).
  • South Wales Postcard Club (PTA club list, read): meets at Ararat, the Church on the Common, Whitchurch, Cardiff, on the second Thursday of each month at 19:30 (not in August). The North Wales club meets in Llandudno, third Tuesday at 14:00, visitors welcome.
  • Nearest fair in the diary: Bristol, Saturday 21 Nov, B.A.W.A. Leisure Centre, Filton (PTA fairs diary, read).
  • Hobbyist UK (read): clubs usually meet monthly, welcome casual visitors, and many run an annual fair.
  • The trade press is shrinking. Reflections of a Bygone Age (read) ended the Picture Postcard Annual with its 2024 edition and Picture Postcard Magic with issue 22 (Dec/Jan 2025-26). Club websites and Facebook groups now carry the news, so a printed ad route is closing.
  • The market is online. Delcampe states more than 1 million active users a month and free registration (its own advert on the PTA page, a vendor claim, not checked). eBay and Delcampe both allow free saved searches with email alerts.
  • Public collections: Amgueddfa Cymru's online collection holds topographical postcards of the area, for example "The Dingle, Penarth", item F76.112.13 (seen in search results only, not opened).

How to do it

  1. Build a watch list (free, no account action needed to start). Write search strings for each place name and its variants: Llandough, Llandochau, Leckwith, Lecwydd, Cogan, Penarth Road, Ely bridge, Llandough Hospital, the chapel and church names, and the farm names exactly as in our catalogue. Search eBay sold and active listings, Delcampe and the PTA dealers' online shops once a week. Record each card in a CSV: found_date, site, listing_url, title, view, publisher, postmark_date, price, seller_type.
  2. Date each card from its evidence: - postmark; - publisher and series number (Valentine, Frith, Tuck and the local photographers' runs); - divided back (UK from 1902); - stamp rate. If a card isn't postmarked, give a range, never a single year.
  3. Rights before reuse. A listing photo is the seller's; don't reuse it. To publish an image, either buy the card (only on the project lead's GO, because it's spend) or ask the owner for a scan and written permission with a credit line. Old photographs can still be in copyright depending on the photographer and date; record what is known, and treat this as a check rather than legal advice.
  4. Go to a club meeting with something to give. Bring a one-page printout: "Llandough and Leckwith views we are looking for", a 6-card grid of what we already have, with dates and catalogue IDs, plus a QR code to a free Cedwyn page. Ask for scans, not sales. Collectors respond to specific want lists, not general appeals.
  5. Give back in public. Add a "Postcard views" page section listing each card's view, date range, publisher and the collector's chosen credit (or "private collection"). It links to the matching evidence or timeline page. A thank-you mention is often the only reward a collector wants.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Start the watch this week as a desk job: a worker runs the weekly search and appends to the project workspace. It contacts nobody and spends nothing. Only real finds go into the morning-brief notes as "postcard of , , ".
  • Club visit: the South Wales Postcard Club meeting (second Thursday, Whitchurch, Cardiff) is in person, so it's the project lead's choice whether he or someone else goes. Prepare the want-list sheet as a draft only. A first email to the club secretary is outreach: it becomes a HubSpot draft task, and nothing goes out without his GO.
  • Editorial rules on the page:
  • neutral view captions;
  • no banned words; never name the project lead;
  • no family holdings or addresses; no insignia (Tuck "heraldic" series cards stay off);
  • Llandough Farm is not Great House Farm, so caption the farm exactly as the card prints it and flag any mismatch as "not checked".
  • E-book tie-in: a "Llandough in old postcards" chapter or a free sampler PDF can come later, once 10 or more permitted images exist. The /ebooks/ link goes in its back matter.
  • Measure: cards found per month, permissions granted, new images published, and visits to the postcard section. Kill rule: no relevant card found in 3 months of weekly searching means dropping to a monthly search.
  • Tools: none installed. Saved searches are the platforms' own free feature, and a CSV is enough.
