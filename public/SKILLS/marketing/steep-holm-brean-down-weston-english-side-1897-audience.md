## Facts (read 31 Oct 2026)
- **The 1897 sequence (BBC News, 13 May 2022, read):**
  - On 11-12 May, transmissions from Flat Holm failed.
  - On 13 May, "CAN YOU HEAR ME" was received at Lavernock.
  - The team then moved to Brean Down Fort, south of Weston-super-Mare, and sent a message nearly 10 miles, a record at the time.
  - Marconi's assistant George Kemp was from Cardiff. The signed message slip is in Amgueddfa Cymru (the National Museum Wales).
  - The Weston-super-Mare Radio Society chair was quoted for the 125th anniversary. It is a ready local voice; the radio-club route is in the Marconi file.
- **The plaque (Read The Plaque, CC BY 4.0, read):** it was erected by the Rotary Club of Cardiff in 1947. It names Lavernock-Flat Holm on 11 May and Lavernock-Brean Down on 18 May 1897. Note that the plaque and the BBC give different dates; we cite each as written and do not reconcile them.
- **Steep Holm:** Kenneth Allsop Memorial Trust Ltd, charity 270059, company 1227490.
  - The trust bought the island in 1976.
  - Members receive "regular newsletters" and an AGM-and-lunch invitation (steepholm.online, read).
  - Total income was £8,365 and spending £15,445 in the year to 5 Apr 2025, with 9 trustees (Charity Commission, read).
  - Its 1867 Victorian Barracks houses the visitor centre, and "research is encouraged and published".
- **Volunteers (BBC, 10 Jul 2023, read):** the trust had 20 volunteers and said it needed about 20 more. Day trips last about 12 hours because of the tide. A trustee: "it's very important to spread the history so everyone can know what's going on out here."
- **Reach, honestly:** both island charities are small (under £10k income each). The English-side value is the **Weston-super-Mare 1897 audience**, which already treats the event as its own (BBC piece framed from Somerset). A Welsh-farm angle on "the mainland side at Lavernock" is new to them. That is our claim to their attention, as an interpretation, not checked with them.
