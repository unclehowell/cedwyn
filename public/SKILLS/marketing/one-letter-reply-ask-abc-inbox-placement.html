One-letter reply ask (A / B / C) to earn replies and inbox placement

Harvest 2026-10-31 ~00:15Z. Internal working note. 'hit reply' had 0 hits in INDEX.md. Not an onboarding-sequence skill (covered elsewhere) and not the reply-as-community skill (reader-replies-and-small-community.md): this is the specific ask format that gets replies, and why replies matter for where the next email lands. Usable in any send (newsletter, e-book delivery email, academy notice).

What the sources show (read 31 Oct 2026)

  • Gmail Email sender guidelines (support.google.com, read): "Messages sent from an address in the recipient's contacts are less likely to be marked as spam." Also: confirm opt-in, periodically confirm recipients want to stay subscribed, "Consider unsubscribing recipients who don't open or read your messages." Gmail does not document replies as a ranking signal; the reply-to-placement link below is practitioner experience, not Google's wording.
  • Yahoo Sender Hub best practices (read): "send timely and relevant email to an active and engaged audience"; spam rate under 0.3%.
  • Jeff Felten's welcome-email test (Chenell Basilio, Growth In Reverse, LinkedIn post, read; one creator's result, not a benchmark): instead of "What's your biggest challenge?" he asks one easy question with three options A/B/C; the reader replies with one letter. 15% of new subscribers replied to the first email; Basilio estimates the usual welcome reply rate at "well under 5%". Her reasoning: a reply tells Gmail/Apple "I want this sender", which can decide Primary vs Promotions.
  • Dan Oshinsky (Inbox Collective, comment on the same post): with local/niche audiences a specific question works - local newsletters asking "What's one local restaurant you've been to recently that we should review?" got strong response (his figure cut off; not checked).
  • Matt McGarry, Newsletter Operator (1 Feb 2025, read): welcome email's jobs include getting subscribers to take deliverability actions (reply, move to Primary); "Ask for a simple 1-word reply"; open-ended closing questions get fewer replies.

How to do it

  1. One question, three lettered answers, reply with the letter. Put it in the first screen of the email, before any link. Example shape: "Which should we open next? Reply A, B or C."
  2. Make the options real decisions we will act on and report back (that closes the loop and earns the next reply).
  3. Reply-to must be a watched inbox on our own domain (never noreply). Every reply gets a short human answer within 48 h; that thread is itself engagement.
  4. Tally: count letters by hand or with a filter; publish the result in the next issue ("62% picked B, so here it is").
  5. Use it sparingly: once per new subscriber (first email) plus at most one poll per month; constant asks lose their pull.
  6. Measure placement, not opens: Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens; track reply rate per send and the Postmaster Tools spam rate. Pair with the list-hygiene sunset rule for people who never open or reply.
  7. Institutions (UK councils, archives) are different: no polls in disclosure/FOI mail. This is for our own readers list only.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • PROPOSAL for the Mailchimp readers list (no send without the normal GO; nothing sent by this run): next newsletter opens with "Which record should we open next? Reply A, B or C: A) the 1824 Bute plan of Ty Mawr, B) the 1841 tithe fields, C) the 1958 newspaper report." Winner becomes the next Story Time video / blog post, announced with the vote share.
  • E-book delivery email (after a £4.99 purchase): one line "Reply with one letter: how did you find us? A) YouTube B) Facebook/Instagram C) search D) someone told me" - doubles as buyer attribution (see how-did-you-hear-buyer-attribution-question.md) and earns a reply from every buyer who answers.
  • Academy notice to members: "Reply A/B/C: which grade topic should we add next?" feeds the question-bank roadmap.
  • Reply-to: a cedwyn/bucklerfamily.estate address someone reads; log reply counts per send in the project workspace
  • Public-text rules apply to the email copy (never name the owner, banned words, nothing about family holdings).
