Pair heritage content with a local anniversary that already has a community: Marconi, Lavernock-Flat Holm, 13 May 1897 (130th = 13 May 2027)

Topic: piggyback a dated local anniversary that an existing, active community already marks every year. Here, that's amateur radio clubs operating special event stations for Marconi's Bristol Channel tests. Don't invent our own date; join an existing date. This is different from the general sector hashtag calendar (heritage-anniversary-hashtag-calendar.md) and from Wikipedia OTD riding: the community is local, recurring and already organised.

The facts (primary sources, read 31 Oct 2026)

  • The date. On 13 May 1897, Marconi's signal crossed open sea between Lavernock Point (near Penarth) and Flat Holm, about 3.5 miles (Visit the Vale; BBC Wales History blog 2012; Roath Local History Society). The 125th was marked in May 2022 (DX-World and other posts). The 130th falls on Thursday 13 May 2027.
  • A second annual date. International Marconi Day (IMD) runs on the Saturday nearest Marconi's birthday (25 April). It's organised by the Cornish Radio Amateur Club, and it was in its 30th year in 2017 (ARRL). "Dozens" of registered award stations take part, and operators earn certificates by working 15 of them. In 2027 the nearest Saturday is 24 April.
  • The local community. Barry Amateur Radio Society (BARS) "has long commemorated the Marconi-Kemp Bristol Channel tests" and has operated from Flat Holm as GB5FI (bars.wales). Its old site said it had been going to the island for 45+ years. RSGB's news listed BARS operating GB5IMD from Flat Holm for IMD 2013. BARS also attended Flat Holm Day at Penarth Pier Pavilion (Saturday 25 July; its Facebook group, year not checked).
  • The reach of such events (numbers). One US museum ship's special event station logged 1,198 contacts in a 2023 weekend (BSFARC). ARRL reported about 100 ships on air in one Museum Ships Weekend. Every contact is logged and often confirmed with a QSL card, and many stations now send digital QSLs. That's a souvenir which can carry a URL. These are radio contacts, not web visits. No click or sales figure from a special event station was found, so this is not checked as a sales channel.
  • The rules for a station (if a partner club runs one). Ofcom issues a special event Notice of Variation (GB prefix) only to a Full or club licence holder, for at most 28 days, at one stated location. RSGB publicises an event in RadCom and GB2RS only if the applicant ticks the data-sharing box. A used GB callsign is not normally re-issued for 12 months (RSGB). We can't run a station ourselves; a licensed club does.

Playbook

  1. Map the dated anniversaries near the subject (5-10 miles), each with an existing community that marks it: a radio club, lifeboat station, civic society or church. Note the date, the community, its channels (newsletter, RadCom/GB2RS, Facebook group) and its next round-number year.
  2. Find the honest link to our records. Use one sentence of real connection. Example (our inference, check against the catalogue first): what the farm's records show in May 1897 or the 1890s, the parish, and how the Lavernock coast fitted the landscape of the time. No connection, no piece. Never fake one.
  3. Make one useful thing for their audience, not an advert. For a radio club that means a short "the parish in 1897" page with dated records, a map, and a printable side for their QSL card or display board. The club gets content; we get a link from their QSL, post or newsletter.
  4. Lead time. Clubs plan IMD and the 13 May events months ahead, and the Ofcom NoV is applied for in advance. Approach by January for April-May 2027.
  5. Measure. Give each partner its own utm_source (e.g. bars-marconi130), and put a QR code on any printed or QSL side. Count visits in the week either side of the date against a baseline. Kill rule: under 30 visits across the 2027 window means you drop the channel for 2028.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Add 13 May 2027 (Marconi 130th) and 24 Apr 2027 (IMD) to the anniversary calendar. Also show them on the Cedwyn Calendar webapp as context events, styled differently from evidence (not catalogue records, not counted in coverage %). That also gives the calendar's empty May an honest neighbour.
  • PROPOSAL: a Cedwyn page "Llandough parish in 1897". It uses only dated catalogue records, verbatim quotes with IDs, and is labelled "our inference" where we join the dots. It must have no outbound links (house rule), so partners link to us, not the reverse.
  • Outreach to BARS (or the Penarth Society / Flat Holm volunteers) only as a HubSpot draft for the owner's GO, offering the page and a QSL-side design. No DMs, no contact, zero spend.
  • Public text: neutral third person, no banned words, no insignia, nothing about family holdings, and Llandough Farm is not Great House Farm.
