Nextory Spain (ex-Nubico) after Bonnier's purchase: who feeds the Spanish shelf, and the recheck rule

Harvested 31 Oct 2026 (skills harvest). It extends kobo-plus-vs-nextory-spanish-edition-subscription-gate.md and publit-publizon-one-title-uk-publisher-fees-no-spain-reach.md, which priced Nextory's two small-publisher routes and found neither covers Spain. New here: the September 2026 ownership change, and the distributor actually named as a Nextory Spain feed. Proposals only.

Facts (read 31 Oct 2026)

  • History: Nextory bought Nubico from Grupo Planeta and Telefónica in June 2021, merged the apps and launched Nextory in Spain, then retired the Nubico brand (Nextory press release on Mynewsdesk/Cision; Publishing Perspectives, Sept 2026).
  • New owner: Bonnier Books, which also owns BookBeat, agreed on 1 Sept 2026 to buy all of Nextory (Publishing Perspectives 2026/09; The New Publishing Standard, 2 Sept 2026).
  • TNPS puts Nextory in ten markets, Spain among them, with 1.4M+ titles and SEK 671M revenue for 2024.
  • Small-publisher policy: TNPS: "Nextory already declines to deal directly with small publishers and self-published authors, routing them instead through distributors such as Publit and Publizon. Bonnier has no obvious reason to relax that policy and every incentive to extend something like it across the combined platform."
  • Nextory's own Spanish help page states the same: "te recomendamos contactar con uno de nuestros distribuidores: Publit; Publizon" (support.nextory.se, article 28166934244882).
  • Who feeds the Spanish shelf: Bookwire names "Nextory Spain" among its subscription channels in its Editorial Planeta deal. That deal covers about 30,000 e-books and 1,200 audiobooks, effective 1 July 2021 (Bookwire newsroom).
  • Planeta's catalogue reaches Nextory Spain through Bookwire, not through Publit or Publizon.
  • Bookwire contracts with publishers, not single-title self-publishers. Its terms for a one-title UK imprint are not checked.
  • Other aggregators: StreetLib One (€299 once) lists Nextory among its partners. Whether that feed reaches the Spanish Nextory catalogue is not checked.
  • The market: the Spanish e-book market is 87.6% unit sales and only 5.6% subscriptions (De Marque 2025, already in spanish-ebook-stores-routes-la-visita-casa-del-libro-bajalibros.md). Per-read pay is about €0.10 (Kobo Plus file).

How-to: decide on any subscription shelf in a country

  1. Read the service's own help page for "small publisher". If it names distributors, list them.
  2. For each distributor, find a page that names that country's catalogue (e.g. "Nextory Spain"), not just the brand.
  3. Price the route per title per year. Divide by €0.10 per read to get the reads needed, then compare with the country's subscription share (Spain: 5.6%).
  4. If no route is free or one-off and below about €50 a year, park it. Recheck only when a trigger fires (below).
  5. Recheck triggers: - an ownership change (as on 1 Sept 2026); - the help page changes its distributor list; - an aggregator we already use adds the country's catalogue.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Decision for La visita: Nextory Spain stays parked. No free or one-off route reaches the Spanish shelf:
  • Publit and Publizon don't cover Spain;
  • Bookwire is publisher-contract only;
  • StreetLib One is €299 and its Spain feed is not checked.

With subscriptions at 5.6% of Spanish e-book sales, the money is in single-copy stores: Kobo, Apple, Google Play, amazon.es, Casa del Libro, and Bajalibros via bajalibros-runs-on-publica-la-marketplace-route-no-direct-signup.md. - Watch item: add a quarterly check of support.nextory.se article 28166934244882 and Bonnier/BookBeat publisher pages. If BookBeat's intake opens to small publishers after the merger, re-run step 3. Log only; contact no one. - If StreetLib One is ever bought (owner's spend decision): ask StreetLib in writing whether its Nextory feed includes Spain before counting it as a benefit.
