Skill: Local Democracy Reporters (BBC LDRS) as the press route for council-records stories

Written 30 Oct 2026 (skills harvest). Press contact happens only on the project lead's GO; this file prepares, it does not pitch.

Why it matters (evidence, primary sources)

  • The BBC funds 165 Local Democracy Reporters (LDRs) employed by local newsrooms across England, Scotland, Wales and NI. Since the start of 2018 the scheme has produced "more than 470,000 stories for publication by partner newsrooms" (BBC Media Centre, 8 May 2025).
  • Contracts re-tendered May 2025: 118 contracts, 15 media organisations, running 1 Jul 2025 to end of 2027. Reach (WalesOnline's owner) is the largest holder: 54 contracts, 83 reporters. Every part of the UK has an LDR.
  • Reach multiplier: every LDR story must go into the shared LDRS Authoring Tool "so all partners see it simultaneously"; any qualifying partner (BBC, hyperlocals, other papers) can run it. One good story can appear in several outlets, and BBC Local Radio can request a 2-way interview.
  • Peak output: 1,321 stories in one week (Sep 2021); almost 250,000 in the first four years (Wikipedia summary of the scheme, secondary).

What LDRs cover and how they work (BBC LDRS operating terms, bbc.com)

  • Core brief: councils (in two-tier areas, district decisions such as planning) and "other public services"; other public-interest local-democracy stories are allowed if they do not displace the core. National politics is out of scope.
  • They report to the contract holder (e.g. Reach), not the BBC. Story ideas go to the reporter; complaints about the service go to their manager.
  • Story titles follow "COUNCIL NAME: Story Name" and must be tagged with a top-tier local authority. So a pitch must name the council and the decision, meeting or record involved.
  • "LDRs should not file a story based on a press release already in the public domain unless it has been significantly developed with new quotes or information." A blast release does not work; an exclusive, evidenced lead does.
  • They must source content and add a Note to Editors on whether qualified privilege applies. Council meetings and official records are the safest ground (privilege); allegations about named people are not.
  • Images used must be rights-free. Offer our own photos or clearly public-domain scans, with the rights stated.
  • A practitioner guide for organisations (IMIX, "How to reach out to a local newsroom"): LDRs "are experts on council processes", "have the time to pursue" a story, follow broadcaster-style neutrality, and are "invaluable if you have a story that you can back up with evidence". They will not run a story based on opinion.

How to do it (steps)

  1. Find the patch reporter. Search "Local Democracy Reporter " and check the newsroom author page (e.g. walesonline.co.uk). Recent search listings name LDRs for Cardiff and the Vale of Glamorgan at WalesOnline (Ted Peskett, Alex Seabrook) and a Reach LDR on X (Kieran Molloy). Not checked as current: confirm on the author page, which lists their latest bylines.
  2. Read their last 10 stories. Note which council decisions, FOI-led and planning stories they ran, and the tone.
  3. Build the lead around a public record or a council decision, not around a dispute: the agenda item, the minute, the FOI/EIR reply ("not held" is a story when a statute requires it to exist), with the dates.
  4. Make the pitch one short email: a "COUNCIL: story" style subject; three lines on what the record shows; links to the primary documents on our evidence pages; what is new (never published before); who can be quoted on the record; rights-free images. Offer it to one LDR as first look and give them a reasonable window.
  5. Never send the same text as a release to everyone at once (it kills the LDR angle).
  6. Log the pitch, reply and any story in HubSpot. If a story runs, the LDRS feed may put it in several outlets: watch with Google Alerts for the story title and the council name for 7 days and record every pickup.
  7. Corrections: if a published story has a factual error, send a short note to the reporter with the document; do not argue in comments.

Pitfalls

  • Defamation and privilege: LDRs will strip anything not covered by a record or an on-the-record source. Stick to what documents show.
  • One reporter covers several councils and busy meeting diaries; avoid committee-week deadlines and follow up once, after about a week.
  • Not a buying channel: expect no direct sales. The value is an independent, dated news source citing the records, which helps search, AI answers and future institutional replies.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Candidate leads (all need the project lead's GO before any contact, and must use the editorial rules: no naming the project lead, none of the banned words, nothing about family holdings or addresses, no accusations about named people): a) A council or public-body FOI/EIR reply where a record that statute requires is "not held", from /research/missing-records/, with dates. b) A historic-environment or planning record about the former manorial house at Llandough, newly found in a public archive (from the online gap finder or first-seen detector).
  • Before any pitch, check the standing holds: the contacts flywheel bars contacting the council and certain parties, and press goes only on the project lead's GO. An LDR is press, not the council, so the bar is the project lead's GO.
  • Tab prepares in HubSpot: one draft task "[PRESS DRAFT] LDR Cardiff/Vale: " holding the pitch text, the document links, the rights note for images, and the reporter's current byline page. the project lead sends it himself if he states go.
  • Measure: pickups count (outlets running the LDRS story), referral visits tagged utm_source= on the evidence page, and any institution reply that cites the story.
