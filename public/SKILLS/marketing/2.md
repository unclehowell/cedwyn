# Skill #2 — Business research & strategy for a niche heritage e-book shop (2026)
Written 26 Oct 2026 by the project lead's Tab agent from practitioner testimony.

## What practitioners report
- Local-history authors sell through talks and stalls, press releases to local papers, library sales, promotional articles in society journals and newsletters, and follow-up publicity after each event. ("How to Write and Publish Local and Family History", hoap.co.uk, chapter 8–9: Lectures and stalls; Press releases; Library sales; Promotional articles.) A US local-history author selling self-published photo books since 2001 reports selling out an edition (Wayne Glenn, Facebook, 2025).
- History niches grow fast on Facebook: one publisher network reports an Ancient Egypt page going from 0 to 40,000 followers in 2 months and a Roman Empire page with tens of thousands of engagements, on a $3/day boost budget, and states replying to every comment multiplied engagement. (Ezoic live case study, Jul 2024.) Cedwyn has zero spend, so the organic half (daily posts, reply to every comment, share-worthy record images) is ours; the $3/day half is the project lead's call.
- Direct sales: a Gumroad sales page averages about 3.2% conversion (insightraider, 2026). With about 17 UK visits/day to Cedwyn, that is under one sale every two days even at an average conversion rate, so TRAFFIC is the bottleneck, not the shop.
- Welcome flows and weekly consistency drive list growth (see skills/1.md).

## How-to (Cedwyn)
1. Each day, pick the single bottleneck stage with numbers: traffic → signup → read → sale. Today it is traffic (17 UK visits/day, 0 real signups).
2. Spend effort where the audience already gathers: Welsh history societies and their newsletters, family-history societies, Facebook history groups, YouTube viewers (1,067 views on the farm videos).
3. Offer talks and articles before pitching books; society journals and newsletters reprint good stories.
4. Every piece of content links a SPECIFIC Cedwyn page with UTM tags; measure by utm_content.
5. Weekly: one decision note in the Cedwyn growth project record (what moved, what to stop, what to try next).

### #2 18/26 What makes heritage posts spread on Facebook (2025–26)
- Meta's 2025 Widely Viewed Content report, as summarised by Buffer (2025): nearly 98% of posts US users viewed had no link. Keep the story inside the post (photo, text, carousel, reel); use links selectively.
- Hootsuite (2025), quoting a social strategist: "Content that drives comments and shares will naturally get more visibility." Reply to every comment.
- Archivists' Facebook groups (2025–26) complain about AI-made fake "history" posts. Our edge is the opposite: a real record image with its source line.
- Link-post cap test for not checked Pages, about 2 a month (Mari Smith, Dec 2025; see facebook-group-community-and-link-limits.md).
- Applied: the Page already carries 2–3 scheduled episode reels a day to Thu 29 Oct. New posts go on empty days only, as photo posts that tell the record in full, ending with one question. The page address goes in the text, not as a link card.
### #2 19/26 Partnerships with local history societies
- Long-run model (hoap.co.uk ch. 8–9): talks and stalls, articles for society journals and newsletters, library sales, then publicity after each event.
- Constraint (the project lead 10:28 GMT): pitches go only to incorporated companies. Most local history societies are unincorporated or CIOs, so we can't send them pitches. What's left: (a) incorporated charitable companies (check on Companies House first); (b) organic presence, i.e. a reply when a society posts a request we can answer from the record, and being useful in their public threads; (c) Local & Community History Month (May) content they can share.
