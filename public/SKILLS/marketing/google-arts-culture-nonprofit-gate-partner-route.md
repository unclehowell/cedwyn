## The gate (primary source: Google Arts & Culture Platform Help FAQ, answer 6002688, read 31 Oct 2026)
- "The Google Arts & Culture platform is open to **non-profit institutions, museums, galleries, and archives** with copyright-free or copyright-cleared content." Access is **by invitation only**: request one at g.co, and "if eligible, we'll send you an invite".
- **No fee.** The partner **keeps the rights**. Only **copyright-free or copyright-cleared** images are accepted, and the partner handles any copyright assertions and takedowns.
- Time to publish: "as quick as a few months" once the agreement is accepted and content plus metadata are supplied.
- The partners page (about.artsandculture.google.com, read) puts it at **more than 2,000 cultural institutions**, and Google helps them "digitize, manage, and publish".

## Evidence on what partnering brings
- **The National Library of Wales is a partner** with published stories, e.g. "Wales & Trans-Atlantic Slavery" and "Five Highlights from The National Library of Wales" (artsandculture.google.com story pages, from search results). The Artichoke Trust ("PROCESSIONS: Banners made in Wales") and the British Council ("Folk Archive") show that small trusts and non-museum bodies also get in.
- Cuberis (museum digital agency, read) warns that the platform keeps visitors on Google's site and does little to drive traffic back to the museum's own site. Their advice: decide on mission grounds, not traffic.
- The REACT case study (react-culture.eu 3.2.10) covers the pros and cons for small and medium institutions. I only read its title and snippet; no traffic numbers. The USF capstone (Udell, 2019) assesses effectiveness but gives no referral figures for small partners in the parts I read.
- **No source found gives referral traffic or sales numbers** from a GA&C story back to a partner's own site. Don't expect it to drive sales.

## Decision rule
1. **Is the publisher a non-profit institution, museum, gallery or archive?** Cedwyn sells e-books and an academy and is not a registered charity, so **no, don't apply**. A request would be refused, and it would claim a status we don't have.
2. **The open route is through an existing partner.** A partner such as the National Library of Wales, Glamorgan Archives (check whether it's a partner first) or a local society that has charity status could publish a GA&C story on *its own* holdings: Llandough, Leckwith, the Penarth/Vale estates. Cedwyn's part is only to suggest the topic and point to record IDs the partner already holds. We supply no images unless they are copyright-cleared, and we get no credit links.
3. **Measure:** nothing to measure on our side (no backlinks). The value is that the records become discoverable, which helps the research, not sales.
