Licensing translation rights (Spanish / Welsh) + the Wales Literature Exchange grant hook

Harvested 30 Oct 2026. Not covered before ('foreign rights', 'translation rights', 'literature exchange' = 0 in INDEX). Read-only; nothing sent.

The model

Licensing = renting a foreign publisher the right to publish in one language, territory and format for a fixed term; you keep the copyright and your own editions (ScribeCount guide, R. Wood, 26 Jun 2026, read).

Numbers from practitioners

  • Royalty: translation licences typically start ~7–8% of retail/net for print and trade editions (Asian markets ~6–7%); broader range 6–10% depending on format, territory and leverage (ScribeCount).
  • Advance: kept whatever the sales; royalties earn it out first. "$5K to $10K is a good overseas offer (depending on genre etc.)" — ALLi's rights adviser, AskALLi "What to Do When You're Approached by an Overseas Publisher" (read). Niche non-fiction from a small press will sit well under that; expect hundreds, not thousands.
  • Term: foreign licences are often 3, 5 or 7 years, so a weak deal expires (ALLi).
  • Fairs: Frankfurt/Bologna deals are made in pre-booked meetings; a rights-arena table costs on the order of $1,000 — only for a developed catalogue (ScribeCount). Online rights catalogues (PubMatch free/paid; Frankfurt Rights, formerly IPR License, about $79/yr per a 2014 author post — old, fee not checked) are the cheap listing route.

Offer checklist (ALLi)

An offer must state: advance, royalty %, term. Also ask: estimated print run, unit price, publication date, publisher info, any extra rights (audio etc.). - Limit term, territory, format. Make "publish within 24 months" a hard date with reversion if missed. - Never grant film/TV/game rights in a translation deal. - Scams exist: genuine licensors pay YOU; anyone asking a fee to translate/publish is a vanity service.

The Wales hook (makes a small deal viable)

Wales Literature Exchange Translation Grants Fund (page read 30 Oct 2026): - Open to FOREIGN publishers translating Wales' two literatures (Welsh- and English-language); UK publishers may apply only for Welsh→English. - On average 50% of the translator's fee is supported; translation cost only, not production; not retrospective. - Two rounds a year; the page banner reads "The deadline for applications is December 14th" (year not stated on the page — check before relying on it). - Paid after publication: 6 copies, PDF, cover, proof the translator was paid; must publish within 30 months of the offer; credit line + WLE logo on the prelims. So a Spanish-language publisher (Argentina — Chubut/Patagonia Welsh-heritage readership — or Spain) gets half its translator cost covered, which turns "too small to bother" into a pitchable title.

How to do it (indie, small)

  1. One-page rights sheet per title: hook, word count, sales/reader proof, existing translations, rights available (language + territory + format), WLE grant eligibility line.
  2. List on a free rights catalogue (PubMatch free tier).
  3. Shortlist publishers who already publish Welsh-Patagonia or Welsh history in Spanish (search their catalogues; WLE's past grant list is a ready target list).
  4. Owner sends the pitch himself (or HubSpot draft for him). Negotiate term/territory/format as above.

How we apply it to Cedwyn (PROPOSAL only)

  • Cedwyn already produces cy/en/es material itself. Licensing a Spanish PRINT edition to an Argentine publisher is complementary: we keep our own e-book editions; licence print-only, Argentina (or Spanish-language Latin America), 5 years.
  • Eligibility to check first: WLE funds "literature from Wales"; whether a heritage/local-history non-fiction title qualifies is not checked — ask WLE (owner's decision to contact; HubSpot draft only).
  • Next step for agents: build the rights sheet + a shortlist of 5 Spanish-language publishers with Welsh-Patagonia lists, as a HubSpot task for the hive. No contact, no spend. Public text rules apply; nothing about family holdings.
