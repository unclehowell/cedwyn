# Oral-history interviews as a content and community engine (with a proper recording agreement)

Harvested 31 Oct 2026 (SKILLS HARVEST RUN). Index check: "oral" and "interview" had no oral-history skill file. reader-memories-call-ugc-consent-local-history.md covers written memories sent in. This skill covers recorded, archived interviews, which carry different legal rules.

## What the practitioner bodies say (read 31 Oct 2026)
- **Oral History Society (OHS), "Legal and ethical: after the interview"** (read):
  - The interviewee owns the copyright in their recorded words.
  - Projects need "clear authority to use those materials", in the form of a signed Recording Agreement (OHS PDF, 53 KB) that allows extracts "in publications, broadcasts, exhibitions or on the internet".
  - Any closures or restrictions are recorded with time codes and end dates, and both sides keep a copy.
  - Simple assignment of copyright is "the most straightforward" option.
  - Creative Commons licences are now "not recommended for oral history" because they conflict with GDPR.
  - UK law offers no route to use untraced interviews without permission.
  - An adapted agreement exists for family history: "Joint statement of intent for oral history recordings for family research".
- **OHS "For community groups"** and **National Lottery Heritage Fund oral-history good-practice guidance** (both read):
  - Oral history must be "substantial records of the past" and is not vox pops or reminiscence chat.
  - It should be recorded, documented and archived to professional standards, then used "as part of the way we explain a … historic building, community or place".
  - Testimonies can contradict each other and are "open to debate, analysis and interpretation".
- **A case nearby with numbers:** the Jewish History Association of South Wales recorded and transcribed **over 70 oral histories**. It put audio clips on People's Collection Wales, with the full recordings and transcripts at the National Library of Wales (PCW collection 1573766, read). PCW and OHS publish a joint oral-history guide.
- **Scale ceiling:** StoryCorps reports conversations from over 700,000 participants, which it calls the largest single collection of human voices (storycorps.org, snippet; the page blocked our fetch).
- **No audience-lift figure was found** for heritage interview clips against other posts. Treat it as not checked and measure our own.

## The method
1. **Scope:** one theme, for example "Living and working around Llandough and Leckwith, 1940s-1980s". Make a list of 5-10 people who would talk on the record: retired farm workers, neighbours, chapel members, local historians. Find them through the society talks route and replies to the reader-memories call, never through DMs to strangers.
2. **Before the interview:** send the OHS-style information sheet (purpose, where it will be kept, how extracts will be used, right to restrict).
3. **Record:** 45-90 min, quiet room, a decent recorder or phone with a lapel mic, WAV kept as master. Use open questions in time order, with photos as prompts.
4. **Recording Agreement signed in person by both sides.** Any restrictions are written with time codes and end dates, and the agreement is stored with the master.
5. **Archive first:** offer the master and transcript to Glamorgan Archives or NLW, and add clips to People's Collection Wales (it has an oral-history upload path). The archive deposit is the credibility.
6. **Publish second:** 60-120 s clips with burned-in subtitles (en, plus cy where spoken) as Shorts/Reels, and a page per interviewee with a summary and a timeline link. An episode or chapter in a later e-book version can quote with the agreement's permission.
7. **Measure:** UTM per clip (`utm_campaign=oral-history`). Compare clip reach, completions and /ebooks/ clicks against the channel's 8-week median. Continue past 3 interviews only if clips beat the median.

## Rules that apply here
- Their words stay theirs. Never edit a clip so that it changes meaning. Label each clip "recollection, recorded <date>" so memory is never presented as documentary record.
- No accusations about named people in published clips. Cut or withhold any passage that names a person in a contested way, or uses a banned word, and log the cut.
- Never name the project lead publicly. The interviewer credit is "Cedwyn".
- Living people: follow the familytree privacy page. Nobody under 18 without a parent's signature.
