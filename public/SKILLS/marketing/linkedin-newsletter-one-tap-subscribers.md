# LinkedIn newsletter: the one-tap subscribe list (2026)

**What it is.** Any LinkedIn member or Page can publish a newsletter (up to five per profile or Page). Readers subscribe with one tap because LinkedIn already holds their email; LinkedIn emails each edition for you. Treat it as a second, rented list beside Mailchimp, not a replacement.

## Evidence (read in full unless marked)
- **Andy Crestodina, Orbit Media** ("Lessons learned from 265k subscribers", page read 26 Oct 2026): their email list took 15 years to reach 16,000 subscribers; the LinkedIn newsletter reached 265,000 in five years. When you publish the first edition, LinkedIn invites your connections and followers to subscribe. A person's newsletter grows faster than a company Page's, and it cannot be moved later. The article title becomes the email subject line and the first sentence becomes the preheader. There is no A/B testing, no automation and no personalisation. You only get impressions, views and engagement, not opens or clicks. Posting two thirds of an article with a "read the rest" link did not drive the traffic they wanted. They now post the whole article with a link to the original at the bottom. They publish weekly, alternating a new piece with a freshened older one, and write that subscriber growth has slowed in later years.
- **Jay Schwedelson, Outcome Media** (quoted on the same page): after the first edition, LinkedIn keeps inviting every new connection and follower to subscribe, even if you never publish again.
- **LinkedIn Marketing Blog, Alexandra Rynne, 18 Nov 2025** (read): you can have up to 5 newsletters per profile or Page. LinkedIn's own "This Week in Marketing" passed 1.3M subscribers, which the post credits to one consistent topic and a fixed weekly day. The subscribe link comes from the newsletter page's Share button and is a link, not an embeddable button. Suggested uses: email signature, other socials, your own email list, a QR code on print, cross-promotion with overlapping newsletters, and the author writing the first comment to start the conversation. Article Ads showed 2.3x CTR vs single-image ads (paid; not for us).
- Any follower minimum to unlock newsletters: not checked (several 2025 guides disagree).

## How to do it
1. **Decide: profile or Page.** This can't be changed later. A profile grows faster; a Page keeps the author anonymous.
2. **Name = "Would you like ___?"** Make it specific and plain, e.g. "Records of a Vale farm, one document a fortnight". Add a one-line description of what readers get and how often.
3. **Make the first edition the strongest piece you have.** It goes to every connection and follower as an invite. Lead with a strong title, because it is the subject line. The first sentence is the preheader, so open with the hook, not "Welcome".
4. **Post the full piece,** with one link at the end to the original page on your site. A pasted copy is duplicate content. Publish on the site first and keep the site page canonical, as in `syndication-canonical-noindex-medium.md`.
5. **Fix the rhythm:** weekly or fortnightly, same day, and alternate new pieces with refreshed archive pieces.
6. **Be present on publishing day.** Write the first comment yourself with a question, and reply to every comment for the first few hours.
7. **Promote the subscribe link:** your email signature, the IG/FB bio-link page, one line in the Mailchimp welcome email, and a QR code on talk handouts. Tag the link `utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=newsletter` on the site-bound link, as in `utm-discipline-ga4-funnel.md`.
8. **Measure monthly:** subscribers, article views, and site sessions arriving from `utm_medium=newsletter`. Stop if three months bring fewer than 20 sessions.

## Pitfalls
- No open or click data, and no segmentation. Keep Mailchimp as the list you own and offer the e-book there.
- Mixing topics stalls growth. Run one topic per newsletter.
- A newsletter left dormant after edition one still collects subscribers. That is fine, but stale readers expect a fixed rhythm once you restart.
