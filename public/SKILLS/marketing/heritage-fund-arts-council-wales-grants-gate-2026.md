# GATE: National Lottery Heritage Fund + Arts Council of Wales grants as funding for marketing and outputs (2026)

Harvested 2026-10-30 (skills harvest). Topic: grant money as a growth route for a heritage archive, its e-books and its documentary. This is a gate file: it states what is actually open, who qualifies and what it would take. Applying would be in the project lead's name and is outward, so this file is **PROPOSAL only**.

## The headline correction
The often-cited **NLHF "£3,000-£10,000 National Lottery Grants for Heritage" is CLOSED**. The programme page (read 30 Oct 2026, revision log updated 25 Jul 2025) reads: "We are no longer accepting applications for grants between £3,000-£10,000", effective 7 Dec 2023. NLHF's funding index now starts at **£10,000**. Any plan, draft or proposal that names the £3k-£10k pot is out of date.

## National Lottery Heritage Grants £10,000-£250,000 (open; page updated 17 Sep 2026, read)
- **Who:** not-for-profit organisations, private owners of a heritage asset, and partnerships. Examples on the page: charities, CIOs, community and voluntary groups, CICs, community/parish councils.
- **Private owners:** must show that "the public benefit of your project outweighs any private gain". NLHF usually expects the owner of the heritage to apply, or else to sign the grant terms. (The closed £3k-£10k page added that applicants need a board of 2+ people who aren't related or living at the same address. Treat that as likely to carry over, but **not checked** for the £10k+ tier.)
- **Under Heritage 2033, every project is judged against four investment principles:**
  - saving heritage;
  - protecting the environment;
  - inclusion, access and participation;
  - organisational sustainability.
- **Money:** NLHF can fund the full cost, but if there is no cash or in-kind contribution you must explain why. Payments go 50% / 30% in advance and 20% in arrears.
- **Not funded:** the owner's statutory duties, buying buildings or heritage assets, new buildings.
- **Timing:** no deadline. Decision aimed at **8 weeks**. The project must not start before the decision. There's an optional Project Enquiry first, and NLHF has published guidance on using AI in applications; read it before drafting.
- **What can be funded** (from the closed small-grant page; the larger tier's list was not re-read): digital outputs, activities that involve people, interpretation. The small-grant page excluded "promoting the cause". So a pure marketing campaign is not fundable, but **public access to heritage** (a free digitised archive, community transcription, talks, an accessible bilingual interpretation site) is.

## Arts Council of Wales: Create & Engage (open; read 30 Oct 2026)
- **Small:** up to **£10,000**, no deadline, but allow **at least 10 working weeks** from submission to project start.
- **Large:** £10,001-£60,000, with deadlines. The next is **1pm Wed 16 Dec 2026**, and the project must start at least 12 working weeks after it.
- **Who:** organisations or individuals. **Individuals must be 18+ and live in Wales** (FAQ + eligibility page snippet). Work must take place mainly in Wales and sit in one of ACW's supported artforms. Whether film/documentary or creative non-fiction qualifies is **not checked**; it's listed on the artforms page.
- **Money:** ACW funds at most **90%** of eligible costs, and **10%+ must come from elsewhere** (in-kind support counts). The project must fit an ACW priority, such as **Welsh language** ("growth in the use and ownership of the Welsh language") or the Wellbeing of Future Generations Act.
- **Rules:** one live application to ACW at a time, and since Apr 2026 only one re-application for the same project. Creative Practice is the separate fund for developing your own practice rather than making work.

## Practitioner numbers
I found no first-hand applicant testimony with success rates this run. NLHF and ACW don't publish award rates on these pages. So budget the time as an 8-12 week decision with no assumed odds.

## How-to (when the project lead states GO)
1. **Pick the vehicle first.** Grants go to a named applicant with a bank account in that exact name. The options are the project lead as an individual (ACW only, if based in Wales), a not-for-profit partner as lead (a local history society, a CIC or a library service), or a new CIC. Don't draft before this choice is made.
2. **Fit test, one line per principle or priority:**
   - Heritage 2033: access, inclusion and environment.
   - ACW: Welsh language.
   - If the line for a principle or priority can't be written honestly, drop that fund.
3. **Scope the fundable part:**
   - public, free access outputs: transcriptions, bilingual interpretation, community talks, a free e-book edition for libraries and schools;
   - keep paid e-book sales outside the grant budget;
   - show private gain is outweighed.
4. **Show the 10% match:** volunteer time, venue, existing archive work, valued at standard rates.
5. **Optional:** submit an NLHF Project Enquiry and get the reply before drafting the full form.
6. **Calendar:** ACW Small any time with 10+ working weeks lead; ACW Large 16 Dec 2026; NLHF any time with an 8-week decision.

  - free Welsh/English documentary scenes with captions;
  - community transcription of farm and parish records;
  - two library talks in the Vale;
  - a free library edition of one e-book.
  This strand hits ACW's Welsh-language priority and NLHF's access principle. It is NOT grant-funded promotion of paid e-books.
- **Gate items only the project lead can settle:**
  - which applicant (individual in Wales / partner / CIC);
  - whether he wants public money involved at all;
  - which bank account the grant would be paid into.
  Nothing is drafted to a funder until he states GO. No application, enquiry or contact from any flywheel.
- **Editorial:** application text follows the public-text rules (no banned words, nothing about family holdings, no legal-case framing). A grant pitch about heritage access must not read as a vehicle for the case.
