# Electronic legal deposit and UK Web Archive nomination (British Library / National Library of Wales)

Date: 2026-10-26 (skills harvest). Free; it's a legal duty, not a paid service. It's an outward step, so it needs the owner's GO before any email is sent. HubSpot draft only.

## What it is (primary sources)
- **The law.** "If you publish anything in the UK and Ireland, you need to give one copy to the British Library". This was updated in 2013 to include electronic publications, and applies "regardless of content, format or language" (bl.uk).
- **Who must deposit.** Any work made publicly available, sold or free, "whether or not they have ISBNs" (legaldeposit.org FAQs). So the free editions count too, and **no ISBN spend is needed** (see uk-plr-ebook-library-loans.md on ISBN as an owner decision).
- **Format.** A publication that exists only electronically is deposited electronically. **One electronic copy covers all six legal deposit libraries**, including the **National Library of Wales**. If a print edition exists, print is the default (the BL gets one copy within a month; the other five get copies on request through the Agency for Legal Deposit Libraries).
- **Route now (26 Oct 2026).** The BL publisher portal and file transfer are still down after the October 2023 cyber-attack. The BL asks e-publishers to email **[address kept in the project workspace]**, which "can advise alternative deposit options and accept smaller files via email". Receipts and catalogue updates are delayed until systems are restored.
- **Websites.** Sites on .uk/.scot/.cymru are crawled automatically. A site on another TLD (ours is **.estate**) "we may not know about": nominate it to **[address kept in the project workspace]** (bl.uk and its web-archiving page). Archived sites are normally viewable only in Legal Deposit Library reading rooms. Open online access needs an extra licence from the site owner, which the UK Web Archive asks owners for.

## Why it's marketing, not just compliance
- **Authority and permanence.** A deposited e-book is preserved in the national collection and catalogued over time. A researcher at NLW Aberystwyth or the BL then finds our register as a library-held work, not just a website. Self-publishing guides call this "cheap compliance that gets your book into the national bibliography" (publishing.co.uk legal-deposit guide).
- **Fits the site's role.** Our site presents itself as the central chronology for the Tŷ Mawr story. A deposited, dated copy at the national libraries shows exactly what we published and when. That's useful when outside narratives shift dates.
- **No outreach needed and no cost.** One email per publisher, then one file per edition.

## How to do it
1. **List every publicly available edition:** title, language, edition/date, format, file size. Free and paid count the same. New editions with changed content count as new publications.
2. **Use the right files:** EPUB that passes `tools/epub_lint.py`, or the PDF exactly as sold. The deposited copy must match the published one.
3. **Draft one email to [address kept in the project workspace] (HubSpot draft; the owner sends):** publisher name, the list of titles, a request for the current deposit route, and an offer of the files by email for small ones (most of ours are under 10 MB). Ask them to confirm that one deposit covers NLW.
4. **Draft one nomination to [address kept in the project workspace]:** cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate and the main subdomains. Say they're UK-published and on a non-UK TLD. Optionally offer the open-access licence so the archive can show captures online.
5. **Log** each deposit (date, edition, file hash) in the project workspace Re-deposit when an edition changes, e.g. the corrected Enwau after the Bute-date fix.
6. **Afterwards:** watch the BL and NLW catalogues for the records. When a record appears, cite it on the edition page ("Deposited with the British Library, [date]"), but only once confirmed.

## Testimony and numbers
- BL: legal deposit has existed "in English law since 1662" and covered electronic works since 2013. The email route is the stated workaround while the portal is down (page read live 26 Oct 2026).
- legaldeposit.org: "You only need to deposit one electronic copy of each publication to meet the requirements of all the legal deposit libraries."
- UK Web Archive: open online viewing needs the owner's licence. Otherwise it's reading rooms only. Its search covers captures only up to December 2022 since restoration (bl.uk web-archiving page).

  - the free cy/en/es editions and the paid £9.99 volumes, as e-only publications;
  - the Enwau names register, the 36-page PDF, re-deposited when it changes;
