# Welsh-learner walking groups: Cymraeg yn y Gwyllt (Dinas Powys) and Welsh-language nature walks

Date: 2026-10-30 · Status: research only, nothing sent · Owner: bizdev / community

## What exists (read 30 Oct 2026)
- **Cymraeg yn y Gwyllt (Welsh in the Wild)**, run by **Siop Dwt**, a small Welsh-learner business (siopdwt.com: a £35/month learners' subscription box, 1:1 lessons, workplace packages). Organiser's own listing for **Sun 11 Oct 2026, 10:30**:
  - Meet at the **Dinas Powys Cenotaph** (opposite the Cross Keys).
  - A **5-mile circular on Vale Trail 6**, the Salmon Leaps walk: fields, forest, Wrinstone Brook, Cwm George Iron Age hill fort. It runs about 3 h and has a 3-mile figure-of-eight option.
  - Format: free, leisurely pace, dog-friendly. **Level stickers** ("to take away the pressure"), from Mynediad (entry) up. **Prompt sheets** "to avoid that 'tongue tied' feeling at the start". It ends at a coffee shop or pub.
  - Bookings go by email, and cancellation notices go out by email the day before.
  - Earlier walks were posted to a large Welsh-learners Facebook group, e.g. Brynna Woodlands. No attendance numbers are published.
- **Wildlife Trust of South & West Wales** (page read):
  - *Cerdded, Coffi a Chlonc*: free **monthly** themed Welsh-practice walks at Teifi Marshes, followed by cake and a cuppa.
  - Welsh-learner guided walks on Skomer: £30 plus a £22 boat fee, held Mon 21 Jul 2025.
  - The page header shows its Welsh-language group at **Forest Farm, Cardiff**.
- **Ceredigion "Walk and Talk Cymraeg"** groups run regular walks for learners. Snippet only, not read.
- **Why this format works** (from the organiser's own design): the barrier for learners is the first five minutes of talk. Stickers and prompt sheets solve it, and the walk gives everyone something to talk about. **A short, level-graded, place-based vocabulary sheet is exactly what organisers already hand out.** That makes it the natural thing for a heritage project to offer.
- **What isn't known:** group size, how often the Dinas Powys walk repeats, and whether organisers take outside prompt sheets. Nothing found on any of these.

## Playbook
1. **Give the organiser something they already use.** Make a one-page bilingual **prompt sheet** for a specific route:
   - 10–12 place names with their meanings (e.g. *llan* = church enclosure, *rhyd* = ford);
   - 6 easy question frames at Mynediad and Sylfaen levels ("Beth ydy ystyr…?", "Wyt ti wedi gweld…?");
   - 3 dated "did you know" facts for stops on the route;
   - one QR code to a free, ad-free page with audio pronunciation.
2. **Match their route and level.** Vale Trail 6 starts at the same cenotaph that has a HistoryPoints QR page (see `historypoints-qr-heritage-network-contributor-credit.md`), so a sheet can point to it. Offer a variant for a future route near Llandough or Leckwith, but leave the choice to the organiser.
3. **Offer, don't ask.** The pitch is a free resource they can use or ignore, with credit at their discretion. No selling at the walk and no sign-up wall on the QR page.
4. **Join as a participant before pitching** (our judgment, no source): turning up first makes the offer a neighbour's, not a vendor's. Only someone who can attend in person can do this.
5. **Measure:** visits to the QR page (`?from=walk-sheet` plus the referrer), and newsletter sign-ups from that page. Expect small numbers (one group, maybe 10–20 people). The value is the relationship and repeat use, not reach.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Build (no spend, internal): "Taith Geirfa: Llandough & Leckwith"**, an A5 bilingual prompt sheet.
  - Place names Llandough / Llandochau, Leckwith / Lecwydd, Cogan, the River Ely / Elái, with meanings from the Welsh Place-Name Society, marked "not checked" where unsure.
  - Farm and season vocabulary, so the sheet works on the Dinas Powys route too.
  - QR to a free Cedwyn Welsh-learner page (the /learn/ academy or a /wales/ page) with audio.
- **Text rules:** neutral public text. Never name the project lead. Nothing about family holdings or addresses, and keep the case out. Don't call the family "tenant farmers". None of the banned public words (states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken).
