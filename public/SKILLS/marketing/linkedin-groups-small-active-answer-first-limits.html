LinkedIn Groups for a heritage archive: join 3-5 small active groups, answer, don't broadcast (2026)

Date: 31 Oct 2026 (skills harvest ~03:58Z). Type: community / LinkedIn growth. Status for Cedwyn: PROPOSAL (posting needs the saved LinkedIn login and the project lead's GO on any outward post). Before writing: 'linkedin group' matched 0 skill files. Profile requests, document carousels, the company page, newsletters, events and LinkedIn Live are already covered elsewhere (see the linkedin-*.md files). This file covers Groups only.

Hard limits (LinkedIn Help a544801, "General limits for LinkedIn groups", page read 31 Oct 2026)

  • A member can belong to 100 groups, with 20 join requests pending at once.
  • Posting caps: 15 posts per group per week, posts in 20 groups per week, and 60 group posts a week in total.
  • A group post can be up to 3,000 characters and a comment up to 1,250. You can mention up to 20 members in one conversation.
  • Admins can send 50 new message requests a day to unconnected members of their own group.
  • Member-to-member DMs: 15 free 1:1 group messages a month across all groups, and unsent ones don't carry over (ERE.net / LinkedIn notice, read; the date of that rule wasn't checked against a current Help page).
  • Where moderation is on, a post waits for admin approval and you get a notification when it's approved (Help a551360, snippet). Some groups auto-approve members by criteria (Help a1396211, read: "available only for selected groups").

What practitioners report (2026)

  • Matteo Giardino, linkedinpreview.com, 17 Jun 2026 (read): Groups were "dead" around 2018-20 because of spam. Since then admins have better moderation tools and the creators who stayed face less competition. Group posts appear in the group feed, and only high-engagement ones surface in members' home feeds. His rules: read for a week first; join 3-5 groups, not 20; comment or post 2-3 times a week in each; no link in your first post; connect afterwards with the people who engaged, naming the thread.
  • A dead-group test from Giardino and evaboot.com (2026, read): a group of 10,000-50,000 members with no post in the last 7 days (Giardino) or none this month (evaboot) is dead, whatever its size. Evaboot: niche groups under 100K members with daily posts beat big inactive ones.
  • A practitioner on Reddit (search snippet only, page not read, not checked): after joining groups of 80-100K members, "the smaller groups (2k-5k members) were way more valuable than the giant ones".
  • No source gives a measured reach or sales figure for group posts. Treat Groups as a relationship channel and measure it yourself (see below).

How to do it

  1. Find. Search the LinkedIn search bar, then the Groups filter: "local history", "Welsh history", "genealogy", "family history UK", "heritage interpretation", "archives and records", "Patagonia" / "Welsh in Argentina", "Cardiff" / "Vale of Glamorgan".
  2. Screen each group. Is there a post in the last 7 days? Do posts get comments, not just likes? Are the rules stated (no promotion / promo-day only)? Do members match the audience (archivists, local historians, heritage professionals, family historians)? Log it: name, members, last post date, rules, link policy.
  3. Join at most 5. Read for 1 week. Answer real questions with a sourced fact and a dated record ID in the text, and no link. A link goes in only where the rules allow it, and then once and late.
  4. Post sparingly. At most 1 original post a week per group, well under the 15/week cap: a record question ("Has anyone seen a 1950s Western Ground Rents lease for the Vale?") or a short find with its source. Native images, no links in the body.
  5. Follow up. Send a connection request with a one-line note naming the thread to anyone who engaged twice. Use the 15 monthly group DMs only for replies, never for cold pitches.
  6. Measure. Count group-sourced profile visits, connections and any replies per month. Links (only where allowed) carry utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=group-<slug> (lowercase, per utm-discipline-ga4-funnel.md). Stop rule: after 6 weeks, drop any group with fewer than 2 meaningful replies to us.

Own group? Not yet

Running a group means daily moderation and seeding. Giardino calls the trade-off significant. Only consider one if 3+ months of participation show demand, and the newsletter / Page are already covered by other skills.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Account: the project lead's own profile, through the saved LinkedIn login ("Cedwyn posting"). Groups are joined by members, not company pages. Joining and posting are outward actions: proposal on the project lead's GO. Until then, prepare only: the screened group list and 10 sourced answer snippets.
  • Targets to screen first: UK genealogy/family-history groups (Welsh ancestry questions, land records), heritage/archives professional groups (for the evidence-catalogue and missing-records method), Welsh-diaspora/Patagonia groups (en/es).
  • Editorial rules apply to every comment: never name the project lead as the subject; none of the banned words (states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken); neutral dated wording ("the 1987 judgment records…"); nothing about family holdings or addresses; no accusations about named people. Llandough Farm is not Great House Farm.
  • Sales path: the profile's Featured section points to /ebooks/. Group activity drives profile visits; no "buy" posts in groups.
  • Log each group in the project workspace under channel linkedin-group.
