# Skill: Wikipedia and AI answers, the gate for a court-case heritage project (COILEGAL + the 2026 LLM rules)
Written 30 Oct 2026 (skills harvest). Complements wikimedia-commons-wikidata-heritage-coi.md (Commons/Wikidata uploads); this file covers Wikipedia articles, AI-citation value and the hard limits.

## Why it matters (evidence)
- Wikipedia is the biggest single source AI chat answers cite. Profound's study of ChatGPT citations (Aug 2024 - Jun 2025) has Wikipedia first at 7.8% of all citations, ahead of Reddit at 1.8%. The 5W "Citation Source Audit Q1 2026" (a synthesis of nine datasets) reports Similarweb Jan-Feb 2026 data: Wikipedia 13.15% and Reddit 11.97% of US ChatGPT citations (~600,000 citation events). Numbers vary widely by study and month (a 30-million-citation study summarised by Search Engine Roundtable gives Wikipedia 47.9%, apparently as a share of top-10 cited sources; not checked), so treat "Wikipedia is the top single domain" as the stable finding, not any one percentage.
- So a sourced line in a Wikipedia place article is one of the few routes by which an AI answer about Llandough history could point at our evidence.

## The hard limits (Wikipedia guidelines, read 30 Oct 2026)
1. Court cases (WP:COILEGAL): "editors should not write about court cases in which they or those close to them have been involved, nor about parties or law firms associated with the cases." For us this covers BP Properties Ltd v Buckler (1987) and its parties. No edits and no drafts on it from anyone connected, Tab included.
2. Family (WP:COISELF): "refrain from creating articles about yourself, or anyone you know, living or dead, unless through the Articles for Creation process". For an existing article, do not edit it directly, and disclose the connection on the talk page.
3. COI editors in general: "propose changes on talk pages (by using the {{edit COI}} template)"; put new articles through Articles for Creation (AfC); never review your own; keep talk posts concise. Disclose on the user page; one real person per account.
4. LLM text: Wikipedia's 2026 guideline on writing articles with large language models (RfC March 2026) and the March-May 2026 RfC make "persistent use of large language models" outside its stated exceptions a blockable disruption. Speedy deletion G15 removes "unambiguously LLM-generated pages" (signs: text addressed to the user, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, placeholders, made-up references). So Tab must not write Wikipedia prose, edit requests or drafts. A person writes in their own words.
5. Self-citation (WP:SELFCITE): allowed "within reason", third person, not excessive; you are "permanently identified in the page history" as the person who added it.
6. AfC timing: no fixed wait. Help desk replies quote "8 weeks or more" in backlog periods; drafts untouched for 6 months are deleted (G13).

## What is allowed and useful (steps)
1. Read-only first: list the Wikipedia articles that already cover our ground (Llandough, Leckwith, the parish church, Vale of Glamorgan history, Welsh settlement in Chubut) and note where a fact is missing or uncited.
2. For each gap, check if an independent reliable source (a published book, an archive catalogue, an official listing, a newspaper) supports it. Our own site is a primary/self-published source: at most a further-reading or external link, and only via talk-page request.
3. A person with a disclosed connection (not Tab) can post a short {{edit COI}} request on the article's talk page: the exact sentence, the independent citation, and a disclosure line. One request per article; accept a no.
4. Prefer the "cultural sector" route (WP:CURATOR): offering a free scan to Commons (see the Commons skill) that an uninvolved editor can add. This is lower risk than text.
5. Watch outcomes: talk-page replies, whether the line or image stays, and whether AI answers start citing the article for our questions (the weekly visibility check's 5 fixed Grok questions).

## Pitfalls
- Paid "Wikipedia writers": the COI page warns of scams; most are banned for undisclosed paid editing. Zero spend and never hire one.
- Promotional wording, buy links and e-book mentions get reverted and draw scrutiny to every related edit.
- A blocked or flagged account makes later legitimate requests harder. A slow pace is the safe pace.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Tab never edits Wikipedia, never drafts Wikipedia text, and never touches anything about the 1987 case, its parties, or the family. This is a gate skill: the safe value is indirect.
- Tab's part: a private list (the project workspace) of place-article gaps with the independent source for each, and pointers to which Cedwyn evidence page a reader could use. the project lead decides whether anyone posts a request, and writes it himself.
- Our own AI visibility comes instead from Cedwyn pages that are citable on their own: dated primary documents, llms.txt, and clear neutral summaries (see ai-answer-citations-geo-sources-quotes-stats.md).
