# Volunteer transcription project: build a community around the records (Zooniverse / FromThePage)

Skill type: community building + list growth. Read 26 Oct 2026 by the skills harvest.

## Why it works (primary sources, numbers)
- Zooniverse transcription guide (help.zooniverse.org, read): when an **official** project launches, Zooniverse publicises it to "an email subscription list of several million people"; transcription projects draw people interested in "history, genealogy, literature, the culture of the place". It finds **2-5 people per page** is enough for a generally accurate transcription; answers are then aggregated into a consensus text.
- Zooniverse launch policies (help.zooniverse.org, read): Project Builder is free to use. Three states: **Public** (anyone with the link; not listed on zooniverse.org; permanent "not reviewed" banner; no review needed), **Private** (named collaborators, practical only well under 100 people), **Official** (passes Zooniverse team review - reply typically within two weeks - then a one-week volunteer beta test, usually scheduled within one to two weeks; listed on the projects page and promoted to the community). Limit 10,000 subject uploads per user. Not suitable for sensitive or restricted material: all media is hosted publicly behind hashed URLs.
- FromThePage (fromthepage.com, read): Fort Worth Public Library's local-history unit had **8,000+ pages transcribed in a little over a year**, and "most of our volunteers were unfamiliar with the library's digital collections before" - the project recruited new audience, not only transcripts. FromThePage's blog (content.fromthepage.com, read; written early 2020) counted 455,846 pages on its site then, and Transcribe Bentham reached 22,740 pages by Dec 2019; it notes monthly local "transcribathons" (Library of Virginia).
- FromThePage pricing (fromthepage.com, read 26 Oct 2026): transcribers always free; hosts pay - Researcher $100/month (1 project, 10,000 pages), Small Organization $360/month; 200-page free trial. Exports TEI, CSV, HTML, IIIF. **Paid, so not for us now** (zero-spend rule); Zooniverse is the free route.

## How to run one (steps)
1. Pick a bounded, rights-clear batch: 50-300 page images that are public-domain or ours to publish (e.g. printed survey pages, newspaper columns, OS map name books). Nothing private, no living people, nothing restricted.
2. Write the task as 1-3 simple questions per image (transcribe line; mark place names; tick "mentions Ty Mawr / Great House"). Keep a one-page field guide with 3 worked examples.
3. Build on Zooniverse Project Builder as a **Public** project first (no review), test with 5-10 friendly volunteers, fix the guide, then decide whether to apply for **Official** status (review + beta) to reach the newsletter audience.
4. Set retirement to 3 transcriptions per page (inside Zooniverse's 2-5 guidance). Export, build a consensus text, and spot-check 10% by hand.
5. Give the work back: each finished page becomes a cited record page on our own site, crediting "transcribed by volunteers" (usernames only if volunteers opt in). Post a short monthly progress note ("412 of 600 pages done").
6. Recruit where the interested people already are: the family-history societies, local history societies, the Wales/Patagonia Welsh communities and our own email list (each has its own skill file). A monthly 1-hour online "transcribathon" gives a date to share.
7. Measure: volunteers joined, pages done per week, share of volunteers who also join the email list (UTM on every link back to us).

## Pitfalls
- No project page traffic unless you promote it yourself while Public (Zooniverse does not list unofficial projects).
- Official status takes roughly 3-5 weeks (two-week review reply + one-to-two-week wait + one-week beta, per the policies page) plus our fixes; acceptance is Zooniverse's decision, and whether a heritage project without a research institution is accepted is not checked.
- Volunteers are "not working on your timetable" (Zooniverse guide): plan for months, not days.
- Never upload anything private, sensitive or about living family members; the platform hosts all media publicly.
