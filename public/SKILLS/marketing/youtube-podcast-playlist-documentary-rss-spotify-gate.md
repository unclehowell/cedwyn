# Video podcast from the documentary and archive readings: YouTube podcast playlist first, RSS/Spotify only if it earns it
Date: 2026-10-28 (skills harvest 02:4xZ). Type: YouTube growth / zero spend. Status for Cedwyn: step 1 is a settings change on our own channel (routine posting work, check RULES.md first); steps 2-3 are PROPOSALS.

## What the platforms say (read 28 Oct 2026)
- **YouTube Help 12751636, "Create a podcast in YouTube Studio":** on YouTube a podcast is a playlist and episodes are videos. You can set an existing playlist as a podcast (Studio -> Create -> New podcast -> "Set an existing playlist as a podcast"; square thumbnail needed). Podcast content may get: inclusion in **YouTube Music** (background play and downloads without Premium), podcast badges, a spotlight on youtube.com, recommendations and better search. It should hold full-length episodes in listening order. Content the creator doesn't own can be ineligible; Shorts don't appear in YouTube Music; MP3s can't be podcasts (episodes must be videos).
- **YouTube Help 13525207 (RSS delivery):** a hosted RSS feed can push episodes into YouTube; YouTube won't redistribute the feed elsewhere, won't update re-uploaded audio, and podcast uploads must declare any branded content.
- **YouTube blog:** more than 1 billion monthly active viewers of podcast content on YouTube. Edison Infinite Dial 2025 (US): YouTube is the service most used for podcasts by 33% of weekly listeners, Spotify 26%, Apple 14%.
- **Spotify for Creators, Partner Program:** free hosting of audio and video podcasts with distribution; payouts need 3+ episodes, 2,000 consumption hours and 1,000 audience in the last 30 days (UK is an eligible market). Not a near-term revenue line for us.

## What practitioners found (The Podcast Host, "These 5 podcasters added video", 2025)
- **Darren Lake (running niche):** 40 audio episodes went nowhere; he banked 25 videos and released them together in 2023; within 18 months he had 3.5x the audience built in 6 years of audio. He re-cut episodes to 10-15 min explainers aimed at search topics. Video was "three times harder". Total spend ~$500.
- **Matty Lansdown (health):** 19,000-24,000 monthly audio downloads; after 200 video episodes over 2 years, 800 YouTube subscribers and 5,000-7,000 views a month -- "pretty uninspiring". Editing was his biggest cost.
- **Joe Casabona (430 audio episodes):** no "appreciable bump" six months after adding video.
- **John Kundtz:** paid $300-400 per episode for editing, stopped after 8 video episodes with little traction.
- Lesson: video wins when the format is built for YouTube search (short, scripted, visual subject). Filmed talk does not win by itself.

## How to do it
1. **Free first (one setting):** make a playlist of our full-length, owned, long-form videos in order (documentary scenes, archive walkthroughs), then set it as a podcast with a square thumbnail. Exclude Shorts and anything with third-party footage or music we don't own.
2. Write episode titles for search, not series numbers ("The 1840s tithe map of ...", not "Door 7"). Add chapters and captions (see youtube-chapters-captions-transcripts-archive.md).
3. Watch 28 days in YouTube Studio: podcast traffic source / YouTube Music plays vs the channel baseline.
5. Stop rule: no measurable podcast-surface views after 2 months means keep it as a plain playlist and spend no more time.
