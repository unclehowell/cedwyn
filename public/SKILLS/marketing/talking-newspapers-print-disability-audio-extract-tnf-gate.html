Talking newspapers (print-disability audio) as a free listening route for a heritage extract — GATE

Date: 2026-10-31 · harvest run · status: how-to note for a later GO; nothing sent, nobody contacted.

What it is (pages read today)

  • Talking newspapers are local volunteer charities that record local news and features as audio for blind, partially sighted and print-disabled listeners. Delivery is a memory stick posted free under Royal Mail's "Articles for the Blind" concession, or streaming/app. No cost to the listener (TNF Listener FAQ).
  • Wales started it: the first UK talking newspaper was Aberystwyth/Ceredigion, 1970 (TNF About; BBC Wales 3 Oct 2023).
  • Scale, as TNF states it: TNF has supported "over 300 Talking Newspapers"; "over 9,000 volunteers produce the Talking Newspapers and magazines for 25,000 Listeners"; "over 1,054 newspapers and magazines" recorded a year (tnf.org.uk home + About). The BBC 2012 piece gives an older, larger figure (400 groups, 100,000 listeners). Use the TNF figure; it is the current one.
  • Per-paper size is small. Ceredigion Talking Newspaper served "almost 150" listeners with bilingual highlights from the Cambrian News, Y Cymro and Golwg before Covid, and relaunched in 2023 (BBC Wales). Expect a local Glamorgan paper to reach tens to low hundreds of listeners.
  • Welsh TNs named by the Wales Council of the Blind (2023 page): Aberconwy, Dolgellau, Colwyn Link, Powys, Swansea & Gower, Tenby. A Rhondda Talking Newspaper Association is on the Charity Commission register (search result, not opened). A Cardiff, Penarth or Vale of Glamorgan TN was NOT confirmed. TNF's "Find a Talking Newspaper" map is script-rendered and showed nothing to a plain fetch; TNF's enquiry line (01793 49755, as printed by WCB) is the route, and calling it counts as contact (GO only).
  • TNF Soundings (tnfsoundings.org.uk) is TNF's free national audio repository. Its articles are "free to download and use for Talking News, Talking Magazines, Hospital and Community Radio Stations", with "Time Out" leisure pieces (poems, biographies, wildlife) and some items specific to Wales. Uploading is through a volunteers' upload tool, so outside material goes in only through TNF's volunteers.
  • National print-disability libraries are a different door: Calibre Audio (over 23,000 books, volunteer-narrated, members only) and Listening Books (members may suggest titles). Neither page shows an author-submission route, so a self-published e-book gets in only if members ask for it. Not a channel we can push.

Why it could matter (and why it is small)

  • Older, rooted, local listeners are the core local-history readership, and many lose easy print reading. A 3-5 minute spoken feature about a Llandough/Leckwith place reaches people no social post reaches.
  • It earns no link and no measurable sales. Treat it as goodwill and accessibility, plus a line we can state truthfully ("an audio extract is available free for print-disabled listeners"). The reach number is unknown until a TN answers. Kill test below.

How to do it (when GO is given)

  1. Make the asset first, at zero spend. Use a 3-5 minute plain-read extract (record-led: a tithe or deed passage, dated, no case argument). Make it from existing audio or a clean read, MP3 at 128 kbps mono, with a spoken credit line: "From Cedwyn, the story of Great House Farm, Llandough. The full text is at cedwyn dot bucklerfamily dot estate."
  2. Put it on our own page (/listen/ already proposed in audio-teaser-self-hosted-feed-podcast-index-gate.md) with a full transcript. The transcript is the accessibility win for screen-reader users too.
  3. Grant permission in writing in the file's notes: free use by talking newspapers, talking magazines, hospital and community radio, unedited apart from trimming. This removes the editor's copyright worry.
  4. Find the nearest TN: TNF enquiry line or the TNF map in a real browser, then the Penarth Times circulation area. HubSpot draft only: two lines and a link to the MP3. Send on the project lead's GO.
  5. Second door: ask TNF Soundings (via its Contact page) whether it takes a Wales-specific "Time Out" heritage piece from an outside producer. Also a HubSpot draft, GO only.

Rules for the content

  • Bilingual is a plus in Wales (Ceredigion ran cy/en). A Welsh read only if it comes from a native or fluent voice; no machine Welsh.
  • No call to action to buy inside the audio. A credit line and URL only. TNs are charities, not ad space.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Now (no GO needed): note in the /listen/ plan that each audio teaser carries a transcript and a free-use line for print-disability services.
  • On GO: one HubSpot draft to the nearest TN and one question to TNF Soundings. Measure MP3 downloads with a ?src=tn tag on the link we give them.
  • Kill test: if no TN uses it within 3 months, or downloads with ?src=tn stay under 10, stop. Keep the transcript page either way.
