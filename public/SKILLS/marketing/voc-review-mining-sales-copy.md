# Voice-of-customer (VOC) review mining: write sales copy in readers' own words

Date: 2026-10-26. Topic: CRO copywriting - mine reviews, replies and comments for exact phrases, then put them in headlines, buttons, blurbs and emails. Not covered by ebook-sales-page-cro-checkout-friction.md (friction) or reviews-social-proof-direct-ebooks.md (displaying reviews); this is about FINDING the words.

## Evidence (practitioner case results)
- Joanna Wiebe / Copyhackers rehab-clinic test (reported in CXL's VOC guide, read): control headline "Your addiction ends here" vs a headline lifted from a review, "If you think you need rehab, you do." Result: >400% more clicks on the main CTA and >20% more form submissions on the next page. (Original Copyhackers post 403'd to us; CXL's account read.)
- Dressipi (Copyhackers, reported by Talia Wolf / GetUplift, read): +123.9% conversions from headline + button copy written together.
- Talia Wolf (GetUplift, read): "a jump of over 60%" in a test for Learn Visual Studio from copy reworked with message mining.
- CXL guide (read): qualitative VOC needs only a small sample - accessible to companies of any size.
- Caveat: these are agency-reported single tests with large traffic; lifts on a tiny site will be noisy (see ab-testing-small-lists.md).

## The method (Wiebe/CXL, condensed)
1. Collect: 30-100 raw quotes. Sources in order of value: your own buyers' replies/surveys; reviews of the 3-5 closest comparable books (Amazon, Goodreads, StoryGraph - 3-star reviews are the richest: they name what was missing); forum and group threads where the audience asks questions; comments on your own posts.
2. Paste each verbatim into a sheet: quote | source URL | date | bucket.
3. Bucket: (a) pain/stuck ("I could never find out what happened to..."), (b) desired outcome ("finally understood my grandfather's village"), (c) hesitation/objection ("too academic", "just a rehash of Wikipedia"), (d) delight words they repeat.
4. Count repeats. A phrase that shows up 3+ times from different people is a headline candidate; objections become FAQ lines and guarantees.
5. Write: headline from (a) or (b) almost verbatim; subhead answers (c); button continues the headline's sentence (Wiebe's headline+button pairing); blurb uses (d) words.
6. Test one change at a time where traffic allows; otherwise ship the VOC version and compare 4-week conversion vs prior 4 weeks (directional).
7. Repeat quarterly; keep the sheet as the copy bank for emails and posts.

## Rules
- Quote only public text; never copy a reviewer's name or identifiable detail into copy. Paraphrase-adjacent wording is fine; lift phrases, not whole reviews.
- Do not use competitor reviews as testimonials - only as language research.
- Mark any number in copy with its source.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Today Cedwyn has 0 buyers, so step 1 uses comparable-book reviews: Welsh local-history titles (Y Lolfa, Gwasg Carreg Gwalch lists), Vale of Glamorgan / Penarth / Cardiff history books, Welsh Patagonia histories, and "lost country houses of Wales" titles on Amazon UK and Goodreads; plus r/Wales and r/Cardiff threads asking about local history or ancestry; plus any reader email replies already in the inbox.
- Target 50 quotes in the sheet (the project workspace, small text file) before rewriting anything.
- Output = PROPOSAL only (public pages): a rewritten /ebooks/ headline + button pair, 3 FAQ lines answering the top objections (likely "is this just opinion?" -> every page cites its record; "too local?" -> what it tells about Welsh land history), and blurb first lines for the 8 titles. - Editorial guard: the copy bank must pass the banned-words list (see the banned-words list in bizdev BRIEF.md) and the citations-only rule before any line goes public.
- KPI: /ebooks/ to checkout click-through and checkout starts, 4 weeks before vs after.
