The gate that decides everything: human authorship

  • Folding Rock (read): "only publishes work that is solely human-authored". Submitters confirm they used no AI, and the magazine may ask for proof. It reads anonymously, takes one piece at a time, and previously published writers wait a year. NWR and the IWA print named authors (the IWA asks for a one-line author bio).
  • So Tab cannot write these pieces. An agent-drafted essay sent under a person's name would breach the outlet's terms. The outlets fit only if a real person writes the piece: a family member, or a willing historian. Tab's part is research support, fact-checking and an evidence pack.
  • Every one of these titles prints a byline. The op-ed file already records the named-author decision, and it is the owner's. The public "never name the project lead" rule stays in force, so a byline needs a different willing author or the owner's explicit GO.
  • Previously unpublished means a piece cannot be lifted from cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate pages or the Substack. It has to be new writing, which the site can link to afterwards.

How to pitch (practitioner pattern, kept short)

  1. One outlet at a time; wait for a no or six weeks before trying the next.
  2. NWR review essay: take two or three books on the Vale, Llandough or Leckwith, or Welsh Patagonia (for example Glamorgan county history volumes, or a recent Patagonia memoir) and write a critical essay. The house's own reading of the record comes in only as context, never as advocacy.
  3. Folding Rock "Lost and Found": a creative non-fiction piece about a demolished house and how its records were lost and found. That theme fits the subject matter almost exactly. Plain, literary, no accusations about named people.
  4. welsh agenda: a constructive policy piece, for example on public access to local planning and land records in Wales, or on Welsh-language place-names in the Vale. Declare the involvement in the case at the top. Never write it as advocacy for the case: the IWA publishes public-interest debate, and the author answers for every fact.

Measured effect: none found

No circulation figures were found for NWR, Folding Rock or the welsh agenda in this run (not checked). Pieces in literary quarterlies sell few books directly. Their value is a credible byline, a bio line with a link, and a citation other writers reuse. Treat a placement as reputation, not sales. Never claim a sales effect.
