# Trevelyan 1909 and T. H. Thomas 1903: public-domain Glamorgan folklore as local content

Written 30 Oct 2026 for Cedwyn. Before this, no skill file named Trevelyan. Aim: real, citable local stories for folklore days, posts and the wiki, at zero cost and with no invention.

## What the source is (read 30 Oct 2026)
- **Marie Trevelyan, *Folk-lore and Folk-stories of Wales* (London: Elliot Stock, 1909).** Trevelyan (b. 1853 as Emma Thomas) came from **Llantwit Major, Vale of Glamorgan** (Wikipedia), so the book is unusually rich in Vale and Cardiff material. It is public domain: the author died in 1922, and the 1909 UK edition is out of copyright.
- **Full text and scans:** Internet Archive `afl2317.0001.001.umich.edu` (University of Michigan scan; djvu text 899 KB, PDF). Wikisource's Folk literature portal lists the same scan, which is the cite-back route (see wikisource-scan-backed-public-domain-records-cite-back.md).
- **Used by practitioners:** academic citation, e.g. A. R. Phillips on Calan Mai, citing "Trevelyan ... 1909, p. 22" with the archive.org stream URL (snippet). "Bridgend Vale Legends" on Facebook posts her Marcross story; a Substack (Bardic Vintage Books) profiles her. The same stories already carry an audience on Facebook.

## How to do it (any local-heritage project)
1. Download the djvu text once; it's small. Search it for each parish and river name **and its old spellings**. Read the context, because place names repeat across Wales (the Llandough trap above).
2. Record for each item the **printed page**, the **original source footnote**, and the archive.org page link (`/details/<id>/page/n<leaf>`). Quote dialogue verbatim and briefly.
3. Follow the footnote upstream. For Leckwith that is T. H. Thomas 1903 in the Cardiff Naturalists' Society *Transactions*; check Biodiversity Heritage Library / Internet Archive for a scan. Cite the earliest source.
4. Frame each item as folklore, recorded on a date by a named collector: "a 1903 collection records ...". Never present it as fact about a property.
5. Reuse each item in three places: a #FolkloreThursday post, a short wiki "local folklore" note with the citation, and a 30-second Short with a period image (see version-hashtags and youtube-shorts skills for formats).

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Status:** content only. It needs no account, has no cost, and is all public domain. Text and images go through the normal editorial lanes; this skill posts nothing.
- **First uses:** queue the Leckwith boatman, Penarth Head and Llanblethian items for #FolkloreThursday on the existing Facebook page and Instagram (see folklorethursday-bluesky-monthly-hosted-days.md). Hand the Leckwith item to the **online gap finder** as a candidate for a "Folklore of the neighbouring parishes" note on /wales/ or /wiki/. Mark it as Leckwith, not the farm.
- **Wording rules:** neutral third person; banned public words ("states", "assertions", "per", etc.), so write "records" or "recounts". No insignia. Never imply that folklore bears on the 1987 case. Don't add an outbound link where the site rule is internal links only: cite the book and page in text and keep the archive.org link in the source note.
- **Next research step** (for a later run, read-only): find the T. H. Thomas 1903 *Transactions* scan, and check whether it has more Leckwith, Llandough or Cogan tales than Trevelyan reprinted.
- **Stop rule:** if 4 folklore posts get fewer than 3 clicks in total and no reshares, keep the items for the wiki only.
