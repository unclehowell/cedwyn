LinkedIn document (PDF carousel) posts: the top LinkedIn format in 2026, video is down

Harvested 30 Oct 2026, ~20:47Z. Before this, "linkedin carousel" had 0 hits and "document post" appeared only in a harvest note. Other files cover LinkedIn requests, the newsletter, a Page proposal and Page Events. This file is the format choice and how to make it.

Evidence

  • AuthoredUp, "Best Performing Content on LinkedIn in 2026", Ivana Todorovic, updated 2 Sep 2026 (read). 3M+ posts from personal profiles, Mar 2025-Feb 2026. Multipliers are against each profile's own median:
Format Reach Engagement Share of posts
Document (carousel) 1.39x 1.30x 4.88%
Image 1.20x 1.33x
Poll 1.78x 0.37x AuthoredUp calls this a "reach trap"
Text 1.07x 0.78x
Video 0.86x 0.93x
Article 0.69x 0.44x
Reshare 0.29x 0.22x
  • Top 5% profiles see documents at 1.72x reach and 1.63x engagement.
  • Video reach fell 36% year on year.
  • Documents are 12.92% of saved posts, about 2.6x their share of posts.
  • AuthoredUp puts it down to dwell time: someone swiping for 15-20 s beats a text post scanned in 3 s.
  • What works: 6-8 slides, one idea per slide, 1080x1350 vertical, a knowledge-gap hook on slide 1, and saveable checklists or step-by-steps.
  • Socialinsider LinkedIn benchmarks (read 30 Oct 2026). 1.3M business Page posts:
  • Q3 2026 engagement: native documents 6.40% and multi-image 6.40% (down from 6.60% in Q2), video 5.25%, image 4.70%. Text is lower still.
  • Video views down 36% YoY across Pages.
  • Polls win impressions only for Pages over 50k followers.
  • LinkedIn Help a518909 / a564109 (snippets). Accepts PDF, PPT(X) and DOC(X), up to 100 MB and 300 pages. Ads spec: a mixed-size PDF must be fitted to one page size (Document Ads spec, snippet).
  • Both studies are vendor analyses of posts, not controlled tests. The format ranking agrees across a personal-profile set and a Page set, which is why we trust it.

How to make one (no install: PIL + an existing PDF writer on the box)

  1. Pick one record or one sequence. Examples: "7 documents in the 1974-1988 sequence, in order", "How a farm becomes a registered title: 6 steps, with dates" (neutral wording), or "What the 1840s tithe map shows".
  2. 6-8 slides at 1080x1350, dark theme matching the site. - Slide 1 is the hook as a question or a gap, e.g. "One farm, six owners on paper — in 40 years". - Slides 2-7 each carry one fact with a source line and catalogue ref. - The last slide carries the call: "Full timeline + sources: cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate/research/…" with the e-book shelf URL beneath.
  3. Export as one PDF with every page the same size. Name it with a clear title, because LinkedIn shows the document title on the card.
  4. Post copy: 2-3 short lines plus one question to prompt comments. - Put the link in the first comment, not the post. Our existing LinkedIn skills already cover link handling; follow those. - At most 3 hashtags.
  5. Measure at 48 h and 7 d: impressions, reactions, comments, saves and reposts. Compare against our own median once we have five posts.
  6. Avoid: polls (empty reach), reshares (0.29x), native articles for reach, and multiple videos in a week.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Account: the saved LinkedIn login is "LinkedIn — Cedwyn posting". BIZDEV #7 16, the LinkedIn login check, is still open because a sign-in could text the project lead a code. So the first document post waits until that check passes on a run where a code can be relayed. Nothing goes out unattended before then.
  • Content pipeline: the weekly QUOTE PACK already makes 3 quote cards plus a cover for IG/FB. A second export of the same cards plus 3-4 extra fact slides, as a PDF, is the LinkedIn version, so there's no new design work.
  • Existing assets: research pages that already have charts or sequences are ready-made decks: /research/parcels/ instruments + registrations, /research/twelve-year-pattern/ and the missing-records register.
  • Frequency: start at one document post a week, Tuesday or Wednesday morning UK. Don't add LinkedIn video until documents have a baseline; the data states video is the weakest native format right now.
  • Editorial rules on every slide: never name the project lead; none of the banned words (states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken); "our case, prospective, not a finding" on any case point; no insignia; nothing about family holdings or addresses.
  • Not checked: whether our posting route can attach a document. If the LinkedIn API isn't connected, the route is the browser with the saved login. The Documents API needs app approval, so expect the browser.
  • Zero spend. Nothing posted by this harvest.
