County journal back-catalogue finder: what Morgannwg and Cardiff Naturalists printed on our parishes

Harvested 30 Oct 2026. A free content-and-credibility skill: find every article the county societies have printed on Llandough, Leckwith, Cogan and Penarth, read them, and turn them into cited "From the journals" posts and a further-reading block. Zero spend, no outreach needed.

What exists (measured 30 Oct 2026)

  • Morgannwg (Glamorgan History Society journal), vols 1-48, 1957-2004, free on Welsh Journals (glamorganhistory.org states the run is online; Vale of Glamorgan libraries page states the same). Welsh Journals collection id 1169834: 48 issues, 1,057 article/review rows.
  • Reports and Transactions of the Cardiff Naturalists' Society, 1900-1981: collection id 1373290, 63 issues, 934 rows.
  • journals.library.wales is Cloudflare-walled from this box; the IIIF manifests at iiif.llyfrgell.cymru are not. tools/nlw_journal_articles.py reads them (stdlib only); this run added a regex flag: python3 tools/nlw_journal_articles.py 1169834 --re "llandough|leckwith|cogan|penarth" (a whole collection takes about 15 s with --delay 0.2). The PDFs are scans; read page by page with pdftoppm -r 80 and eyes, then delete the temp files.

The two-Llandough trap (the main lesson)

Glamorgan has two Llandoughs: Llandough-juxta-Penarth (Llandochau Fach; our parish, St Dochdwy's, the hospital) and Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge (Llandough Castle). Two of the three "Llandough" titles in Morgannwg are the Cowbridge one. Before a title becomes a post or a citation, read the first page and look for Cowbridge, the Castle, St Mary Church, Penllyn (Cowbridge side) versus Penarth, Cogan, Leckwith, Ely river, Dinas Powys hundred (our side). Write which one it is in the table.

Playbook

  1. Run the finder on both collections with the parish regex plus neighbours (sully, lavernock, dinas pow, wenvoe, michaelston, ely). Save hits to a table like the one above.
  2. Triage by reading the first page of each; mark "ours / other Llandough / neighbour".
  3. For each "ours" article: one 150-250 word "From the journals" note — the finding in our own neutral words, a short quotation at most (NLW notes copyright may apply: never re-host the PDF), full citation as text (author, title, journal, vol., year, pages).
  4. Add a "Printed in the county journals" further-reading block to the matching Cedwyn research or wiki page: citations as plain text, no outbound links (site rule).
  5. Re-use the notes on social on the matching hashtag day (see the #FolkloreThursday and heritage hashtag skills), each with the internal Cedwyn page link.
  6. A neighbour article that names our places in passing is still a lead: log it, read later.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • First read: "The Manors of Sully, Cosmeston, Penarth and Cogan" (CNS LXVII, 14 pages) — the manorial frame for Cogan next to Llandough; then the Pierce place-names review for the Leckwith and Llandough entries. One note each, queued as HubSpot tasks for the research lane.
  • Put the two-Llandough check in the research editorial checklist, beside "Llandough Farm is not Great House Farm": a Cowbridge source must never be cited for our parish.
  • Further-reading block on /research/sources-and-archives/ (citations only), proposed through the engineering lane, not committed from this lane.
  • Editorial rules: neutral third person, never name the project lead, banned words, no insignia, nothing
