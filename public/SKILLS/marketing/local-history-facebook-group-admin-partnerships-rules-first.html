Local-history Facebook groups: admin partnerships, rules first (no spam)

Harvested 29 Oct 2026 23:15Z (skills harvest). Topic: reaching the people already gathered in other people's local-history groups (Penarth, Llandough, Cardiff, Vale) by working with the admins, not by posting links. Our own Group is a separate skill (facebook-group-community-and-link-limits.md); the Page link cap is in facebook-page-link-limit-meta-one-link-budget.md.

What practitioners and the platform show (read 29 Oct 2026)

  • These groups are big, volunteer-run and protective. Fast Company, 14 Feb 2025 (Steven Melendez, read via Wayback): local-history groups "can attract hundreds of thousands" of members; many are run by amateurs who "volunteer their time to moderate posts, removing spam". The museum curator interviewed (John Marks, Historic Geneva) spends "a few hours a month" preparing posts, captions each image from what the museum knows, records reliable member memories (owners of a shop), and when a member states "my mom worked there, I have X from that factory" replies inviting a donation. The value flows both ways: posts in, memories and artefacts out.
  • Rules are explicit and often absolute. Real group rules seen in search (29 Oct): Southampton Sotonians & Friends - "Members voted unanimously against any advertising" on a free sharing history group; Rockwall history group - "not for spam, self promotion or selling"; another - "limit self-promotion ... to once a month"; a gold-trails history group's admins discussed allowing history-book authors to post their work (so permission is an admin decision, case by case). Historical Novel Society moderators report member anger whenever self-promotion creeps in.
  • The tooling enforces it. Meta Help (Admin Assist, help/436275657385753, snippet): admins set criteria and Admin Assist "will automatically decline an incoming post if any criteria is met" (link posts, keywords, new accounts). Group rules (help/462230500886400) and entry questions where joiners click "I agree" to the rules (admin practice, snippet). A link post in a strict group is usually declined before a human sees it.
  • What admins ask for instead (author-group advice in the same results, snippets): connect to groups tied to your book's place, offer a talk/presentation, give something to the group.

The playbook

  1. List the groups, read the rules, log them. For each candidate group: name, URL, size, admin names (as shown publicly), the promotion rule verbatim, whether links/selling are allowed, promo day if any. One row each in a sheet. Skip any group whose rules ban advertising: we only contribute there, never promote.
  2. Join as a person or Page that contributes first. 2-4 weeks of real contribution before any ask: answer questions with a source, add a dated photo caption, correct a date politely with the record. No links in these posts (they trip Admin Assist and read as selling).
  3. Approach one admin privately with an offer, not a request. Offers that serve the group: (a) a free, fully captioned image set for their members to post (public-domain or our own photos only); (b) a free online talk for the group (link to the talk route skills); (c) a free e-book copy for the group to give away or pin in Files; (d) answers to the group's recurring questions as a pinned fact sheet. Ask how they want it: their post, a guest post, a pinned item, an event, or nothing.
  4. Let the admin post it. An admin's own post is seen as the group's content, not a pitch. Supply text + images + one plain URL to a free page (not the shop). Our CTA lives on that page.
  5. Bring something back. Record memories members add (with what they said and when) as leads for the evidence catalogue - with consent; never copy private people's names or photos to our site without permission.
  6. One ask per group per quarter at most. If an admin states no, it is no for 6 months. Never DM members.

Numbers to track

  • Per group: posts made, admin-approved items, clicks on the free page's group UTM (utm_source=fbgroup-<slug>&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=admin-partner), signups, and member leads (memories/artefacts offered).
  • Kill rule: under 10 clicks from a group across two admin-posted items, stop asking that group; keep contributing only if members value it.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Targets (to verify rules first, nothing posted yet): Penarth Local History Society's own Group (facebook.com) and Page; the Penarth Community Network page (it shared the PLHS Page launch); large Cardiff/Vale nostalgia groups found by search. Our talks skill (civic-local-history-society-talks-penarth.md) already covers PLHS as a venue; this skill covers the online group.
  • Offer pack (draft in the project workspace): 6 captioned images of Llandough and Leckwith from our own or public-domain sources, each with a one-line source; a free-edition e-book link (/ebooks/ free title, not the paid one); an offer of a free online talk. Short, plain, no banned words, never names the project lead or any private person, nothing about family holdings or addresses.
  • Who posts: the Great House Farm II Page or the project lead personally - joining groups and messaging admins is a public, outward act, so each admin approach is a HubSpot draft for the project lead's GO. No DMs to members, ever (RULES.md).
  • Feed the research: member memories go to the project workspace as leads with date, group and post URL, marked "not checked".
