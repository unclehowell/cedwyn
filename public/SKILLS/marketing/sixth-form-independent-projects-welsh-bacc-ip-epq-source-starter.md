## Why this audience
Each year about 12,000 Welsh 17–18-year-olds must find their own research topic and their own sources, and Cedwyn is an organised primary-source set on a Vale of Glamorgan place.
- **Wales, Welsh Bacc Advanced Skills Challenge Certificate** (WJEC final report, summer 2024, read):
  - The Individual Project had **11,883 entries from 161 centres**, "almost the same as in June 2023".
  - The successor, the **Advanced Skills Baccalaureate Wales** (first teaching Sept 2023; Qualifications Wales, read), keeps an Individual Project worth **50%** of the grade: "an independent research project (extended written project or artefact)". It is assessed as non-exam assessment (NEA).
  - Qualifications Wales: selective universities that don't require it still value it, "particularly through completion of the Individual Project".
- **England and Wales, EPQ** (Cambridge Assessment, Gill 2022, read):
  - 45,687 EPQ entries in 2018/19, up from 1,706 in 2008 and 38,548 in 2015/16. That is about the size of A-level History (46,051).
  - Entries can now go in May and in November.
  - WJEC offers its own Level 3 Extended Project (wjec.co.uk, search preview only).
- **What the examiners say students lack** (WJEC 2024 report, read). This is the gap Cedwyn can fill:
  - "missed opportunities to undertake numerical analysis of secondary data";
  - rationales that "simply referred to using primary and secondary research" without naming the sources and why;
  - titles "too broad or [with] a very close personal interest", which lead to anecdote over research;
  - top projects "comment on reliability, validity, currency and comprehensiveness" of sources.
- **Precedent:** the National Library of Wales runs a Welsh Bacc Individual Project course for students (library.wales, search preview only; the page returned 403 to our fetch). National institutions already treat this as an audience.

## The offer: one free "project starter" page, not a sale
The buyers are 17-year-olds and their teachers, so the free page is the product and the e-books are optional further reading.
1. **5 narrow, researchable titles**, each answerable from public records. Examples:
   - "How did land use on one Llandough farm change between the 1841 tithe survey and the 1910 valuation?"
   - "What do census returns 1841–1911 show about who worked the land at one Vale farm?"
   - "How far do newspaper reports 1890–1914 match the official record for one parish?"
   - "Llandough to Chubut: what do passenger and chapel records show about local emigration to Patagonia?"
   - "How reliable are 19th-century estate maps as evidence? A comparison of three surveys."
   Each title lists 4–6 named sources with catalogue IDs and where they are held.
2. **One small CSV dataset per title** (for example tithe parcels with acreage and land use, or census households by occupation). This answers the examiners' "numerical analysis of secondary data" point directly. Mark each CSV up per dataset-structured-data-google-dataset-search-evidence-catalogue.md.
3. **A "how reliable is this source?" box** on each record page: who made it, when, why, what it leaves out. Students can cite it for the credibility criterion.
4. **A citation line** for every record (Harvard-style), so referencing is copy-paste.
5. **A teacher note:** a free class set, no sign-up needed to use the page, plus an optional e-book list for the school library at the existing institution licence tier.

## Rules that keep it safe
- **Neutral heritage topics only:** land use, census, tithe, emigration, buildings. No project title, prompt or dataset about the 1987 case, the FOI correspondence or the family dispute.
- The editorial rules apply: never name the project lead, no banned words, nothing about family holdings or addresses.
- **No collecting data from students:** no email gate on the starter page, and no Mailchimp pop-up on that page.
- **Outreach to schools and colleges is a proposal**, and drafts go to HubSpot only. Contact is through school or college library and Welsh Bacc coordinators via their published addresses, on the project lead's GO. Never DM a student.
- Don't claim any exam-board endorsement. Write "useful for" and never "approved for".

## Timing
- Projects are usually chosen and proposed in the autumn of Year 12/13 and researched over winter. Centres set their own deadlines (Qualifications Wales, read).
- EPQ has May and November windows, so the page should be live and indexed by **January** for the next selection round. Not checked against any centre calendar.

## Measure
- **Visits to /learn/projects/:** count via our /r/ links and the human-visitor proxy. Exclude bots.
- **CSV downloads:** count with the own beacon.
- **Inbound teacher emails:** count, and convert each into a HubSpot task.
- **Kill rule:** if after one selection window (by end of March 2027) the page has fewer than 50 human visits and 0 teacher contacts, stop promoting it and leave it up as a static page.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Build `/learn/projects/` (the academy already lives under /learn/) with the 5 titles, 5 CSVs, reliability boxes and citation lines. Reuse existing tithe, census and Patagonia material, so the job is page assembly, not new research.
- Add a "Doing a Welsh Bacc or EPQ project?" line to the free e-book back matter and to the /research/ footer.
- A Welsh-language version of the titles (cy) is needed, since many Individual Projects are written in Welsh. Use the existing bilingual layout skill.
- Draft one HubSpot outreach task, "[OTHER AGENT'S TURN v0.1.0] Welsh Bacc coordinators, Vale/Cardiff colleges: free project-starter page". Send only on the project lead's GO, with the never-pay and sign-off rules.
- In the Sunday growth report: page visits, CSV downloads, teacher contacts.
