Cousin-bait ancestor pages: turning pedigree and research notes into searchable posts that bring descendants (and readers) to the site

Harvested 2026-10-31 (skills harvest). Topic: content marketing from genealogy notes. Family-history bloggers call a post written to be found by descendants searching a name or place "cousin bait". It brings a small but steady stream of high-intent readers, plus new documents and photos. That is the audience most likely to buy a local-history e-book.

What practitioners report (primary testimony)

  • Marian B. Wood, Climbing My Family Tree (Jan 2019, read): "Currently, I receive 1-2 inquiries every month" through a contact gadget beside her surname list. Her method:
  • a landing page per main surname with that family's stories;
  • Blogger labels for surnames, places and institutions;
  • key words in the blog description, subheads and image captions.
  • Why it works: one second cousin found her through a post about the synagogue where their ancestors married, because the post "ranked high in the results". Another blogger gets inquiries from a post about an orphanage. Places and institutions bring searchers, not just surnames.
  • Amy Coffin, We Tree (Oct 2012, read), how to write the post:
  • pick ONE family unit (parents + children), not "all the Millers";
  • put individual full names in the post (that's what ranks);
  • state your connection, so the reader knows why you're asking;
  • say exactly what you lack (stories, work, photos).
  • A commenter (Linda McCauley) got an email from a 5th-great-grandparent cousin while reading it, "over a year since I blogged about that family". The payoff is slow and long-tail.
  • The counterpoint, Gathering Leaves for My Family Tree (May 2021, read): "Excepting a few cousins, I can't say that I have learned much from those who contact me." The author thinks cousin bait "works best for those ... who have a sizeable profile". So expect contacts, not guaranteed evidence. Publishing consistently is the hard part.
  • 52 Ancestors in 52 Weeks (Amy Johnson Crow, running since 2014; page read):
  • free weekly prompts by email (e.g. "an ancestor I admire", "a record that adds colour", "Written");
  • share any way you like (blog, social post, video, email to cousins);
  • hashtag #52Ancestors.
  • WikiTree runs a parallel G2G weekly thread and profile badge (Help:52_Ancestors; page not readable from here, search snippet only).
  • Use the prompts as a ready editorial calendar. No participant counts were published on the pages read, so the reach is not measured.

The recipe (one post per week, about 45 minutes)

  1. Pick one person or one family unit from a note we already hold (a pedigree line, a census row, a burial, a lease).
  2. Title = full name + dates + place variants, e.g. "John Williams (d. 1908) of Great House, Llandough juxta Penarth / Tŷ Mawr". Put the Welsh and older spellings in a subhead and the image alt text.
  3. Body: - what the record shows, quoted verbatim with its source; - what it does NOT show (a surname in a pedigree is a lead, not proof of standing); - one open question ("Do you have a photograph of the Great House before 1988? A family Bible naming John Williams?").
  4. One contact door at the end: "Descended from this family, or hold papers about it? Tell us." Use a form or the existing signup tagged by post (UTM utm_source=cousin&utm_campaign=<slug>). One CTA only, then the e-book card under it.
  5. Internal links: person page -> family/surname landing page -> evidence record (EV-xxxx) -> /ebooks/.
  6. Optionally share on the week's #52Ancestors prompt when it fits (a social post, not a DM or group spam).

Guardrails

  • Deceased people only. Follow family-tree-page-living-privacy-proof-standard.md (living people stay private).
  • No insignia. Nothing about family holdings or addresses.
  • Public text never names the project lead. Banned public words apply (states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken); write "not checked".
  • Treat replies as leads. A descendant's story is recorded as received, not as proof.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Series: "People of Great House / Tŷ Mawr", one page per person or family unit on cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate, starting with the Williams line (John Williams Sr d. 1908, John Williams Jr). Then the Vaughan-era names that appear in G. T. Clark's Limbus Patrum Morganiae et Glamorganiae pedigrees for Llandough and Leckwith, written as "what the printed pedigree lists". Our inference, the link to Great House, stays labelled as inference unless a record joins them. Llandough near Cowbridge and Llandough Farm are excluded.
  • Place pages: Marian Wood's institution lesson, applied here: separate short pages for Llandough church (St Dochdwy), Leckwith bridge, Cogan, the Ely river crossing. Searchers looking for a place, not a surname, land on these.
  • Measure (honest counts):
  • inquiries per month from the cousin form (benchmark from one long-running blog: 1-2/month);
  • organic landings on person pages (Search Console, query = name);
  • e-book clicks from those pages.
  • Review after 12 posts. If 0 inquiries and fewer than 20 organic landings in total, fold the series into the evidence catalogue and stop.
  • Owner: BIZDEV weekly content slot. The book flywheel and the evidence catalogue supply the notes, and every quote keeps its source line.
