Wales Week London / Worldwide: free listing for a St David's fortnight event (Feb–Mar 2027)

Written 28 Oct 2026 (skills harvest). Topic not in INDEX before this run (only a "next ideas" mention).

What it is (primary pages read today)

  • Wales Week London is a volunteer-run programme of events over the fortnight around St David's Day (1 Mar). Co-founders Dan Langford and Mike Jordan run it in their "spare time" (walesweek.london/about).
  • Growth, from its own About page: 56 events in 2017, 81 in 2018, 100+ in 2019, 136 in 2020, 70 online events in 2021 (lockdown), up to 90 in 2022, 111 in 2023 and 130 in 2024. 2026 was the 10th year. A Commons St David's Day debate on 26 Feb 2026 marked the anniversary (Hansard search snippet; the page itself didn't load).
  • Hosting is a form, not a fee. The "Host an event" page has a form (name, email, company, event name, venue yes/no, dates, description) and an "Event organisers' toolkit". The categories it lists include heritage, the arts, the media, academia and tourism.
  • Wales Week Worldwide: sister weeks have been held in Paris, Melbourne, New York, Ohio, Kansas, Pittsburgh, Toronto, British Columbia, Dubai, Tokyo, Osaka, Hong Kong and others. They're run by local volunteers, so locations change from year to year. The page states: "there are no fees / charges". It also states a Wales Week can "boost engagement and membership" for a diaspora society. The plan is a single online programme across all locations, so online events reach people anywhere.
  • Programme style: the past-events pages show a mix of formal and community listings, e.g. a Hay Festival "Wales in Words" evening, a charity raffle for Tenovus and the London Welsh Centre, and the "Wales-made His Dark Materials at the V&A". A Facebook post from Velindre Fundraising shows charities announce their listing to their own followers.

What people involved say (testimony, light on numbers)

  • Baroness Wilcox of Newport, quoted on the Get Involved page: "Wales Week London couldn't be a better launchpad for people to see what we're about."
  • Wales Week Berkshire started from a single conversation with the co-founder, who told the volunteer to "crack on" (Facebook post, snippet only, not checked).
  • No sales or attendance numbers per event were found. Read it as a visibility and goodwill calendar, not a sales channel. The upside is a dated reason to post, a listing on a well-followed Welsh programme, and an online audience that includes the diaspora.

How to do it (any small heritage business)

  1. By early December: sign up for the Wales Week email updates (form on every page) so you hear when the 2027 programme opens.
  2. Pick a format that needs no venue cost: a 45-minute online talk with Q&A, or a short bilingual reading.
  3. Fill in the Host-an-event form with title, date (within the fortnight around 1 Mar), "online" as the venue and a 2-line description. Download the toolkit and follow its logo and hashtag rules.
  4. Registration: use a free sign-up page on your own site (not a third-party ticket page) so every registrant joins your list with a source tag.
  5. Promote it: announce on your own channels about 3 weeks out, again a week out and on the day. Tag @walesweeklondon and @walesweekworld.
  6. Afterwards: within 48 hours, send registrants the recording plus one link to the product page. Count registrations, attendees, signups and sales by the UTM tag.

How we apply it to Cedwyn (proposal; needs the project lead's GO)

  • Event: "Tŷ Mawr through 2,000 years: the farm in maps, drawings and photographs". A free 45-minute online talk on Wed 24 Feb or Mon 1 Mar 2027 (the date is our choice), bilingual titles (cy/en) with an es subtitle line for Argentina. The content is heritage only: no case narrative, no named people, and none of the banned public words.
  • Why it fits: the diaspora angle. The Worldwide page invites diaspora groups, and the Chubut societies route is already researched (welsh-patagonian-societies-chubut-outreach.md). One online event can be offered to them as their St David's item rather than as a cold pitch.
  • Registration: a cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate/events/ page with a Mailchimp form tagged walesweek2027 and UTM utm_source=walesweek&utm_campaign=stdavid27. The follow-up email links to /ebooks/ (free v0.0.1 first, then the £4.99 book). No discount and no countdown, per the price rules.
  • Measure it: registrations, live attendees, new subscribers and e-book sales within 14 days.
  • Kill rule: under 15 registrations by 22 Feb means it becomes a recorded video post on the day. Under 5 tracked sales by 31 Mar means we don't repeat it in 2028.
  • Timeline: submit the form by 15 Jan 2027, or as soon as the programme opens if earlier. Ask the project lead for the GO on submitting (public event in the company's name) in the same December decision batch as the Small Awards and Local History Month items.
