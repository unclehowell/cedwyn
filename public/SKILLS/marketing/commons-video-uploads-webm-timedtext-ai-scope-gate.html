Wikimedia Commons video uploads for the Cedwyn clips: format, licence, AI-scope rules, subtitles, and a gate

Harvested 31 Oct 2026 (skills harvest, ~11:15Z). Companion to wikimedia-commons-wikidata-heritage-coi.md (images, COI) and wikidata-p10-video-placement-measured-gate.md (where a clip gets seen). This file is the upload craft for VIDEO only.

The rules (Commons policy pages, read 31 Oct 2026)

  • Format (Commons:Video): preferred WebM with AV1, VP9 or VP8; Theora/MPEG-1/MPEG-2 allowed. H.264/MP4 is not allowed and must be converted first. Our clips are already 720p WebM, so they fit as they are.
  • Size (Commons:Maximum file size): host limit 5 GiB; uploads over 100 MiB need chunked upload (UploadWizard does this). Our clips are under 25 MB.
  • Tools: UploadWizard (browser), or video2commons on Toolforge (converts + uploads; its "Import subtitles" does not work for .vtt, phab T368298, so give it .srt).
  • Licence (Commons:Video): video AND audio must be free (e.g. CC BY-SA 4.0 / CC BY 4.0). Every underlying work (music, archive photos, maps) must itself be public domain or freely licensed. Copyrighted background music gets the audio stripped or the file deleted.
  • AI-generated media (Commons:AI-generated media): only media "realistically useful for an educational purpose" are in scope; AI media must go in subcategories of "AI-generated images by subject/by software"; media featuring living people go in "AI-generated media of living people (PIP)". Out-of-scope AI files get deletion requests.
  • English Wikipedia (WP:AIIMAGES, Image use policy): "Most images wholly generated by artificial intelligence (AI) should not be used in mainspace", subject to common sense and exceptions. So AI-rendered drama scenes will not be accepted into articles even if Commons keeps them.
  • Subtitles (Commons:Timed Text): captions live on pages named TimedText:..srt, one per language, so cy / en / es subtitles attach to one file and the player offers all three.

How to do it (steps)

  1. Pick only clips made wholly of material we can license: real footage we shot from public places, maps we drew, and scans of public-domain records (pre-1900 documents, PD-old-70). Exclude every OmniHuman/AI-rendered scene, every clip with a real person's likeness, and every clip with licensed music.
  2. Re-check rights on each frame's sources; write the source line for each record shown (catalogue ref) into the description.
  3. Encode check: WebM VP9 + Opus, keep 720p; under 100 MB so the plain upload path works.
  4. File name: descriptive and neutral, place first ("Llandough, Vale of Glamorgan - 1840s tithe map explained (cy subtitles).webm"). No brand words.
  5. Description: {{Information}} with a neutral one-paragraph description, date, source "own work" plus each underlying record's citation, author = the account holder; licence template (CC BY-SA 4.0 is the Commons norm; CC BY 4.0 if the owner prefers easy reuse).
  6. Categories: the place (Category:Llandough, Penarth) and the subject (maps / historical records of Glamorgan). Add structured data "depicts" (see commons-structured-data-depicts-own-uploads.md).
  7. Upload the .srt files as TimedText pages: .cy.srt first (Welsh is the period language), then .en.srt and .es.srt.
  8. Log file name, date and URL in the project workspace; measure with the mediarequests API at 30 and 90 days.

Gate

  • Pilot 3 clips that pass step 1. At 90 days: keep uploading only if the median clip passes 400 requests/12-month pace (the "unused" median above) or one clip is placed in an article by an independent editor. Otherwise stop; the Internet Archive mirror (internet-archive-movies-clips-mirror-captions-gate.md) already gives a permanent copy.
  • Any deletion request = stop and read it; never argue scope for an AI scene.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • PROPOSAL, owner approval needed: it needs a public Commons account held by a person (no shared accounts, no bot uploads), and a free licence on the clips, which lets anyone reuse them, including commercially. That is the owner's decision, not ours.
  • Candidate material: map and record explainer cuts, not the dramatised documentary scenes. The documentary's AI scenes stay on our own pages and YouTube.
  • Tab prepares the batch (file list, descriptions, .srt files, categories) in the project workspace; the account holder uploads.
  • Public text rules apply to descriptions: never name the owner, banned words list, no family holdings or addresses, neutral dated wording, no outbound promotional links (Commons descriptions are not adverts; the source citation is enough).
