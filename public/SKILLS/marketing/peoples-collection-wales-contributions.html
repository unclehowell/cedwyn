People's Collection Wales (PCW): contributing records, choosing a licence, and using it for discovery

Harvested 2026-10-26 (skills harvest 14:37). Primary pages read 26 Oct.

Why it matters

  • Free national platform run by Welsh heritage bodies (National Library of Wales, Museum Wales, RCAHMW). Live counts on the Discover page, 26 Oct 2026: 168,505 items, 3,618 collections, 1,606 users, 847 stories. Stories are the thin layer (847 nationally), so a well-built local story stands out.
  • Items rank for place names in search (a "site:peoplescollection.wales Llandough Penarth" search returns PCW item pages first), and PCW items are what teachers, local historians and Welsh media reuse.

How contributing works

  1. Create a free account; upload images, documents, audio or video; add a short title and description. Free training and support on request.
  2. Every item passes PCW moderation for high-risk copyright or GDPR issues before going live; PCW runs a takedown policy.
  3. Formats: Item (single file), Collection (a set), Story (narrative with items), plus tours.

Copyright rules (the part that decides what we can upload)

  • Upload only if: you hold the copyright, or have the holder's permission (ideally in writing), or the work is out of copyright, or already under a sharing licence.
  • Owning a copy is not owning copyright: a family photo's copyright usually sits with the photographer; a newspaper cutting's with the publisher.
  • Orphan works (holder unknown): do due diligence (check the item, ask locally, search archives, keep a dated search log), weigh risk (commercial origin such as newspaper or pro photos = higher risk), label "Author/rights holder unknown - please contact us", use low resolution.
  • Each item carries one of two licences:
  • Creative Archive Licence: copy, share, adapt for personal/educational use, non-commercial only, with credit.
  • CC BY 4.0: any use including commercial, with credit.
  • Site terms 2.2: content owned or licensed by PCW is for non-commercial research or private study unless stated otherwise.

Decision rules

  • Our own photographs, maps, drawings and transcriptions we made: upload under the Creative Archive Licence. Others can share with credit but not sell; our own paid e-books are unaffected because we stay the copyright holder.
  • Newspaper reports (e.g. the 1974 articles), ITV footage, Cadw or RCAHMW images: do NOT upload. Describe and cite them, and link to the holder.
  • One item = one dated record with its source line. No accusations about named people, no banned words, nothing about family holdings or addresses.
  • Moderated text is public and permanent in practice: write it as we would print it.

Discovery tactics

  • Build one Story ("Tŷ Mawr, Llandough-juxta-Penarth: a house in the records") with 6-10 of our own items in date order, each linked to its evidence page on Cedwyn where the item form allows a source/URL line (check in the upload form; if links are stripped, put the Cedwyn page name in the source field).
  • Use the place names the platform already uses (Llandough, Penarth, Vale of Glamorgan) as tags so items cluster with existing local collections.
  • Bilingual title (Cymraeg / English) on each item; PCW is fully bilingual.

Research lead found while harvesting (not a marketing step)

  • PCW collection 2261401, "Aerial Photographs of Penarth, Dinas Powys and Llandough" (Vale of Glamorgan Libraries, created 12 May 2025, 377 views): large undated oblique aerials of unknown source plus dated 250x200 mm prints from the former Welsh Industrial and Maritime Museum. Worth a frame-by-frame check for Tŷ Mawr before 1988; any hit goes to the object/evidence register, and a date query to Vale Libraries is a HubSpot draft only.
  • PCW also hosts a 1985 Llandough parish church booklet PDF (chs02627) to read as a source.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  1. Owner decision first: account in whose name (never name the owner publicly; a "Cedwyn" or estate account name is fine if he agrees).
  2. Shortlist 6-10 items we hold the copyright in (our maps, our site photos, our transcription tables). Log each with its licence in the project workspace
  3. Story text follows current rules: 1667 stated as the family's acquisition, cited to the 1974 newspaper reports and ITV News 1988; the 1824 David Stewart survey as the first record mention found; no Bute-dated entries.
  4. Measure: PCW item views (shown per item), referral visits from peoplescollection.wales in the Sunday growth report.
