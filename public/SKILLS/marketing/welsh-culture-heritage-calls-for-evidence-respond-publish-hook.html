Welsh culture/heritage calls for evidence: respond, get published, use as a dated hook

Harvested 2026-10-30 02:45Z. New topic: the index already covers Senedd petitions, UK e-petitions, PQ watch and GOV.UK consultations as news hooks; nothing on answering calls for evidence.

What it is (primary sources, read 30 Oct 2026)

  • Senedd committees run inquiries; each "consultation" (also called a "call for evidence") sets terms of reference and sometimes questions. Evidence goes by email or online form, Welsh or English; anyone with work, study or lived knowledge of the topic may respond (Senedd Guide to written evidence, published 14 Dec 2023). Normal practice is to publish evidence (Senedd 'Preparing written or digital evidence' page, search snippet; it also accepts video or audio clips as evidence), with the responder's name or organisation, on the committee's consultation page.
  • Culture, Communications, Cymraeg and Sport Committee (set up 16 Jun 2026; [address kept in the project workspace]): ran "Priorities for the Seventh Senedd" (written evidence by 28 Aug 2026), has published the responses, and next meets 2 Nov 2026 (committee page, read today). The Museums Association published its own response on 1 Sep 2026 and posted it on LinkedIn: the response itself became a dated content item for them.
  • Welsh Government call for evidence: "A vision for culture and sport in Wales" (first published 5 Aug 2026, updated 2 Oct). "Culture" explicitly includes heritage, museums, libraries and archives. Responses are welcome from individuals and communities, and may draw on data, research, lived experience, case studies or practical proposals. Send to [address kept in the project workspace]; deadline Fri 18 Dec 2026, midday. Published responses are kept indefinitely; FOI applies. Gov.wales ran a "2 months to go" press push (Oct 2026).
  • Business pages on business.senedd.wales sit behind a JavaScript firewall (403 to plain fetches): read them in the browser, or through policymogul.com mirrors and the committee page.

Why it is marketing, honestly

  1. A published response is a permanent, dated page on a .wales/.gov.wales domain naming the responder: authority, findable by journalists and researchers (not a dofollow link play; links in PDFs may not count).
  2. The deadline is a news hook with a date: "Welsh Government asks the public about the future of heritage and archives: responses close 18 Dec" is a legitimate post/newsletter item for a Welsh heritage archive.
  3. Committees invite some written respondents to oral evidence; that is the press-worthy step (not checked how often small respondents are invited).
  4. Responses that cite specific records (catalogue refs, dated documents) are the ones officials quote; generic opinion is not.

How to do it (checklist)

  1. Weekly: check the Senedd consultation list (browser) and gov.wales filtered to Culture/Heritage; log title, body, deadline, terms of reference.
  2. Gate: respond only where the terms of reference genuinely cover the archive's knowledge (heritage access, archives, local history, Welsh language heritage, digital collections). Skip anything about a live dispute, a named person or a planning decision.
  3. Draft 1-2 pages: who the responder is (1 line), 3-5 numbered points each tied to a question, each point backed by one concrete example from the archive with its catalogue reference; one practical proposal; offer to give oral evidence.
  4. Bilingual is a plus, not required; an organisation with Welsh language standards must meet them.
  5. After submission (owner's GO only): one post + one newsletter line "we responded to X; here is what we said", linking our own page that hosts the response; when the body publishes responses, add that dated link to the evidence catalogue.
  6. Diary the committee meeting dates and report publication: a report that quotes the response is a second hook.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Live opportunity: Welsh Government culture and sport strategy call for evidence, closes 18 Dec 2026 12:00. Cedwyn's angle: public access to historic land and estate records, digitised local archives, Welsh-language heritage, and how small community archives can surface documents (with 2-3 catalogue examples from the evidence catalogue). Neutral, policy-level; nothing about the court case, the council, named people or family holdings.
  • Watch: Culture Committee meets 2 Nov 2026; its first inquiries after the priorities consultation will follow. Add new calls to the hooks calendar.
  • Outward-step rule: a submission goes out in the owner's name, so it is a draft for the owner's GO (HubSpot task with the draft), never sent by an agent. Public text: never names the owner, banned words apply.
  • Content use: a /research/ page "Responses to public consultations" (neutral, dated) and one social post per submission once sent.
