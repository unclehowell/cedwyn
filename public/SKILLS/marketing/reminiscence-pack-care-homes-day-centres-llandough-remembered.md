# Reminiscence packs for care homes and day centres: a free, printable "Llandough remembered" set

Skill for: reaching the oldest local audience, people in care, many of them 75+ and offline, and the carers and families around them, with a free reminiscence resource built from records that are already public. This is community building and goodwill, not a sales tactic; it earns local word of mouth, press interest (on GO) and society partnerships. Written 31 Oct 2026, harvest run. The index had 0 entries for 'reminiscence', 'care home' or 'dementia' before this run.

## What practitioners do (read 31 Oct 2026)

- **Royal Mint Museum, "Borrow a box"** (read): "We loan Royal Mint Reminiscence Boxes, completely free of charge, to care homes, day centres, community groups, hospital wards and dementia groups."
  - Each box is a "Museum in a Box": genuine and replica items, audio clips triggered by placing objects on the box, storytelling, and "activity guidance for care workers".
  - Themed boxes over time: Decimalisation, Jubilee, and now the coins in your pocket.
  - It "has continued to expand each year" (their words; no numbers on the page).
- **M&S Archive, Leeds, community page** (read):
  - Free reminiscence outreach visits to care settings across Leeds, plus loan Memory Boxes. Each box holds original clothing, accessories and images, plus "image packs and guidance notes on running a reminiscence session".
  - Boxes are lent for up to two weeks, booked through a short form.
  - They "previously offered a Memory Box loan delivery service". In other words, they cut delivery and kept collection only: start with what you can sustain.
- **National Museums Liverpool, "My House of Memories" app** (MuseDCN case study, 2015, read):
  - Co-created with people living with dementia.
  - "More than 4500 carers have downloaded the app since its launch in June 2014."
  - An independent evaluation (Institute of Cultural Capital) found it "promoted interaction and engagement" and that objects tied to local and UK life provoked "personal and happy memories". Local content is the point.
- **Royal Collection Trust**: publishes free reminiscence resource sheets "using historic photographs … to help start conversations". Search result only; the page refused our fetch (403), so the format is not checked.
- **Commercial copies of the format:** Nestlé (its own reminiscence pack) and Twinkl (paid care-home packs with "pictures, quotes, information and discussion questions") show the standard shape. Search results only, not opened.

## The standard shape (common to all of the above)

1. **One theme per pack.** For example: the farm year, the village before the hospital, the dock, school days.
2. **8–12 large photos or record images, one per A4 page.** High contrast, a short caption in 18pt+ type, no dense text.
3. **3–4 open prompts per image.** Examples: "Did anyone in your family work on a farm?", "What did a market day smell like?" Never quiz questions with right answers.
4. **A one-page guide for the activity coordinator:** session length (30–45 min), group size (4–8), let people wander off-topic, no correcting memories.
5. **Free, printable PDF, plus an optional loan box later.** Start with the PDF: no delivery logistics, no stock. That is the M&S lesson.
6. **A feedback ask:** "Tell us one memory it brought up (with permission)". This is the bridge to oral-history collection, which has its own skill file.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn

- **Build (proposal, zero spend):**
  - A cy/en "Llandough a Leckwith: cofio / remembered" reminiscence pack: 10 pages.
  - Images only from already-public evidence pages and open-licence images (see `europeana-flickr-commons-open-licence-images-rights-check.md`); the archive-image licensing skill applies before any third-party image.
  - Themes: the 1840s tithe-map farm fields, the farmhouse and yard, Llandough village and church, Penarth Dock, the hospital's arrival.
  - Neutral heritage only: **no case-dispute material, no family holdings or addresses, no living people, no insignia, no banned words.** Host it as a free PDF on an internal Cedwyn page (e.g. /learn/reminiscence/), with no outbound links.
- **Welsh first:** many older Vale and Cardiff residents grew up with Welsh. Bilingual captions (cy first) follow `bilingual-welsh-english-layout-posts-cards-email.md`.
- **Reach (HubSpot drafts only, the project lead's GO before anything goes out):**
  - Draft first-contact emails to activity coordinators at care homes and day centres in Penarth, Llandough, Dinas Powys and Cardiff South: these are companies or organisations, not private individuals.
  - Also draft to Llandough Hospital's patient-experience or arts team, and to Age Cymru's local group.
  - One clear ask per email: "Here is a free printable pack about your area; would you like it, and would residents' memories be welcome on the project?" Never mention prices or the shop.
- **Partners:** offer the pack to the local history society and U3A groups (covered elsewhere) as something they can take into care settings. Their volunteers do the visits; we supply the material.
- **Consent:** any memory shared back is recorded only with written permission from the person, or from their attorney/family where capacity is in doubt. Never publish identifiable health information. Memories go to the oral-history workflow, not straight onto the site.
- **Measure:**
  - PDF downloads per month (a first-party beacon, or a coded link like `?src=carehome`).
  - Homes using it.
  - Memories received.
  - Any society or press interest.
- **Judge after 3 months:** if fewer than 3 homes have used it, keep the PDF up and stop the outreach.
- **Why it matters for sales:** indirect. Families visiting residents are the 40–65 readers who buy local-history e-books. The pack's last page carries one quiet line: "More about Llandough's history: cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate", with the site name only, no price.
