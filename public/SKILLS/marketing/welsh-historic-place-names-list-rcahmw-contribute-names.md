# Welsh historic place-name records: the List of Historic Place Names + Welsh Place-Name Society (contribution route)

Checked 30 Oct 2026 (pages read on this box; record counts from live searches of the List the same day).

## What it is
- **List of Historic Place Names of Wales** (Rhestr o Enwau Lleoedd Hanesyddol), kept by the Royal Commission (RCAHMW) under the Historic Environment (Wales) Act 2016: historicplacenames.rcahmw.gov.uk (Welsh mirror enwaulleoeddhanesyddol.cbhc.gov.uk). Launched with ~350,000 names; a later announcement (headline seen in search, not opened) put it at 660,000+ records after adding 515,902 names. Sources include OS 2nd edition maps via GB1900, tithe schedules (1840s), Edward Lhuyd's Parochialia (1699), estate maps, and **names sent in by the public** (the source list has named individuals and school projects as "Source").
- It is free to search, has Advanced Search (county, parish, source, year range, place type: settlement/field/topographic/admin), postcode radius search (50 m–5 km), a map, and a **CSV download** of any result set (Tools tab).
- **How to contribute (RCAHMW, read):** send name + location + the year since which the name is known to the enquiries form, [address kept in the project workspace], or the Place Names Officer after one of his talks ("he doesn't charge an appearance fee"). Since Apr 2024 the map has a coordinates button so a contributor can click and copy coordinates (blog "Now YOU can help record place names!", 22 Apr 2024: one member of staff, geolocation was the bottleneck). Registered users can also add comments on a record; Community Rules ban untrue, defamatory or privacy-breaching content; comments are moderated and published only in the language submitted.
- **Welsh Government DataMapWales form "Record Welsh Place Names"** (datamap.gov.wales, read): any name not on digital maps (fields, houses, bridges, old mills, ruins...), click the map, fill the form; names are shared with Wikipedia, Mapio Cymru/OpenStreetMap AND the List. Photo rules: no people; other people's property only with permission; from public places.
- **Cymdeithas Enwau Lleoedd Cymru / Welsh Place-Name Society** (founded 2011, cymdeithasenwaulleoedd.cymru): aims to support people recording and interpreting names; newsletter (Bwletin), annual conference (2022: online, free to members, £10 public), "Gwarchod" (protect) project, a Society map with a "Contributors' corner", a form for recording evidence about a name. Contacts listed in search snippet: [address kept in the project workspace] (general), [address kept in the project workspace] (bulletin). Membership is by printed form and a fee (amount not read; site's join page 404'd today). Glamorgan Archives has run a "Casglu Enwau Lleoedd Cymru / Collecting Welsh Place Names" session (ticket page seen in search, not opened).

## How to do it (steps)
1. Search the List for the parish (Advanced Search > Glamorganshire > parish) and download the CSV. Keep it as the baseline.
2. Build a sheet: name as written, modern spelling if obvious (never "correct" the source), place type, approximate grid reference (use the map's coordinates button), earliest year + source citation (document, archive reference, page).
3. Only names from public documents or names in ordinary local use. No house numbers, no living people, nothing that marks out a private holding as "ours".
4. Send in one batch by the enquiries form or email with a short covering note; offer the source scans' references, not the scans. Alternatively one name at a time via the DataMapWales form (it also feeds OpenStreetMap and Wikipedia).
5. After publication (RCAHMW adds them "as soon as possible"), re-run the CSV and diff. Each accepted record shows the contributor as "Source", a permanent, citable government record.
6. For interpretation (meaning of elements) use the List's Glossary (~900 elements) and, for hard cases, the Welsh Place-Name Society's discussion groups / bulletin.

## Why it is marketing (honestly)
No sales numbers exist for this route. Its value is authority and discovery: a crown-copyright register that historians, Wikipedia editors and AI answer engines read, with our documents named as source; a reason for a neutral talk or bulletin note; and Welsh-language credibility. Treat as trust-building, not a sales channel.
