Crowdfunded publishers (Unbound): a closed route, and who holds pre-order money

Checked 30 Oct 2026 ('unbound' had 0 hits in INDEX.md before this; the existing crowdfunding skills are Kickstarter/Crowdfunder pages and matched-crowdfunding-heritage-evidence-gate.md, not crowdfunded publishers).

What happened (primary reporting, read)

  • Unbound went into administration in March 2025. Its assets were bought for £50,000 by a new company, Boundless, "which took on no legal obligation for the predecessor's debts" (Publishers Weekly, read).
  • At the collapse, 238 authors and agents were owed £657,000, nearly 8,000 customers were owed £391,000 for pre-ordered books, and trade creditors were owed £829,000 (PW, read).
  • Boundless said it would pay authors in full, then suspended the "goodwill payments" in May 2025 ("We simply do not have the cash at the moment...", CEO letter quoted by PW, read).
  • On 1 Aug 2025 Boundless Publishing Group was put into liquidation (PW, read; ALLi news 12 Aug 2025, read). Rights for Boundless/Unbound authors revert automatically, but what happens to existing stock was still unclear when reported.
  • Author testimony (Guardian, 18 Mar 2025, read): Tom Cox, whose 21st-Century Yokel was Unbound's fastest-ever crowdfunded title, was owed what he believed to be "a five-figure sum". After his rights reverted, 5,342 copies were delivered to his house. Supporters of his unpublished book were first told they would be refunded, then told they would not be, because Boundless "will not be fulfilling orders or offering refunds for books that haven't transferred."
  • ALLi (read): many authors are still owed royalties, with "diminishing prospects" of getting them.

The rule this teaches (applies beyond Unbound)

  1. Do not hand readers' pre-order money to a middleman that holds it for months before publication. In this case the readers lost their money and the author lost goodwill with them, which is worse than losing the sale.
  2. Pre-orders and crowdfunding stay on rails we control. Payment links pay into our own Stripe account, so money only moves when there is a deliverable. Preferably, sell only what already exists (the v0.0.2 e-books are already finished files).
  3. If someone does pre-pay for something unfinished (for example a print run), state the delivery date and the refund rule at checkout, and keep the money unspent until delivery. That respects UK consumer rules and makes refunds possible.
  4. Do a counterparty check before signing with any publisher or platform, crowdfunded or not: latest Companies House accounts, any administration or liquidation notices in the Gazette, ALLi watchdog ratings, and whether other authors report late royalties.
  5. Rights reversion clause: any contract must revert rights automatically on insolvency and on a sale of the business. Unbound's clause did not trigger, because the administration was "immediately followed by a reconstruction" (PW, read).

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Verdict: NO to Unbound/Boundless. It is closed and in liquidation. Remove it from any future list of publisher pitches. It is not among the three Welsh publisher pitch drafts (Seren, Graffeg, Carreg Gwalch), so nothing there needs changing.
  • If a print edition or a documentary DVD is ever crowdfunded, use a platform where pledges are charged only when the target is met (Kickstarter's all-or-nothing, Crowdfunder UK, already covered). Funds settle to our own account, and we fulfil ourselves. Never use a publisher that keeps backers' money.
  • Prices stay fixed (v0.0.2 £4.99, bundle £19.96, academy £49.99/mo). Nothing here changes a price or creates a product. Any pre-order idea is a PROPOSAL needing the project lead's GO.
  • Add "counterparty check" as step 0 in the publisher-pitch checklist (welsh-publisher-proposals-seren-graffeg-carreg-gwalch.md) the next time that file is touched.
