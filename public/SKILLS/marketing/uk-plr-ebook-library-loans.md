# UK Public Lending Right (PLR) for e-books and remote library loans

Harvested 26 Oct 2026 (skills harvest 15:23). Registration is free; the blocker is the ISBN (see below).

## What it pays (primary numbers)
- DCMS consultation, published 21 Oct 2026 and closing 15 Nov 2026: the proposed rate for the 2025/26 scheme year is **13.63p per loan**, up from 12.40p in 2024/25. The rise mainly reflects fewer registered qualifying loans. The scheme covers physical books, audiobooks, e-books and e-audiobooks, and the **remote lending of e-books and e-audiobooks** from UK public libraries.
- Loans are calculated from national data collected from all statutory UK public libraries (DCMS, 2026 wording).
- The British Library sets a minimum payment of £1 and a cap of £6,600 per contributor per year. Over 60,000 contributors are registered.
- Timing (publishing.co.uk guide, July 2026, practitioner): register by **30 June** to count for that scheme year. Statements come in January and payment in February. Payment is **not retrospective** for years before registration. Registration is one-off per ISBN and pays for the life of the book.
- E-book eligibility for remote lending dates from July 2018.

## Eligibility rules that bite (British Library PLR pages)
1. **ISBN required, one per edition and format.** Paperback, e-book and audiobook each need their own ISBN and their own registration.
2. **Not eligible:** Kindle editions (Amazon licenses no UK library lending and uses ASINs), and **ISBNs starting 123**. Kobo assigns those to titles without a real ISBN, so a free Kobo identifier does not qualify. Crown Copyright, newspapers and journals are also excluded.
3. **Authorship must be personal:** a named person on the title page, not a company or organisation. Publishers and packagers are excluded. Co-contributors split by agreed percentage shares. A foreword alone does not qualify.
4. Register in your legal name with online ID verification. A pseudonym can be linked on the record.
5. The registrant must live in the UK or EEA when registering for UK PLR.
6. Not available if a publisher already charges libraries a licence fee for that e-book (you confirm to the best of your knowledge).
7. Irish PLR (same portal) pays on print only, not e-books.
8. Neighbouring income: ALCS collects some foreign PLR for UK authors. This is a separate registration and also free to join.

## The ISBN cost problem (zero-spend rule)
- Nielsen UK ISBN Store, read 26 Oct 2026: 1 ISBN £93; a block of 10 £174; 100 £387 (incl. VAT). For a UK/Irish publisher, ISBNs bought here never expire.
- Our e-books currently carry no ISBN, so **PLR income needs an owner decision to spend about £174** (10 ISBNs covers en/cy/es editions of the paid volumes). Tab never buys. Put the numbers in front of the owner and let them decide.
- Honest payback: at 13.63p per loan, £174 needs about 1,280 loans. A niche local-history e-book may take years to reach that, so the case is mainly long-term catalogue presence plus library discovery, not quick money. An ISBN also helps library and bookshop systems find the book (Nielsen's own wording: ordering, listing and stock control).

## Steps (once ISBNs exist)
1. Assign one ISBN per edition and format. Record each ISBN against its file in the project workspace
2. Owner creates the contributor account at bl.uk address, date of birth, bank details, ID check.
3. Register each ISBN with title, year and role, plus shares if there is a co-contributor or translator (cy/es translators count as contributors if they are named on the title page).
4. Get the e-books into lending catalogues (library-elending-overdrive-libby-via-kobo.md). PLR only pays when a library actually lends.
5. Do it before 30 June every year a new edition appears. Check the January statement.
6. Legal deposit is a separate duty to the British Library. Note it, but it is not part of PLR.
