# Probus club speaker slots (Vale of Glamorgan / Penarth)

Skill: getting a 40-60 minute local-history talk into Probus club programmes, the retired professionals' clubs that meet monthly with one guest speaker each time.
Harvested 26 Oct 2026 (skills harvest 16:52). Zero spend.

## What the primary sources show (read 26 Oct 2026)
- **The Vale Probus Club (Cowbridge)**: founded 1997, 46 members, meets the last Tuesday of each month in the St Quentin's suite, The Bear Hotel, Cowbridge. 10:15 coffee, short formal meeting, then the guest speaker, then a drink and optional light lunch. Its programme (headed 2026-27) leans on local and Welsh history: Welsh follies, Owain Glyndwr's siege of Coity Castle, a Llandow WW2 RAF pilot, Gareth Jones the journalist. The News page thanks an outgoing Speaker Secretary at the February AGM, so the current holder should be confirmed before writing. Members also visited the Barry War and History Museum. Read in full: probusclub.net and /news/.
- **Penarth Probus** (men's club): Sully Sports & Social Club, 3rd Tuesdays at 10:00, no meetings in August or December; no club website listed. Read in full: probusglobal.org club page GB-1773. A 2022 Penarth Times piece describes a 10:00 for 10:30 start with guest speakers (search snippet only, not checked).
- **How a Probus club fills a slot** (Golden Valley Probus, read in full): each month a member is "Speaker's Friend" and either talks, books a guest, or welcomes one; talks run 40-60 minutes (shorter at the AGM); divisive subjects such as politics and religion are avoided; some guests charge, most want expenses covered; the guest is normally invited to stay for lunch; it "can take several weeks to find a speaker", and members are pointed to the Women's Institute and Ladies Guild, which also book guest speakers.
- **National speaker registers**: probusonline.org keeps a Register of Speakers sorted by category, then by country and county (read 26 Oct; page last updated 29 July 2026). Examples on it talk for 30-45 minutes with slides and are free or donation-based. How a speaker gets added was not on the page read (not checked). Probus Global's Speaker's Corner directory lists speakers "recommended by one or more of our members"; some speak free or for a charity donation, a few charge (one listing quotes £100 by Zoom and £60-80 live; snippet, not checked).
- **Sales**: no figure for book or e-book sales per Probus talk was found. Treat it as a reputation-and-referral circuit, like Rotary (see rotary-club-talks-civic-audience.md).

## How to do it
1. One talk title and a 60-word blurb pitched as heritage, not as a dispute: Probus avoids divisive subjects, so the title is about the farm's recorded history and the Llandough landscape.
2. Length: build a 40-minute core with a 10-minute extension and 10 minutes of questions. Slides are fine (most Probus venues are hotel suites or social clubs; ask about a screen).
3. Route: the club secretary or Speaker Secretary, through the contact on the club's own page. Ask for the next free slot; programmes are set a year ahead, so expect 2027 dates.
4. Fee: offer free or expenses only. A Probus slot is a morning meeting with lunch after; the lunch conversation is where referrals happen.
5. On the day: one printed handout with a QR code to the free edition, no selling from the front (same norm as Rotary).
6. After: thank-you email and one ask: "would you recommend the talk to another Probus club or society?" Then ask to be put forward for the Probus registers once one club has heard it (both registers work on member recommendation).
