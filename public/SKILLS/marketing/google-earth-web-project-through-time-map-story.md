# Google Earth web project: a "through time" map story with placemarks, slides and historical imagery (free, proposal)

Researched 31 Oct 2026. New ground: before this file the index and skill files had 0 hits for "google earth", "arcgis" and "esri". It sits beside tithe-map-storymap-georeferenced-parcels-linkable-asset.md (our own self-hosted StoryMapJS on the site) and historypin-tour-archive-pins.md (third-party pins). This one lives inside Google Earth itself, where people already go to look at a place.

## What the tool does (Google's own pages, read)
- **Projects in Google Earth for web** (Earth Outreach tutorial "Create a map or story in Google Earth Web"): a project holds placemarks, lines and shapes; each placemark can carry photos, video and text, a styled pin and a saved 3D view. **Fullscreen slides** can introduce the story, split it into chapters and close it with credits. **Present** mode flies the viewer from place to place in order.
- **Sharing:** every project is private by default. "Get shareable link" makes it viewable by anyone with the link (view-only unless you choose edit). Projects can also be shared with named Google accounts as viewers or editors. Projects are stored in Google Drive and open from a shared link.
- **Mobile:** projects open in the Google Earth app (menu > Projects) but cannot be edited on mobile.
- **KML import** is local-browser only: an imported KML cannot be shared or synced. Build the shared version natively in the project, not from an import.
- **Historical imagery** reached Earth on web and mobile in 2024 (Google's "20 years of Google Earth" post), so a viewer can scrub back through older aerial and satellite images of a place without installing Earth Pro.
- Scale (same Google post): people searched for places in Google Earth more than 2 billion times in the past year.

## Evidence on results (honest)
- No practitioner case study with sales or traffic numbers from an Earth project was found in this run. Google's tutorial and the Core Education guide (core-ed.org) show it as a teaching and storytelling tool; the MDPI review (Luo et al. 2018, cited ~150 times) shows its use in archaeology and heritage research, not marketing.
- Earth projects show no view counts to the owner (not checked in a live account). Measure by tagged links only.
- So treat it as a **linkable asset and a credibility piece**, not a sales channel. Expected effect: small; effort: about 2 hours.

## How to do it (steps)
1. Sign in at earth.google.com with the project's Google account. Projects > New project > "Create in Google Drive".
2. Opening slide: title, one-line premise, one period image we have the rights to (see archive-image-licensing-fees-welsh-sources-ebook.md).
3. 6-10 placemarks in date order, one per moment. Each: a short neutral caption (2-3 sentences), the catalogue ID, one image, a saved 3D view. Use the historical-imagery view where the change over time is the point.
4. One chapter slide per era. Closing slide: "The full record, sources and e-books: cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate", typed as text and, if the info box takes a link (not checked), one tagged link through our own redirect, e.g. `/r/google-earth`.
5. Present it start to finish on a phone in the Earth app before sharing.
6. Share > Get shareable link (view only). Put that link in our social bios rotation, one post per channel, and the newsletter footer for one issue.

## Rules that apply
- Public text never names the owner; banned public words apply; no insignia; nothing about family holdings or addresses: place placemarks only on public sites, records and other parties' land as shown in the catalogue. Llandough Farm is not Great House Farm.
- The Cedwyn site itself keeps its no-outbound-links rule; the Earth project links IN to the site, not the other way round.
- Images: only ones we hold rights to or that are open-licence with credit.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Status: PROPOSAL.** It needs a Google account to own the project. The connected Google account is the owner's personal one and no Google login is saved in the browser, so the build waits for the owner's GO on which account owns it.
- Content: "Great House Farm through time, 1877-1994": the title-chain moments already checked against the catalogue for the timeline graph on /research/timeline/ (reuse its dates and catalogue IDs; label inference as "our inference").
- Measure: clicks on `/r/google-earth` in Cloudflare analytics, weekly in the growth report. **Kill rule:** under 10 clicks in 8 weeks = stop promoting it; leave it up.
- Zero spend; nothing to install (a browser tool).
