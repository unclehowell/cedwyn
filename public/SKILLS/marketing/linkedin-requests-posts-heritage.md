# LinkedIn for a heritage archive: connection requests, posts, and the link question (2026)
Harvested 2026-10-26. LinkedIn can't be automated from this box (see the project workspace), so this is a playbook for **the project lead's own profile**, plus post texts Tab can prepare.

## What the data states
- **Connection notes lower acceptance for cold requests.** ReactIn (80K+ requests, 2025-26): no note 55-68% accepted; generic note 28-45%; hyper-personalised note that names *real shared context* 45-60%. Across their campaigns a note cut acceptance by about 25% on average. Botdog's dataset agrees: requests without notes (66% of sends) had higher overall acceptance.
- Contrast: Richard van der Blom (LinkedIn algorithm researcher) reports 46% acceptance for personalised requests, and states personal brand (a profile that looks credible) is the real multiplier. **Read:** a note helps only when it names something specific and true, such as their society, their article or a shared group. Otherwise send none.
- Healthy acceptance: 30-45% (Leadriver 2026). Under 20% means poor targeting or a weak profile. 88% of acceptances happen within 7 days.
- **Weekly invite cap:** about 100 a week is the widely observed ceiling (LinkedIn Help: hitting it restricts invites, usually for a week, and withdrawing pending invites does not lift it). Plan about 15 a day on weekdays.
- **Posts:** document/PDF carousels are LinkedIn's strongest engagement format. Buffer 2026: median engagement 21.77%, about 3x video or images (LinkedIn counts clicks in engagement). The algorithm weighs dwell time and saves. Hootsuite 2026, citing a 3M-post analysis: a save gives about 5x the reach of a like and 2x a comment.
- **External links: contested.** One reading of van der Blom's 2025 data is a ~30% penalty for a link in the post body (and for the "link in first comment" workaround). A March 2026 cross-read of six reports finds only a modest 5% *gain* for posts with 1-3 links. Treat it as unknown and test it on our own posts (below).

## The playbook
1. **Profile first** (it multiplies everything): a headline that names the work, e.g. "Researching a Vale of Glamorgan farm's 200-year paper trail · Cedwyn archive". Featured section: one deep Cedwyn page plus the free first chapter. Banner: the 1824 plan crop.
2. **Connection requests (about 15 a weekday, max 100 a week):**
   - Cold, with no shared context: **no note.**
   - Shared context (same society, they wrote a relevant article or post, met at an event): a note under 200 characters that names it, with no pitch and no link.
3. **After they accept (within 7 days):** one short message, 2-3 lines. Use one evidenced fact relevant to *their* interest, then one deep link to the matching page, with UTM tags (utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=dm-<week>&utm_content=<page>). No second message unless they reply.
4. **Posts (2 a week):**
   - Tuesday: a 6-8 slide PDF carousel (one record per slide).
   - Thursday: a text post of about 150-250 words telling one story from the records, ending in a question.
   - Reply to every comment within the first hours; comments that turn into conversations extend reach.
5. **Link test:** alternate 4 posts with the link in the body and 4 with no link ("link in Featured / comment"). Compare impressions per follower. Decide from our numbers, not the blogs.
6. **Log** each request and message in HubSpot (contact note: "LinkedIn request sent <date>, note y/n, accepted <date>, DM sent, clicked"), so nobody is messaged twice. That matches the email rule of no repeat within 14 days.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Update the project workspace:** the short connection notes there should be used *only* where the note names real shared context. For cold names on the project lead's list, he sends a blank request and then the prepared 2-3 line follow-up after acceptance. (Tab prepares texts; the project lead sends from his own profile, since the box is blocked.)
- Carousel PDFs: reuse the weekly quote-pack slides, exported as one PDF at 1080x1350. Same evidence, zero extra work.
- The do-not-contact rules carry over: nothing to case bodies on the project workspace; never name the project lead in public Cedwyn text (his own LinkedIn posts are his voice and his choice).
- Banned public words apply to the prepared texts: states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken.
