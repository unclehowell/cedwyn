Menter Bro Morgannwg (Vale of Glamorgan Welsh-language initiative): talks, learner sessions, Gŵyl Fach y Fro and the directory

Harvested 2026-10-30 (skills harvest run). Topic: community route into Welsh speakers and learners in the Vale, the county Great House Farm sits in. It is the Vale counterpart of the Cardiff skill (cardiff-welsh-community-menter-caerdydd-echlysur-tafwyl.md). Both Mentrau share a chief executive and run some events jointly.

What it is (read 30 Oct 2026 unless marked)

  • Menter Iaith Bro Morgannwg is a charity (1179555, company 08867706) that promotes the social use of Welsh across the Vale (Barry, Penarth, Cowbridge, Llantwit Major, Dinas Powys). Its office is Yr Hen Lyfrgell, Cardiff, shared with Menter Caerdydd. Channels: menterbromorgannwg.cymru, Facebook /MIBroMorgannwg, Instagram, X.
  • Its activities page (read) lists, for adults:
  • Sgwrs y Mis: a free monthly Welsh-language lecture, Zoom, run jointly with Menter Caerdydd. The November 2026 speaker is Dr Sam Parry. The page notes it suits "fluent speakers and higher level learners but everyone is welcome". You can register for the whole year with an optional donation, or per session when each month's notice goes up.
  • Bore Coffi ar Zoom: a free monthly coffee morning for learners.
  • Sesiynau sgwrsio: chat sessions for new speakers, run by Learn Welsh the Vale.
  • Paid courses: e.g. a Welsh poetry course on Zoom (£25), plus gigs (£4–£18) and a free guided Welsh-language exhibition visit at National Museum Cardiff.
  • The Facebook page also promotes partners' learner events, e.g. "Join Bethan Gwanas at Barry Library's Welsh Learners' Coffi a Chlonc" (search snippet). Library learner sessions are a recurring partner slot.
  • Directory (read): free listings in these categories: Businesses (2), Primary (7), Secondary (1), Nurseries (7), Choirs (5), Religion (2), Welsh for Adults (2) and Welsh Societies (3). The societies are Cymdeithas Gymraeg Dinas Powys, Cymrodorion y Barri and AFAITH. The Businesses category is tiny (a will-writer and a financial adviser), so a Welsh-language heritage publisher would stand out. How a business gets listed is not checked (ask in the first contact).
  • Newsletter: seasonal PDF (Autumn 2026 posted).
  • Press releases page: the Menter publishes partner announcements, e.g. a 2026 Lleuwen tour and Welsh Language Music Day.

Practitioner numbers: Gŵyl Fach y Fro 2026 evaluation (Menter's own report, 17 May 2026, read in full)

  • Gŵyl Fach y Fro is a free Welsh-language festival on Barry Island. About 9,000 attended in 2025. In 2026 the festival day (Sat 16 May) was down because of weather and a clashing Cardiff gig, but pairing with the Run 4 Wales Barry Island 10K the next day reached "more than 10,000 people" over the two days.
  • The festival had 20 craft, food and drink stalls. Stallholders included Cant a Mil, the Urdd, Dysgu Cymraeg y Fro, Mudiad Meithrin and independent makers. The stall application deadline was announced on 9 and 13 March, about 9 weeks before the event.
  • New in 2026 was Hafan Iolo, an indoor tent for talks, workshops and Q&A: a Book Workshop (Rolant Tomos), a learners' panel (Helen Prosser) and a live podcast recording (Pryd ar Dafod). 25% of the audience sampled it, and 56.3% of those rated it good or excellent. The report also notes that a few non-Welsh speakers "did not fully understand the concept".
  • On social media, Facebook and Instagram were run to a two-month calendar: headline acts at 2 months out, then countdown posts at 4 weeks and 2 weeks, then one post per act per day in the final fortnight, with a dedicated social person on the weekend. Meta insight metrics rose +331% (Facebook) and +720% (Instagram) from early April to the festival.
  • How people heard about the festival: 43.8% by word of mouth, despite coverage on S4C Heno, Radio Cymru and Radio Wales and live broadcast on Bro Radio. 87.5% were repeat visitors, and 62.5% were aged 26–40. The report's own lesson: "the school network and other community circles should not be underestimated."

The doors for us (free unless marked)

  1. Sgwrs y Mis talk slot (Zoom, Welsh): pitch a 2027 talk on one Vale farm's documents, framed as local history with no case argument. The 2026 programme is set, so aim for the 2027 timetable (exact deadline not checked; ask in Q1).
  2. Hafan Iolo at Gŵyl Fach y Fro 2027 (May, Barry Island): offer a 20-minute Welsh and English talk or a book workshop. Two cautions from the report: - make it bilingual, because the report flags confused non-Welsh speakers; - a stall may cost money (not checked), so under zero spend ask for a free talk slot, not a stall.
  3. Directory listing under Businesses (or a heritage category if they add one), linking to /ebooks/.
  4. Learner sessions (Bore Coffi, sesiynau sgwrsio, library Coffi a Chlonc): offer a free graded reading sheet, a 15-word geirfa page drawn from the farm records. Pairs with heritage-learner-packs-cadw-deg-diwrnod-diddorol-learn-welsh-library.md.
  5. Welsh societies (Cymrodorion y Barri, Cymdeithas Gymraeg Dinas Powys): approach through the Menter's directory, as speaker offers. Use role addresses only; keep secretaries' personal phones and emails out of files.

Rules

  • Zero spend: no paid stalls, ads or tickets; free talk offers only.
  • One institutional contact per Menter and one HubSpot draft; nothing is sent without the owner's GO. No mass emails to schools or nurseries. Never use the children's or schools listings for outreach.
  • Public text is in neutral third person, never names the owner, and uses none of the banned words. Never write "tenant farmers". No family names of living people.
