History magazine and journal articles as a traffic and authority route

Harvested 2026-10-27 for Cedwyn. Tied to the 13:32 finding that reach is the main reason for 0 e-book sales (~32 non-US visits/day to /ebooks/). A published article in a history title puts the archive in front of exactly the readers who buy local-history e-books, and most titles print a short author line naming the website.

What the sources show (27 Oct 2026)

  • History Today (historytoday.com, snippet; page blocked from here): accepts article proposals by brief email to [address kept in the project workspace] with "a few lines about your career"; work must be original, exclusive to History Today and authoritative. Check the full page in a browser before any pitch (one exclusion line was cut off in the snippet).
  • The Historian, Historical Association journal (history.org.uk, read): quarterly; written by academics, public-history contributors, heritage organisations and members; editorial board wants a SYNOPSIS first, shared with the board before they see a full piece; NO payment. Sections: Feature 3,000 words (up to 8 images, original research), Doing History 1,500 words (local or family history, method + result, up to 5 images), History Out and About 1,500 words (a location or heritage site, up to 5 images), Real Lives 1,000-1,500 words (an unfamiliar historical figure). Email [address kept in the project workspace]
  • Writer's Digest freelancer roundup (read): UK paying titles include History of War (pitch only, no on-spec; Frontline 800-900 words, quoted rate as printed there) and Britain at War. Military titles only fit if a strand is genuinely military.
  • Family-history titles: Family Tree (US) states allow 6-8 weeks for a response (snippet). UK titles (Who Do You Think You Are? Magazine, Family Tree UK) have no public submission page found in this run - not checked; find the editor via the masthead before drafting.
  • r/freelanceWriters thread (snippet): newcomers report better odds with a specific angle plus prior clips than with a CV.

How to pitch (practitioner pattern)

  1. Read 3 recent issues; match a named section and its word count.
  2. One email, 150-250 words: working title, the specific new thing (a primary source the reader has not seen), why now, section + length, image list with rights status, 2-line author bio, one link.
  3. Exclusive means exclusive: one title at a time; wait the stated period (6-8 weeks if none stated) before moving on.
  4. Images: only ones we hold the rights to or that are public domain/OGL, captioned with catalogue IDs.
  5. Ask for the author line to carry the website; do not ask for links in body text.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Best fits: The Historian "History Out and About" (the farm site and parish, 1,500 words) or "Doing History" (how the evidence catalogue was built from tithe maps, newspapers and FOI replies). Unpaid, but the readership (HA members, teachers) matches the academy and e-books.
  • Second: a History Today proposal on the wider story (manorial house to modern dispute) only once an editor-ready synopsis exists.
  • Drafts only, as HubSpot tasks for the project lead to review and send himself; press and media pitches go only on the project lead's GO. Never name the project lead or private residents in the piece; banned public words apply; no accusations about named people; nothing about family holdings or addresses.
  • Measure: give each published piece its own landing link (e.g. /ebooks/?utm_source=historian&utm_medium=print) and log visits for 30 days in the project workspace
