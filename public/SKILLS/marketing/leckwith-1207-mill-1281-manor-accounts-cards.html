Leckwith in the records: the king's mill (1207) and the manor accounts (1281), card pack

Written 30 Oct 2026 by the skills harvest. Both texts are printed in Clark's Cartae (2nd ed. 1910) and were read from the archive.org full text with skills/tools/cartae_find.py. Leckwith is a Cedwyn parish, and these are its earliest working-economy records in print.

The two records

  1. Cartae no. CCCIII, vol. 2 p.306 (1207). Source: Rotuli de Finibus, p.39. Walter de Sully gives 20 marks for King John to quit him of "the deterioration and ruin of the lord king's mill of Lequid" (Leckwith), and of the king's lost rent and multure, from while the mill was in Walter's custody. Walter is to repair the damage, and he is not to be forced to take the mill at farm again. Letters patent were issued. - Card line: "In 1207 the king's mill at Leckwith was ruined, and its keeper paid 20 marks to settle it."
  2. Cartae no. DCCXLIV, vol. 3 pp.820-822. Accounts of the manor of Leckwith, 20 April to 29 December, dated 1281 by Clark. - Rents of free and customary tenants are paid at Midsummer and Michaelmas. - Grass and pasture sold in gross "and no more for lack of a buyer because of the war" ("pro defectu emptoris causa guerre"). The court profits carry the same phrase. - A fishery "in aqua de Ely" (the River Ely) was let at farm for 3s. - The tenants' customary works were sold: weeding, mowing and reaping, priced per work. - Underwood was cut in Leckwith wood to make a new fold for the beasts of those buying the lord's pasture. - The reeve of Leckwith, also the harvest-reeve, was paid for 60 days from 1 August. - The money totals in the OCR (total receipts ~£11 9s 7½d; rents ~41s 3d; expenses ~10s 1½d) are rough. Mark them "not checked" until read off the page image. - Card lines: "1281: Leckwith's grass went unsold 'because of the war'." / "The River Ely fishery at Leckwith was let for three shillings in 1281."

Dating caution

Clark heads the account "1281". The "war" is most likely the Welsh war of 1282-83, which would mean the regnal year was converted wrongly. Don't name the war on a card. Write "an account Clark dates to 1281 notes the war" until the original roll is checked. Clark's source bracket reads only "De eodem". It belongs to the same run as DCCXLIII, "Accounts of the same John Giffard" (Kaerdif town and castle, 1281). Trace the run's first bracket back to find the original roll before citing it.
