## How a nonfiction proposal is built (Jane Friedman, janefriedman.com)
1. **Overview** first in the document, written last: 2-3 pages, a "water-tight business case"; becomes the query letter.
2. **Comparable titles** (5-10): "People who've read X will want this because..." Never claim there are no comps.
3. **Target audience**, defined by real groups and numbers.
4. **Marketing plan**: only what you *will* do, with numbers; "never discuss what you hope to do". Weak: "I plan to...". Strong: "Our list of N subscribers; the site has N visitors/month; talks booked at...".
5. **Author bio**: why you, credentials, platform.
6. **Chapter outline** + **sample chapter(s)**. Typical length 10-25 pages double-spaced excluding samples.
- For nonfiction, "the marketability of the premise, topic, or author" often matters more than the prose.

## Rules for us
- Respect each publisher's channel: Graffeg = form; Seren = wait for a call-out (do not email now); Carreg Gwalch = short enquiry first.
- One publisher at a time where they forbid simultaneous submissions (Seren). Graffeg sets no such rule; track the 12-week clock.
- AI disclosure: Seren bars AI-generated work. Our drafts are machine-assisted; a submission to Seren must be the project lead's own writing, or not go. Say so honestly if asked.
- Sender details: a real name, contact email and postal town/postcode is normal courtesy. **Not checked**: a legal duty for an individual's one-to-one pitch email to carry a postal address (Companies Act letterhead rules and the E-Commerce Regulations bind companies / online services, not a private author's letter). Don't tell the project lead the law requires it without a source.
