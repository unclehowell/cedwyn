# Skill: "What the next sales pay for" — a costed, true research meter (sales fund the archive work)

## What the evidence shows
- **Goal proximity pulls support, reaching the goal stalls it.** Kuppuswamy & Bayus (Kickstarter data; J. Business Venturing 2017 "Does my contribution to your crowdfunding project matter?" and the 2013 Berkeley working paper): "Crowdfunding support increases as a project approaches its target goal" and "decreases once the target goal is reached"; backers are "less likely to support a project after it reaches its funding goal". (Abstract/highlight lines from search results; full papers not opened: ScienceDirect blocked the box.)
- **Counter-evidence: a standing generic goal does little.** Patreon removed its Goals feature from May 2023: "Starting in May 2023, we're saying goodbye to our Goals feature ... Goals were originally intended to help creators promote and grow their pages, but ..." (Patreon's notice as quoted by comicschau.de; the rest of the sentence not read). Creator threads paraphrase Patreon's reason as its data showing goals did not help creators do better (r/patreon, snippet only, not checked).
  - Our read: a vague, permanent "help us reach £X/month" bar is decoration; a **small, specific, finishable** target that ends in a visible result is what the goal-proximity effect needs.
- **Real costs we can quote (Glamorgan Archives, read 31 Oct 2026):**
  - Copying (glamarchives.gov.uk): single image incl. printout **£15.60**; further images from the same document **£4.80** each; multiple images from multiple documents **£60/hour**; UK postage £1.80; order fulfilled within 15 working days.
  - Licence to reuse an image on a **commercial website: £78 per image per year** (not-for-profit £1.80). Printed/digital media under 1,000 run: £30 UK / £57.60 worldwide per image.
  - Paid research (the-shop/research/): **£120/hour incl. VAT for commercial firms** (£60 for others), half-hour search £30; payment with the request.
- **UK honesty rules already in our files apply:** a "your purchase funds X" claim must be true and specific (DMCC Act unfair practices; see uk-dmcc-act-urgency-reviews-price-copy.md and honest-launch-deadlines-uk-dmcc-banned-practice-7.md). No fake deadlines or invented remaining amounts.

## The playbook
1. **Pick one finishable item, costed from the price list,** not a monthly target. Example shape: "Next: an archive copy of item REF — £15.60 image + £78 web licence = £93.60."
2. **Count in sales, not pounds,** if the e-book price is fixed: "7 more e-book sales" reads closer to done than "£63.84 to go" (net per sale after fees, computed from Stripe/Gumroad receipts, not guessed).
3. **Start it part-filled only with real money** (sales since a dated start). Never seed the bar with a number that did not happen.
4. **Close the loop in public:** when the target is met, buy the item, publish the result (or say it was refused/unavailable), and only then show the next target. The finished result is the post that sells the next round.
5. **One meter at a time.** Several half-full bars dilute the proximity effect and look like a donation drive.
6. **Ring-fence:** log each funded purchase (date, item, receipt) in a private ledger so the public line stays true.
7. **No meter on the reading pages.** It belongs on /ebooks/ and the thank-you page, beside the buy button, not in the evidence text.
