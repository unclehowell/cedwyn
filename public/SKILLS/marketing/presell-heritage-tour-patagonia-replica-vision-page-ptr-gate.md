# Pre-selling a heritage tour + Patagonia replica holiday from a vision page, before anything exists (UK law gate + interest-first funnel)

Written 2026-10-31 by the skills harvest. Sources read tonight unless marked "snippet".

## The legal gate (UK Package Travel Regulations 2018, amended 2026; GOV.UK business guidance, Oct 2026, read)
- **What is a package:** "generally the combination of two or more different types of travel services". The four types are carriage of passengers, accommodation, motor vehicle hire, and "any other tourist service" (guided tours are named as an example).
- **The Llandough guided walk on its own is ONE service (an other tourist service), so it is not a package.** Selling it, with or without a pre-sale, is ordinary consumer selling (pilot-walk-presale-minimum-numbers-hold-then-charge.md covers that).
- **The Argentina "package holiday" (stay + transfers + tours, with or without flights) IS a package.** A tour combined with only one of transport/accommodation/car hire makes a package when it is "advertised as an essential feature" or worth about **25% or more** of the price. For a replica-farm stay sold for the farm, the tour is the essential feature.
- **Insolvency protection:** it must cover "any payments made for packages". Flight packages need **ATOL**. Non-flight packages use one of three routes:
  - bonding through a government-approved body;
  - insurance;
  - a **trust account** where "all money paid by the traveller [is] held by an independent trustee until the contract has been performed".
- **Selling to UK buyers from abroad doesn't avoid this:** traders outside the UK "directing such activities to the UK, must comply with the UK insolvency regime" (reg 19(4)). A UK retailer selling a package put together abroad takes on its performance and insolvency cover (reg 27).
- **Penalties:** criminal offences for missing pre-contract information (reg 5), a missing contract or confirmation (reg 7), and no insolvency cover (reg 19(9)). Enforced by Trading Standards; fines are unlimited in England and Wales.
- **Exemptions that don't fit us:** trips under 24 hours with no overnight stay, and "occasional, not-for-profit, limited group" trips. Ours is commercial.
- **Bottom line:** taking ANY money (deposit, "founder price", voucher) for the Argentina holiday before a licensed, protected organiser exists is the regulated act. Interest can be collected; money for the package cannot.

## The playbook: interest first, money only through a protected seller
1. **Vision page (no price, no checkout):**
   - "Planned: a faithful replica of a Welsh farmhouse in Patagonia". Show the replica plans, 3D model and measurement register as proof of seriousness (sketchfab-3d-virtual-tour-replica-views.md; the /argentina/replica/ build), and pair it with the Llandough walk.
   - The verbs are "planned", "being designed" and "join the founding travellers list". Never "book", "reserve", "secure your place" or a date.
2. **Founding travellers register (free, no deposit):**
   - Fields: email, country (UK / Argentina / other), "which would you come for?" (Llandough walk / Patagonia replica stay / both), preferred season, group size. Single opt-in to the existing Mailchimp list with a tag. Waitlist mechanics are in coming-soon-notify-me-waitlist-referral.md.
   - **This is demand evidence for partners and grant bodies.** Count it weekly. Treat it as soft: vendor and founder write-ups (getwaitlist.com blog, read, anecdotal) show waitlist-to-buyer rates anywhere from 1–3% to 25%+. Don't plan on a number until a real offer has been tested.
3. **Sell the part that's legal now:** the Llandough walk (a single service). Dated pilot walks with a minimum number, charged only once the minimum is reached (pilot-walk file). Each walker then gets a "you're on the Patagonia founding list" tag. Walkers are the warmest Patagonia leads.
4. **When the Argentina side is real, sell through a protected organiser rather than becoming one:**
   - A UK ATOL/ABTA operator or retailer already selling Welsh Patagonia carries the protection; we supply the audience and the replica as the essential feature.
   - Snippet, not opened: a Porthcawl (Bridgend) travel agent advertises a Welsh Patagonia trip at "£4,849 per person". That's the price band and the kind of partner a local agent offers.
   - An Argentine receptive operator selling direct to UK buyers must still meet the UK regime (reg 19(4)).
5. **Benchmarks to set expectations:**
   - Tour-operator sites convert 1–3% of visitors (tourpreneur.com, snippet).
   - A guided "best-fit tour" chooser booked at 5.05% (about 4.1% implied before) (+24% trips) and revenue per visitor from $40.45 to $44 (pathmonk.com, snippet, vendor).
   - On a vision page, measure register sign-ups per visitor instead. Above 5% of visitors means the vision is landing; under 1% after 500 visits means rewrite the hero.
6. **Language and honesty:**
   - No price or date until an organiser has signed.
   - Say plainly: "No bookings are taken yet. Joining the list costs nothing and commits you to nothing."
   - Never describe the replica as built.
