## Why this audience
BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2 is taught in UK land law under adverse possession (the "unilateral licence" point). Read tonight:
- lawexplores.com "Adverse possession" (read): a student-revision page that files the case under "possession with permission cannot be adverse", with a facts paragraph and "Use this case to support your argument that...". That is the format students revise from.
- Quizlet GB flashcard set "LAND: ADVERSE POSSESSION" carries the case as a card (search snippet, not opened).
- OUP Land Law Directions 8e online resources has a chapter-6 "Advanced topic: implied licences and human rights" page on exactly this point (page fetched; the case name is in the JS-rendered text, not checked in the HTML).
- CaseMine commentary "Termination of adverse possession through unilateral licences: insights from BP Properties Ltd v Buckler" (snippet).
So the demand already exists as a search for the case name every exam season; nobody serves the primary records behind it.

## The routine
1. Search Console, monthly: filter queries containing "buckler", "bp properties", "unilateral licence", "implied licence adverse possession". Record impressions/position in growth-report notes. Those queries are the audience size; no impressions = do nothing more yet.
2. One student-facing page on Cedwyn (markup and structure by the onpage flywheel; body text PROPOSED for review): "The records behind a land-law case" — the court's own words quoted as the court's view, then the primary documents in date order, each with a source line. Answer the questions a revising student asks (what was the licence, when was it given, why did time stop running, what did later cases say) in short headed sections. Neutral wording only.
3. Timing (not checked against a timetable; typical UK pattern): adverse possession is commonly taught in the autumn/spring terms; revision peaks March-May before May/June exams. Publish and refresh before January; push the student page in March.
4. Format students buy: a short PDF "case file" (10-20 pages: timeline, documents, court's reasoning, later cases), priced as an entry offer (low-price-entry-offer-tiny-offer-first-buyer.md), plus a free 2-page timeline as the sample.
5. Reach without DMs: (a) a LinkedIn document post (linkedin-2026-format-benchmarks-document-vs-image-vs-video.md) aimed at land-law tutors; (b) law-school library / land-law module leaders as INSTITUTIONS via hive drafts only, offering the free timeline as a teaching resource (never Cardiff or Bangor universities — on do-not-contact; never private individuals); (c) Reddit only under reddit-forum-participation-heritage.md rules.
6. Measure: visits to the student page, free timeline downloads, case-file sales; drop the channel after 2 terms with zero.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- The case page must follow every editorial rule: court's "owner" wording shown as the court's view, BP = registered proprietor (1982/83), never "owner" in our voice; banned words (states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken) — write "not checked"; never name the project lead; no family holdings or addresses; living people unnamed except where the editorial note allows; provenance = source only.
- No promise that the records change the law: present what the documents show and what the court decided, side by side. Students and tutors punish overclaiming faster than anyone.
- Stuvia listing is a PROPOSAL for the programme-office queue (needs the project lead's account, payout details and a check of its terms on publishers); Oxbridge Notes is ruled out.
- The free timeline doubles as a LinkedIn document and a Tes/Hwb resource (school-resource-marketplaces-tes-hwb.md covers schools; this skill covers universities).
