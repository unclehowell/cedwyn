Skill #1 — Email, CRM & follow-ups for a niche heritage e-book business (2026)

Written 26 Oct 2026 from practitioner testimony; refresh when evidence changes.

What practitioners report works (sources)

  • The welcome flow is the highest-performing automation: Omnisend May 2026 benchmarks put automated welcome emails at ~35.5% open, 3.9% click, 2.1% conversion; a 3-email series drives ~90% more orders than a single email; the first 48 h after signup are the highest-engagement window and set sender reputation. (digitalapplied.com, "The Welcome Email Sequence: a 2026 onboarding framework", 4 Jun 2026)
  • Consistency beats optimisation for small lists: one newsletter a week, same time, short and conversational; growth came from content found elsewhere (LinkedIn posts, Substack Notes) pointing back to the list. (Greg, The Introverted Networker, 2,500 subs without ads, 30 May 2025)
  • Zero-to-4,000 subscribers in 12 months with no prior platform came from steady, high-quality posting plus cross-promotion; no shortcut. (Jeannine Ouellette, Writing in the Dark, Dec 2023)
  • Aggressive list-building (frequent sends, swaps, big freebies) brings unsubscribes; a relevant reader magnet + a gentle cadence keeps the list warm. (r/selfpublish, "I tried aggressively growing my author newsletter for 30 days")

How-to (Cedwyn)

  1. Every contact in HubSpot carries a source (outreach/heritage-2026, signup, buyer, prospect) and lifecycle stage; institutions are companies, people are contacts. Never import outreach targets into the reader list — only opt-ins join it.
  2. Welcome sequence (day 0 / 3 / 7): deliver the free first edition → one striking record + a specific Cedwyn page → soft mention of the £9.99 latest edition / bundle. One link per email, UTM-tagged.
  3. Weekly reader email, max once a week, same day/time: story first, one e-book featured, never a book the reader already owns (check the buyers ledger).
  4. Follow-ups: reply to every reply within a day; positive reply → thank, offer the free edition, ask them to share with their members/followers.
  5. Log every touch as a HubSpot note; opt-outs go on the do-not-contact list at once.
  6. Measure: signups/day, welcome open & click, reads (soft conversion), sales (strong conversion), replies.

Double opt-in leak: confirm mail and thank-you page for a lead magnet (added 27 Oct 2026, BIZDEV run 2)

Evidence (27 Oct): D1 cl_subscribers shows 2 of 2 /ebooks/ sample-chapter sign-ups on 26 Oct never confirmed (confirm mail accepted by Resend; 1 re-send). Practitioner/vendor figures: the confirm step typically loses 20-30% of sign-ups; a subject that names the thing asked for ("confirm to claim ...") lifts confirmation 10-15%; one reminder 24-48 h later recovers 10-15% of the unconfirmed (sequenzy.com double-opt-in templates, 2026 - a vendor blog, figures not independently checked). Marketers in r/marketing (EU, 2026) report the same leak on lead-magnet double opt-in. How-to: 1. Subject and button name the lead magnet ("Your free Cedwyn chapter: one click to open it" / "Confirm and open my chapter"), not "confirm your subscription". 2. First line restates what they asked for, not "someone, hopefully you". 3. The thank-you page delivers the thing at once (button straight to the chapter), then ONE next step (the free first edition) and ONE record page. No shop pitch on that page. 4. One reminder only, 24 h later, then stop (we already re-send once). Never add unconfirmed addresses to HubSpot or the list. Applied: patch the project workspace (cedwyn-mail Worker, topic-aware confirm mail en/cy/es + chapter thank-you). Measure: confirmed / sign-ups for sample-chapter topic, weekly.

Refresh 29 Oct 2026 (BIZDEV run 5): newsletter growth testimony with numbers (#1 26)

  • beehiiv "State of Newsletters 2026" (5 Jan 2026, platform data): 28 bn emails sent in 2025 to 255 M unique readers; opens 41%+; newsletters launched in 2025 reached a first dollar in a median 66 days. Read: beehiiv.com
  • A local US publisher (Colorado group, case study by David Arkin Consulting / Penny Riordan): +25% subscribers and +50% opens after (1) sending only to readers engaged in the last 3-6 months, (2) subject lines under 40 characters, specific, curious but not misleading, (3) a signup invite everywhere: inline in articles, QR codes in print, events, email signatures, (4) the ESP's own recommendation feature. Read: davidarkinconsulting.com
  • Jenny Ouyang (Build to Launch, 31 Dec 2025): 0 to 4,500 subscribers in 12 months from zero online presence; growth came from showing up steadily and commenting in overlapping communities; virality was random and not repeatable. Read: buildtolaunch.substack.com

Apply to Cedwyn (list = 1 confirmed subscriber on 29 Oct)

  1. The signup must sit inline on the pages people actually land on (/research/*, /wales/, /documentary/), not only /ebooks/: patch #6 12 is the vehicle.
  2. Every printed or spoken asset (talks circuit, open-day leaflets, QR on cards) carries a QR to the signup page with utm_medium=print.
  3. Weekly email subject: under 40 characters, one specific record fact ("The 1974 open day that saved a farmhouse").
  4. Expect months, not days: judge the list by confirmed sign-ups per 100 /ebooks/ visitors, read weekly.
