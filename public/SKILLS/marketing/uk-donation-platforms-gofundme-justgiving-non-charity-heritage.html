UK donation platforms for a non-charity heritage project: GoFundMe vs JustGiving Crowdfunding (fees, payout, fit)

Facts (read on the platforms' own pages, 1 Nov 2026)

  • GoFundMe UK (gofundme.com, read):
  • "No fee to start or manage" and a £0.00 platform fee.
  • One transaction fee, 1.9% + £0.20 per donation (GBP, VAT included).
  • Donors can tip GoFundMe at checkout, which is optional and "never required".
  • Open to "individual or business" fundraisers as well as charities.
  • International and conversion fees may apply.
  • JustGiving Crowdfunding (justgiving.com, read):
  • Free to create, "there is no platform fee".
  • Processing costs 2.9% + 35p per donation.
  • Payout: the earliest withdrawal is 14 days after the first donation, then 4–6 or 4–7 working days to arrive (help-centre snippets).
  • JustGiving dropped its 5% platform fee for charities and now asks donors for a voluntary contribution (JustGiving blog snippet).
  • Net per donation (our arithmetic):

£10 £9.61 £9.36
£26 (UK median gift, CAF 2025) £25.31 £24.90

CrowdJustice on £26 nets about £24.24, and only for legal action; see legal-case-crowdfunding-crowdjustice-uk-heritage-records.md. The project's own Stripe/PayPal button keeps the most, but it has no platform audience and none of a big platform's trust signal. - Who gives (UK): CAF figures via UK Fundraising, 16 Mar 2026: - 55% of people gave in 2025; - mean monthly gift £65 (down from £72), median £26 (down from £28); - 22% of people who do not give say they cannot afford to. - What the platform's own guidance states moves money (GoFundMe updates guide, read): post updates at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of the goal, show impact ("how they've already made an impact can inspire more to give"), and thank donors when you hit the goal. They publish no lift figure; treat this as practice, not proof. - Not Gift Aid eligible: neither platform can add Gift Aid unless the beneficiary is a registered charity. A non-charity project cannot claim the 25%. This is general HMRC rule knowledge, not re-checked this run.

Choosing

  • Own /support/ page (already designed): the default. Lowest fees, and the money stays on the project's site.
  • GoFundMe UK: use it when the project wants an audience beyond its own site. It is the cheapest of the big UK platforms per gift, keeps what it raises (no all-or-nothing), and is known to a general audience. Its search and discovery surface is the reason to pay the fee at all.
  • JustGiving Crowdfunding: dearer per gift, with a 14-day payout hold. It only makes sense if the donors are already JustGiving users, for example UK charity-run circles. For Cedwyn, GoFundMe wins on fees for the same model.
  • One page only. Never run two platforms for the same ask: it splits the social proof.

How to do it

  1. One concrete, costed goal per page. For example, "copy fees for named record series at Glamorgan Archives and HMLR: £X", backed by the archive's or HMLR's own fee schedule. Never "support our fight".
  2. Seed before sharing. Get 5+ named supporters to give in the first 48 hours. The matched-crowdfunding evidence holds here too: momentum comes from your own connections, and 48% of backers first heard through social media.
  3. Updates at 25/50/75/100%, plus one per record obtained, with what it showed, quoted verbatim.
  4. The donor tip is the platform's own revenue. Leave it at the platform default and never add copy pushing donors either way.
  5. Measure gifts, the median gift, and the share coming from the project's own links (UTM on every outbound share). If there are no gifts outside the warm circle after 30 days, close the page and put the ask back on /support/.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Gate: these are new public channels and fundraising, so they need the owner's GO. Never mention the never-pay-for-disclosure policy on the page.
  • Copy fees paid to an archive for public record copies are ordinary charges. Still, describe them neutrally ("record copy costs") and have the owner confirm the wording.
  • Best candidate ask: a "records fund" for copies and in-person viewings. The owner's 1 Nov message states physical checks and in-person viewings of records come before any certainty, and those cost travel plus copy fees.
  • Each update can publish what a viewed record shows, which feeds the forensic-assessment pages and the e-books.
  • Page copy rules:
  • Never name the owner.
  • Banned words: states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken.
  • No accusations against named people or bodies.
  • Nothing about family holdings or addresses.
  • Next step for a run: draft the GoFundMe page text plus three update templates as a NOT_STARTED HubSpot task "[CEDWYN] Records fund page (GoFundMe UK): owner GO needed". Do not open an account.
