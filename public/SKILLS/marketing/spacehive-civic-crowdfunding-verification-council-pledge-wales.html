Spacehive civic crowdfunding: verified place projects with council match pledges (UK / Wales)

Harvested 2026-11-01 ~02:15Z (skills harvest). Fills the gap left by the earlier runs: Spacehive had no file. The matched-crowdfunding file (Crowdfunder match pots, Nesta "Matching the crowd") and the GoFundMe/JustGiving/CrowdJustice files cover the other platforms.

What it is

Spacehive (UK, founded 2012) only crowdfunds projects that improve a shared or public place: a community space, a trail, interpretation, an event or a building. Before a page goes live it is verified. Councils and funders run "programmes" on it, and those programmes pledge into projects that pass their criteria. That pledge is the reason to use it.

The numbers (with the source and how far each was checked)

  • Fees (CCIN councils' crowdfunding toolkit, read 2026-11-01): free to set up. The platform fee is 7.5% + VAT of the target, added to the target before launch. Card processing through Stripe is about 2.9%, charged separately. There is no automatic tipping. With "Keep What You Raise", the fee applies to whatever was raised. Compare Crowdfunder in the same toolkit: no platform fee for not-for-profits, 1.9% + 20p + VAT per pledge, and an optional tip.
  • Success rate: the toolkit states 85% of projects succeed, and 94% when a local authority partner pledges. Spacehive's help centre (14 Apr 2026, read) states "nearly 9 out of 10" and puts it down to verification. These are the platform's own figures. An older independent figure is lower: Shareable, June 2017 (read) counted 306 projects and £6.7m, an average of £21,895 per project, with only 52% reaching their goal and 99% delivered. Treat 85% as a recent self-report, not an audited number.
  • Scale: the toolkit gives "nearly 3,000 projects", £32m+ raised and 40+ local-government partners. Spacehive's figures for the "+Extra" match funding are not checked.
  • Wales precedent: Spacehive's first project was the Glyncoch community centre (Rhondda Cynon Taf). It raised £700k of a £730k target, then found the last £30k in six weeks: door-to-door small gifts first, then local businesses, then chains and corporates, each shown the backing of the group before it (Cooperative City interview with the founder, 2017, read).
  • A Welsh council programme: Cyllido Torfol Abertawe / Crowdfund Swansea. The programme page (read) is fully bilingual, part-funded through UKSPF, and runs rounds with a free workshop and a deadline by which a project must be launched with its permissions secured. From the council's post (snippet, not checked): each fund pledges up to £5,000 or 60% of the project total, and an eligible project can apply to both funds. Enfield's equivalent is up to £5,000 or 70% (snippet).
  • Heritage example: Preserving Preston's Heritage raised £54,036 for a heritage centre, including the first six days live (their Facebook group post, snippet, not checked).

How verification works (help centre, read)

  1. Permissions: written agreement from the landowner (or a lease or licence), and the council's consent if it is public land. Building work needs planning permission, building regs, and listed building consent for historic buildings. Events need a TEN (Temporary Event Notice), insurance, and PRS/PPL licences for any music.
  2. Cost evidence: every cost line over £500 needs a supplier quote, invoice, or a dated screenshot of an online price. Your own spreadsheet is not accepted as evidence.
  3. Delivery plan and timeline, funds already raised, insurance and policies, and safeguarding/DBS if children or vulnerable adults are involved.
  4. Only after verification does the page go live. A programme pledge must be pitched for before the programme's round deadline.

Playbook

  1. Pick a place project, not a cause. Spacehive will not host a legal fund or a general donations pot. Good fits: an interpretation panel, a heritage walk with printed trail leaflets, a QR plaque series, a free public talk or event, or restoring a public feature.
  2. Get permission first. Most delay is landowner consent. Get it in writing before you build the page.
  3. Collect quotes for every line over £500 (panel fabrication, print, installation, insurance). The 7.5% + VAT is added to the target automatically.
  4. Find the programme. Search Spacehive's Programmes list for your council area. Join the round workshop and note the deadline. If your area has no programme, national funds can still match through "+Extra" (not checked).
  5. Run the campaign in the Glyncoch order: about 30% from people who already care (small pledges), then local businesses, then larger funders, and announce each step. A council pledge tends to arrive once crowd backing is visible.
  6. Run it bilingually in Wales. Crowdfund Swansea's own pages are cy/en. A Welsh-first page title and video subtitles fit that.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Status: GATED, nothing opened. It needs the owner's GO, plus a written landowner permission that we do not hold. Draft it only as a HubSpot task proposal. No account, no contact with any council or landowner from this run.
  • Best fit: a public, place-based piece, not the e-books or the case. For example, a bilingual heritage interpretation panel or a self-guided trail leaflet for public land or a churchyard, if the owner agrees. It would cover documented early-medieval and Roman Llandough, using only catalogue-sourced facts and the villa-site wording already on the site. Nothing about family holdings or addresses. No insignia, and no assertions about named people.
  • Which council: no Spacehive programme was found for the Vale of Glamorgan or Cardiff (search 2026-11-01). The Vale of Glamorgan is also a body under forensic review on the site, so asking it for a pledge would be a conflict. Prefer a national +Extra fund, or a project under a council that has a programme (Swansea is the Welsh model).
  • Copy: use the Cedwyn page voice: "a public panel telling 1,800 years of this ground, sourced line by line". Banned public words apply. Link only to Cedwyn internal pages, and give the /ebooks/ link on the thank-you page only.
  • Measure: pledges per day for the first 7 days. Glyncoch-style momentum means at least 30% of the target from the crowd before asking businesses.
