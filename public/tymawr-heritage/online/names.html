Names on the land

The house was known as Tŷ Mawr and, in English, Great House. Older names for a holding at Llandough appear in research notes on the wiki: Cwm-Cedwyn, the wooded dell on the River Ely; and Cydwin or Cydfin in estate papers. The documents behind those notes are still being traced, so they are given here with their status plainly marked.

Cwm-Cedwyn — a boundary-records note (under forensic examination)

Outside note:

EV-0001 (Legal Wiki, Evidence Register)

What the note shows. A research note on the wiki cites Llandough boundary records for the name Cwm-Cedwyn, the wooded dell on the Ely. It is an outside paper, not a record of Tŷ Mawr.

What it does not show. The boundary document itself is not yet held, and the date the note gives has not been checked against it; the reference is SOURCE PENDING. The note makes no link to the house.

The Cydwin lease (under forensic examination)

Outside note:

EV-0002 (Legal Wiki, Evidence Register)

What the note shows. A research note records a 1,000-year lease of Cydwin in the manor of Llandough, granted by Sir George Herbert to Vaughan. It is a third-party paper under forensic examination, not a record of Tŷ Mawr.

What it does not show. The lease is not yet held; its parties, date and extent have not been checked against the document and are SOURCE PENDING. The note makes no link to the farmhouse.
