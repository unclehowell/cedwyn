The family’s case about Great House Farm is… … read more

The family’s case about Great House Farm is that the 1877 Bute–Thomas agreement (GHF-E115) and related records describe two historically distinct parcels: Parcel A, the house/quarrying holding, and Parcel B, the eastern agricultural fields. The family argues that later use of “whole farm” language blurred that distinction. This is a working title reconstruction, not a final judicial finding. The exact legal extent must be tested against the original agreements, filed plans, tenancy records and registration files. See the linked Parcel A/B title claim for the source-by-source dependencies, the BP v Buckler scope analysis for what the litigation did and did not decide, and the evidence-dependency tree.

Definitions

Parcel A — the full house/quarrying parcel, not merely the retained house and garden

Parcel AParcel A means the full 1877 house/quarrying parcel: the farmhouse and associated house group; barns, stable and yard west of the house; garden; the forest/woodland north of the house; and the ground south-east of the house and garden that later became village green VG41. The house-and-garden description used in later proceedings referred to the occupied centre that Mary defended; it was not the geographical limit of Parcel A. Mary (Nancy) Williams described the farmhouse/buildings and about 10 acres as the Thomas/Williams side of the 1877 division. The family’s established title case is that when the quarrying arrangement ended in 1928, the Parcel-A freehold included the northern woodland and the south-eastern green ground as well as the house/buildings land (1928).

“The freehold of the greater part of the farm consisting of the farmhouse and buildings and about 10 acres which I still occupy was sold by the Bute Estate to Daniel Thomas and another portion consisting of about 9 acres up on the top was retained by the Bute Estate.”

Evidence: Mary (Nancy) Williams’s statement, c. 1974 (extract) (cat. GHF-E011-02) · Bute–Thomas conveyance and Thomas tenancy, 1877 (missing; cat. GHF-E011-01)

Parcel B — the eastern fields

Parcel BThe separate fields to the east: on the family’s reading, the “about 9 acres up on the top” that the Bute Estate retained in 1877 [record pending: EV-0006]. Mary says this portion “was given up later, and does not concern this action”. It was later let under the 1916 tenancy and the 1939 revised tenancy. The family does not dispute its subsequent passage to WGR and BP.

“Whole farm” — wording that blurred distinct parcels A and B

‹A + B described as one›The phrase appears in the 1916 tenancy, 1955 order, later conveyances, 1982 registration and 1987 judgment [record pending: EV-0014]. It is evidence of how the property was described in those records, not proof that Parcel A and Parcel B were the same legal holding or that WGR had an entitlement to both. The family’s case is that WGR’s rights concerned Parcel B, while the 1928 operation of the 1877 arrangement had already transferred Parcel A to the Williams family. The 1955 wording then made it possible to treat Parcel A land as part of the fields possession.

Research note. The 1916 tenancy is the first record identified here to describe both parcels as one. The family’s case is that this nomenclature did not undo the 1877/1928 division: by then Parcel A was the Thomas/Williams house-and-quarrying parcel and Parcel B the eastern fields retained by Bute and later let to WGR. Subsequent use of “whole farm”—especially in 1955—blurred rather than resolved that distinction.
The title test

The central question is not whether later records call the property the “whole farm”, but whether each later instrument actually carried Parcel A. The research therefore tests the 1877 split, the 1924/1928 material, the 1938 conveyance, the 1939 fields-only plan and the 1969–83 registration chain against one another. Research note — the chronology of conflation. Mary (Nancy) Williams’s witness statement c.1974 (Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony) records the freehold passing when quarrying ceased and the last rent paid in 1928; on that basis the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928, when quarrying and rent to Thomas ended, and Parcel A—including the northern forest and the later VG41 green—became family freehold. The 1949 step that sought to characterise occupation as permissive came before Mary (Nancy) Williams recorded the title papers’ disappearance in 1950–52 (witness statement c.1974); that the attempt failed is SOURCE PENDING. In 1955, wording that described the “whole farm” blurred Parcel A and Frederick’s Parcel-B tenancy, and execution seized the north forest and south-east green while Mary defended the still-occupied house/buildings/garden group. Her practical defence of that central area, under extreme physical and family constraints, should not be misread as a claim limited to that area. On the family’s limitation case, twelve years from the 1928 change would have matured by 1940 if the relevant conditions were met; the later 1955 recharacterisation made the clock appear to start later. At first registration, the 1969 root/1971 abstract discrepancy must be investigated, but is not by itself Parcel-A-specific proof. The precise 1982–83 filings and title plans remain the test of how the conflated description entered the register.
Same-name records — check which farm before assigning a parcel

Records that say “Great House”, “Ty Mawr” or “Llandough” are not automatically about this farm. There are three traps: Llandough Farm, a separate farmstead in the same village south of the church (Roman villa site, built over for housing 1978–79); Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge, a different parish about 20 km west with its own Great House Farm and the Llandough Castle estate; and Great House, Llandow, near Cowbridge. A record is only assigned to Parcel A or Parcel B once its parish, plan, acreage or tenant ties it to Llandough-juxta-Penarth.

Currently unassigned: Amgueddfa Cymru MS 3360/3, the 1924 “Particulars … of the Sale of Nine Well-Known Farms in the Vale of Glamorgan forming part of the Llandough Estate”, which has a page headed Great House Farm, and Museum Ref 13.2, a waterwheel “from Llandough Farm” (1979). Neither is yet shown to concern either parcel; reasons at R33 location check. The 1924 sale particulars are not the 1877 split Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler, describes, and not the 15 May 1924 agreement on the WA231076 file.

Acreage trail
Acreage control — do not add these figures as separate parcels. Mary (Nancy) Williams’s “about 45 acres” describes the earlier holding, including land across Leckwith Road; her account of 1877 describes about 10 acres with the farmhouse/buildings and about 9 acres retained by Bute. The separate “about 33 acres” limeworks figure comes from an uninspected D153 lead whose item description, extent and relationship to the later parcel model are not verified. It could describe a working area, overlap other areas or refer to a different tract. Do not subtract or add it to the 45, 10 and 9 acres, or assign it to Parcel A/B, until the D153 record and any plan are checked. See Llandough Limeworks and SC-21.
