Names: the elder woman is Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler; her youngest daughter is Mary Buckler (later Godfrey). When could two women both be “Mary Buckler”? See Two Mary Bucklers? Name and identity timeline.

The Williams family, later Williams / Buckler, made their home at Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr), the Great House of Llandough, from 1667 (Mary Williams’ statement, 1974 newspapers; ITV News, 1988) until 1988. On the record: John Williams on 36 plots, the whole holding, in the c.1841 tithe; John Williams “for many years a juror” of the manor court that met in the house (1908); and Mary (Nancy) Williams, who refused the tenancies offered to her in 1959 and 1965, asserted title through her grandfather, and died at the house in 1983. The house was demolished in 1988. Her descendants run the restoration campaign and this archive.

Evidence description: on 31 December 1969 WGR conveyed to BP Pension Trust.

‹A + B as one›Parcel reading. The conveyance itself is missing, but it appears to have passed the farm as one, although WGR had never obtained possession of, or any signature for, Parcel A. 🕒

Interpretation & wider context: when. Frederick was alive when the sale came out, so it came before his death in 1967, and before the son’s household left in September 1968 – September 1969. The eldest son was probably born about 1937–40 (after the 1936 marriage), so he was old enough to sign a deed; William, born around 1948–49, was under the age of majority (21 until 1 January 1970) throughout. The tightest fit is spring 1965, just after Mary refused WGR‘s tenancy, to 1967. The 1961 deed is an earlier candidate.
Interpretation & wider context: what. He could not have sold Mary’s house (Parcel A): she was alive, in occupation, asserting ownership, and never signed. A solicitor could have completed a release or surrender of whatever interest he claimed as Frederick’s heir; a sale of a share he believed he had; a deal over the fields (Parcel B); a quitclaim WGR wanted on file before selling to BP; or a sale of a different parcel. WGR had just failed to get Mary’s signature, and a paper signed by another family member is exactly what it lacked. On this view the 1965 offer to Mary and the son’s sale were two routes to the same end: making the family’s hold on the farm look permissive.

To find: copy of the 29 March 1961 deed (HM Land Registry) · WGR and BP conveyancing papers 1964–69, including the missing 1969 conveyance and BP’s abstract of title: any Buckler party other than Frederick, Mary or William · a solicitor’s file or deed of 1964–67 in the eldest son’s name · General Register Office entries for the eldest son’s birth (Buckler, about 1936–41, Cardiff or East Glamorgan) and Frederick’s 1967 death. 🕒

1983 – 1990: Defending the house, and William’s death

Evidence description: after Mary’s death on 26 March 1983 her son William inherited her estate and defended BP Properties Ltd v Buckler. BP won in the High Court (heard 10 July 1986, judgment 24 July 1986) and the Court of Appeal on 31 July 1987. Demolition of the farmhouse began on 6 December 1988.

Parcel AParcel reading. What William inherited and defended was the house, garden and yard (Parcel A), and it was the farmhouse on Parcel A that was demolished. BP ultimately relied on a registered title that brought both parcels together in 1982/83; the disputed question is the historic root for the Parcel A component.
