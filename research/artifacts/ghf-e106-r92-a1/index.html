Dear Customer 
I write further to your correspondence about the registration of Ty 
Mawr Farm. I apologise for the delay in responding. 
It may be useful if I briefly set out the role of HM Land Registry. 
HMLR is an administrative body, and must carry out all of its 
functions within the law and must only do what it has the power to 
do. The work of HMLR is covered by the Land Registration Act 2002
Read more
(“the Act”) and the Land Registration Rules 2003 (“the Rules”). This 
legislation is designed to provide a registration system providing title 
holders with a state backed guarantee of ownership. 
HMLR’s main role is to keep a register of ownership and interests in 
land and to enter information on to the register following receipt of 
satisfactory applications. 
Prior to completing any application, we are required to check that 
each application complies with the requirements under the Act and 
the Rules. When applications do not comply with these 
requirements, or if it is established that additional information is 
needed to consider an application, we are able to request further 
information under rule 17 of the Rules, before completing an 
application. Our role is to be reactive in responding to applications 
received rather than pro-active in establishing any rights which may 
exist. 
Once an interest is registered, HMLR guarantees the title. The 
guarantee means that the title owner can rely on the registration as 
evidence of legal ownership and that there is no longer any need for 
the owner to produce the original title deeds as proof of ownership. 
However, please note that the guarantee does not mean that HMLR 
has a responsibility for overseeing transactions relating to land, or
