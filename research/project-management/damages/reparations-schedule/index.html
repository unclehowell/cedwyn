2. Ongoing costs of pursuit — running ledger

A litigant in person’s own time is claimable only under a costs order (CPR 46.5) at £24 an hour unless a higher financial loss is proved. Disbursements need receipts. Work done by an assistant is not the litigant’s own time; only the litigant’s own hours instructing and reviewing count. Each session or payment has its own row. Figures are only entered where they are recorded.

3. Proposed apportionment between the parties

Caveat: this is the family’s proposal, not a finding. Liability is unproven against every party named. The shares are a starting point for negotiation, weighted by each party’s asserted part in the chain of loss: who acted, who profited, and who could have prevented it. Statutory routes do not share loss this way: for example, an HMLR indemnity would be paid by HMLR in full, and HMLR could then seek to recover it from a party whose fraud caused the loss (LRA 2002 Sch 8 para 10). Illustrations use the route C mid total (£3,443,760).

Shares total 100%. Remedies in kind (records, return of items, acknowledgement) are proposed alongside money and do not reduce any share.

4. Opportunity to remedy

Each party is to be given a fair chance to put things right before any claim. Proposed standard: a letter before claim giving a reasonable time to respond. The Practice Direction on Pre-Action Conduct treats 14 days as reasonable in a simple case and up to 3 months in a very complex one; this case is complex, so 3 months is proposed.
