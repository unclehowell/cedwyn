The restoration campaign is the Williams / Buckler family‘s effort to establish what happened to Great House Farm, correct the public record, and obtain redress.

Aims
Research note. The family’s current working thesis is that the historic Parcel A ownership evidence became unavailable or was overlooked from 1950–52 onward, and that later records then carried forward a competing permissive-occupation narrative. This is the family’s current working thesis under documentary testing. The family’s aims are:

  • Regain possession of the woodland half of the land (title WA735527).
  • Have ownership of the house parcel (Parcel A) recognised in the family’s favour.
  • Seek reparations, not possession, for the homes built on the land.
  • Give every party involved the chance to offer a remedy before any court action, consistent with principles of peace before conflict, truthfulness, good faith, fairness and justice tempered with mercy.

RESTORATION PROJECT. The campaign includes a proposed Welsh heritage replica / interpretive museum project in Puerto Madryn, Patagonia, connected with the Welsh settlement history there. This is a live restoration and interpretation project. It is not presented as restitution already achieved, and no transfer of property or heritage has been completed on this page.

Legal routes being examined
Research note. Routes the family is examining:

  • Land Registration Act 2002, Schedule 4: alteration of the register for mistake.
  • Land Registration Act 2002, Schedule 8: indemnity from the Land Registry.
  • Limitation Act 1980, section 32: postponement of limitation for concealment.
  • Challenges to the 1962 order, a Senedd route and an ombudsman referral.

All face the binding 1987 judgment and depend heavily on recovering the 1877 papers.

Earlier preservation campaign — the open day of 15 April 1974

DOCUMENTED FACT (press). On 15 April 1974 Mary (Nancy) Williams, then 60, opened Great House Farm to the public “to show what the nation is losing”. Godfrey Francis reported in the Daily Telegraph next day that hundreds attended. He described the ten-roomed house, its oak beams and old furniture, and Mary’s refusal to move because the house was “part of the nation’s heritage”. He also reported a petition of about 1,000 signatures, planning permission for about 13 houses, and an ownership dispute of 25 years. Full transcript: GHF-E090-N03.

DOCUMENTED FACT (press). A letter headed… … read more

DOCUMENTED FACT (press). A letter headed “Grievous loss”, from William Rees, appeared in the Western Mail, dated in the copy “19/11/7?” with the last digit unclear. It argues that destroying the site would damage Glamorgan’s historic and religious heritage, and it outlines the manorial descent: Tewkesbury, then the Crown, then Herbert, then Bute. Full transcript: GHF-E090-N04.

Change control, 3 October 2026: this section… … read more

Change control, 3 October 2026: this section was headed “Additional preservation evidence — 19 November 1977” and treated the Telegraph report and the Rees letter as one Western Mail page of 19/11/77. On re-examination the cutting carries the handwritten note “D. Telegraph 16 April 74”. Mary’s age of 60 fits 1974, and RCAHMW (NMR 6048944) catalogues a Daily Telegraph article of 16 April 1974. The open-day report is therefore re-dated to 16 April 1974. The Rees letter is a separate Western Mail cutting whose year is unresolved (1974, 1977 or another year of the 1970s). 🕒
