Controlling correction — 9 October 2026

The 6 October cross-review below is retained as a time-stamped assessment, but its priority framing has been superseded by the family’s clarified Parcel-A loss case. Parcel A is the full 1877 house/quarrying estate: farmhouse/buildings, barn, stable and yard west of the house, garden, forest north of the house, and the ground south-east later registered as VG41. The family’s title case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928; the 1949 effort to recast Mary’s occupation as permissive failed; the title deed was reported missing in 1950–52; and WGR’s “whole farm” wording was applied in 1955 when enforcing Parcel B, resulting in seizure of the north forest and south-east green along with Parcel B. Mary’s defence of the occupied house group was practical and did not limit her title claim to that area. The 1939 agreement/plan is already held and depicts Parcel B only. The work now traces Parcel A through the 1955 process and onward title chain; it does not reopen the Parcel-B-only plan finding. The 45–55% figure below remains only the cross-review’s historical paper-root estimate on evidence then seen, not a legal finding or an estimate of the full case’s merits. 🕒

Executive finding

The strongest case is not the 1877 “reverter” standing alone. The more coherent forensic case is the chain from the 1939 fields-only plan, the 1949 surrender/re-grant, the absence of an identified independent Parcel A root in the WGR/BP chain, the 1982–84 registration papers, the 1987 amalgamation and the documentary gap in the possession litigation.

Working probability — for research prioritisation only: the independent review estimated roughly 45–55% that BP never held a valid paper title to Parcel A on the evidence then seen. This is not a legal finding or probability of success; it is a reason to obtain the decisive documents before incurring major litigation cost.

8 October 2026 — New forensic paradigm and control-point model

New analytical model: do not begin by assuming that all institutions were coordinated or that every later actor was wrong. Instead identify the first materially wrong, incomplete or unconfirmed premise, the actor that introduced it, every later actor that relied on it, and the independent safeguard that should have detected it. A single foundational error can be amplified by later reliance without establishing dishonesty by every participant.

The immediate forensic question is therefore: what exact physical land object did each legal instrument, title, register, court order and development record refer to? The investigation should trace historic land identity → ownership/tenancy → acquisition chain → first registration → 13 January 1984 registration activity → 2 February 1987 amalgamation → court evidence → enforcement → heritage/planning → record custody → present remedy.

Reopening / indemnity firewall

Keep four questions separate: (1) title/extent; (2) registration mistake and alteration/rectification; (3) statutory indemnity for qualifying loss; and (4) fraud-based reopening of the 1987 judgment. Proving a title or registration defect does not by itself prove dishonesty. A fraud-based reopening route requires an identified person and evidence that deliberate dishonesty was an operative cause of the judgment. The present evidence therefore supports investigation, not a finding of fraud. 🕒
