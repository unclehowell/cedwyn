1000 Cuts: a dated chronology of the Williams family at Great House Farm (1870s–1988)

A dated chronology of the Williams family's position at Great House Farm in law and in fact, 1870s to 1988, each step linked to its evidence.

Each entry gives a date, what happened, what it changed in law or in fact, and the evidence pages it rests on. "Depends on" and "Enabled" link each entry to the earlier entries it built on and the later ones that built on it. Open both ends of a link to check it.

Record = read in a court judgment, registry, archive, legislation or contemporaneous press. Family testimony = the family's own account. Not yet confirmed = no source located yet. Open question = what the record does not yet answer, and what would answer it.

  • No court decision on the record determines who owned the farmhouse as a question of title; the 1986 and 1987 judgments decided possession and limitation EV-0075 EV-0080.
  • No record of compensation for the demolition, and no finding against any party for it, has been located. The outcome of the Vale councillors' 1989 decision to take legal action EV-0117 is not yet located.
  • The House of Lords outcome papers are not yet held EV-0076. See C33.

What the courts found against the family

  • The Court of Appeal held that the 1962 order stopped the 12-year period before it ran, and that from the 1974 letters Mary's possession was no longer adverse EV-0080. These holdings stand unless set aside.
  • The judgment records rent arrears in the 1930s and 1940s and a last payment in 1953 EV-0080, against the family's account of rent ending in 1928 EV-0016.
  • The trial judge found that Frederick had no intention to possess as owner, relying on the March 1965 offer EV-0080. See C12.
  • BP did not depend on the 1983 registration to sue: the 1984 action succeeded on the 1974 letters EV-0073 EV-0080.
  • No record yet shows BP knew of Mary's final illness, or that the 1983 filing was timed to it.

Competing theories, most plausible first

Competing theories that could account for the entries above, ordered from most to least plausible given all the evidence held today: how much of the record each accounts for, and how much stands against it. Each is a theory, not a finding. The courts' view is included and ranked like the others.

T1Theory 1 of 5
The courts' view: possession was lawfully recovered and the paper title was never extinguished. The family held as tenants until the 1955 notice to quit, then as adverse possessors; the 1962 order stopped the 12-year period before it ran, and the 31 Oct 1974 letters made Mary's later possession a licence, so the paper title running from Bute through the 1938 conveyance to BP was never extinguished and possession was recovered lawfully.
Evidence for: The 1916 tenancy of "the whole of the farm" EV-0014; the 1953 rent payment under a judgment EV-0080; the 1962 possession order EV-0041; the 1974 letters EV-0050; the High Court judgment EV-0075 and the Court of Appeal EV-0080. These holdings bind unless set aside.
Evidence against: The 1974 adverse-possession plea "has never been finally heard" EV-0049; the family's account of rent ending in 1928 EV-0016; the 1939 plan shows only the fields EV-0023; whether the 1938 conveyance included the farmhouse parcel has not been read EV-0019.
Would be confirmed or refuted by: Confirmed by a 1938 conveyance plan that includes the farmhouse parcel and by the absence of any written rejection of the 1974 licence. Refuted by a plan that leaves the farmhouse out, or a letter rejecting the licence after 31 Oct 1974.
T2Theory 2 of 5
Lawful steps, sequenced against the limitation clock. Each step was lawful on its own, but together they kept the family's possession from ever running its 12 years, without the ownership plea being decided: the 1962 order, its revival about 12 weeks before the period from it ended, the licence letters, and the 1983 registration while Mary was alive.
Evidence for: Leave to execute in Sep 1974 against an 11 Dec 1974 expiry EV-0041 EV-0080; the letters of 31 Oct 1974 EV-0050; the undecided 1974 plea EV-0049; the 1983 application made in her lifetime EV-0066; BP's spokesman in 1988 on waiting until her death EV-0088.
Evidence against: Waiting for her death is also what the 1974 letters promised EV-0050; the 1982 field registration was compulsory EV-0265; BP's 1984 action succeeded on the 1974 letters, not on the 1983 registration EV-0080; no record yet shows BP knew of her final illness.
Would be confirmed or refuted by: Confirmed or refuted by the 1974 Cardiff County Court file (HMCTS reply due 2 Nov 2026) EV-0049 and BP's own files on the 1974 and 1983 steps.
T3Theory 3 of 5
The farmhouse parcel stood outside the paper chain. Parcel A, the house parcel, was never conveyed in the Bute → Western Ground Rents chain, so the possession cases started from a paper title that did not cover the house.
Evidence for: The 1939 agreement plan highlights the fields only EV-0023; the family's account of the house standing apart EV-0016; the separate 1983 registration of the farmhouse EV-0066.
Evidence against: The 1916 tenancy covers "the whole of the farm" with plan EV-0014; the courts took the paper title to the farm as their starting point EV-0080; the 1938 conveyance and its plan have not yet been read EV-0019.
Would be confirmed or refuted by: Settled either way by the 1938 Bute–Western Ground Rents conveyance and plan, and the 1905 Bute conveyance.
T4Theory 4 of 5
The 1983 registration misdescribed the occupation. BP's 1983 application for the farmhouse, made while Mary lived there and examined after her death, did not tell the Registry the true position of the occupier.
