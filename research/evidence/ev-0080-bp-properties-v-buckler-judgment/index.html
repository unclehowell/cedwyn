Quotation: “The plaintiffs, BP Properties Ltd, have the paper title to the farm, and indeed since November 1982 they have been the registered proprietors of it at HM Land Registry following first registration.” (Dillon LJ)

October 1974 letter and its effect. The judgment quotes BP Properties’ offer to let Mrs Buckler remain “in occupation of the house and garden rent free … for the rest of your life”. Dillon LJ treated the letters as creating a licence, absent repudiation, so her subsequent possession was not adverse for the limitation argument decided on appeal. This explains the court’s legal reasoning; it is not a finding that the family held the freehold title it now argues should be investigated. Read the judgment · Case summary.

Proves: Appeal limited to farmhouse and garden; BP held paper title and was registered proprietor since November 1982; Mary’s title documents never produced; Limitation clock started February 1955; 1974 letters made her possession permissive; Dillon LJ noted, conditionally, that adverse possession would bind BP’s registration as an overriding interest.

Does not prove: Does not establish which propositions were decided versus assumed or background, or whether the 1939 plan or 1938 conveyance were shown to the court; Not a finding Mary established ownership; Establishes no judicial wrongdoing or fraud.

The 1962 → 1974 → 1983 sequence

THEORY · I-01Working hypothesis on the sequence, set out as dated steps for the reader to test. 11 Dec 1962: possession order in Cardiff County Court (EV-0041); the 12-year period from that order ran to 11 Dec 1974. In the 1974 County Court action she pleaded that the claim was barred (EV-0049). 17–19 Sep 1974: BP Pension Trust sought and obtained leave to enforce the 1962 order (EV-0080). 31 Oct 1974: letters permitting her to stay rent-free for life (EV-0050); the Court of Appeal later held that from then on her possession was by licence and no longer adverse (EV-0080). 30 Nov 1982 and 23 Feb 1983: first-registration applications for the fields and for the farmhouse (EV-0059, EV-0065). The hypothesis is that these steps were connected, each taking occupation the family treated as ownership closer to a registered title. No document read so far shows them planned together; W-04 and the discarded entries below set out readings that cut against it.

Rests on: EV-0041 · EV-0049 · EV-0050 · EV-0080 (this page) · EV-0059 · EV-0065

Would be confirmed by: The 1974 County Court file and leave application (HMCTS reply due 2 Nov 2026) and the 1983 application papers (HM Land Registry subject access request, due 28 Oct 2026) showing the steps were planned as one course of action.

Would be refuted by: Records showing each step was a separate routine act with its own cause (for example, the 1982 internal BP conveyance arising from a group restructuring unrelated to the farm).

Status: Open

INFERENCE · I-02The Court of Appeal records that BP Pension Trust's 1974 County Court action, in which she pleaded that BP's claim was barred, "has never been finally heard" (record). Working hypothesis: BP held no fresh order from 1974, so after 31 Oct 1974 its position rested on the licence letters alone; the 1982–83 registrations were the next route open to it.

Rests on: EV-0080 (this page) · EV-0049 · research log E134(b)

Would be confirmed by: The 1974 plaint and court book showing the action left undetermined (HMCTS request on the 1974 records).

Would be refuted by: A later order, judgment or discontinuance in the 1974 action.

Status: Open; the "never finally heard" words are confirmed in the judgment

Why 1983

INFERENCE · W-01Two records bear on the timing of the 1984 action. BP's letter of 31 Oct 1974 promised that "no proceedings will be commenced until you personally no longer live there" (EV-0050). A BP spokesman quoted in the 1988 press cutting "Chainsaw farmer vows to fight on" (EV-0089; our working note places it in the South Wales Echo of 29 Apr 1988; the catalogue records the newspaper as not yet identified) gave her ill health as the reason she lived there rent-free, and added: "When Mrs Buckler died in 1983 it became clear her son was not going to leave … in May 1984 we started court proceedings." Working hypothesis: her death, rather than any new fact about title, set the time of the 1984 action. W-02 sets out the reading that the same timing simply kept the 1974 promise.

Rests on: EV-0089 · EV-0050 · EV-0080 (this page) · EV-0073

Would be confirmed by: BP's internal file for 1983–84 (BP Archive request, reply due by 16 Nov 2026) recording a decision to wait for her death; identification of the newspaper and full page for the cutting.

Would be refuted by: Evidence that BP planned or began proceedings against her during her lifetime, or that the 1984 writ had a different documented trigger.

Status: Open (the main working hypothesis on "why 1983")

CUTS AGAINST THE CASE · W-02Cuts against the case: the same timing is fully consistent with BP simply keeping its 1974 promise not to evict her in her lifetime. Waiting for her death is, on this reading, the conduct the letters promised, not concealment. The Court of Appeal itself described the device: a unilateral licence lets an owner who is "not prepared to incur the obloquy of bringing proceedings" keep "his title alive for very many years until it suits him to evict" — a criticism of the device, not a finding of bad faith.

Rests on: EV-0050 · EV-0080 (this page) · research log E132

Would be confirmed by: BP correspondence showing the delay was kept for a reason beyond the promise (for example, to let a registration settle before anyone could contest it).

Would be refuted by: BP correspondence showing the delay was only the promise being kept.

Status: Open (cuts against)

DISCARDED THEORY · W-05DISCARDED: the theory that a 12-year deadline in Oct 1986 forced BP to register and act by then. Reason: the 12-year period that mattered ran from the possession order of 11 Dec 1962 and ended on 11 Dec 1974; BP admitted in the Court of Appeal that she was in adverse possession from 1965–67 until 31 Oct 1974; no record carries an Oct 1986 deadline. After her death the only live period was her son's own.

Rests on: EV-0080 (this page) · EV-0041 · research log E134(c)

Would be confirmed by: Would be revived only by a BP or solicitors' document referring to an Oct 1986 limitation date.

Would be refuted by: Already refuted on the judgment's own dates.

Status: Dropped (25 Oct 2026)

The 1974 licence letters

THEORY · L-01Ground R1: the Court of Appeal answered the "wrong company" argument only from the letters' own words that BP Properties Ltd "had agreed to buy". Completion came on 23 May 1975, and HM Land Registry's schedule lists a plan to the sale dated 23 Feb 1975, almost four months after the letters. Working hypothesis: if no binding agreement existed on 31 Oct 1974, BP Properties had nothing to licence.

Rests on: EV-0050 · EV-0052 · EV-0080 (this page) · EV-0161 · ground R1

Would be confirmed by: BP Archive or Registry papers showing no agreement before Feb–May 1975.

Would be refuted by: A dated contract or board minute before 31 Oct 1974. Cuts against: an unwritten agreement is not void (E28); a press snippet suggests the two companies shared an officer in 1972, so a group decision recorded only internally is plausible (E124, not yet confirmed); the 23 Feb 1975 date may be a clerical carry-over in the Registry's letter (E81).

Status: Open (ground R1, scores a1 b3 c2 d1)

THEORY · L-02Ground R2: the decisive finding was that she never repudiated the letters ("she did not, however, do that"). The family's account, as read here, is that she rejected the permission offered. A letter from her or her solicitors to BP after 31 Oct 1974 rejecting the licence would meet the exact point the court said might have changed the result.

Rests on: EV-0050 · EV-0049 · EV-0051 · EV-0080 (this page) · EV-0073 · ground R2

Would be confirmed by: Such a letter in the family solicitors' 1984–87 file, BP's list of documents, or a reply to the Western Mail letter of 19 Nov 1974.

Would be refuted by: A complete solicitors' file with no rejection. The 1974 defence alone does not help: the court recited it and still found no repudiation (E130).

Status: Open (ground R2, scores a1 b5 c1 d1)

Title chain and registration

THEORY · R-02Ground R3: the farmhouse was registered separately (WA240304, Form 1C by Linklaters & Paines, edition 23 Feb 1983) while BP's own 1974 action "has never been finally heard"; the court was told the whole farm had been registered in November 1982. Working hypothesis: the farmhouse application, made with eight-plus years of admitted adverse possession behind her, is the point where the occupation and the unresolved action would have had to be declared.

Rests on: EV-0063 · EV-0065 · EV-0066 · EV-0080 (this page) · EV-0078 · ground R3; research log E91, E98

Would be confirmed by: A Form 1C (Co.) statutory declaration silent about her occupation or claim (subject access request item 11).

Would be refuted by: A declaration that disclosed both. Cuts against: the court's words match the register as merged on 2 Feb 1987, and paper title was never in issue.

Status: Open (ground R3, scores a2 b1 c3 d2)
