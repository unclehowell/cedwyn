The 1990 report

“Land at Great House Farm, Llandough: An Archaeological Assessment”, Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust, August 1990. Holbrook & Thomas (2005) list it as an unpublished report: it was never printed. A copy is held here (EV-0126, 16 pages, with a full transcription). The National Monuments Record of Wales holds it as NMR ref. 6524751, and the Heneb HER cites it as the source of events GGATE003872 and GGATE000877.

  • Commissioned by BP Properties Ltd after the demolition of December 1988, through its agents Debenham Tewson.
  • Required by the outline permission of 13 March 1990: “no development shall take place until a field evaluation survey and archaeological assessment report has been carried out by a qualified archaeologist to the satisfaction of the Local Planning Authority.”
  • GGAT advised the council and BP at the same time. The report calls this a “dual role”.
  • 8 trenches. Human bone in trenches 3 and 4 (“numerous human bones, most of which seemed to be disarticulated”). The north-east corner of the house, probably the dairy, in trench 7.
  • It names the Disused Burial Grounds (Amendment) Act 1981 and “recommends that before any decision on the options given above is taken, advice should be sought by B P Properties Ltd on any legal requirements which may pertain to the human skeletal remains.”
  • Recommendations: watching briefs; around trenches 3 and 4 and east of the churchyard, raise the ground level to avoid disturbance, or excavate and record before building.

The 1992 consent was approval of reserved matters (92/00671/RES) under the 1990 outline permission, granted on 3 September 1992, 38 days after GGAT’s letter of 27 July 1992. The “March 1992” in Holbrook & Thomas (2005) first appears in the developer’s 1994 preliminary report; the layout drawings are dated March 1992. The Vale’s online planning register starts in 1996, so neither application is online.
