Proves: Not held, so nothing yet. Reported: as recited, not from a held copy: Judge Temple Morris QC ordered possession of farmhouse and garden, with mesne profits since 1955, against Frederick and Mary (Nancy) Buckler née Williams on WGR’s claim; Order not enforced before 1974.

Does not prove: Particulars of claim, any plan, and what WGR said it owned are unknown. Whether Mary pleaded ownership or why mesne profits ran from 1955 if that order excluded house and garden is unknown.

The 1962 → 1974 → 1983 sequence

THEORY · I-01Our reading of the sequence: the 11 Dec 1962 possession order tried to end the occupation; in 1974, with the 12-year period from that order about to expire (11 Dec 1974), BP first sought leave to enforce it and then, by the 31 Oct 1974 letters, recast her occupation as permission; when that did not end the matter, the 1982–83 first registrations turned the position into a registered title. Each step, on this reading, moved occupation that the family treated as ownership one stage further towards a paper title held by BP.

Rests on: EV-0041 (this page) · EV-0049 · EV-0050 · EV-0080 · EV-0059 · EV-0065

Would be confirmed by: The 1974 County Court file and leave application (HMCTS reply due 2 Nov 2026) and the 1983 application papers (HM Land Registry subject access request, due 28 Oct 2026) showing the steps were planned as one course of action.

Would be refuted by: Records showing each step was a separate routine act with its own cause (for example, the 1982 internal BP conveyance arising from a group restructuring unrelated to the farm).

Status: Open

INFERENCE · I-02The Court of Appeal records that BP Pension Trust's 1974 County Court action, in which she pleaded that BP's claim was barred, "has never been finally heard" (record). Our reading: BP held no fresh order from 1974, so after 31 Oct 1974 its position rested on the licence letters alone; the 1982–83 registrations were the next route open to it.

Rests on: EV-0080 · EV-0049 · research log E134(b)

Would be confirmed by: The 1974 plaint and court book showing the action left undetermined (HMCTS request on the 1974 records).

Would be refuted by: A later order, judgment or discontinuance in the 1974 action.

Status: Open; the "never finally heard" words are confirmed in the judgment

THEORY · I-03On 17–19 Sep 1974 BP Pension Trust obtained leave to enforce the 1962 order "as successors in title to Western Ground Rents", on two days' notice, while its own action against her was part-heard. The WGR → BP Pension Trust conveyance is not on either HM Land Registry first-registration file. Our reading: succession to the 1962 order was asserted in court without the conveyance being produced.

Rests on: EV-0041 (this page) · EV-0043 · EV-0080 · EV-0161 · ground R4

Would be confirmed by: The 1974 leave application and its supporting affidavit showing what proof of succession was put to the court.

Would be refuted by: The 31 Dec 1969 conveyance, or a certified copy, on the 1974 court file or the 1982–83 registration files (HM Land Registry relied on recitals and a 1971 abstract, E21).

Status: Open (ground R4, scores a1 b3 c1 d1)

Why 1983

DISCARDED THEORY · W-05DISCARDED: the theory that a 12-year deadline in Oct 1986 forced BP to register and act by then. Reason: the 12-year period that mattered ran from the possession order of 11 Dec 1962 and ended on 11 Dec 1974; BP admitted in the Court of Appeal that she was in adverse possession from 1965–67 until 31 Oct 1974; no record carries an Oct 1986 deadline. After her death the only live period was her son's own.

Would be confirmed by: Would be revived only by a BP or solicitors' document referring to an Oct 1986 limitation date.

Would be refuted by: Already refuted on the judgment's own dates.

Status: Dropped (25 Oct 2026)
