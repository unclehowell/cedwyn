Frederick Buckler (c. 1908–09 – 1967) was the husband of Mary (Nancy) Williams. He married her in 1936 and came to live at the farmhouse of Great House Farm; they had seven children. From 1944 he ran the farm as manager for his father-in-law, John Williams, and from 2 February 1949 he held an unwritten yearly agricultural tenancy from Western Ground Rents. The already-obtained 1939 WGR–John Williams agreement/plan depicts Parcel B, the eastern agricultural fields, only. The 1949–1953 unwritten yearly tenancy is treated by the Court of Appeal as a real protected periodic agricultural tenancy. Read against that 1939 plan [EV-0023], its scope followed the Parcel-B agricultural line and did not make Mary’s separate Parcel-A occupation permissive. A distinct further attempt to make Frederick a tenant is identified for 1954; see GHF-E182, whose original source is pending. Frederick was the only defendant to the 1955 possession order. The family’s established account is that on 4 July 1955 WGR took Parcel B and also seized the north forest and south-east green ground of Parcel A under the “whole farm” wording. He died in 1967, aged 58.

Evidence: Court of Appeal judgment, 1987 (Dillon LJ), [1987] EWCA Civ 2

What the judgment says (verbatim):

“… a new yearly agricultural tenancy commencing at February 2 1949 to be granted to Mr Williams’ son-in-law, Mr Frederick Buckler …”

“It was intended that Mr Frederick Buckler should have a written tenancy agreement, but this did not happen because Mr Frederick Buckler also failed to pay the rent.”

“There is a letter from the landlords’ agents to their solicitors of October 10 1952 stating that they did not propose to proceed with the letting to Mr Frederick Buckler.”

“However, the conclusion must be that, even though there was no written agreement, Mr Frederick Buckler had a periodic tenancy which was protected by the Agricultural Holdings Act.”

What it means: there was never a written tenancy; in 1952 the landlords’ own agents said they would not go ahead with the letting; the court still treated Frederick as a periodic agricultural tenant. His last rent was paid in 1953, under a judgment for arrears. None of these passages says which land the tenancy covered.

Parcel BAn agricultural tenancy following John Williams’s 1939 tenancy of the fields (Parcel B). The farmhouse was already his wife’s family home.

Research note. Because the tenancy was never written down, its extent was never proved. The 1939 plan shows Parcel B only [EV-0023], and the tenancy is read here as continuing the 1939 tenancy of Parcel B only. [SOURCE PENDING] Treating it as a tenancy of the whole farm was the first step in making Mary’s occupation of Parcel A look permissive.
