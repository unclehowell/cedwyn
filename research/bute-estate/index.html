The Bute Estate of the Marquesses of Bute… … read more

The Bute Estate of the Marquesses of Bute was the historic landowner of Great House Farm and landlord of the Williams family before 1877. It held the estate interest from which the 1938 reversion to Western Ground Rents Ltd is recorded. The Wiki records Mary (Nancy) Williams’s account that the farmhouse parcel was conveyed to Daniel Thomas in 1877. Because the original deed and plan have not been located, the legal nature and exact extent of that arrangement remain unresolved. The Wiki therefore tests Mary (Nancy) Williams, formerly Buckler, witness statement c.1974 (Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony) against the 1916 tenancy, the 1938 conveyance and the relevant plans rather than treating the reported 1877 transfer as independently proved. Bute estate records are split across repositories. For the unresolved 33-acre limeworks lead, the Wiki’s Source Conflicts Register records Glamorgan Archives’ advice that D153 is an NLW reference and NLW correspondence referring to D153/1–8 (see SC-21). The original item and any plan have not yet been inspected.

New archival lead — D 219. The National Library of Wales Bute Estate Records series D 219 (Llandough manorial leases and agreements, 1552–1829) explicitly identifies leases of “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm, (107 a.)”, 1552–1824. This is potentially important to the unresolved question of the Great House/Tŷ Mawr pre-1877 title and the route by which the Bute Estate acquired the early-19th-century freehold. The individual D219 deeds and plans still need to be examined before any title or parcel conclusion is drawn. See the dedicated Llandough/Penarth evidence page.

Events

  • Early nineteenth century — DOCUMENTED FACT: the Bute Estate acquires the freehold of Great House, previously the Vaughan family’s chief freehold farm, and the manorial courts for Llandough and Leckwith are held in the house (Great House Farm — earlier history; HER GGAT02038s). How Bute acquired it, and from whom, is UNRESOLVED: unsourced leads (Valentine Morris, Sir Mark Wood, the 1820 Bute–Plymouth exchange, David Stewart’s 1824 survey naming the farm ‘Cedfin’, Lambert Williams’s agreements with Bute in 1818–35) are held on Research Leads from the bpvsbuckler Archive.
  • Before 1877 — Mary’s grandfather holds the farm as a Bute tenant and receives notice to quit in 1877 (details; Mary (Nancy) Williams, witness statement c.1974 (Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony)). Priority record to locate: Bute rentals for Llandough, especially 1877–78 (cat. GHF-E016-02). Repository is not established by the material cited here; check NLW, Glamorgan Archives and Cardiff Library finding aids rather than presuming one custodian.

Correction and identity control: Cydfin / Tŷ Mawr Farm is not Llandough Farm

The National Library of Wales catalogue for Bute Estate Records, series D 219, describes these as separate named holdings with separate date ranges and deed references:

  • Cydfin Farm or Tŷ Mawr Farm — 107 acres: leases dated 1552–1824, including D219/1, D219/4–5 and D219/8 et seq.
  • Llandough Farm — 166 acres: leases dated 1824–1832, D219/9–13.

These are not two acreage descriptions of one farm. For this Wiki, the 166-acre Llandough Farm entry must not be attributed to Cydfin / Tŷ Mawr Farm or Great House Farm. Keep the deed groups and their histories separate unless an actual instrument proves a specific connection.

Separate survey measurement: the 1824 Stewart survey, plan 19, is catalogued and photographed as “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm”; the provisional transcription at EV-0211 records a different surveyed total of 139a 3r 8p including woodland. Do not silently equate 107 acres, 139a 3r 8p and 166 acres. Differences between the catalogue’s lease description and the survey extent still require examination of dates, scope, field schedule and plan.

Source: National Library of Wales, D 219 — Llandough manorial leases and agreements. The catalogue establishes distinct described holdings; the individual deeds and plans are still needed to map their exact footprints and to test the Williams family’s later title claim.
