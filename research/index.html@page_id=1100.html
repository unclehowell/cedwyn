Video 2 — Tŷ Mawr, the Great House of Llandough: the story on the map
Read the case summary

Great House Farm (Tŷ Mawr) is recorded here as the historic home of the Williams–Buckler family, occupied by the family since at least 1667. The site’s working reconstruction of the property and its changing names is set out in the two-parcel account and timeline.

The reconstruction begins with the 1877 Bute–Thomas agreement, involving the Bute Estate and Daniel Thomas. It distinguishes Parcel A — the farmhouse, garden, buildings and associated land — from Parcel B, the eastern fields. Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 statement records that the last rent was paid to Alfred Thomas in 1928, after which her father “regarded the farm as his own” (Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony); her account of the final rent payment stands alongside the disputed Land Registry title records. The later chain involving Western Ground Rents and BP Properties is examined separately; the Wiki’s reconstruction sets out the case that BP’s title came to treat the distinct house parcel as part of a synthetic whole-farm title.

The family disputes repeated attempts to characterise Mary (Nancy) Williams’s occupation as permissive. The relevant record includes the 1955 possession order, 1962 possession proceedings and 1974 licence letters. SOURCE PENDING: reports that Mary underwent a leg amputation and that her water was cut off have no document or named witness on file yet. These are points to be tested against the missing-records register and surviving evidence.

Particular questions concern the 1982–83 registration and opportunity to object, the 1987 Land Registry amalgamation during the appeal, the BP Properties Ltd v Buckler litigation, and the 1988 demolition followed by redevelopment. The Wiki asks whether the sequence supports a case of lawfare, deception or human-rights violations, concealment or missing-records concerns, or improper public-authority involvement. The analysis is tested against the evidence library, sources and archives, records-access chronology and counterarguments; nothing here is presented as a finding already made by a court.

In the family’s case, three attempts to make Mary (Nancy) Williams’s occupancy permissive each failed: 1949, 1958/9 and 1974. Each was followed by a registration of part of Parcel A in another name: the woodland (the 29 Mar 1961 deed; Forest of Cardiff, WA735527, from 1994) and the house (BP Properties Ltd, WA240304, 23 Feb 1983). Frederick Buckler married Mary (Nancy) Williams in 1936 and, from 1944, ran the farm as manager for her father (her statement, c.1974). 4 Jul 1955 · Parcel B repossessed
4 Jul 1955 · Possession of Parcel B, later misread and conflated with Parcel A; the woodland and the green were also seized, shortly after Mary returned from hospital with her leg amputated
VERIFIED · judgment
The Court of Appeal records that WGR obtained a High Court possession order against Frederick Buckler “in respect of the whole of the farm”, and: “On July 4 1955 that possession order was enforced, and possession was taken in respect of all parts of the farm except the farmhouse and garden.” It gives the reason: Mary “had recently come out of hospital after having had a leg amputated and was objecting very strongly to leaving the farmhouse.” What most likely happened: the order was made against Frederick, whose farm tenancy had ended, and the land WGR let is the eastern fields, Parcel B, on the 1939 WGR plan (EV-0023). On the 1947 Ordnance Survey (25-inch, Glamorgan sheet XLVII.6, revised 1941) the Ty-mawr house and yard sit between the wooded old limekiln and quarry ground to the north and the open ground to the south-east. The Three Parts of Parcel A page identified those two areas as Parcel A’s woodland and green; the family’s boundary plan shows the Parcel A line ending before the green begins, so the green is separate ground outside Parcel A. Taking “all parts of the farm except the farmhouse and garden” therefore took the woodland and the green along with Parcel B, and left Mary in the third part, the house.
