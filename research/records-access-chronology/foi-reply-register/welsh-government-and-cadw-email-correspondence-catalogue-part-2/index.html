Dear Dr Wigley,

Thank you for your response and for the further clarification concerning the surviving Cadw records and the historic retention arrangements.

Having considered your response alongside the evidence set out in my report of 10 August, I think it is important to distinguish between two quite separate propositions:

1. *Cadw has released all records concerning the 1988 assessment which it can now locate*; and
2. *the surviving records establish the evidential and administrative basis upon which the December 1988 decision was made.*

Your response appears to establish the first, but not the second.

That distinction is important because the surviving evidence is exceptionally limited: two photographs. The absence of the underlying assessment papers means that a number of historically significant questions cannot now be answered from Cadw's records.

I am therefore asking for clarification of the position rather than asking Cadw to recreate records which no longer exist.
1. What the surviving record establishes

Could Cadw please confirm whether the following is an accurate description of the present evidential position:

*Cadw's surviving records do not establish the evidential or administrative basis of the December 1988 decision not to list Great House Farm. In particular, the surviving records do not establish what evidence was considered by the inspector or decision-maker, whether the H.J. Thomas RCAHMW survey of 26 April 1974 was considered, whether the relevant SMR/GGAT archaeological material was considered, what inspection findings were recorded in December 1988, or whether any internal recommendation or consultation took place.*

If that is correct, I would be grateful if Cadw would confirm it expressly.

I am not suggesting that the absence of records proves that those matters did not occur. Equally, however, the absence of records means that Cadw cannot now establish that they did occur.

That is the central evidential limitation created by the loss of the 1988 working papers.
2. The 1974 H.J. Thomas RCAHMW survey

Your response says:

"we are unable to determine whether or not the 1974 survey was taken into account at the time."

That is a particularly important clarification.

The RCAHMW survey was not a document subsequently created in response to the dispute. It was a professional survey undertaken on 26 April 1974 and recorded architectural features including the late-17th-century construction, Type A sub-medieval cross-passage plan, substantial timber framing and the barrel-vaulted cellar.

I therefore ask Cadw to confirm simply:

*Cadw cannot now establish from its surviving records whether the inspector or decision-maker responsible for the December 1988 assessment had considered the H.J. Thomas survey before concluding that Great House Farm was "not of sufficient architectural merit".*

If that is Cadw's position, I will record it as such.
3. The archaeological evidence

The same issue appears to arise in relation to the archaeological assessment.

My report identifies evidence predating December 1988 concerning Roman, Iron Age and medieval activity at the site, including the recorded SMR material and the 1978–80 archaeological investigations.

I appreciate that the later 1994 excavation cannot simply be projected backwards and treated as evidence which Cadw necessarily possessed in 1988.

The relevant question is narrower:

*Can Cadw now establish, from any surviving record, what archaeological information was actually considered when the December 1988 conclusion was reached that the site was "not of sufficient archaeological interest"?*

If it cannot, please confirm that this cannot now be established.

This would avoid conflating the later archaeological discoveries with the separate question of what information was available to Cadw in 1988.
4. The July "YYY" coding

There remains an important unresolved point concerning the July 1988 photographs.

Cadw has previously explained that the "YYY" coding indicated that the building did not meet the listing criteria. The photographs were taken on 29 July 1988.

The contemporary press report of 3 December 1988, however, records Cadw as saying that it was then "considering" asking the Secretary of State to protect Great House Farm as a listed building and that an inspector would visit that weekend.

I appreciate that the records necessary to resolve this may no longer exist.

Nevertheless, could Cadw confirm whether it can establish:

– what "YYY" specifically represented within the July 1988 process;
– whether it was a final assessment or provisional coding;
– whether it was applied by the July inspector;
– whether the July assessment was subsequently reviewed in December; and
– whether the December inspector had access to the July photographs or any associated assessment.

If none of these matters can now be established, I would be grateful for that to be confirmed rather than inferred.
5. Identity of the December decision-maker

One further factual gap remains.

Can Cadw establish the identity of:

– the inspector who undertook the December 1988 inspection; and
– the person who made or authorised the decision communicated on 5 December 1988 that Great House Farm was not of sufficient architectural merit and archaeological interest?

If the relevant individual(s) cannot now be identified from surviving records, please confirm that.

This is not a request for personal information beyond what would ordinarily form part of the historical administrative record. It is simply an attempt to establish whether the decision itself can still be attributed to an identifiable office-holder.
6. The retention explanation

I also wish to clarify an important qualification in your latest response.

You state that current colleagues have advised that, based on their knowledge of the systems then in place, unsuccessful listing files were *"likely"* to have been retained for approximately three years.

I understand that this is necessarily a reconstruction of historic practice because the relevant records no longer exist.

Could you therefore confirm whether Cadw has located:

– a 1980s retention schedule;
– a disposal or destruction policy applicable to the relevant records;
– a disposal schedule;
– a destruction certificate or log;
– a transfer/accession record to RCAHMW or another repository; or
– any other contemporaneous record showing what happened to the Great House Farm material.

If none has been located, I would be grateful if Cadw could state expressly that *the approximately three-year retention period is an account of the general historic practice as presently understood by current staff, and not documentary evidence establishing that the Great House Farm records were actually destroyed after three years.*

That is an important distinction for the historical record.
7. What I am seeking from Cadw

I am not asking Cadw to speculate about matters for which the documentary evidence has disappeared.

Indeed, the purpose of my report was deliberately to separate:

*what is established; what is a reasonable inference; and what can no longer be verified because the records do not survive.*

Your latest response is helpful in establishing the third category.

I would therefore be grateful if Cadw could confirm, insofar as its records permit, the following concise position:

*The surviving Cadw records relating to the December 1988 Great House Farm assessment consist of two photographs. Cadw cannot now establish from surviving records the detailed evidential or administrative basis of the decision, including whether the 1974 H.J. Thomas RCAHMW survey or relevant archaeological records were considered. Cadw has not located records establishing the disposal or transfer of the missing assessment material, and the approximately three-year retention period represents the best present understanding of historic practice rather than documentary evidence concerning the disposal of this particular file.*

If that accurately reflects Cadw's position, I would regard that clarification as extremely useful.

I will separately approach Heneb concerning the Historic Environment Record, as you have advised, and will pursue the question of any appropriate heritage interpretation or marker with the relevant local authority.

I also appreciate your clarification that Cadw cannot require or facilitate installation of a marker.

My purpose in continuing this correspondence is therefore narrower than seeking to reopen the FOI request. I am seeking to ensure that the historical record does not inadvertently suggest that the absence of surviving records means that the basis of the 1988 decision has been established.

On the evidence presently available, the more accurate position appears to be that *the decision is documented, but its underlying evidential and administrative reasoning is no longer recoverable from Cadw's surviving records.*
