Mandatory dated-status rule — as at 10-10-2026: Any statement that a record was not found, is not held, or that an institution was not involved must be read as a source- and search-limited assessment, not proof of universal non-existence or non-involvement. Distinguish the authority’s response date from the Wiki’s current assessment date; attribute negative findings to the named source and identify the scope of the material reviewed. Update the as-at date when the evidence changes. Do not convert an unresolved discrepancy into a finding of wrongdoing.

Names are not listed as conflicts. The farm went by several names and functions; those are handled by the Nomenclature & Identity Map and its alias pages. A name appears below only where a body’s search or record was mis-assigned because of it (SC-07).

How the two parcels frame the conflicts

FAMILY CASE — contention, not a finding. Parcel A means the full 1877 house/quarrying estate: farmhouse/buildings, barn, stable and yard west of the house, garden, forest north of the house, and the ground south-east of the house/garden later registered as VG41. Parcel B is the separate eastern agricultural fields. The family’s established title case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 and Parcel A passed to the Williams family; Mary’s later practical defence of the occupied house/buildings/garden group did not limit the full estate she claimed. The 1939 WGR–John Williams agreement/plan is already obtained and depicts Parcel B only. [SOURCE PENDING] The 1949 effort to characterise Mary’s Parcel-A occupation as permissive failed (no written record of the 2 February 1949 re-grant; GHF-E122). Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 witness statement (Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony) records that the title papers disappeared in 1950–52. [SOURCE PENDING] WGR’s 4 July 1955 enforcement of Parcel B also seized Parcel-A north forest and south-east green under “whole farm” wording (writ and execution record sought: GHF-E126). Whether later actors knowingly promoted that conflation is a separate evidential question; it must not be inferred from the physical event or missing title papers alone. (A-058; 1877 Agreement — papers taken). 🕒

Most conflicts below come from one pattern: a body describes “the farm” as a single holding, or dates an event from that single-holding description, where the documents show two parcels and two registered titles until the amalgamation of 2 February 1987. The entries record what each body said; they do not, by themselves, decide what the documents show.

SC-47 — DSA/6/1158: 1938 sale particulars not yet assigned to the Penarth site

TYPE: NAME / LOCATION / TRANSACTION IDENTITY. The 11 June 2026 Glamorgan Archives email, retrospectively catalogued at GHF-E106-R52, is reported to describe DSA/6/1158 as Stephenson & Alexander particulars dated 15 November 1938 for “Great House Farm, Llandough”; the supplied summary identifies D.J. Jenkins as owner and a sale for £5,500 to L.D. Thomas of Porthcawl.

Status: OPEN — identity unconfirmed, not yet a demonstrated contradiction. The reference’s wording alone does not establish that the sale concerned Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr at Llandough-juxta-Penarth. The Wiki already has same-name holdings that must remain distinct unless parish, plan, acreage or other identifiers connect them. The original particulars and plan have not been attached to R52 in this update.

Why it matters if the location is confirmed: the reported Jenkins/Thomas parties would need to be reconciled with the separate Bute-to-Western Ground Rents conveyance of the reversion on the 1916 tenancy in 1938. Do not combine the two transactions, or use DSA/6/1158 as proof of a title transfer, before examining the source.

Test: obtain and inspect the complete DSA/6/1158 particulars and plan; check parish, homestead location, acreage, plan boundaries, vendor and purchaser; compare any identified property parcels against the 1938 WGR conveyance and 1939 tenancy plan. If the particulars concern another Llandough, record the correct site and close the Penarth link.

SC-45 — MS 3360/3 now has positive location identifiers

TYPE: NAME / LOCATION. The 5 October Amgueddfa Cymru reply left MS 3360/3 location-unconfirmed. The Museum’s follow-up of 7 October 2026 now records that “Great House” formed part of the 1924 Llandough Estate sale particulars, was occupied by W.G. Thomas & Sons, comprised approximately 202 acres, was situated in the Parish of Llanfair, and had its homestead in the Village of Llandough.

Status: Wiki corrected. These are positive identifiers and materially narrow the identity of the holding. They do not, by themselves, establish Llandough-juxta-Penarth or Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge because the Museum does not use either modern disambiguation in this reply and the underlying MS 3360/3 page/plan has not yet been independently examined. The Wiki should therefore quote the identifiers and keep the modern attribution open.

Separate point: Ref 13.2 remains catalogued only as “Waterwheel from Llandough Farm (opposite hospital)”. It is not thereby evidence of Great House Farm/Tŷ Mawr. The Museum also confirms that it does not hold a separate recorded audit schedule of item-by-item exclusions from the earlier search results; the next step is documentary reconstruction from the disclosed records, not asking the Museum to create such a schedule.

SC-46 — NPCC search methodology, recorded retrieval instructions and results

TYPE: SEARCH SCOPE / RECORDS CUSTODY / FRAMING.

What the earlier Wiki/register said: the NPCC FOI 3156/2026 response stated that no relevant information was identified in accessible NPCC/ACPO searches, and the record initially lacked the full search terms and retrieved lead responses.

What the three source letters now show: the 16 September FOI 3156/2026 response says no references to Great House Farm, Llandough, BP Properties Ltd v Buckler or the described 1988 operation were found in ACPO archive listings/catalogues available to the NPCC, and says no record is held of transfer, retention, disposal or current location. The 29 September reviews 3157/2026 and 3176/2026 uphold separate decisions. The 9 October FOI 3065/2026 response lists “Great House Farm” and “Llandough” as search terms; names NPCC shared drives, ChiefsNet, ACPO Archives, the Counter Terrorism Coordination Committee folder, and “Records of the Association of Chief Police Officers 1856–2005”; and identifies Crime Coordination Committee, Business Support, Counter Terrorism Coordination Committee and NaCTSO as consulted functions. It says no Q4–Q6 information is held, but supplies an actual retrieval email and says the results were captured by email and disclosed through earlier responses. 🕒

Assessment: the wording creates a legitimate clarification question about the boundary between formal search logs/consolidated results and recorded emails/instructions. It does not, by itself, prove an actual contradiction, that further records are held, deliberate concealment, destruction, or historic NPCC/NaCTSO operational involvement. The two-term list also does not itself demonstrate whether other search methods or terms were used.

Next test: reconcile all individual policing-lead responses with the earlier disclosures; inspect the full WhatDoTheyKnow incoming message and attachments; ask whether terms such as “Buckler”, “BP Properties”, the case citation and operational descriptions were used; identify the exact catalogue/reference for the “ACPO … 1856–2005” source; and pursue local-force/archive records separately. Do not ask the NPCC to invent a search log it says it does not hold.

SC-48 — When the farm’s water was cut off: c.1948–58 or c.1988

TYPE: DATE / RESPONSIBLE PARTY.

Source A (1958 press): Western Mail, 20 Nov 1958, p.7 (snippet only): “Mrs. Buckler claimed that the water was cut off from her house and grounds for nearly 10 years by order of Western Ground Rents.” That puts the start at about 1948–49 (inference) and names the landlord (EV-0037).

Source B (wiki review and 2026 correspondence): the Dŵr Cymru review dates the reported cut-off to 1988 and says the date and the responsible party are not established. Dŵr Cymru’s 2026 replies to Sion Buckler refer to “the disconnection which you believe took place during the 1980s”.

Oral version: per Branwen (voice testimony), the water was cut off while it was a dairy farm and a council was named (testimony). She lived at the farm until 1988, so any 1950s detail may be second-hand.

Assessment: open. There may have been two cut-offs; no document yet shows that. Source A is a report of Mrs Buckler’s claim, not proof of who ordered it.

Next test: page image of the 1958 article; Penarth UDC and Cardiff Waterworks Committee minutes, 1948–58; whether the farm was on the public main or a Bute estate spring (EV-0236).

SC-49 — Year of the Williams–Buckler marriage: 1935 or 1936

TYPE: DATE.

Source A: per Sion Buckler (19 Oct 2026), Mary (Nancy) Williams married in 1935.

Source B: the wiki’s family pages give 1936, citing a marriage certificate that is still a placeholder (to be obtained from the General Register Office) (The Williams / Buckler Family). Western Mail, 5 Aug 1936, p.10, reports the wedding at Llandough Church of Mr. Fred Buckler of Barry and Miss Mary Doreen (Nancy) Williams of Great House Farm (EV-0230, snippet only; the ceremony date is not in the snippet).

Assessment: open; the 1936 press report favours 1936. Next test: GRO marriage index, 1935–36, or the certificate.

MOD FOI2026/09190 — entry added 10-10-2026

Evidence: GHF-E169 — MOD internal review dated 10 October 2026.

The MOD states that its searches across MOD archives and targeted searches in the Army Secretariat and Counterterrorism and United Kingdom branches did not locate information matching the request. Separately, it neither confirms nor denies whether it holds Special Forces-related information under FOIA 2000, section 23(5). The review acknowledges delay and concludes that timeliness requirements were not met, without clearly identifying in its concluding paragraph which stage caused that finding. It says personal-information elements were referred for a separate subject access process.

Interpretive control: this is a dated record of the MOD’s statement, not proof that relevant historical records never existed or that military personnel were or were not involved. Keep FOI2026/09190 distinct from FOI2026/09019 unless the MOD confirms a relationship. Do not mark the subject access strand complete until its reference and substantive outcome are recorded.

Cross-reference — Cedwyn / Cydfin place-name evidence: References on this page to variants such as Cedwyn, Cydfin, Cedfin, Cydwin or Tŷ Mawr should be read alongside the GHF-E173 complete reference index and, where relevant, the Llandough (Penarth) pre-1877 manorial evidence page. Name similarity is a research lead, not proof of identical location, boundaries, ownership or title.

Cedwyn Estate theory cross-reference: Use the Cedwyn Estate Working Theory as a cross-cutting hypothesis when evaluating conflicts between authority statements and the wiki’s evidence and testimony. A conflict may be important evidence of an incorrect description or missing record, but it is not automatically evidence of deliberate concealment. Identify the disputed proposition, source language, parcel and alternative explanations.

SC-20 addendum — do not conflate the two D 219 farm entries

The NLW series D 219 catalogue explicitly distinguishes “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm” (107 acres; D219/1, 4–5, 8 et seq., 1552–1824) from “Llandough Farm” (166 acres; D219/9–13, 1824–1832). The 166-acre entry is not the Tŷ Mawr entry and must not be used to supply Great House Farm’s acreage. Source: official NLW D 219 description.

Keep the 1824 DB/E/1 plan 19 survey figure for “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm” separate as well: its current transcription gives 139a 3r 8p including woodland, with readings still provisional. These figures cannot be harmonised without comparing the deed boundaries, the survey schedule and the individual plans.

For the full dispositions of false matches, source duplication and contextual-only evidence, see the Kissed Frogs screening register. The 6 November 1877 Clyde Street deed is a Cardiff building lease; the reported Llandough instrument remains unlocated.
