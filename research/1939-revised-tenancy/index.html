Page ID: 21 · Title: 1938–39: Sale to WGR and the Revised Tenancy · Evidence status: Not yet labelled — see the Editing Protocol · Version: 0.1.1 · Editing Protocol

In 1938 the Bute Estate conveyed its reversion on the 1916 tenancy to Western Ground Rents Ltd (WGR) [record pending: EV-0019]. In May 1939 WGR granted John Williams a revised tenancy [EV-0023]. The 1939 agreement and plan are already obtained and analysed: the plan depicts Parcel B, the eastern agricultural fields, only [EV-0023]. The family’s established title case is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 and the full Parcel A passed to the Williams family; the 1939 plan is closed evidence of the distinct agricultural tenancy, not an open retrieval question.

What came before: 1877 sale of the house parcel · 1916 tenancy · 1928 last rent / Parcel A freehold  |  What happened next: 1944 WAEC; 1949 surrender · 1949 Frederick’s unwritten tenancy · 1955 order

1938: Conveyance to WGR
Deed Conveyance of the reversion, Bute Estate to Western Ground Rents, 1938

NOT YET DISCLOSED BY THE HOLDING PARTIES

The 1938 Bute–WGR conveyance is named as root of title in the 1965 contract supplied under Vale of Glamorgan FOI 00211027. The Vale has disclosed 2 of at least 10 documents in deed wallet 1756; no copy has been found in the catalogued Bute papers at Glamorgan Archives; HM Land Registry titles WA3996 and WA7355 have not yet been copied. An undisclosed copy is a retrieval status, not proof that the document is missing. See GHF-E121.

INFERENCE / PROVISIONAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT A TRANSCRIPTION OF THE 1938 DEED

The working hypothesis is that the 1938 conveyance transferred to WGR the reversion associated with the earlier agricultural tenancy, while the 1939 plan shows the fields east of the farmhouse rather than Parcel A [record pending: EV-0019]. If the operative schedule and plan confirm that limited description, it would help explain why the later agricultural-tenancy record does not itself establish that WGR acquired the house plot. The deed’s actual words, schedule, reservations and plan must be checked before treating this inference as confirmed. Compare the 1916 tenancy and plan and GHF-E121.

Catalogue: GHF-E021-01

Location: Leckwith Archives, Cardiff (Glamorgan Archives)

Evidence description: the conveyance of Bute’s reversion to WGR in 1938.

Parcel BOn the family’s title case, the 1877 arrangement placed the full house/quarrying estate on the Thomas/Williams side and took effect in 1928. The family’s interpretation of the 1938 reversion conveyance is that WGR received the remaining eastern agricultural fields, Parcel B; its operative wording and plan must be read with the already-established 1877/1928 chronology (Parcel B).

Interpretation & wider context. The family’s interpretation is that the 1938 reversion conveyance concerned WGR’s retained/eastern fields, not the full Parcel A. Its operative wording and any plan should corroborate that extent. The 1938 conveyance has not yet been disclosed; the targeted routes are the remaining wallet 1756 documents (Vale of Glamorgan Council) and HM Land Registry titles WA3996 and WA7355, and whether it was disclosed in the 1986–87 litigation.

Separate sale-particulars lead (R52): the 11 June 2026 Glamorgan Archives email summary reports DSA/6/1158, Stephenson & Alexander particulars dated 15 November 1938 for “Great House Farm, Llandough”, reportedly naming D.J. Jenkins as owner and a £5,500 sale to L.D. Thomas. The location of that property has not been established from the underlying particulars and plan. Do not merge this reported Jenkins–Thomas sale with the Bute-to-WGR reversionary conveyance recorded in this section. Resolve parish, plan, acreage and parties first; see GHF-E106-R52 and SC-47.

c.1940: Alternative limitation case — 12 years from 1928

Evidence: Mary (Nancy) Williams, statement, c. 1974 (catalogued here as GHF-E021-04; family archive; full card on the 1974 action)

Evidence description: Mary’s statement records the last rent to Alfred Thomas in 1928 and says she did not pay rent to, or acknowledge the title of, another landlord thereafter.

Full Parcel ARent-free occupation after the 1928 end of rent/quarrying: Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 witness statement (Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony) records the last rent paid in 1928 to Alfred Thomas and that she never paid rent to or acknowledged anyone as landlord.

Interpretation & wider context. The primary title case, resting on Mary (Nancy) Williams’s c.1974 witness statement (Mary (Nancy) Williams testimony), is that the 1877 arrangement took effect in 1928 and transferred the full Parcel A to the Williams family. Separately, the alternative limitation argument is that twelve years from 1928 would have matured by about 1940 if the applicable legal conditions were met. This calculation does not replace or narrow the underlying-title case, and counsel must check the historic statutory requirements.

Why it matters later: the 1939 plan shows Parcel B only [EV-0023], and Frederick’s unwritten 1949 agricultural tenancy continued Parcel B only and was also used in the first failed attempt to make Mary’s Parcel-A occupation appear permissive. In 1955, “whole farm” wording then blurred the distinction and WGR seized Parcel-A north forest and south-east green along with Parcel B [SOURCE PENDING: no execution record yet]. The 1962 order concerned the remaining occupied farmhouse/buildings/garden group, not the full Parcel A [record pending: EV-0041]. The 1938 conveyance and already-obtained 1939 tenancy are closed/held evidence; the downstream task is to trace later instruments and registrations against the full Parcel-A extent.
