What this measures

We read 19 clarification questions from public bodies in the case correspondence (February to October 2026). For each one we checked whether an email or link sent to that body, before the question, already gave the answer. 3 are rated strong, 5 moderate, 0 weak, and 11 were fair questions (see What cuts against).

This index describes conduct and its measurable effect. Each failing is framed as an open question, or a possible breach of a named duty: the FOIA s.10 time limit, the s.16 duty to advise and assist, the ICO's guidance on clarifying requests, the Civil Service Code, and the Nolan principles of openness and accountability. It makes no finding about motive.

The score

Score = 3S + 1.5M + 0.5W + D/10 + 2T + H − 0.5F

S
strong: a question whose answer was plainly in an earlier email or link to that body
M
moderate: answered before, but with a fair reading the other way
W
weak: arguably answered before
D
days lost, as counted on each card
T
statutory deadlines that passed (documented)
H
automatic notices that stopped a request with “no action”
F
fair questions to the same body (counted against the score)

How to prove it wrong. Each instance links to the question and to the earlier answer. An instance drops out, and the score is recomputed, if the earlier message was not sent to that body before the question, if it does not contain what was asked for, or if the body shows it never received it. Corrections from any body named here are welcome, and the inputs on each card are published so anyone can recompute the scores.
