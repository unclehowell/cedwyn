1. Manorial and ownership chain before 1877

PARTLY ARCHIVE-SUPPORTED — see the dedicated source page. The pre-1877 Llandough chronology can now be anchored to the Bute Estate’s own archival catalogue and to published historical and archaeological sources. The identity lock remains Llandough-juxta-Penarth / Cardiff, beside St Dochdwy’s; Cowbridge-area Llandough material is excluded. The individual D219 deeds still need to be inspected before the 107-acre Cydfin/Tŷ Mawr holding is mapped precisely onto the later Great House footprint.

  • c.1106: the published 1994 excavation report states that Llandough was granted to Tewkesbury Abbey at the close of the 11th century and that a grange was subsequently established.
  • 1535: Cardiff Records states that the manor of Llandough East belonged to the Abbot of Tewkesbury, with free and villein tenants paying rents of assize.
  • 1543–1545: Cardiff Records states that Henry VIII granted Llandough manor in 1543 to Lord Clynton and Say and Robert Turwitt; by 1545 it had been acquired by Sir George Herbert, as shown by a rent-roll; it further states that Sir George appears to have conveyed the manor to his grandson Sir William Herbert.
  • 1596: the same source records Landoche-juxta-Cardif as having free tenants, demesnes and copyholds.
  • 1767–1793: Cardiff Records places Llandough East, Dinas Powis and Cogan in the hands of William Hurst of Gabalva and Calvert Richard Jones in 1767 and says the subsequent history of Llandough followed that of Cogan, which was purchased by the Earl of Bute in 1793. This is wider manorial context, not proof by itself of a Great House conveyance. 🕒
  • 1552–1829 — major new archival lead: NLW Bute Estate Records series D 219 is a Llandough manorial lease series. Its catalogue explicitly identifies leases of “Cydfin Farm or Ty Mawr Farm, (107 a.)”, 1552–1824, including D219/1, D219/4–5 and D219/8 onward. The Bute Estate’s M17 description separately identifies the Llandough manorial material as being in Llandough near Penarth.
  • Later D219 material: the later deeds in the series are a priority target for reconstructing the early-19th-century ownership, tenancy, acreage and boundaries of Cydfin/Tŷ Mawr and testing the link to Great House Farm.
  • Research page: the detailed evidence, source links, caveats and outstanding checks are now recorded at Llandough (Penarth) — Cydfin / Tŷ Mawr and Pre-1877 Manorial Evidence.

Dating note. The archive’s own research notes withdraw an earlier 1215 construction date and suggest c.1500–1530 (attributed to Sir Mathew Cradock). Neither date is sourced. RCAHMW classes the house as sub-medieval, probably seventeenth-century, possibly with a medieval core (Where things stand).

2. Family evidence awaiting a source

  • Marconi, 1897 (FAMILY EVIDENCE). The archive names Thomas Williams of Great House as carting Marconi and his equipment to Lavernock Point. Canonical page: The Williams / Buckler Family, where the Marconi connection is recorded as unproved.
  • 1974 judge (FAMILY EVIDENCE). The archive names the judge who gave leave to enforce the 1962 order on 19 September 1974 as Judge Watkin Powell. To confirm from the county court file. Canonical page: The 1974 Licence Letters.
  • 1985 (FAMILY EVIDENCE). The family asked Cadw to investigate the site before any demolition and were told a formal application with evidence was needed. No correspondence located yet; compare Cadw 1988.
  • 1988 (FAMILY EVIDENCE). Before the demolition the family’s lawyers sought an injunction pending heritage review, a temporary injunction was granted, and the family held a press conference; local residents and councillors met in protest. No court or press record located yet. See 1988: Possession and Demolition.

3. Site and land-use leads

  • 1963 (UNRESOLVED). The Marconi Company (then part of English Electric) built a telecommunications facility near Great House Farm. Location and land to be checked against the parcels. 🕒
  • 1970 (UNRESOLVED). Llandough Primary School opened on land said to have formed part of the farm holding. To be checked against the parcels and the village green VG41 (Redevelopment). 🕒

Kissed Frogs: source-screening and scope register

What this page is about → Read the Docs

Scope test. Every item is assessed against three distinct questions: (1) title and parcel identity; (2) an evidenced act of fraud, concealment or knowing misrepresentation; and (3) a documented public-authority decision, handling step or involvement. A source may be irrelevant to title but useful to the government-involvement chronology. A source-code assertion, a repeated Wiki paragraph or an archive search result is not independent proof of the claim it repeats.

Leads retained as in-scope or potentially in-scope — not “kissed frogs”

  • 1881 “Llandough Home Farm” (EV-0224): in scope. The census label; per Sion Buckler it is Great House Farm. Directories put the same John Williams at “Great house” 1868–1901 (EV-0225). No other source uses “Home Farm”.
  • 1824 DB/E/1 plan 19, “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm” (EV-0211): relevant to identity, survey extent and mapping; the present transcription of 139a 3r 8p and the name “John Gedrick” remain provisional. It does not prove a later lease or freehold.
  • 1872–1876 Llandough poor-rate books (EV-0212): potentially important because the provisional read shows John Williams rated for a farm under Lord Bute and Daniel Thomas & Son for a lime kiln/quarry. The volume reference, row alignment and link to Tŷ Mawr must be verified.
  • Reported Bute–Thomas arrangement and reported John Williams lease (EV-0006; EV-0008): core unresolved leads for the 1877 transaction. The original and matching plan remain unlocated; do not substitute a Cardiff lease.
  • 1983 registration files and the 1987 title-file amalgamation: core title/public-administration evidence. Obtain the actual applications, supporting instruments, plans, correspondence and scope of the register change before asserting an error, concealment or knowledge.
  • 1987 Llandough Community Council minutes (EV-0214): retain under the public-authority/government-involvement track for the recorded right-of-way, VG41 and BP-agent discussion. They do not by themselves prove ownership, the content of legal advice, or a fraudulent act.

Current conclusion: This screening has excluded specific records as matches for the Llandough farm lease; it has not disproved a separate Llandough agreement. No item in this register, merely by being excluded or repeated online, proves fraud, concealment or government complicity. Keep original evidence, record each reason for exclusion, and reopen a lead only when a new reference, full image, plan or independently documented fact changes the test.
