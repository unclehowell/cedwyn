Purpose

This is the controlling legal-case layer for the Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr project. It connects the published evidence archive to the private execution record and prevents research activity from being mistaken for a legal claim.

Learn more

Control rule: no issue is treated as established merely because a record is missing, a public reply conflicts with another source, or a named family member’s testimony alleges wrongdoing. Each proposition must be tested against primary evidence, counter-evidence, standing, limitation, causation and remedy. 🕒

Case-control spine

Detailed control matrices: Limitation & Procedural Deadline Firewall · Claimant / Standing Register · 1987 Judgment Reconstruction · Title Chain / Parcel A–B · Mary (Nancy) Williams Estate Dossier · Evidence-to-Court

Learn more
  • 1. Limitation & procedural deadline firewall — claimant, cause of action, accrual, alternative accrual/discovery, limitation, procedural deadlines, preservation deadlines and last safe date.
  • 2. Claimant / standing register — identify the actual legal claimant for each issue. “Williams/Buckler family” is not itself a legal claimant.
  • 3. 1987 judgment reconstruction — proposition decided → evidence relied upon → evidence available then → evidence now → contrary evidence → materiality → possible remedy.
  • 4. Title / parcel chain — keep Parcel A, Parcel B, farmhouse and later combined-registration descriptions distinct until the underlying instrument proves the connection.
  • 5. Mary (Nancy) Williams estate evidence dossier — identity, occupation, possession, title evidence, correspondence, probate/death records, inheritance and post-death registration.
  • 6. Who knew what, when? — record each material fact, first-known date, decision-maker/custodian, evidence and later action.
  • 7. Counter-evidence & disconfirmation — actively record the strongest evidence against the family’s preferred interpretation.
  • 8. Evidence-to-court matrix — every material proposition must trace to its source, provenance, witness/document status and proposed legal use.

Separate legal workstreams

  • B — Registration: first registration, amalgamation, later dealings, alteration/rectification and indemnity.
  • C — 1987 judgment: pleadings, evidence, findings, appeal and later-discovered material.
  • D — Fraud / deceit: parked as an allegation until evidence identifies an act, actor, knowledge/dishonesty where legally required, materiality and causation.
  • E — Public authority: statutory function, decision, record trail, fairness, disclosure and correction.
  • F — Heritage / planning: planning, archaeology, listing and knowledge chronology, kept distinct from title.
  • G — Loss / remedy: possession, land, money, documents, artefacts, correction, acknowledgement and other legally available remedies.
  • H — Family / narrative justice: the historical and public record, without allowing narrative language to substitute for proof.

Decision gates

Learn more
  • Evidence gate: source identified, acquired or a defensible negative-search record exists.
  • Identity gate: the document, person, place, parcel and event are correctly identified.
  • Contradiction gate: the strongest contrary interpretation has been recorded and tested.
  • Legal gate: standing, jurisdiction, limitation and procedural route have been checked.
  • Action gate: complaint, disclosure request, correction, referral, ADR, pre-action or proceedings is selected for a defined reason.
  • Remedy gate: the requested outcome is legally available, proportionate and causally connected to the established facts.

Legal-status convention: DOCUMENTED FACT = supported by the cited record; FAMILY EVIDENCE / CONTENTION = evidence requiring assessment; LEGAL INFERENCE / ISSUE FOR COUNSEL = a legal proposition requiring qualified legal review. “Fraud”, “concealment”, “unlawful” and similar conclusions are not upgraded to fact without the necessary evidential and legal threshold.

New control model — cascade of accepted premises

The case-control spine now treats the central forensic question as a chain-of-premises problem: identify the first materially wrong, incomplete or unverified proposition; identify who introduced it; identify every later actor who relied upon it; identify the independent safeguard that should have caught it; and identify the document that proves whether the safeguard operated. This does not presume a conspiracy and does not excuse a later actor where the evidence establishes an independent duty or opportunity to correct the problem.

The new Forensic Control-Point Chain is now part of the case-control spine.

New control requirements

Learn more
  • Property-description mutation: record the exact land description and plan at each material date; do not assume “Great House Farm” is one invariant legal parcel.
  • Authority chain: record who requested, recommended, authorised, certified and executed each material step, and the source of that authority.
  • Evidence-before-court: compare the propositions actually placed before the 1984–87 court with what BP and the family possessed and disclosed at the time.
  • Remedy firewall: keep title, registration mistake/alteration, indemnity, fraud-based reopening and current administrative remedies as separate routes.
  • Institution isolation: each institution/company/individual is assessed on its own dedicated page; cross-links do not merge responsibility.

Real loss control — now operative

Learn more

The Control Room now treats the 1955 Parcel-A Loss Dossier as the central property-loss work package. Its chronology is fixed as the family’s case: 1928—1877 arrangement took effect and Parcel-A entitlement matured; 1949—first later effort to recast occupancy as permissive, before the title deed was reported missing in 1950–52; 1955—the “whole farm” wording blurred the Parcel-A/Parcel-B boundary and force was used to take the northern forest and south-eastern green along with Parcel B. Mary’s determined defence of the still-occupied house group, in the context of a recent leg amputation and seven children, must not be misread as her claiming only that smaller area or as surrendering the perimeter she could not physically defend. 🕒
