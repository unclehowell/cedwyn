Forensic Assessment — Vale of Glamorgan Council

Scope: the Council as (1) local planning authority holding the post-1988 development file for Great House Farm / Church View Close (including 89/01396/OUT and 92/00671/RES); (2) FOI/EIR controller for its own records; (3) Registration Service; (4) Democratic and Scrutiny services; (5) Monitoring Officer / Head of Legal and Democratic Services; (6) ward representation. Private title merits remain for courts and HM Land Registry; the Council is not asked to determine who owned Parcel A.

Family supplied images and the wiki link the same day. 30 September 2026: Registration Service stated the record is not held in the Vale and had been referred to Cardiff colleagues. No further Vale Registration Service reply appears in the Gmail set after that referral.

No further Democratic Services reply is present by the cut-off.

Monitoring Officer / DPO / Complaints

28 September 2026: formal concern, preservation notice and subject-access request addressed to Victoria Davidson (Monitoring Officer and Head of Legal and Democratic Services), DPO@valeofglamorgan.gov.uk and complaints@valeofglamorgan.gov.uk. No reply from any of those three addresses appears in the scanned Gmail set by 17 October 2026.

Ward councillor

28 September and 5 October 2026: correspondence with Cllr Christopher P Franks. Reply limited to personal acknowledgement and a prayer note. No substantive records, process or planning engagement is recorded.

Planning file gaps (recurrent)

Repeated requests for the ownership certificates that underpinned 89/01396/OUT, for appendices B–E of the 1989 planning officer’s report, for the condition 16–18 discharge chain, and for the 1994 Cotswold Archaeology / counsel’s-opinion material have produced only partial disclosure. Appendices C, D and E remain missing and are the subject of the 10 October 2026 formal chase under 00211557 and earlier references.

Forensic control tests

  • FOI/EIR controller: for every open request, record the statutory clock, the exact request wording, any clarification, the internal-review trigger date, and every chase. Silence after the internal-review request is itself a recordable event.
  • Planning authority: identify the decision-maker, the ownership certificate relied upon, the representations received, the conditions imposed, the discharge evidence, and any missing appendices. The Council is not asked to re-decide private title; it is asked to produce the documents it holds or held.
  • Registration Service: record the search performed, the negative result, and the onward referral. A negative search is useful only when the search parameters are stated.
  • Democratic / Scrutiny: record the topic proposed, the reason for decline, and any stated future opportunity.
  • Monitoring Officer: record the date of the formal concern / SAR / preservation notice and the absence or presence of any acknowledgment or substantive reply.

Current finding

As at 17 October 2026 the material reviewed does not establish Council-wide wrongdoing. Specific acts and omissions must be assessed individually. The outstanding internal review of 00210772, the still-missing planning appendices C–E, and the absence of any reply to the 28 September Monitoring Officer formal concern are the principal live accountability points. Partial disclosure on the planning file is not treated as full compliance.
