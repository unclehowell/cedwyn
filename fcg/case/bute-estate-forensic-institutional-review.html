iOutside records
How we treat outside records. Tŷ Mawr is first named in the record we have found in David Stewart’s 1824 survey (EV-0211). The Williams family acquired Tŷ Mawr in 1667 (sources: Mary Williams’ statement in the 1974 newspapers; ITV News, 1988). Earlier documents on this page that come from the Bute estate or other third parties, such as the Cydwin lease or a Bute catalogue heading, are not records of Tŷ Mawr: their application to it is under forensic examination, and we test them rather than repeat them. Assessed 14 October 2026. Scope: the Bute Estate of the Marquesses of Bute, lord of the manors of Llandough and Leckwith. Whether it ever held the Tŷ Mawr holding as freehold is what this review tests: no conveyance into Bute’s hands has been found, and the first record of the holding is Bute’s own 1824 survey. Its papers are held at Glamorgan Archives and the National Library of Wales. Inclusion is not a finding of fault. Substantive history is on The Bute Estate.

Current finding

As at 14 October 2026, the material reviewed does not establish wrongdoing by the Bute Estate. The wiki records the family evidence that the house parcel was conveyed to Daniel Thomas in 1877, so it does not treat Bute as holding an unbroken title to both parcels through 1938. How the estate acquired the freehold in the early nineteenth century is unresolved.

Forensic control test and next step

Examine the individual National Library of Wales D 219 deeds and plans and the Glamorgan Archives Bute catalogue items before any parcel conclusion is drawn. See also the Glamorgan Archives and National Library of Wales reviews. Acceptance: the 1877 and 1938 instruments and plans are read against each other and the result recorded.
