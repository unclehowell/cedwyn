4. Close-of-week update — 18 October 2026: Parcel A perimeter chronology

Reporting close: Sunday 18 October 2026, ahead of the week commencing Monday 19 October. Overall status: AMBER. A substantial new forensic assessment has been published, but its highest-value historical instruments remain uninspected and no finding of fraud or coordinated wrongdoing is recorded.

Published deliverable: Three Parts of Parcel A — Divergent Chronologies before 1987 Amalgamation. The page distinguishes the northern woodland, occupied farmhouse/buildings/garden, and south-east ground later associated with VG41. It tests the family’s proposed 1955 taking of the outer areas and the possibility that later dealings progressively established control around the occupied centre. The “perimeter capture / hostile acquisition / lawfare” framing is recorded as a hypothesis to be tested—not a concluded finding.

Material findings and unresolved control points

  • 1955 physical taking: the published Court of Appeal judgment records that on 4 July 1955 possession was taken of all parts of the farm except the farmhouse and garden; it records that Mary had recently returned from hospital after a leg amputation and strongly resisted leaving. This supplies the judicially recorded physical distinction, while the exact modern boundaries of the north and south components still need mapped proof. Source: BP Properties Ltd v Buckler (1987), paragraphs 6–7.
  • WGR knowledge: the same judgment records that by 1959 Mary was asserting title through her grandfather and refused a WGR tenancy offer for the house/garden. This supports a finding that WGR knew she disputed its claim to the occupied home. It does not, without more, prove that WGR knew a valid family title covered the woodland or VG41 ground, or that other bodies had equivalent notice.
  • 1961 northern deed lead: GHF-E129 identifies a 29 March 1961 deed involving WGR, South Western Brick & Tile and “Cardiff City”. The instrument and plan remain uninspected. Exact land, legal nature of the dealing, recipient identity, consideration, title basis and knowledge are unknown. Do not describe it as a completed transfer of the northern half until the deed and plan establish that.
  • 1962 order scope: the 1987 judgment describes the 11 December 1962 order as relating to the farmhouse and garden. A family-side proposition that the order or its underlying claim covered a larger Parcel-A extent including the forest is therefore a live documentary conflict. Obtain the sealed order, claim and plan, and identify any separate order before adopting that proposition.
  • 1970 VG41 — important statutory route: GHF-E133 records registration on 16 June 1970, but its original map, owner entry and full file are not yet obtained. Under the then Commons Registration Act 1965, a particular process could lead to a local authority being registered as owner and the land vesting in it if no owner had been registered and a Commons Commissioner was not satisfied that anyone else owned it. This is potentially much more specific than a generic adverse-possession theory, but only if the owner/Commissioner record proves that route was followed. Green registration by itself is not proof that this vesting process occurred; nor is it “adverse possession”. Official historic legislation: 1965 Act, section 1 and section 8.
  • 1972 planning: the Master Timeline indexes an earlier housing permission on 25 July 1972 while Mary still occupied the house. Read alongside the 1955, 1961 and 1970 events, this raises a credible question whether outer land transactions and development permissions progressively constrained the occupied holding. The planning file, landownership certificates, maps and correspondence are needed to test causation or coordination.
  • 1987 appeal scope: the appeal was limited to the farmhouse and garden. However, the judgment states that the possession order for the rest of the farm was not challenged on appeal because the adverse-possession claim failed on first-instance factual findings. Therefore, it is unsafe either to say the appeal determined every separate documentary-title issue over the north/south parcels or to say the rest was legally unaffected. Obtain the pleadings, trial findings, orders and plans to analyse what was actually determined and the effect of the unappealed part.
  • Limitation clock: Mary’s 1959/1965 protests may evidence her rejection of WGR’s permissive framing; they do not automatically interrupt a separate authority’s alleged adverse-possession period over a different polygon. The claimant, land, possession, intention, notices, proceedings and statutory mechanism must be matched. The 1987 judgment also records that WGR’s advisers wrongly assumed that visits and assertions of ownership alone preserved its recovery rights under the 1939 Act.

Close-of-week conclusion

The most significant new line is a possible two-sided perimeter chronology: the allegation under investigation (SOURCE PENDING) is that the north woodland and south-east ground were taken in 1955 while Mary remained in the central home; a 1961 deed may show later dealing with northern land; VG41 was registered in 1970 under a statute that could, in a specific procedural sequence, vest unowned green land in a local authority; and housing permission is indexed in 1972. If the same polygons can be matched and contemporaneous records show knowledge of Mary’s competing claim, the sequence could be materially probative of an overbroad title assertion or coordinated dispossession. At present the 1961 deed, VG41 ownership process and planning file are gaps, so coordination, fraud, concealment and “lawfare” remain investigative hypotheses.
