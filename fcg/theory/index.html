Competing theories, most plausible first

Five theories that could account for the record, ordered from most to least plausible on the evidence held today. Each is a theory, not a finding; the courts’ view is ranked with the rest. Evidence for and against each, with links to every record: 1000 Cuts on Cedwyn.

  1. The courts’ view. On this theory, possession was lawfully recovered and the paper title was never extinguished: the 1962 order stopped the 12-year period, and the 31 Oct 1974 letters made the later possession a licence. Would be settled by the 1938 Bute–Western Ground Rents conveyance plan, and any written rejection of the 1974 licence.
  2. Lawful steps, timed against the 12-year clock. On this theory, each step was lawful on its own, but together they kept the family’s possession from running 12 years without the ownership plea being decided. Would be settled by the 1974 Cardiff County Court file (HMCTS reply due 2 Nov 2026) and BP’s own files on the 1974 and 1983 steps.
  3. The farmhouse parcel stood outside the paper chain. On this theory, Parcel A, the house parcel, was never conveyed in the Bute → Western Ground Rents chain. Would be settled by the 1938 conveyance and plan, and the 1905 Bute conveyance.
  4. The 1983 registration misdescribed the occupation. On this theory, the farmhouse application, made while she lived there and examined after her death, did not give the Registry the occupier’s true position. Would be settled by the Land Registry’s 1983 application papers and letters (reply due 28 Oct 2026).
  5. A 1988 sale drove the clearance. On this theory, the site had become an asset for sale in 1988, and the clearance served that sale. Would be settled by sale particulars for 1988–93, or the Land Registry history of WA231076.
