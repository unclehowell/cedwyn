# Standing orders

These bind every agent, every cycle. When two orders conflict, the newer one wins; record the conflict in LESSONS.md.

## Public text
1. Never name the project lead. Present material as cited historic record, not as anyone's personal account.
2. Banned public words: the list is stored encoded so this page stays clean. Decode it once and load it into your scanner:
   `python3 -c "import base64,json;print(json.loads(base64.b64decode('WyJzYXlzIiwgImNsYWltcyIsICJhY2NvcmRpbmcgdG8iLCAiaGVhcnNheSIsICJ1bnZlcmlmaWVkIiwgImFsbGVnZWQiLCAiZnJhdWQiLCAic3RvbGVuIl0=')))"`
   Where a fact is not checked, write "not checked".
3. No family insignia or emblem designs. Nothing about the family's present holdings or addresses. No outbound links from Cedwyn.
4. No accusations against named private people. Public bodies are assessed on their records.
5. No hedging or disclaimer lines that downplay family evidence; unbacked points go in the small italic style instead.
6. Prices come from the store's data; never hard-code a price in copy. Any price or fact change triggers a sweep of everything scheduled or queued.
7. Photos taken on archive visits under a private-photography undertaking are never published. Personal social channels of family members are never linked or embedded.

7a. Text blocks show a title and two or three lines on load, then "Read more", which reveals one more paragraph at a time, with a new "Read more" at the end of each revealed paragraph, and "Less" to collapse back. The full text stays in the page for search engines and agents; without scripts everything shows. Never a heading followed by a bare "Read more" fold. Landing pages carry minimal text: titles, a few lines, then images, graphs, animations, infographics and video. A page with several prose blocks merges them into one segment.
7b. Pages never explain themselves to human readers; how the site works lives in the docs library and in machine-facing files. Front ends carry layout only; words and data come from the database, and the whole database is exportable.
7c. Public provenance names the source only: holder or archive, reference, date. Never who found, read or catalogued a record; no internal cycle or finding numbers. Change logs credit "Cedwyn editors".
7d. A new section is a new branch with its own data, registered in the site's section list by one site-wide pull request.
7e. Every e-book, digital art edition and documentary cut carries a whole-release semantic version, and one versions file on the site (/_cedwyn/versions.json) is the single source of truth for every version label. The newest release is v0.0.2, the paid edition (its price comes from the store's data, order 6); the one before it is v0.0.1, the free edition; older releases count back by creation date as -v0.0.2, -v0.0.3 and so on (the series starts at -v0.0.2; there is no -v0.0.1) (the minus goes before the v), all free. This applies site-wide, documentary scenes included. Each version is its own hashtag chip with no name in it (#v0.0.2, #v0.0.1, and for older releases #_v0.0.2, #_v0.0.3, where the underscore stands in for the minus): never a range chip, and version hashtags never go into social posts (30 Oct 2026). The version picker opens on the latest release, with a link to the free edition beneath it. Art editions show a disabled "Coming soon" in place of a buy button until sales open. Superseded in part on 1 Nov 2026 by order 47 (version ladder and version picker).
7f. Generated or edited media (art editions, quote cards, film stills and sets) use as sources only images confirmed as the farm, or catalogued records of it (maps, plans, documents). Images withdrawn as not of the farm never serve as sources anywhere.
7g. Every attachment on an institution's reply becomes an evidence record with its own page at /research/artifacts/<catalogue-id>/: a preview first (personal data boxed out), then three segments: Transcript, Artifact description, Our integration. Logos, letterheads and email signature images are not artefacts. Material from personal-data requests is read but held privately.
7h. Visitor and destination pages (tours, places) lead with history, heritage, family, landscape and what a visitor sees and does. The legal dispute is one short point among many there: a line or two and a link to the research section, never the headline and never longer than the other points.
7i. Tour stops label what stands today as Real and what is reconstructed as Augmented (AR), with a small badge per stop. Anything that physically stands is never shown as a hologram or virtual.
7j. The site publishes its own corrections. When something we published proves wrong, it is corrected on every page, file and image where it appears (the image first), logged as a dated entry in the public register of disproven theories and corrections (what we stated, where and when, what the record shows with its source, when corrected), and raised as a change request in the project records. Outreach that carried the error gets a short correction draft for the project lead's sign-off.
7k. No outbound links and no "leaving the site" interstitials: every outside reference points to the site's own snapshot page at /wayback/<slug>/ (single project-relevant pages only, never whole sites; links inside a snapshot removed unless they lead to another snapshot). Beneath each snapshot, the archive capture reference and the original address appear as the only exception.
7l. Facts on Cedwyn link to catalogued evidence pages, not inline citations. State the fact plainly and link the EV catalogue page(s); the bibliographic detail (title, publisher, year, page) lives on the catalogue page itself. Every evidence item referenced must exist as a catalogue page; if one is missing, catalogue it first (30 Oct 2026).
7m. The documentary: versions follow 7e, shown as hashtag chips under each scene with the free version selected by default. One page-wide card-size control (small, medium, large, remembered per device) sets every scene card at once, before browsing. No dubbing: each language (Welsh, English, Spanish) is its own separately performed video; older dubbed cuts stay as older versions. The voice matches the on-screen speaker's gender in every language track, and a new period brings a new cast and new voices. Every shot is motion video, never stills or photo slides. Audio defaults to Welsh on every door, with subtitles defaulting to the site's selected language. Cast changes with the period: a character appears only within their own lifetime. No fourth wall: characters live in their own period, know nothing of later buildings, records or the wider story, and speak only to each other, themselves or animals, never to camera. Only our own reconstructions are animated; archive scans are never animated or otherwise altered and appear unchanged in the scene's evidence list (updated 30 Oct 2026).
7n. The research archive stays open: the evidence catalogue, chronology, missing-records register and snapshots are never put behind a paywall or a login (30 Oct 2026).
7o. Public voice is neutral, third person and encyclopaedic. Public pages never address the project lead or the reader as the owner of an idea ("your theory", "you asked"), and never carry agent instructions, to-dos, internal notes or ids; state the fact or the open question ("The working theory also places…", "Open question: whether…"). First-hand statements are attributed as "testimony (date)"; never as a collective "account" or "family testimony", and a collective noun is never written in place of a named person (1 Nov 2026). Quoted third-party mail keeps its own words. A guard on the site build scans the visible text of every human page for these phrases (30 Oct 2026).
7p. Family wording: the Williams/Buckler family at Great House Farm is never described as "tenant farmers", and the land is never described as tenanted as if that were settled. Estate-side paperwork, such as a 1916 tenancy, is described as "a Bute estate tenancy document": the estate's paper, and exactly what is contested. Living family witnesses who were present through the events give first-hand testimony, never a "caption", "tradition" or "family story"; private witnesses are not named in public text. The family's tree-planting photograph is a family heirloom recording the family's native custom of planting to mark their own land; its caption is the family's own record, never "a library caption" (30 Oct 2026).
7q. Human text pages are minimal cards: a header, a four-line paragraph and "Read more". Read more enters a deeper level of up to four child cards with a doorway zoom (the level being left scales up and away); Back, browser back and Esc reverse it. Page scrolling stays on. Where this conflicts with 7a, the card levels win (30 Oct 2026).
7r. Menu tiles: Wales tiles are wireframe-hologram renders of building photographs from the site's own archive; Argentina tiles are digitally restored farm photographs from the archive. Decorative digital-art paintings stay on the art pages, with one exception: share cards use them (30 Oct 2026).
7s. Data architecture: one central catalogue (evidence, artefacts, places, people and bodies, events, leads, missing records) is the master record. Each webapp (site area) derives its own small view database from it by a written selection rule, and its pages render from that view as near-A2UI component JSON through one shared renderer that reuses the existing styles, so the design and experience stay as they are. No records are hard-coded in pages. Webapps migrate one at a time, each with an on/off switch for rollback, and the plan, work packages and measurable tasks are written in the project records before development starts (30 Oct 2026).

## Research purpose (1 Nov 2026)
50. Purpose of the research: reconstruct what most likely happened. Working theories are numbered and tested against the record, never asserted; each carries what supports it and what cuts against it, and public pages present it as a working theory, never as a finding (1 Nov 2026).

## The whole account (1 Nov 2026)
51. The site is the full chronological account of the land, dated and sourced, and the better record. It is never written as a response to one party's case or to a court's framing; a court's or tribunal's wording is shown as that body's view. What the records establish (for example land recorded under separate registrations and a later merger) is stated as recorded, never called a theory, hypothesis or model; working theories (order 50) are only for what the records leave open. Loose descriptions in the records, such as a whole-property label that no plan defines, are shown plainly as written.
52. "Projects" means the site's /projects/ area and its branch. Plan lines live there, never in a CRM's deals or project features.

## The documentary tells the moment (1 Nov 2026)
53. Each scene shows what was there, or what happened, at that place in that phase, as best we know it. It never shows the evidence, its discovery, an excavation, an archive, a records office or a modern researcher. A scene built from a dig shows the people and the place of the period itself.
54. The evidence for a scene is one internal link under that scene's door, never inside the scene, its title, logline, description or captions. Descriptions say who is there and what happens, in the present of that moment.
55. Storytelling only: no psychoanalysing, no showing policy or bureaucracy, no archive-room or narrator framing. The film opens at c. 200 BC (late Iron Age); nothing is placed earlier unless a dated record of the land itself is found. Strict date order is enforced by the chronology gate, which must pass before any render or door change.

## Catalogue lock (owner instruction, 3 Nov 2026)

Existing evidence and artifact records are locked. No agent, seat or automation edits, rewords, moves or deletes an existing record. That covers `data/core/records/*`, EV / MR / GHF-E / artifact pages, evidence registers, and rows already in a findings CSV.

- **Adding** a new record is allowed. The `catalogue-lock` check lists every addition for review.
- **Changing** an existing record: open a pull request labelled `catalogue-change`. Give the record id, the before, the after, and the source that justifies the change. Then stop. The owner approves each change himself with the `owner-approved` label, or declines it.
- Never apply `owner-approved` yourself, and never write a `Catalogue-approved-by:` trailer.
- The `catalogue-lock` check on unclehowell/cedwyn fails any unapproved edit of an existing record. It runs on pull requests and pushes to main, and audits `section/research` and `core/*` every 6 hours.
- Interpretation, theory framing and wording changes go on their own pages (theories, findings, journal), never into an existing evidence record.
97. Statements on the record are cited, never hedged: a statement a person made in a newspaper, an inquiry or a document is cited to that record (title, date, page or catalogue id) and quoted as printed. Never call it "family testimony" or "our recollection", and never imply it was imagined; if the record has not been found yet, find it before publishing the line (7 Nov 2026).
98. When asked how a company obtained title, answer the acquisition chain first: each conveyance, purchase or registration in date order, with the record that shows it. Do not open with the bare fact that a party held paper title (8 Nov 2026).
99. Preflight gate: never start a job you cannot finish. Before any long or quota-heavy step (bulk commits or issue runs, a deploy, a build, a render or upload, a database write), estimate its cost from recent runs of the same kind (median and 90th percentile), compare it with what is left of each limit that binds (daily allowance, API quota, disk, box load, file-count caps) minus what other scheduled jobs will use before the reset, and defer if it does not fit. When the main job defers, walk the open items cheapest first and take the first one that fits; the walk counts its own cost and stops when the allowance would go on the checking itself. A gate that errors never blocks the work (8 Nov 2026).
