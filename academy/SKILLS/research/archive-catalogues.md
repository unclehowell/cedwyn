# Skill: reading archive catalogues

**Use when:** a catalogue entry, not the document itself, is the evidence.

1. A catalogue heading is the archivist's description, not the document's words. Quote it as "catalogued as …".
2. A date range on a collection heading is the range of the collection, not the date of any one mention.
3. Catalogue entries of later assignments can carry the terms of lost earlier deeds; record them as pointers to the deed, then seek the deed.
4. Some national catalogues sit behind bot protection: plain fetches fail; use a real browser session and save the page text with its URL and date.
5. Record each item as: repository, reference, catalogue words, date range, what it would prove, whether the document itself has been read.
6. Local series worth knowing: Glamorgan Archives council series DCVG/C/4/36/11, DCVG/C/1/2, DCVG/C/2/1 and SC/C/1/2 (1974); the National Library of Wales Bute estate catalogue (heading D 219; the D 205/6–7 exchange papers).
7. Searching inside printed books (1 Nov 2026): the Internet Archive's full-text search (with an identifier scope to search inside one book) and Open Library's search-inside are the routes that keep working; one large book search service hit its daily quota and a library full-text service answered with a challenge page. Keep a list of retired phrases so a run never repeats a search an earlier run already exhausted.
8. National catalogue search (1 Nov 2026): rare name variants return almost nothing, while short place-and-subject phrases (a railway, a lease, a neighbouring township with the parish name) still surface new records.
9. Printed directories and the Gazette (1 Nov 2026): a university's historical directories collection holds the Glamorgan directories of 1849–1918 with item-level search and page text; the London Gazette's full-text notice search can be limited by publish date and returns page PDFs. Gazette scans are two-column, so the text interleaves: re-join it by hand and cite the issue and page.
10. Neighbouring names (1 Nov 2026): the Cardiff directories print Llandough under their adjacent villages, next to Lisvane, which has its own Tŷ Mawr. Before a directory line becomes a row, check the village heading above it, and never take a same-named house in a neighbouring village as the farm.
11. The national archives catalogue has an open search interface that answers JSON (1 Nov 2026). Quoted phrases and long queries return nothing: use one to three plain words and filter by holding repository in code. A reply that is not JSON means try again later, not "no results". It also carries collection-level entries for the county archive.
12. Some county archive catalogues render only with JavaScript, so a plain fetch returns no record text: read them in a real browser session, or start from the national catalogue's entries for that archive (1 Nov 2026).
13. Parliamentary debates (1 Nov 2026): the live debates search answers plain fetches with a bot check; use the printed volumes through full-text search, or the static historic pages by date.
14. Source lists in journals carry archive references (1 Nov 2026): an identifier-scoped full-text search on a distinctive phrase, then on the next phrase, rebuilds a whole list entry from a loan-only book. For a large directory, read a few page heads to bracket the alphabet before searching page by page.
15. When the national catalogue's record page answers "accepted, try later", its open interface's record-details call usually answers at once; archived PDFs download through the capture's original-file form (the timestamp followed by `id_`) with an ordinary browser identity (3 Nov 2026).

- Parliamentary records by company name (4 Nov 2026): search the parliamentary record (Hansard) by the exact name of each company in the title chain, not only by place. It surfaces official mentions the site may hold only as snippets; log each hit with its sitting date and column.
- The company register's public search and company pages read cleanly by plain script fetch, so officers, filing history and dissolution dates can be logged with their page URL and read date (4 Nov 2026).
- Printed city records with a place-name schedule (for example the Cardiff Records volumes) are a rich source of minor field and farm names with dated forms; read the whole schedule, not only the first letters of the alphabet, and log each form with volume and page (4 Nov 2026).
- **Out-of-county holders (5 Nov 2026).** A National Archives Discovery search restricted to records held by other repositories, on the parish name and its neighbours, surfaces holders outside the home county (one turned up a West Country record office). Run it each pass before deciding which archives to ask.
- **Whole catalogue descriptions (7 Nov 2026).** Where the national library's catalogue search cuts a description short, the record's XML view carries the whole text (unescape it before quoting).
- **Archaeology Data Service (7 Nov 2026).** Its data-catalogue search interface returns JSON with the full text of regional historic environment record and royal commission entries; page through results. The national legislation site lists statutory instruments by place name in a data feed.
- **Probate calendars, full text (8 Nov 2026).** The national probate search service's calendars for 1858–1996 can be searched by words across a range of years in one query, with no sign-in; the page text of each calendar entry comes back with the result. Search the surname with the forename in quotes, then read the entry for the date of death, the grant date and the value; log each with calendar year and page.
