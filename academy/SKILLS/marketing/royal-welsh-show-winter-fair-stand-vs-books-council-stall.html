Royal Welsh Show and Winter Fair: a stand, or the Books Council's stand?

What it is: Wales's biggest show audience. - The Royal Welsh Show brings about 250,000 visitors over 4 days (a Welsh Pony & Cob Society post; a WalesOnline report of an earlier year gives "almost 240,000"). The 2027 show runs Mon 19 – Thu 22 July at Llanelwedd, and stand Expressions of Interest are open now. - The Winter Fair runs 30 Nov – 1 Dec 2026, with "hundreds of trade stands" and Christmas shopping (Visit Wales listing).

There are two routes. Rent our own retail stand, or get the books onto the stand the Books Council of Wales runs at the show (it posts its "bestsellers at the Royal Welsh Show 2026").

Facts read (30 Oct 2026)

  • Business Wales, RWAS trader survey (read):
  • visitors spend almost £10m at the show;
  • 82% of traders are returning;
  • 83% say the visitor profile met their expectations;
  • 9 in 10 would come back;
  • 70% came to promote their brand and 55% hoped for direct sales;
  • 96% would recommend it.
  • 2027 "Before You Apply" booklet (rwas.wales PDF): the server refused direct reads with a 403, so these come from the search snippet only, not checked:
  • £11.94/m² for one category;
  • £26.74/m² for commercial/retail stands;
  • a minimum of 3 m frontage × 6 m depth.
  • So a minimum retail pitch of 18 m² ≈ £481 (VAT treatment not checked) before passes, power and furniture (not checked).

  • Books Council of Wales:
  • It's a national charity, and its distribution centre supplies shops through Gwales ([address kept in the project workspace], [number kept in the project workspace]; books.wales).
  • It takes a book stall to the Royal Welsh and the Eisteddfod (its Instagram).
  • Its distribution generally needs a printed ISBN edition (not checked for e-book-only).

How to do it (steps)

  1. Decide by expected sales vs. cost. At £26.74/m² the pitch alone is about £481. With the 55% of traders who come for direct sales, you need roughly 100+ units at £5 just to cover the pitch. E-books don't sell well from a field: no stock to hand over, and patchy signal.
  2. The cheaper route first: a printed short edition, or a postcard/bookmark pack with a QR code, distributed through the Books Council. Ask (by draft) whether a Welsh-heritage local-history title qualifies for its catalogue and its show stall.
  3. If you take a stand: - share one with a heritage or food partner to split the 18 m²; - run a bilingual banner, a free taster (a QR to the free documentary scenes), and an email signup on a tablet that works offline; - give a show-only voucher code so sales can be counted after the show; - log scans, signups and voucher redemptions per day.
  4. Measure voucher redemptions plus UTM visits (?utm_source=rws27) in the 14 days after the show. RWAS's own survey shows most traders judge on brand, not till takings, so set the bar in advance.
