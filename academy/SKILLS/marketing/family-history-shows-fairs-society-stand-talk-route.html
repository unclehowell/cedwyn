The play

  1. Ride a society's stand rather than renting one. - Societies staff their own virtual or physical tables and stock local material. - Offer the society something for its stand: a free one-page "records for your parish" handout, or a QR card linking to their parish's page on our site. - Ask nothing in return beyond the society showing it. - This builds on the society-journal relationship, it doesn't replace it.
  2. Pitch a talk, not a stand. - Online shows run timetabled talks. A 20-30 minute "how to read a 19th-century farm lease / tithe apportionment, with a real Glamorgan example" talk is a teaching session, not a sales pitch, and it fits the audience: half of them are society members. - Talk calls fill months ahead, so pitch 4-6 months before the show.
  3. Small-press tables are the fallback only if money is ever approved. The cheapest real option seen is a £200 + VAT tabletop, with full payment 8 weeks out and no refund. Record it as a PROPOSAL, never book it.
  4. Bring a trackable handout. One QR to a single landing page tagged ?src=fhshow--, a free first edition offer, and the signup. Count the scans afterwards.

  • Offer it to Glamorgan FHS for their virtual stand, through the existing society-journal contact plan.
  • The outreach is a HubSpot draft only, sent by the project lead on his GO. Never contact private individuals.
  • Target 2: a talk pitch to the Family History Show organisers and to the FHF for a 2027 Really Useful show.
  • Subject: reading a Welsh farm's records, with one real dated record from the evidence catalogue.
  • The draft stays in HubSpot, in neutral teaching tone. Banned words don't appear, and nothing about family holdings or any case outcome is included.
  • Proposals, not actions: a £200 + VAT Family Tree Live tabletop or any paid stand gets one line in the project workspace (zero-spend rule).
  • Measure: scans and signups tagged fhshow-*. Stop if a show brings fewer than 10 visits.
