Goodreads Giveaways: what they cost, who can enter, and why we skip them (a gate)

Harvested 2026-10-30 09:15Z (skills harvest). Topic: e-book launch / reader discovery. 'goodreads giveaway' had 0 index hits before this run (Goodreads listing/author basics and LibraryThing giveaways are covered elsewhere).

What the evidence shows (read 30 Oct 2026 unless marked)

  • Price. Goodreads blog (Jan 2018 program, page read): Standard $119 per giveaway, Premium $599, print or Kindle ebook. The original Kindle beta post (read) set $119 for up to 100 Kindle copies. Authors in 2024-2025 still cite "$119 standard or $599 premium" (r/selfpublish thread, snippet only; Reddit blocked) and Goodreads' own author group repeats $119 + books + shipping for print (snippet). No free tier exists today.
  • Kindle giveaways need a Kindle edition in Amazon's store. Beta post (read): open to "KDP authors with an ebook in the Amazon store"; even ARCs "must be in a Kindle ebook format and loaded into the Kindle store before you list". A PDF sold direct is not eligible.
  • Who can enter. Giveaway Terms (read): legal residents of the 50 US states/DC and Canada with a Goodreads AND an Amazon.com or Amazon.ca account. Live listings show many as "US only". No UK, Wales or Argentina entrants.
  • What you get. Giveaway About page (read): entrants must mark the book Want to Read; Goodreads emails followers and Want-to-Read shelvers; Premium adds a featured slot and one email to non-winners. Reviews are optional ("Winners may review your book").
  • Real entry counts (Goodreads History giveaways, read live 30 Oct 2026): 50 print copies, 4,295 entries (Yap!, trade press); 10 print, 1,424 (The Comet); 5 print, 75 (The Political Supreme Court); 10 print, 6,200 (America Obscura). Spread is huge; small/niche titles sit at the low end.
  • Reviews are rare (practitioner testimony, snippet): self-publishers report giveaways "usually result in few or no reviews, which for the hefty $120 price tag..." (r/selfpublish 2024, snippet only, page blocked). Treat entrants as shelf-adds, not readers.

Decision rule (use before any paid reader-discovery promo)

  1. Is the book in the store the promo requires (Kindle/KDP here)? If no -> stop.
  2. Do the eligible entrants match our buyers' countries? If under 30% overlap -> stop.
  3. Is it free? If not -> it is a spend proposal for the owner with an expected cost per shelf-add and per review (assume <=2 reviews per 100 copies until our own data states otherwise).
  4. If run: one giveaway, track Want-to-Read shelvings before/after (Goodreads stats) and any sales lift on the store, compare with LibraryThing Member Giveaways (free).

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Verdict: SKIP. All three gates fail: the e-books are PDFs sold direct via /ebooks/ (no Kindle edition on KDP), entrants are US/Canada only while our readers are UK, Wales and Argentina, and it costs $119-$599 (no spend; prices and paid promos are the project lead's decision).
  • Free route instead: LibraryThing Member Giveaways (see librarything-early-reviewers-member-giveaways.md) and the ARC team skill.
  • Revisit only if the project lead chooses to publish a Kindle edition on KDP; then it becomes a spend proposal with the rule above, never automatic.
  • Nothing posted, no account touched, nothing sent.
