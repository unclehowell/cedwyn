Rotary club talks: the civic-audience speaker slot (no-pitch rules)

Date: 2026-10-26 (skills harvest 16:37). Zero spend. Drafts only; nothing sent.

What it is

Rotary clubs meet weekly or fortnightly; many reserve one meeting a month for a guest speaker. Audiences are local business owners, professionals and civic leaders: small (20-30) but well connected, often to local press, schools, libraries and other societies. Rotary in Southern Wales (District 1150) has 53 clubs with over 1,000 members (district homepage, read in full).

The rules matter more than anywhere else on the talk circuit. Rotary Club of Dumfries speaker guidelines (read in full): - after-lunch slot of 15-20 minutes; 20-30 members and guests at a typical meeting; about 50% of speakers use slides (bring your own laptop plus a flash drive); - send a one-paragraph bio and topic well before the date; the club publishes it on its website events page, Facebook and newsletter; - "Your presentation must avoid overt sales. Making a 'sales pitch' or directly soliciting purchases or donations is not in the spirit of Rotary"; non-political, non-sectarian; handouts welcome (bring your own copies); - speaker is the club's guest for lunch.

A US club's guidelines (Rotary Club of Enid, read in full) state the same: content "not designed to promote ... specific business, its services, or products", no request to purchase; printed materials can be placed on tables; 20-30 minute talk plus questions.

Local fact: Cowbridge Rotary (Vale of Glamorgan) meets Mondays 19:15 at The Bear Hotel, with the 3rd Monday a Speaker meeting; upcoming speakers listed on its page include a CPR demonstration (26 Oct 2026) and a Welsh actress and storyteller (16 Nov 2026) (read in full). So a heritage story fits their programme.

How to do it (steps)

  1. Pick clubs. Rotary GB&I club finder (rotary-ribi.org) lists each club's page, meeting pattern and often a "Speakers" page or contact. Vale first: Cowbridge, then Penarth, Barry, Cardiff clubs (pages not checked beyond Cowbridge).
  2. Cut the talk to 20 minutes. One story arc, 8-10 slides or none, a strong opening document image, finish on time (lunch clubs leave at the hour).
  3. Approach (draft only): to the club's speaker secretary/programme chair via the page's contact link: title, one-paragraph bio, 15/20/30-minute options, equipment needs, dates. Mention no product.
  4. On the day: no pitch. A handout per table (one side: three record images and dates; reverse: a QR code to the free edition and the newsletter sign-up). The handout is information, which the rules allow; the paid volumes are not mentioned from the lectern.
  5. The real payoff, ask after: the programme chair is often a connector. In the thank-you, ask whether another club, society or local paper would welcome the story (IngramSpark's speaking guide, by Jodee Blanco, updated 1 Apr 2026, read in full, calls this "Catalytic Outreach": ask your contact which other organisations might be interested). Log every referral in HubSpot.

Numbers and honesty check

  • District 1150: 53 clubs, 1,000+ members (district page, read in full).
  • Dumfries: 15-20 minute slot, 20-30 attendees, ~50% of speakers use slides (read in full; one club).
  • No source found for book or e-book sales from Rotary talks; the rules make direct sales unlikely by design. Measure referrals and sign-ups instead.
