What the evidence shows

  • Google Play Books, "Auto-narrated audiobooks" page (read in full, 26 Oct 2026):
  • Converts an existing EPUB into an audiobook "in under 2 hours". Languages: English, Spanish, French, German, Hindi, Brazilian Portuguese (no Welsh).
  • No charge to create, publish and download "for a limited time". Publisher gets 52% of auto-narrated sales on Google Play.
  • The e-book must be on sale on Google Play. Audio files can be downloaded and sold elsewhere, but anything sold elsewhere must also be on Google Play.
  • 50+ narrator voices; an editor lets you choose or suggest pronunciations for words.
  • Best fit: non-fiction with little dialogue, explicitly including history and biography; poor fit: books built on images, tables or data graphics.
  • Publisher quotes: Richard Charkin (Mensch Publishing) recommends it to non-fiction and academic publishers; University of Michigan Press values the usage reporting.
  • The Creative Penn interview with Ryan Dingler of Google Play Books (Aug 2022, read): 95% of e-books have no audiobook; fixing a mispronounced word takes minutes (re-publish); Google advises pricing auto-narrated audio closer to the e-book than to studio audio; no exclusivity; reader comment notes KDP Select (KU) authors cannot use it because Google needs the e-book (or a 20% preview) in its store. "Significant downloads" were mentioned with no figure given.
  • Revenue share also reported by Publishers Weekly (snippet; 35+ voices then): not checked.
  • No practitioner sales figures for auto-narrated titles were found this run. Treat it as a cheap extra format and shelf, not a traffic source (the Play e-book file already records one author with zero sales in 30 days on a bare listing).

How to do it

  1. Have the e-book live on Google Play Books (Partner Center, own publisher name, EPUB that passes EPUBCheck: epub-editions-sphinx-epubcheck.md).
  2. In Partner Center choose the book > create auto-narrated audiobook; assert audio rights for the chosen language and countries.
  3. Pick a British voice for EN, a Latin American Spanish voice for the ES edition.
  4. Fix pronunciations before publishing: go through every Welsh name (Llandough, Tŷ Mawr, Cedwyn, personal names) in the editor; save each fix. Ask a Welsh speaker to listen to the first 5 minutes.
  5. Remove or describe image-heavy pages (maps, tables) in the audio text; add a spoken line pointing to the website for the images.
  6. Price close to the e-book (Google's own advice). Any price is a proposal for the owner.
  7. Download the files. Optional: offer the audio as a bonus file in the direct bundle on /ebooks/ (allowed, because it stays on sale on Google Play too).
  8. Promote with a 60-90 s audio sample as a YouTube Short / IG reel, linking to the Play listing and /ebooks/ with UTMs.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Order of work: (1) owner decides the Play publisher name and supplies tax/bank details; (2) list the best-selling e-book first; (3) make the EN audio, then ES for Welsh Patagonia readers (an audio edition in Spanish is something they cannot easily get elsewhere).
  • Candidate first title: the one with the least reliance on maps and tables. The survey e-book is a poor fit unless the plan descriptions are rewritten for listening.
  • Wording inside the audio follows the book text as published: the 1824 David Stewart survey is the first record mention found; the family acquired Tŷ Mawr in 1667 (1974 newspapers; ITV News 1988); no Bute-era dates beyond 1824; no banned words; the owner is never named.
  • Disclosure: state on the listing and the /ebooks/ card that the narration is computer-generated (honest, and some stores refuse undisclosed AI audio).
  • Cost: £0 now; the "limited time" free creation may end, so check the page before starting.
  • Next step: a HubSpot task "[BIZDEV] audiobook pilot - blocked on Play account decision" under the existing Play-account owner item, not a new ask.
