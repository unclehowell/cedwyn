Skill #2 — Business research & strategy for a niche heritage e-book shop (2026)

Written 26 Oct 2026 from practitioner testimony.

What practitioners report

  • Local-history authors sell through talks and stalls, press releases to local papers, library sales, promotional articles in society journals and newsletters, and follow-up publicity after each event. ("How to Write and Publish Local and Family History", hoap.co.uk, chapter 8–9: Lectures and stalls; Press releases; Library sales; Promotional articles.) A US local-history author selling self-published photo books since 2001 reports selling out an edition (Wayne Glenn, Facebook, 2025).
  • History niches grow fast on Facebook: one publisher network reports an Ancient Egypt page going from 0 to 40,000 followers in 2 months and a Roman Empire page with tens of thousands of engagements, on a $3/day boost budget, and states replying to every comment multiplied engagement. (Ezoic live case study, Jul 2024.) Cedwyn has zero spend, so the organic half (daily posts, reply to every comment, share-worthy record images) is ours; the $3/day half is the project lead's call.
  • Direct sales: a Gumroad sales page averages about 3.2% conversion (insightraider, 2026). With about 17 UK visits/day to Cedwyn, that is under one sale every two days even at an average conversion rate, so TRAFFIC is the bottleneck, not the shop.
  • Welcome flows and weekly consistency drive list growth (see skills/1.md).

How-to (Cedwyn)

  1. Each day, pick the single bottleneck stage with numbers: traffic → signup → read → sale. Today it is traffic (17 UK visits/day, 0 real signups).
  2. Spend effort where the audience already gathers: Welsh history societies and their newsletters, family-history societies, Facebook history groups, YouTube viewers (1,067 views on the farm videos).
  3. Offer talks and articles before pitching books; society journals and newsletters reprint good stories.
  4. Every piece of content links a SPECIFIC Cedwyn page with UTM tags; measure by utm_content.
  5. Weekly: one decision note in the Cedwyn growth project record (what moved, what to stop, what to try next).

#2 3/26 How Welsh heritage places grow audiences

  • Scale context (gov.wales, AMION study for Cadw, 2025): Wales's historic environment brings in £738m a year and supports almost 18,000 jobs; castles and monuments draw over 13m visits a year. The audience is there; small sites ride the big calendars rather than build their own.
  • No small-site digital growth case study with numbers turned up in this pass (Museums Association "Museums Change Lives: Wales" case studies are qualitative). Honest read: thin evidence; treat as direction.
  • Applied: ride the national calendars already mapped in skills/heritage-anniversary-hashtag-calendar.md (Explore Your Archive 10 Nov and 21–29 Nov; St David's Day 1 Mar; Local & Community History Month in May; Cadw Open Doors in Sept, which needs the project lead's go-ahead to register).

#2 18/26 What makes heritage posts spread on Facebook (2025–26)

  • Meta's 2025 Widely Viewed Content report, as summarised by Buffer (2025): nearly 98% of posts US users viewed had no link. Keep the story inside the post (photo, text, carousel, reel); use links selectively.
  • Hootsuite (2025), quoting a social strategist: "Content that drives comments and shares will naturally get more visibility." Reply to every comment.
  • Archivists' Facebook groups (2025–26) complain about AI-made fake "history" posts. Our edge is the opposite: a real record image with its source line.
  • Link-post cap test for not checked Pages, about 2 a month (Mari Smith, Dec 2025; see facebook-group-community-and-link-limits.md).
  • Applied: the Page already carries 2–3 scheduled episode reels a day to Thu 29 Oct. New posts go on empty days only, as photo posts that tell the record in full, ending with one question. The page address goes in the text, not as a link card.

#2 19/26 Partnerships with local history societies

  • Long-run model (hoap.co.uk ch. 8–9): talks and stalls, articles for society journals and newsletters, library sales, then publicity after each event.
  • Constraint (the project lead 10:28 GMT): pitches go only to incorporated companies. Most local history societies are unincorporated or CIOs, so we can't send them pitches. What's left: (a) incorporated charitable companies (check on Companies House first); (b) organic presence, i.e. a reply when a society posts a request we can answer from the record, and being useful in their public threads; (c) Local & Community History Month (May) content they can share.
  • Applied: assets/talk-offer.md stays ready for incorporated bodies only. No contact made this run.

#2 21/26 Press angles for Welsh media (prepared; press only on the project lead's GO)

  • Desks: Nation.Cymru takes news and press releases at its general editor address (nation.cymru) and has a features editor. BBC local radio and regional TV "are often hungry for local interest stories" (Definition, "Our tips on pitching the BBC", May 2025). Pitch one desk and one angle at a time, never scattergun.
  • Angles from the record, Williams-first per the project lead's 12:01 rule: (1) one family on the same Llandough land for generations; the 1841 tithe lists John Williams with 36 plots, and the 1908 manor court remembered him as "for many years a juror of the court"; (2) a farm three miles from Lavernock Point, where Marconi's 1897 signals crossed open sea; (3) Great House II, the replica rising in Patagonia; (4) Enwau, a free register of over 100 names for one house; (5) Calan Gaeaf, Welsh winter-eve folklore (31 Oct).
  • Needs the project lead: a GO for any press contact.

Evidence (search snippets read 27 Oct; numbers NOT re-checked against the full source pages — treat as directional): - Base rates for self-published books: ~20% of new titles sell over 100 copies and ~6% over 1,000 (rollingindoh.substack.com, 'Untold story of book sales'); a 2026 r/selfpublish thread on a history book selling 40 copies in its first week repeats the folk figure '90% sell under 100'. => Our first 10 sales are the realistic near-term goal, not 100s. - Market: US adult nonfiction units down 9% in Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025 (Publishers Weekly via tim.blog, Jun 2026); AAP StatShot shows nonfiction +14% in July 2026 (janefriedman.com). Mixed — do not count on category tailwind. - Lead magnets: landing pages for lead magnets convert ~18% on average (shno.co compilation); MailerLite's analysis of 41,000 forms is the primary dataset behind most such figures. The much-quoted 'quiz 40.1% vs PDF 3.1%' is repeated everywhere with an unclear origin (gigradar.io calls it 'the most-quoted stat'), so it is NOT used as a fact. Short lead magnets (2,000-5,000 words, 10-25 pages) were reported to convert better by 58.6% of respondents in Designrr's survey. - Local history specifically: practitioners who sold their first copies did it through local history societies, clubs, schools and talks (justcuriousjane.com tips; historum.com thread on promoting a self-published history book). How we apply it to Cedwyn (actions, in order): 1. Shorten the free first edition hand-off: a 10-25 page free 'first edition' PDF converts better than a 27.7 MB wiki PDF on a phone (#23 4/4 found the wiki free PDF is 27.7 MB). Keep the free Cedwyn PDF (950 KB) as the default free download everywhere. 2. Free edition -> paid path: the last page of the free edition carries ONE next step (v0.0.2 at £4.99 via /ebooks/), per skills/ebook-back-matter-one-next-step.md. 3. Treat institutions (societies, heritage centres, walking operators) as the first-sale channel: queued 8 incorporated orgs today for the outreach engine; the engine is stalled until someone runs its daily step (flagged to the root). 4. Measure the funnel weekly with human visitors only (cf_traffic non-US/GB as the proxy), not raw hits. Sources: rollingindoh.substack.com ; reddit.com ; tim.blog ; janefriedman.com ; shno.co ; mailerlite.com ; gigradar.io ; designrr.io ; justcuriousjane.com
