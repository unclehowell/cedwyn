Paid-version pages in search: paywalled-content markup, lead-in, and the 10% paywall gauge

Checked 30 Oct 2026, ~11:00Z. Search for "isaccessibleforfree" in the skills index found 0 hits before this file.

What the primary sources say (read 30 Oct)

  • Google, "Structured data for subscription and paywalled content" (CreativeWork), read in full.
  • The markup exists so that Google can tell paywalled content apart from cloaking. Cloaking means showing Googlebot something different from what users see, and it breaks the spam policies.
  • It applies only to content you want crawled and indexed. If the paid text or video is never served to the browser, you don't need the markup.
  • Required: "isAccessibleForFree": false on the CreativeWork. Recommended: hasPart with {"@type":"WebPageElement","isAccessibleForFree":false,"cssSelector":".paywall"}.
  • Use .class selectors only. Don't nest sections. For several gated sections, give an array of hasPart items.
  • Supported types are CreativeWork and its subtypes, for example Article, Course, WebPage, and ["Article","LearningResource"].
  • If you don't want the content in the browser at serve time, use a paywall that doesn't send it. Client-side JavaScript paywalls have their own guidance page.
  • AI Overviews and AI Mode follow the normal preview controls (nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet).
  • Google, "Flexible sampling", read in full. This comes from Google's experiments with publisher partners.
  • "Even minor changes to the current sampling levels could degrade user experience and … unintentionally impact article ranking."
  • Lead-in (show the opening, gate the rest) is called "a good practice". It shows readers the value of the content and "generates user curiosity … which may assist in conversion."
  • The gauge: "general user satisfaction starts to degrade significantly when paywalls are shown more than 10% of the time (which generally means that about 3% of the audience has been exposed to the paywall)."
  • Users who have seen only a little content before they must pay lose interest "greatly".
  • The metering starting point for news is 10 free items a month, with 6-10 typical. That suits news; it is not a rule for books.
  • Playwire's guide (snippet only, not read) gives the same JSON-LD patterns and anti-cloaking rule. It adds nothing new.

Caveat: these are Google's own experiment summaries. No numbers outside Google back them, and they were measured on news publishers, not book or film sellers.

The how-to

  1. Decide per page what is indexable. There are three page types: - Fully free version (whole text or film on the page): isAccessibleForFree: true, or leave it out. No paywall markup. - Paid version product page (description, sample, buy button; the paid content is never in the HTML): no paywall markup needed, because nothing is hidden from users that Googlebot sees. Use normal product or book markup. - A page that serves the paid content in the HTML but hides it with CSS/JS until unlock: needs isAccessibleForFree:false plus hasPart.cssSelector on the wrapper class. Otherwise it risks being read as cloaking. Better still, don't send the paid content until unlock.
  2. Always give a lead-in on paid pages. Show the first paragraph, chapter or scene for real, and put the gate after it. Never put a bare gate first.
  3. Measure paywall exposure. - Exposure rate = sessions that see a lock or buy gate ÷ all sessions. - Gate-shown rate = page views with the gate ÷ page views. - Above ~10% of views or ~3% of visitors, expect satisfaction, and possibly ranking, to suffer. Make the free path more visible rather than gating more.
  4. Check with tools. Run the Rich Results Test and URL Inspection ("View crawled page") so you know what Googlebot actually got.
  5. One change at a time, dated in an annotations log. Google states small changes in sampling can move rankings.

How we apply it to Cedwyn (e-books, documentary, academy, site)

Site facts checked live 30 Oct, cache-busted curl: - /ebooks/cedwyn/ shows v0.0.2 and v0.0.1 and carries canonical /ebooks/cedwyn/. It has no isAccessibleForFree markup. Free downloads go through /ebooks/api/free?book=<id>@<semver>, so the paid file is not in the page. That makes it page type 1 or 2, and no paywall markup is needed. - /documentary/ has no canonical tag, no VideoObject and no isAccessibleForFree. Its only JSON-LD is an agent-instruction WebPageElement. documentary.js 1.6.2 picks a cut with ?v= and unlocks the newest cut (v0.0.2, £4.99) per device after checkout (?unlock=cs_…).

Actions. All are PROPOSALS for the documentary and onpage owners. No price change, and no buy.stripe.com request from any script. 1. Documentary. - If the v0.0.2 scene file or subtitles are in the HTML or manifest before unlock, wrap the gated player in a .paid-cut class. - Add a VideoObject or Movie scene JSON-LD with isAccessibleForFree:false and hasPart → .paid-cut. - Give the free v0.0.1 cut its own VideoObject with isAccessibleForFree:true. - If the paid file is fetched only after unlock, mark only the free cut. 2. Lead-in on the paid cut. Show the first ~20-30 s of the v0.0.2 scene, or its first captioned line, before the lock, rather than a still and a price. This matches the existing sample-chapter skill (free-sample-chapter-read-before-buy.md) for the e-books. 3. Academy (/learn/). Do the same for any member-only lesson whose text is in the HTML: ["Course","LearningResource"] with isAccessibleForFree:false plus hasPart on the members' block. Public course outlines stay free. 4. Gauge. Add a weekly line to the KPI snapshot: gate views ÷ documentary views, and lock-clicks ÷ visitors, using the site's first-party beacon (campaign-tags skill). If gate views go above 10%, make the free v0.0.1 path more prominent. Don't gate more. 5. Verify. After any change, run the Rich Results Test and URL Inspection on /documentary/ and one e-book page, and record the result in the project workspace notes.
