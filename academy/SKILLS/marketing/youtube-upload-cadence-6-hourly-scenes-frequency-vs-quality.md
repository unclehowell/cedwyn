# Upload cadence for 6-hourly documentary scenes: frequency vs per-video quality

Date: 2026-10-27 · Trigger: the project lead 11:50 27 Oct moved the documentary to a new scene every 6 h; 12:50 "keep it free" (free tools only, so quality per scene is the constraint to watch).

## What the record shows
- vidIQ upload-frequency study (vendor data, read 27 Oct): 10,210,278 channels with 1,000+ subscribers. Channels averaging 12+ uploads/month had median monthly view growth 2.18% vs 0.53% for under 1/month (4.1x) and vs 0.89% for 1-3/month (2.45x); median subscriber growth rose from 0.00% to 0.91%. vidIQ's own wording: "an opportunity, not a guarantee". It is correlation across channels, not a test; channels that post more may differ in other ways.
- A second creator-tool analysis (Overseeros, snippet only, not checked) reports the same direction: faster median growth as frequency rises.
- YouTube's growth team (Todd Beaupré on Creator Insider, 2023/2025/2026 algorithm episodes; titles read, transcripts not checked) has repeatedly framed recommendations as following viewer satisfaction per video rather than a schedule. Treat "frequency itself is rewarded" as not checked.
- The top tier in the vidIQ data is 12+/month. A scene every 6 h is ~120/month, ten times past the measured top tier: there is no data that more than that helps, and every extra upload competes for the same small audience.

## How to do it
1. Separate the PRODUCTION cadence (a scene every 6 h, the project lead's rule) from the DISTRIBUTION cadence per channel. Production can stay 6-hourly on the owned page (/movie/); each external channel gets its own rhythm.
2. Per channel, start inside the measured top tier: YouTube long-form 3-4/week (12-16/month); Shorts/Reels 30 s cuts can run daily. Queue the rest as scheduled posts rather than dumping four a day.
3. Quality floor per upload, checked before it goes out: hook in the first 8 s, subtitles readable (subtitle_check.py), audio with no holes (audio-proof inspect), title/thumbnail tested where possible (YouTube Test & compare skill). A scene that fails the floor waits for the next slot.
4. Measure per upload, not per channel: views at 48 h and 7 d, average % viewed, subscribers gained. If views per upload fall steadily as cadence rises, the audience is saturated: slow distribution, keep production.
5. Notifications: subscribers' bell notifications on every upload at 4/day invite unsubscribes (no numbers found, not checked). Use the playlist + one weekly "new this week" post instead of four alerts a day.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Keep the 6-hourly scene on /movie/ exactly as the project lead set it (never change the cadence without asking him).
- On YouTube, publish the full 2-min scene at most once a day (or 3-4/week), with the newest scenes added to the series playlist; the 30 s cut of each scene goes to Shorts/Reels/Facebook as its own daily post.
- Record per-upload numbers in the Sunday growth report; if the 7-day views per scene drop for three weeks running, propose to the project lead a slower external rhythm (proposal only; his call).
- With free tools only (12:50 rule), the quality floor in step 3 matters more than volume: one strong scene beats four weak ones on the same day.
