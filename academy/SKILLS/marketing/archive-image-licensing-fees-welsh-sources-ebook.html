Archive-image licensing for a local-history e-book: what each Welsh source charges, and the free routes

Topic: costing and clearing archive photographs (RCAHMW/Coflein, Glamorgan Archives, NLW) before they go into a paid Cedwyn e-book or a paid documentary version. Harvested 29 Oct 2026.

What the sources say (read live 29 Oct 2026 unless marked)

RCAHMW / Coflein (rcahmw.gov.uk guide + Licence Fees page, read) - Coflein holds about 125,000 images, all free to view. Many are Crown copyright, administered by RCAHMW "sometimes for a fee"; many others belong to third-party donors, who must give permission themselves. - Free, no permission needed: education, personal/family/local-history research, personal use, and "social media and non-commercial websites (e.g. Facebook history pages, local history websites)". Crown images are covered by the Non-Commercial Government Licence. - Commercial use needs a licence before any reproduction. Per-image fees: - Books, e-books, commercial magazines, reports: run 1-500 = £10 UK / £25 world; 500-1,000 = £25 / £50; 1,001-3,000 = £35 / £70; 3,001-5,000 = £50 / £100. - Academic journal or charity publication: 1-500 = £5 / £15, up to £25 / £35 at 3,001-5,000. - RCAHMW generally asks for one copy of the publication to be deposited in its library.

Glamorgan Archives (glamarchives.gov.uk, read) - Digital image from existing files: £15.60 for the first image, £4.80 for each further image from the same document. Multiple documents: £60 per hour. Turnaround target is 15 working days. Payment is by cheque or similar. - Those are copying fees. Whether a separate publication permission or fee applies was not checked. Ask in the same order.

National Library of Wales (library.wales licensing page via Wayback 14 Oct 2026, read) - Restricted uses need a licence from the Enquiries Team. The request must state: reference or title, format and resolution (e.g. 300 dpi), the product (pamphlet / poster / website / e-book), the run (e.g. 500), the term (e.g. 3 years) and the territory (Wales / UK / worldwide). - Fees follow a "Rights fee schedule" (PDF not read). If the item is still in copyright, clearing the rights is the publisher's job.

UK law baseline (IPO Copyright Notice: digital images, photographs and the internet, read) - Image copyright is generally life + 70 years, varying with creation date. - Simply copying an image does not create a new copyright. However, there is "uncertainty" over whether retouched digitisations of out-of-copyright images carry one. - A picture library can still restrict out-of-copyright images by contract through its terms of supply. Clearing copyright does not clear the contract you accepted when you downloaded.

The playbook

  1. Inventory first. For every image in a planned paid product, record: source, reference number, creator, date, copyright status (Crown / third party / expired / ours), and the terms it was supplied under. Put this in one CSV beside the e-book build.
  2. Use free routes before paying: - images we made ourselves (photos, maps drawn from data, AI reconstructions clearly labelled); - our own archive copies of documents the family holds; - images whose copyright has clearly expired and that came with no restrictive contract, e.g. Wikimedia Commons public-domain files (see wikimedia-commons-public-domain-archive-images-glam.md); - non-commercial pages on Cedwyn, which keep Coflein images legal under the NCGL with the credit line.
  3. Licence only images that carry the story. At Cedwyn's real volumes, an e-book licence is a small fixed cost: 1-500 copies with UK rights is £10 per image at RCAHMW. Five key images cost £50 (UK) or £125 (world). Glamorgan Archives copies of five images from one document cost £15.60 + 4 × £4.80 = £34.80 before any permission fee.
  4. Pick rights to match the real channel. Stripe and Gumroad sell worldwide, so a "UK rights" licence does not fit a global download. Either licence world rights, or keep licensed images out of the paid file and show them on a free web page that the e-book links to.
  5. Write one licence request per source with NLW's six fields (ref, format/dpi, product, run, term, territory). The same fields work for RCAHMW and Glamorgan Archives. Ask for the exact credit line and keep the reply.
  6. Credit every image under it, in the source's own wording, and log the licence reference in the CSV.

Kill rule

If one image's clearance costs more than one month's e-book revenue, swap it for a free-route image or a link to the free page.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Build the project workspace at the next e-book build. Any row marked "Coflein, third party" or "unknown" blocks that image from the paid file until it is cleared.
  • Default: paid e-books and paid documentary versions (v0.0.2+) use our own images and AI reconstructions. Archive images stay on the free Cedwyn pages and evidence catalogue under the NCGL/non-commercial terms, linked from the e-book.
  • If an RCAHMW image is essential (e.g. a Coflein aerial of Llandough), the request is a HubSpot draft for the project lead's GO. Budget £25 per image for world rights at 1-500 copies, plus the deposit copy. It is never sent by us without his GO, and zero spend until he approves.
  • Glamorgan Archives orders (cheque route) are the project lead's to place. Note the publication-permission question in the same draft.
  • Public text on the site credits sources in their own wording. Nothing here changes the editorial rules: no banned words, no names of living private people.
