## Two routes, very different
1. Library-run event (the library invites you, promotes it, often free to you). Hard to get as an unknown local author: a librarian in r/Libraries writes that their last library held a yearly local-author day and did not host individual local authors, even framed as an "informative" programme (search snippet only, not checked). Langemack's librarian advice points the same way: if local authors "are yet to be famous", host several at once (panel, showcase) or build a series so patrons trust the slot.
2. Room hire (you book and promote it yourself). Always available, small fee, you carry the audience risk.

## How to pitch a library-run talk (Langemack's checklist, turned round)
Librarians decide on: why (library mission: promote reading, bring people in, local connection), who the audience is, expected numbers (twenty or two hundred), single speaker or panel, one-off or series, budget, promotion. A pitch that answers all of these up front is easy to say yes to.
- Lead with the community angle (local history of their own parish), not the book.
- Offer it free, with your own slides and kit, and a hand-out.
- Offer to join an existing slot (local-history month, a panel of local authors, Open Doors in September).
- Give long lead time; libraries plan months ahead.
- Ask if a short sales table or a QR card is allowed; many councils restrict selling.

## Run sheet for a hired room
1. Pick the room by realistic audience: start small (Rhoose 50 seats at £25, or a 12-seat makerspace). A full small room beats an empty big one.
2. Free, booked places; oversubscribe about 2x (free-event no-shows ~50%, see free-online-talk-listings-eventbrite-meetup.md).
3. Listings: local what's-on sites and the library's own noticeboard (local-whats-on-listings-newsquest-evvnt-cardiff.md); in-person talk page with Event markup (event-structured-data-in-person-talk-page.md).
4. On the night: 35-40 min talk, 15 min questions, one QR card to /ebooks/ and the newsletter signup (utm_source=library-talk&utm_campaign=<venue-date>). Sign-up sheet only with a clear consent line.
5. Afterwards: thank-you email to the library, notes and slides on the talk page, count bookings / attendees / signups / sales.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Owner decision first: who presents (in person), whether to spend the hire fee (Cedwyn rules are zero spend, so a hire fee needs the owner's explicit GO; a library-run free slot needs none).
- Fallback with owner GO: Rhoose Community Library, £25 for 3 hours, 50 seats.
- Success bar to repeat: 15+ attendees or one sale or 5 newsletter sign-ups.
