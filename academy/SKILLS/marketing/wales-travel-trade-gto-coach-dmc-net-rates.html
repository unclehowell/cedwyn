Wales travel trade for the Llandough guided tour: GTOs, coach operators and DMCs, net rates, Visit Wales listings and fam visits

Harvested 28 Oct 2026 (skills harvest run). Serves the programme-office objective "Launch the Llandough guided tour (Wales)". The marketplaces skill (tour-marketplaces-airbnb-viator-gyg-heritage-walk.md) already covers consumer OTAs. This file covers the trade: the people who put a walk inside someone else's coach trip or itinerary.

1. Who buys a small heritage walk (Southern Wales toolkit, Visit Wales page; both read)

Buyer What they do What they want from us
Group Travel Organisers (GTOs) Often volunteers running trips for social clubs and special-interest groups. The toolkit names U3A, Probus and Ramblers as examples. A clear group price, one email to book, something exclusive to groups. Repeat bookings and word of mouth.
Coach tour operators Fixed-itinerary day trips and breaks with a tour manager on board. Mostly older travellers, clubs and schools. Coach drop-off close to the start, toilets, timing they can rely on, looking after the driver and tour manager.
DMCs / ground handlers Local partners for overseas tour operators. They don't sell to the public. Named examples: Cambria@ASA, Janet Redler. Static net rates, reliable availability, quick replies. One supplier letting them down damages their standing with the overseas operator.
Tour wholesalers Package tried-and-tested products for coach firms and GTOs, mostly for the over-50s. Proven product with a track record. Not a pilot.

The toolkit states a product doesn't need to fill a day. "Even something as simple as a one-hour storytelling session ... can add major value" for overseas independent travellers (FITs), because multi-destination trips need short stops.

2. The money (Southern Wales toolkit p.14–15, read)

The toolkit's own worked example:

DMC / tour operator (static net rate) OTA (dynamic pricing)
Public rate £150 £150
Net rate / commission £120 net, no commission 15–20% commission
Income to you £120 £120–£127.50
Booking lead time 3–12 months often under 30 days
Cancellation risk low (mostly prepaid) higher
  • Rule of thumb from the same page: give trade partners (tour operators, DMCs, GTOs) a discount in line with what you'd give OTAs. If OTAs get 15–25%, offer the trade the same.
  • What this means: trade income comes slowly (3–12 months ahead) but it's predictable. A tour aimed at the 2027 season has to be in front of the trade this winter.
  • Evidence strength: one regional toolkit (research and copy by Aled Rees of Cambria@ASA, a DMC, so a practitioner, but an interested one). Royal Mint Experience testimony is qualitative: group-only packages work (their "guided tour + Strike Your Own coin + cream tea"). There are no conversion figures. Treat the percentages as the trade's norms, not a forecast.

3. The gates (read on the Visit Wales and Cadw pages, 28 Oct 2026)

  1. Visit Wales Tourism Product Database. Listing on visitwales.com goes through an account in this system (support: Visit Wales Data Steward, [address kept in the project workspace], [number kept in the project workspace]). Attractions: the Visitor Attraction Quality Scheme is recommended. Activity providers may need the Adventure Activity Assurance Scheme. A guided walk most likely registers as an attraction or experience; which category it is hasn't been checked.
  2. Travel Trade Wales site (traveltrade.visitwales.com). To appear there you need "a quality Wales product which is Travel Trade friendly", a website, and you must offer trade rates (commission or net). No trade rate, no listing.
  3. Visit Wales fam trips. To be included you must "demonstrate you actively work with the Travel Trade market" and already be on the trade site. So it goes in order: listing → trade rates → fam invitations.
  4. Regional partnership. The Vale of Glamorgan is in Southern Wales Tourism (council tourism inbox: [address kept in the project workspace]). The toolkit asks businesses to keep their local tourism officer updated "so we can promote these on your behalf" at trade shows.
  5. Cadw's Tour Operator Scheme gives trade rates and free fam visits at Cadw properties. It's useful as a pairing: a coach day of "Llandough walk + a nearby Cadw site" is easier to sell than the walk alone. Which Cadw sites are near enough hasn't been checked.

4. Trade-ready kit (toolkit p.16–19 checklist, condensed)

  • One-page PDF factsheet: what it is (60–90 min walk, 9 stops), group size min/max, languages (cy/en/es audio), season and days, coach drop-off point and parking, toilets, accessibility (terrain, stiles), wet-weather plan, public price + net/commissionable rate, cancellation terms, booking contact (one email), 3–5 professional photos with credit lines.
  • Group-exclusive option (the Royal Mint pattern): for example a guided walk + archive handling session + tea at a partner café, sold only to groups.
  • A trade page on the site: factsheet download, image folder, enquiry form. The toolkit suggests tracking trade enquiries separately from public bookings (a HubSpot pipeline or a spreadsheet column is enough).
  • Fam visit plan: a free 45-minute version for organisers and tour managers, then asking for feedback afterwards.
  • Insurance and guide: the tour's open questions still apply ("who guides the pilot", insurance cover). Trade buyers will ask for public liability evidence before they book. The amount needed hasn't been checked.

5. How we apply it to Cedwyn (all PROPOSALS: nothing contacted, nothing listed)

  1. Order of work (after the pilot walk and insurance are settled, not before): - factsheet with a net rate - Visit Wales Tourism Product Database listing - Travel Trade Wales listing - one email to the Vale tourism inbox - GTO outreach second (the U3A/Probus skills already cover the group-talk route)
  2. Pricing proposal for the project lead's decision: public price P, trade net = 0.8 × P (the 20% middle of the toolkit's 15–25%). Use the same rate for all trade partners, as the toolkit states ("simple and consistent").
  3. Target season: pitch now for spring/summer 2027, given the 3–12-month lead time. Record "2027 trade-ready" as a milestone on the tour objective's page.
  4. Pairing: draft a "half-day Vale heritage" itinerary of walk + café + one Cadw site for coach operators. Check distances and whether the site takes groups first.
  5. Rules: all outreach is HubSpot drafts for the project lead to send, companies only, never the community council. Public trade copy never names the project lead and states nothing about family holdings or ownership. The walk is described as a heritage walk past public viewpoints, not as access to private land unless the project lead confirms access. Welsh and Spanish copy needs native review (standing note).
  6. Measure: trade enquiries per quarter, kept apart from public bookings. Review after the first season: if it's under 2 trade bookings, the trade route goes back to "list and wait".
