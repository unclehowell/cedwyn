## What 2026 practitioners report (snippets only; full pages not read, so these are not checked)
- "Viewed vs swiped away" is the first gate: the Shorts feed tests a clip on a small audience and only widens it if people stay past the opening (vidIQ, "How does the YouTube Shorts algorithm work in 2026"; Medium/A. Ershov calls it "the single most" important signal; quso.ai states to read it next to average view duration and loops).
- The hook window is the first 1-2 seconds, not the first 30 (webtonic.io, "How the YouTube algorithm actually works").
- Watch time per view, loops, and shares follow; a loop that lands back on the hook adds replays.

## How we make one (steps)
1. One question per Short. The title asks it and the first frame shows the answer coming (a year or a striking record line, big and gold, on screen from frame 1; no logo intro).
2. 5-6 beats of 4-6 s. Each beat = one year/number + one plain sentence + a small source line. Captions burned in (most views are muted).
3. Last beat = the specific Cedwyn page (not the homepage) + "free first edition at /ebooks/". End card fixed by the template.
4. Keep the same folk bed (music_bed.py, licence-clean) and the header strap so the series is recognisable.
6. Description: page link with UTM (utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=short&utm_campaign=<slug>), 3-5 tags. Queue in the project workspace when quota is out (resets 07:00 UTC); IG/FB copies go in social-queue.json within the 6/day cap.
7. Measure after 48 h: viewed-vs-swiped (YouTube Studio), average % viewed, subs gained. Rewrite only the first beat if swipe-away is high.

Every Short opens on a record year (1824, 1841, 1877, 1939) or a quoted record line, links one research page, and ends on the free first edition. Sensitive subjects (deaths, incidents, the 1987 appeal, disclosure) are held as scripts until wording is checked against the editorial rules.
