YouTube vertical live: a stream that shows in the Shorts feed (2026)

What it is. A vertical (9:16) live stream opens full-screen on phones in a scrollable live feed, and viewers who are not subscribers can find it while browsing Shorts. Streams started from YouTube Studio's Live Control Room now go out in horizontal and vertical at once by default. After the stream, YouTube's AI highlights can cut ready-made Shorts from it. For a tiny history channel, the value is discovery through the Shorts feed plus free Shorts made afterwards, not a live crowd.

Evidence (read in full unless marked)

  • YouTube Help, "Get started with live streaming" (read 26 Oct 2026). To go live you must have had no live-streaming restrictions in the past 90 days, have a verified channel, and be 16 or older. Ways to go live: mobile, webcam, encoder or console. Vertical streams open a full-screen experience in which viewers, "not limited to existing channel subscribers", can find them while browsing Shorts. By default, a stream from the Live Control Room goes out in 16:9 and 9:16 at the same time. The vertical layout options are centre crop, fit to phone width, or stacked; you set them under Manage → Edit layout. An encoder can send both feeds over RTMP(S).
  • YouTube Help, "Create a live stream on mobile" (read). Mobile streaming needs at least 50 subscribers, a verified channel and live streaming turned on, and the first stream can take up to 24 hours to unlock. A recap and an archive are created when the stream ends. The webcam and encoder pages did not show a subscriber minimum in what was read; that remains not checked.
  • YouTube Blog, Aaron Filner, 16 Sep 2025 (read). Over 30% of daily logged-in YouTube viewers watched live content in Q2 2025. The post announced practice mode (rehearse with a mock chat before going public), dual-format streaming with one shared chat, live reactions to other live streams, and AI highlights that turn the best moments of a stream into Shorts.
  • Samir Chaudry (Colin & Samir), YouTube Blog, 16 Sep 2025 (read). Vertical streams automatically appear in the Shorts feed. 48% of their own show's views come from TVs, so the horizontal feed still matters.
  • No small-channel numbers found. I found no practitioner testimony with figures for vertical live on a channel under 100 subscribers. Treat this as an experiment, not a proven route.

How to do it

  1. Unlock it. Verify the channel and turn on live streaming in Studio, then wait up to 24 hours. Use a computer (webcam or encoder) if the channel is under 50 subscribers.
  2. Rehearse. Run one stream in practice mode first, checking audio level, captions and the vertical layout ("fit to phone width" or "stacked" for documents).
  3. Choose a format that suits vertical: one record per stream, shown large, read aloud and explained. Keep it to 20–30 minutes and title it with the record, e.g. "Reading an 1824 estate survey, live".
  4. Schedule it 24–48 hours ahead so a watch page exists to share. Post it once to Facebook/IG within the daily caps, and once as a YouTube Post.
  5. During the stream, say the title and the question in the first 10 seconds, because Shorts-feed viewers arrive mid-stream. Repeat the context every 5 minutes. Pin one comment linking to the Cedwyn page.
  6. After the stream, take the AI highlights, or cut 2–3 Shorts yourself. Add chapters and captions to the archive (see youtube-chapters-captions-transcripts-archive.md), and put the archive in the right playlist.
  7. Measure after 4 streams: live views from the Shorts feed (Studio → Reach → traffic source), new subscribers per stream, and highlight-Short views. Stop if 4 streams bring fewer than 5 new subscribers in total.

Pitfalls

  • Re-streaming third-party footage breaks originality rules and can trigger Content ID matches. Show your own scans and renders; cite broadcast material in words.
  • A stream with no talking reads as a slideshow. Shorts-feed viewers swipe away in seconds.
  • Mobile live is unavailable under 50 subscribers. Don't plan around it.
  • Each stream uses YouTube API quota only if it is created through the API. Creating it in Studio avoids touching the daily upload quota our Shorts uploader depends on (not checked how live creation is counted).

How we apply it to Cedwyn (PROPOSAL, needs the owner's GO)

  • This is a new public format, so it is a proposal only. The channel is around 7 subscribers, so mobile live is out; it would be a Studio/encoder stream from this box (ffmpeg → RTMP, no install needed) or from a computer the owner uses.
  • Pilot: 4 fortnightly "One record, live" streams. 1. The 1824 David Stewart survey, the first record mention found. 2. 1667: the family acquired Tŷ Mawr (1974 newspapers; ITV News 1988), stated plainly. 3. A field-name map. 4. Viewer questions.
  • No Bute-era dates. Narration is our own and never names the owner. None of the banned public words.
  • If the owner prefers no live voice, the fallback is a pre-recorded YouTube Premiere. That is not tested here; Test & compare does not support Premieres.
  • A highlight Short from each stream goes into the existing Shorts queue. Pin a comment linking to the matching Cedwyn page, tagged utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=live.
  • Cost: £0. Effort: about 1 hour per stream including prep.
