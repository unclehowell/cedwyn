GATE + playbook: National Lottery Awards for All Wales (£300–£20,000) for community heritage activity

Harvested 2026-10-30 04:15Z. Before this run the index had 0 hits for "awards for all" or "community fund". This is the community-grant route the root asked about, beside the NLHF/ACW gate (heritage-fund-arts-council-wales-grants-gate-2026.md).

The rules

From the Fund's Wales page (read 30 Oct 2026): - Amount and timing: £300–£20,000, projects up to 2 years, decision in 12 weeks, open to applications. - What it funds: starting or continuing an activity, or helping an organisation adapt. Projects must do at least one of these: - bring people together and build relationships in and across communities; - improve places and spaces that matter to communities; - help people reach their potential early; - support people under cost-of-living pressure.

Detail below is from Grantsights' four-nation guide, which states it checked the Fund's own pages on 17 Oct 2026 and analysed the Fund's 360Giving open data (OGL v3). It is a third-party summary; the Wales pages were not re-read line by line here: - Who can apply: constituted voluntary or community groups, registered charities, CIOs, not-for-profit companies, CICs, schools (wider-community projects only) and statutory bodies including community councils. Wales has no community benefit societies on its list. A CIC limited by shares only if it follows the CIC Regulator's profit guidance. - Who can't apply: individuals, sole traders, profit-distributing companies, anyone applying for another organisation, organisations outside the UK. - People: at least 2 unrelated board or committee members. Married, partners, same address or family all count as related. - Bank: a statement under 3 months old in the organisation's legal name. - Wales-specific: the project must be delivered in Welsh and English. Budget for translation; leaving it out is a common reason a budget looks incomplete. - Can't fund: - fundraising or profit-making activity; - costs already paid; - paid bid writers (AI help is allowed if the answers are accurate and your own, per the England page); - overseas projects; - statutory services. - Heritage activity: fundable in England and Wales only when it does more than run the activity, for example by reducing isolation or bringing people together. - Award pattern, Wales, 1 Jul 2025–30 Sep 2026: - 1,088 grants, £18.1M in total; median £19,676; middle half £15,000–£20,000; 60% were £19,000 or more. - Recipients: CICs 24% (the largest group), unregistered groups 19%, not-for-profit companies 18%, charities 17%, CIOs 13%, statutory bodies 3%. - Success rate: England states about 1 in 3 applications are funded. Wales publishes no rate. - Repeat grants within 15 months are rare (237 of 14,768 recipients across the UK).

Practitioner playbook

  1. Lead with community, not product. Show who asked for it (a survey, a meeting, local leaders) and what changed because of their answers.
  2. Cost it honestly to the real figure. Winners mostly ask near the top, but the form wants a costed budget, so don't pad.
  3. Make it bilingual from the start: translation, bilingual materials and Welsh-speaking facilitators as budget lines.
  4. Have the paperwork first: governing document, 2 unrelated committee members, an organisation bank account, and a safeguarding policy if children or adults at risk are involved.
  5. Count back at least 12 weeks (Wales warns it can take longer) and spend nothing before the decision email.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Cedwyn can't apply as it stands. An individual or family project, and e-book sales are profit-making activity. Any route needs a constituted not-for-profit vehicle (for example a community heritage group or CIC) with 2 unrelated committee members. That's a decision for the project lead, so it goes in the programme office queue as a PROPOSAL, never as a request to him from a harvest run.
  • A fundable shape, if a vehicle exists: "Llandough memories": free bilingual community heritage sessions.
  • Local residents bring photos and memories of the farm and the village. Volunteers scan them and older residents record first-hand accounts with consent.
  • Free guided walks; a free bilingual exhibition at a local hall or library.
  • Outcomes: brings people together and reduces isolation among older residents.
  • Costs: venue, translation, a scanner, refreshments, volunteer expenses, printing.
  • This sits on the community side of the line. No e-book promotion, no case advocacy in the funded activity, and nothing about family holdings.
  • Marketing benefit, indirectly: a funded free public project creates press hooks, People's Collection Wales uploads (peoples-collection-wales-contributions.md) and local goodwill. The e-books stay entirely outside the grant.
  • Guardrails:
  • never present the e-books or the documentary as grant outputs;
  • never use a paid bid writer;
  • contact nobody (no community council: standing rule);
  • zero spend;
  • public text never names the project lead; banned words apply.
  • Next step for the programme office: add "Awards for All Wales: needs a constituted vehicle; median Wales grant £19,676; bilingual delivery" to the funding map beside NLHF £10k+ and ACW Create & Engage.
