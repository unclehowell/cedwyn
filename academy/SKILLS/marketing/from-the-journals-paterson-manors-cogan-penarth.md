## Two-Llandoughs check: PASSED
This article's "Llandough"/"Landoch" is Llandough near Penarth: Cogan chapel is "a chapel of
Llandough" (its mother church), Coganesmor marsh and Cogan Pill lie on the Ely side, and the
1545 Herbert rental puts Cogan manor lands "in the parish of Llandough". Safe to cite for our parish.

## Fact sheet (printed page numbers; Paterson's sources in brackets)
- p.17: method — Liber Niger 1166, the 1262 Extent of Glamorgan, inquisitions post mortem, and
  Clark's Cartae et Munimenta de Glamorgan are the backbone. (Good "how historians know" post.)
- p.27: Penarth charters name Cogan as a neighbouring fee (Cartae p.984-985); a third charter:
  land in Penard conveyed by "Christina widow of Maurice of Landoch" (Cartae p.727).
- p.27: "ville de Gymyn" 1429 (Cartae p.1540); a 1731 will names "the Kemmin ... and the great
  barne"; "A house, The Kymin, is still on the site" (as of the article).
- p.27-28: Cogan named from Old Danish "kog", "ditched land" (Paterson's etymology; Pierce 1968 may
  differ, not checked). Cog Farm holds such land. Do not confuse with Cogan in Wentllwch (Mon.).
- p.28: Liber Niger 1166: Miles de Cogan held 2 knights' fees; went to Ireland with FitzStephen,
  granted the kingdom of Cork 1177, slain 1182.
- p.28: Annals of Tewkesbury 1238: dispute over Cogan chapel; it "remained in its first condition,
  as it is, a chapel of Llandough". 1184-5: R. de Sumeri presented to the chapel of Cogan,
  "Llandough being referred to as the mother church".
- p.29: Walter de Cogan witnessed a land transfer "in the fees of Llandough and in Cogan";
  IPM of Joan de Clare 1307: John de Cogan held a messuage and two ploughlands at Cogan, 13s 4d.
- p.29: Maurice Ysaac of Landoch confirmed to Margam "all his marsh called Coganesmor"
  (place-names Brodeslyme, Sammeliswere, Penmardismore, Niwere); 8s a year, still paid from the
  Grange per a Minister's Account of 1492.
- p.29-30: 1487 Sir James Tyrell held Cogan Pill; Sir Mathew Cradoc (d.1535) to grandson Sir George
  Herbert, granted Cogan manor by Henry VIII in 1544. 1545 rental: Cogan manor very probably
  included lands in Llandough parish; "Cogan Pill is mentioned in both ... Llandough and Cogan".
- p.30: 1610 Herbert rental: dairy at Cornerswell, 32 cows at 6s 8d = GBP 10.3.4; Cogan valued
  GBP 53.8.6; manor with its mansion of Cogan Pill stayed with the Herberts to the mid-18th century,
  then sold in 1793 (to the Bute estate; leave the buyer out of public cards if in doubt).

## How to run the "From the journals" series (works for any NLW article)
1. Pick one fact with a printed page number. One card = one fact + one citation line
   ("Paterson, Trans. Cardiff Nat. Soc. LXVII, p.28"). No outbound links (house rule); the citation
   is the link.
2. Write 40-70 words, neutral third person, past tense, the record's own words in quotes.
   Banned public words apply (write "not checked" where needed). No family holdings, no insignia.
3. End with a soft Cedwyn door: "More on the parish in the records: cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate"
   (internal only), and, on the shop card, /ebooks/.
4. Keep a ledger row per fact used (source, page, card date) so nothing repeats.
5. Weekly rhythm: one card on Thursday beside #FolkloreThursday is folklore only; history cards
   go Tuesday. One article yields 8-10 cards = two months of Tuesdays.
