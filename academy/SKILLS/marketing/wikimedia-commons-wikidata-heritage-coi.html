Skill: contributing to Wikimedia Commons and Wikidata as a heritage archive, inside the conflict-of-interest rules

Written 26 Oct 2026 (skills harvest). Policy pages change; re-read the linked ones before a first upload.

Why it matters (evidence)

  • The National Library of Wales's Wikimedian in Residence reported that in 16 months the library shared nearly 15,000 images on Commons and they generated over 100 million views (Jason Evans, talk "Sharing digital content with Wikimedia"; figure from the talk's published description, not re-measured here).
  • Wikipedia explicitly welcomes the cultural sector: "Museum curators, librarians, archivists, and similar are encouraged to help improve Wikipedia, or to share their information in the form of links to their resources" (WP:CURATOR). The traffic comes from files being reused in articles, with the source line naming the archive, not from links in article text.

The rules that bind us (read these as hard limits)

  • Wikipedia COI guideline (nutshell): "Do not edit Wikipedia in your own interests, or in the interests of your external relationships. Paid editing must be disclosed." COI editing is "strongly discouraged". Writing about yourself or family is a listed COI.
  • Citing yourself (WP:SELFCITE): allowed "within reason", only if relevant, in the third person, not excessive; "When in doubt ... propose the edit on the article's talk page and allow others to review it". Adding many references to your own work and none by others is treated as spam.
  • No shared or company accounts on Wikipedia: one real person per account (a pseudonym is fine), with any COI declared on the user page.
  • Commons is for freely licensed "educational" media; material that is only self-promotion is out of scope (COM:SCOPE). Every file needs a free licence or a public-domain tag with the reason.
  • Wikidata: an item must meet the notability policy (WD:N): a sitelink to a Wikimedia page, OR a clearly identifiable entity described by serious public sources, OR a structural need. Its 2015 COI RfC closed with "no consensus to make it a Wikidata policy", but paid editing must still be disclosed under the Wikimedia Terms of Use, and biased selection of statements is the stated concern.
  • Automated or AI-driven editing on Wikipedia needs bot approval; unattended agent editing is not acceptable. So a person reviews and submits each batch; Tab prepares it.

How to do it (steps)

  1. Account: one named individual (pseudonym allowed) holds a Wikimedia account; user page states the connection to the Cedwyn archive. Never a shared "Cedwyn" login.
  2. Pick only files that are clearly free: documents whose author died 70+ years ago, or anonymous works created 70+ years ago (UK term), photographed or scanned flat. Commons treats faithful scans of 2-D public-domain works as public domain ({{PD-Art}} with the underlying PD reason, e.g. {{PD-old-70}}). Our own photos of the land or buildings: licence CC BY-SA 4.0 or CC0. Skip anything modern, third-party, or whose rights are unclear.
  3. Describe neutrally: what the document is, date, place, creator, holding archive or reference number, transcription if short. No case narrative, no assertions about people, no buy language. Source field: "Scan by the Cedwyn archive" + the evidence-page URL (no UTMs; Wikimedia strips referrers anyway).
  4. Categorise precisely (e.g. Llandough, Vale of Glamorgan; Maps of the Vale of Glamorgan; 1820s documents of Wales) and add structured data "depicts" (Wikidata Q-ids) so files surface in searches.
  5. Upload small first (5-10 files via the UploadWizard), see whether anyone objects, then batch the rest (Pattypan or the UploadWizard's multi-file mode, both free).
  6. Wikidata: add statements to EXISTING items (the place, the parish, the historic building if it has a Cadw/Coflein listing) with a reference to the primary record. Create a new item only if it meets WD:N with independent sources. Don't create an item for the archive or the e-books unless independent sources describe them.
  7. Wikipedia: never edit articles about the family, the farm dispute, or BP Properties v Buckler. For place articles, propose additions on the talk page with {{Edit COI}} / {{request edit}} and let an uninvolved editor decide. An image added from Commons to a place article is lower-risk, but still disclose.
  8. Track reuse with the free GLAMorgan tool (views of pages using a category's files) monthly.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Candidate set: the dated pre-1900 estate plans and deeds already on Cedwyn evidence pages (e.g. the 1824 plan) where the original's rights are clear; make a list of 10 with date, creator, holding archive and licence reasoning. Anything held by an archive under its own reproduction terms stays out until that archive's terms are checked.
  • Tab prepares: files, filenames ("Llandough estate plan 1824 (plan 19).jpg"), wikitext description, licence tag, categories, depicts Q-ids, in the project workspace A person reviews and uploads. Tab never submits edits itself.
  • Category "Great House Farm (Llandough)" on Commons only once there are 5+ files and the building or site has an identifiable record.
  • Rules carried over: never name the project lead, banned public words, no insignia, nothing about family holdings or the dispute in any Wikimedia text.
  • Value: slow but durable. Expect reuse-driven views, not clicks; the payoff is the source line and the archive being the cited holder of record.
