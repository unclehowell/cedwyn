Reader street team: a small opt-in circle that shares launches (2026)

Written 31 Oct 2026 (skills harvest). Distinct from arc-team-zero-cost-advance-readers.md (review copies) and author-group-promos-bookfunnel-storyorigin-free-organiser-run.md (author swaps): a street team is readers who share and give feedback. ARCs ride the ARC skill; review law rides uk-dmcc-act-urgency-reviews-price-copy.md.

What the source shows (practitioner testimony, with numbers)

Melissa Dymond / Lexi Davis, BookBub Partners blog guest post (2026, read): - 600+ street-team readers across two pen names, beside a 25,000-subscriber newsletter. She does NOT invite every subscriber: recruits mainly via social, from readers who like interacting and making content. - Sign-up = one Google Form (in her link-in-bio): name, email, social handles, Goodreads profile, newsletter opt-in, permanent-ARC opt-in, content/trigger confirmation. The form feeds a spreadsheet. - Groups of ~100 per chat so nobody feels lost; contact 3-5 times a week between launches, near-daily before a big promo. - Launch kit in a shared folder: covers, art, caption ideas, teaser quotes, keywords, retailer links, story-size graphics, timeline. "The easier you make participation, the more likely people are to actually participate." - Rallying the team on key posts gave "two to five times more views" than similar posts without it; she saves that ask for release days, deal days and cover reveals only. - The team also picks covers/quote wording (fast clear preference), and supplies alpha/beta readers (started with ~10, kept 2 alpha + 2-4 beta). - Members churn; recruiting is ongoing. "Even a group of 5-10 readers can make a difference." (Single practitioner, romance genre, her own figures; not independently checked.)

How-to

  1. Start at 5-10: invite only people who already replied to a newsletter, commented, or bought. Opt-in form with: first name, email, which channels they use, "happy to read early chapters?", consent line.
  2. State the deal plainly: early looks, first sight of new records/e-books, a say in covers and titles, name in the acknowledgements if wanted. Nothing is required; no review is expected for anything given.
  3. One home: a Mailchimp tag + one monthly "circle" email (cheapest; no new platform). Add a chat space only past ~30 members.
  4. Launch kit per release: a folder with cover (square + story size), 3 caption options, 2 quote cards with source lines, the /ebooks/ link with utm_source=streetteam (off-site sharing only), dates.
  5. Ask for action 3-4 times a year max (launch day, a price event, a big record reveal). Between asks, give: a behind-the-scenes find, a cover poll.
  6. Thank publicly (with permission) and keep a simple sheet: member, joined, last active, shared yes/no. Re-invite quiet members once a year, then let them go.
  7. Measure: utm_source=streetteam visits and sales per launch; views on rallied posts vs the median of the last 10 unrallied posts (her 2-5x is the benchmark, not an expectation).

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Recruits: Mailchimp subscribers who replied, Academy members, buyers of any e-book, and people who commented on the Facebook page or YouTube. Opt-in only; no DMs to strangers; invitation goes in the regular newsletter.
  • Name it "Cedwyn readers' circle"; members see draft e-book chapters and new evidence pages first and vote on covers (fits the quote-pack and e-book pipeline).
  • First ask: the next e-book launch or the Black Friday/Cyber weekend price event (see black-friday-cyber-weekend-small-ebook-shop-uk.md).
  • Rules: never name the project lead publicly; launch-kit captions pass the banned-words scan (states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken); nothing about family holdings; any free copy is a gift, never conditional on a review (DMCC fake-review rules).
  • Status: PROPOSAL for QUEUE-after-framing (a new newsletter segment + public invitation needs the project lead's GO). Prep that needs no GO: draft the form questions and the first launch-kit folder structure in the project workspace
