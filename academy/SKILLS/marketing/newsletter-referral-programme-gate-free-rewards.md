# Reader referral programme for a tiny history newsletter: gate first, free rewards only

Date: 2026-10-26 (skills harvest 21:11). New email journeys and reward pages are PROPOSALS.

## What practitioners report (sources read)
- SparkLoop (Louis Nicholls; analysis of 1,000+ newsletter referral programmes): small newsletters grow on average 10-20% faster with referral rewards that cost nothing; lists over 20,000 grow 30-80% faster. Free rewards that work: an "insider" edition or section unlocked at 1-3 referrals (Jay Clouse, Brennan Dunn, James Clear), a PDF download (James Clear "thousands of referrals every month" with a PDF), a back-catalogue of best editions, a local guide. Show a blurred preview of the locked section to create pull.
- beehiiv blog (newsletter referral guide): first reward must be low (if it needs 25 referrals "most readers won't even try"); tiers 3 / 10 / 25; progress reminders lifted referrals about 40%; one writer: after six months referred subscribers were nearly 30% of the list; The Brink: 14,000 new subscribers in the first month (large launch, not comparable to us).
- These are platform-vendor blogs (SparkLoop and beehiiv sell referral tools); their averages come from their own customers and are not checked independently.

## The gate (our rule, derived from the above, not from a source)
A referral programme multiplies existing readers; with 1-5 subscribers it multiplies nothing. Start the full tiered programme only when the list has 100+ subscribers AND the newsletter has gone out on a regular cadence for 4+ issues with open/click data. Until then do only the zero-tool version below.

## Zero-tool version (works on Mailchimp free, no installs)
1. One line at the foot of every email: "Know someone from the Vale, Cardiff or Chubut who'd like this? Forward it - or send them <signup page link with utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=forward>." Mailchimp's forward-to-a-friend merge tag exists in its docs (exact tag name not checked); a plain signup link is simpler and trackable.
2. Thank-you page after signup and after purchase: one share line + the same link with utm_campaign=thankyou_share.
3. Signup form: optional field "Who sent you?" (doubles as attribution; see self-reported-attribution skill). Count named referrers by hand.
4. Free reward, manual: anyone who sends 1 friend (named in that field) gets a reply email with a free PDF already owned by us (e.g. a 6-page "what the records show" sampler drawn from the e-books, or a printable timeline). Delivered by hand, logged in HubSpot.

## Full version (after the gate)
- Tiers: 1 referral = insider section / sampler PDF; 3 = early copy of the next chapter or episode notes; 10 = name in the acknowledgements of the next edition (ask consent). No cash, no discounts that undercut paid buyers.
- Tooling options (all PROPOSALS, check pricing then): SparkLoop/beehiiv are paid or platform-bound - out under zero spend; a hand-run referral ledger in HubSpot (contact property "referred_by") is enough below ~500 subscribers.
- Monthly reminder line with the reader's own count only if tracked; otherwise skip (beehiiv's +40% depends on automated progress emails).
- Watch for junk signups (fake addresses to farm rewards): reward only confirmed double opt-in referrals.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Today (list about 1 subscriber, 0 sales): gate not met. Prepare, as drafts only, the one-line forward/share copy and the UTM link, and pick which existing free PDF could be the thank-you reward. No email journey changes and no form field added without the owner's GO (PROPOSAL; the signup form is site-wide and another worker is editing it tonight).
- When the list reaches 100: propose the tier table above with the acknowledgements tier tied to the next edition.
- Measure: share of new signups naming a referrer or arriving on utm_campaign=forward/thankyou_share; keep only if it reaches 10% of signups within 3 months.
- Public text: never name the owner; avoid the banned words; rewards must never touch family holdings.
