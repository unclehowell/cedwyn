# LibraryThing Early Reviewers + Member Giveaways for heritage e-books
Skill harvest 26 Oct 2026. Free review-seeding route to readers who catalogue and review. Zero spend.

## What the programme is (primary: LibraryThing's own author page, Wayback copy 18 Dec 2025; live site blocks scripted fetches)
- **Early Reviewers (ER)**: free. Close to 100,000 ER members get a site notice + email each monthly batch; the batch also sits in the State of the Thing newsletter (1M+ members), the homepage news item, an ER discussion thread, Facebook and X posts.
- **Batch clock**: runs from the first business day of the month to the 25th, 6 pm US Eastern. Titles must be in **3 days before the batch launches**. Winners are picked by algorithm (library contents, past review record, likelihood to talk about the book), not at random.
- **Copies**: authors/publicists may offer **5-10 e-book copies** (or 5-30 paper). Publishers (an imprint page) 5-30. Each book can run in **two** monthly batches.
- **Eligibility**: new or forthcoming only: **published no more than 6 months before the batch month**. **No AI-authored titles.** You **cannot give away an e-book that is freely available elsewhere** (e.g. free on your own site). Format cannot change after launch.
- **Delivery**: email attachment (you get winner contacts) or a download link LT messages to winners. E-books must reach winners within **one week**; download links must stay live a week after winners are picked (~26th).
- **Contact rules**: winner details only for delivery; no newsletter enrolment, no reminder messages (LT sends those). Reviews due within **90 days**, on the LT book page; cross-posting encouraged, not required. Author gets perpetual non-exclusive permission to quote ER reviews.
- **Member Giveaways** (separate, looser): open to a LibraryThing Author, or a member with 50+ books catalogued, or a paid account (Training Authors guide, 2015, updated 2024; not re-checked on the live site). Same no-auto-enrol courtesy applies.

## Real numbers from practitioners
- Matthew Iden (Member Giveaway, e-books): 550 copies offered, **67 takers in ~3 weeks**, 40 requested copies within 3 days of close; first 24 h: 3 Amazon + 3 Smashwords + 3 Goodreads reviews; **half agreed to a follow-up email**. (Asking for that consent is now off-limits under ER rules; ask only on Member Giveaways and only if LT's current terms allow it.)
- r/selfpublish 2024 (ER): 25 copies offered, 11 downloaded, 2 reviews on LT (one 2-star). A KBoards author put long-run review return at ~10%.
- Read: expect 1-3 honest reviews per batch of 10 e-books. Value = reviews on a catalogue used by librarians and serious readers, plus the 100k-member notice. Not a sales engine.

## How to run one (checklist)
1. Pick a **paid** title published in the last 6 months that is **not free anywhere**. Confirm authorship status first (see AI rule below).
2. Request LibraryThing Author status (free account -> search the author -> "Is this you?"), or set up a publisher page via the first giveaway.
3. Entry: EPUB + PDF (both, so no one is excluded), download link from our own /ebooks/ delivery with a one-week expiry, countries = UK, IE, AR, US, CA, AU (where we sell).
4. Blurb: first line = the strongest record fact, second line = what the reader can check. No hype.
5. Submit 3+ days before the 1st business day. Log batch month, copies, takers, reviews at 30/60/90 days in the project workspace
6. Run batch 2 only if batch 1 produced at least 1 review.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Fit test first**: ER bars AI-authored titles. Before entering any Cedwyn volume, the owner decides whether the title qualifies (human author/editor of record, AI-assisted research only) and states it truthfully on the entry. If it does not qualify, use **Member Giveaways** only if their terms allow it, else skip. Owner call, not ours.
- **Which title**: the paid £9.99 volumes, never the free editions (Enwau etc. stay free and are therefore ineligible). The bundle (£31.99) is not offered.
- **Blurb example (rules-compliant)**: "Tŷ Mawr, Llandough: 1667 is the family's acquisition, reported in the 1974 newspapers and by ITV News in 1988. The first record mention found so far is the 1824 David Stewart survey. Every page links to its source." (run the banned-word sweep before submitting).
- **Category**: History > Wales / Local history; tags: Llandough, Vale of Glamorgan, Welsh Patagonia (for the Chubut volume).
- **Winners**: deliver via the /ebooks/ link, nothing else. No follow-up, no list add.
- **Owner steps**: LT account + author status; authorship decision. Drafted wording sits in HubSpot only; nothing submitted without the owner's GO.
