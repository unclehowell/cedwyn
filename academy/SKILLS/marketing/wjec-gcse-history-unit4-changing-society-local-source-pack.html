Made-for-Wales GCSE History Unit 4 ("A changing society"): a local-period source pack teachers can use

Skill date: 30 Oct 2026 (skills harvest ~21:15Z). Status for Cedwyn: PROPOSAL only. Nothing built, listed or sent; any school outreach or price is the owner's decision. Before this run 'wjec' and 'gcse' had 0 hits in INDEX.md; the school skills already on file are school-resource-marketplaces-tes-hwb.md (where to list) and school-institution-multi-user-licence-tier-ebooks.md (how to price). This one is about what teachers in Wales now need to build, which tells us what to make.

What the specification requires (WJEC GCSE History spec, version 5, read in full via pdftotext)

  • Timing: first teaching moved from Sept 2025 to Sept 2026; first award 2028 (spec summary of amendments, v2; WJEC news "Made-for-Wales GCSE History update", read). The legacy GCSE runs until summer 2027. Spec p.59 lists Units 1-3 from 2026 and Unit 4 from 2027; re-check that page before quoting dates, as the amendments touched pp.59-60.
  • Four units: Unit 1 Welsh history in depth (exam, 30%); Unit 2 European/world in depth (on-screen NEA, 20%, WJEC sets the items each June); Unit 3 world period study (exam, 30%); Unit 4 "A changing society" (NEA, 4 h 15 min, 20%, 80 marks, marked by the centre and moderated by WJEC).
  • Unit 4 is teacher-designed. Learners study "a continuous period of c.75 to c.100 years" in Great Britain and/or Ireland; "the study may use all of the region or identify specific areas". The teacher picks the period, the place and the topics, which must not overlap the examined units.
  • Topics must cover nine themes: culture; equality and inequality; ethnicity; international relations; peace and conflict; politics; religion; sex, sexuality and gender; social and economic issues.
  • Evidence load: "learners should engage with four items (two historical sources and two interpretations) per theme." That is 36 items per course, chosen by the teacher. WJEC releases two set themes each June for the next year's enquiry, so a pack has to cover all nine.
  • Assessed skills: continuity and change, similarity and difference, significance and impact, cause and consequence; selecting and evaluating sources; analysing how interpretations were developed. The spec's aims also name "learners' own localities and in Wales".

Why this matters commercially (inference, not checked against sales data)

Every History department in Wales teaching the new GCSE has to assemble a 75-100-year local or regional period with 36 sources and interpretations, at the same time, with no exam-board item bank for Unit 4. WJEC and Hwb publish free general resources (Hwb "Made-for-Wales GCSE: History" blended-learning units, snippet read; WJEC news states its Digital Resources Team publishes free materials), so paid packs compete with free ones. A pack wins only on what the free ones can't give: one specific locality with real, dated, citable records arranged by theme. No brand-level sales numbers for Unit 4 packs exist yet (first assessment 2027); treat demand as a hypothesis to test with one school.

How to build a Unit 4-ready pack (the how-to)

  1. Pick the window to fit the evidence, not the reverse: 75-100 years in which you hold dated primary records across at least six of the nine themes. List the item count per theme before writing a word.
  2. Per theme, supply four items: two primary sources (with archive reference, date, a transcript, and a provenance note) and two interpretations (published secondary works or heritage/archaeology reports, quoted in short extracts with full citation). Interpretations must be other people's published readings, not ours.
  3. Tag every item with theme, date, second-order concept it suits (change, significance, etc.) and reading age. Teachers search by theme because WJEC names two themes each June.
  4. Leave out live disputes. Moderated NEA needs material a teacher can defend; anything contested or in litigation stays out of the pack.
  5. Bilingual from day one: Welsh-medium schools sit the same spec (WJEC/CBAC). At minimum Welsh item titles and the teacher notes.
  6. Licence: single-school site licence (see school-institution-multi-user-licence-tier-ebooks.md). Free sample: one full theme (four items) on Hwb/Tes (see school-resource-marketplaces-tes-hwb.md).
  7. Timing: heads of department plan over summer and in the first half of the autumn term. A sample theme live by June 2027, when WJEC releases the set themes, is the realistic first window. Earlier only if a school asks.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Candidate window: Llandough-juxta-Penarth / Leckwith c.1880 to c.1980. It is inside the period where Cedwyn already holds dated records (trade directories 1895/1909/1911 naming Great House Farm, OS editions, the cemetery excavation reports, parish material), and a changing-society story with land use, farming to suburb, Welsh to English language shift, war years and health/hospital change. Each theme needs its four items confirmed against what is actually on /research/ and the evidence catalogue before anything is promised. Llandough near Cowbridge is a different place; only Llandough-juxta-Penarth records count.
  • Themes Cedwyn can cover from held records (to check): social and economic issues, culture (language), politics (parish/council), peace and conflict (war years), religion (church/chapel). Ethnicity; sex, sexuality and gender; and international relations need sourcing, possibly via the Welsh-Patagonia link for international relations. A pack that can't fill all nine is a supplement for teachers, not a full pack, and should be sold as one.
  • Editorial rules hold for the pack: neutral third person, no banned words, owner never named, nothing about family holdings or addresses, nothing from the 1987 case as an "interpretation".
  • Next step for the bizdev lane: a one-page theme-by-item audit (the project workspace) listing the items Cedwyn holds per theme for 1880-1980. Then a proposal to the owner: one free sample theme, one site-licence price. No school is contacted without the owner's GO.
