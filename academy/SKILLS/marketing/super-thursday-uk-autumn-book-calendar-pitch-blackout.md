# Super Thursday and the UK autumn book calendar: when NOT to pitch book pages

Status: skill (internal). Written 31 Oct 2026 by the skills harvest. Nothing sent.

## What it is
Super Thursday is the UK book trade's heaviest publication day of the year, the start of the pre-Christmas selling season, usually the first or second Thursday of October. Review pages, bookshop tables and book podcasts are saturated with big-name hardbacks that week.

## The numbers (The Bookseller's counts, as reported)
- 2008-2019: an average of **445 hardbacks** on Super Thursday (The Bookseller, 2021 article snippet; page paywalled, not read in full).
- 2024 (10 Oct): about **1,900 books, 457 hardbacks**, a 30% rise on the two post-pandemic years (Tingle Press summary of The Bookseller, read; RLF piece by Caroline Sanderson agrees).
- 2023: seven Super Thursday titles each sold over £1m (Nielsen BookScan TCM, via RLF Substack snippet).
- 2025 (9 Oct): "just over 1,000" new books, **316 hardbacks**: "not-so-super", publishers cutting autumn lists (The Bookseller headline + snippet; page not read).
- 2026: The Bookseller called it the **earliest-ever Super Thursday**, moved into September (a trade post gives 3 September with about 590 hardbacks; that figure is from a social post snippet, not checked against the article); 8 October still carried 349 hardbacks. Booksellers now describe a "rolling Super Thursday" across September-October.

## What practitioners take from it
- A debut author's account (Laura Waddell, The Scotsman) describes the day as books "fighting for press" and shelf space: small titles launched or pitched into it disappear.
- The window is good for one thing: shoppers are in a gift-buying frame from October; the gift-guide route is a separate skill (christmas-press-gift-guides-pitching-digital-heritage-product.md).

## How to do it
1. **Find the date early.** The Bookseller usually names the year's Super Thursday and hardback count in late summer; search "Super Thursday <year>" each July and note the date plus the rolling weeks either side.
2. **Blackout for book-page pitches:** no review requests, book-podcast pitches or "new book" press releases in the week before and two weeks after the date.
3. **Use the quiet sides instead:** late August / early September (before the rolling season) and January-February (post-Christmas lull) for book-page and podcast pitches.
4. **Pitch the non-book desk during the blackout:** local news, heritage and history outlets, family-history magazines care about a story, not a release slot, so they are unaffected.
5. **Price/promo timing:** don't run a launch-week discount into the Super Thursday weeks; run gift messaging (download cards, gift path) from mid-October instead.
