Pitching a heritage book to small Welsh publishers (Seren, Graffeg, Carreg Gwalch)

Harvested 2026-10-27 13:40Z for the three publisher pitch drafts (bizdev run 500c1e95). Read before any of those drafts goes READY.

What the publishers' own pages say (read 27 Oct 2026)

  • Seren (serenbooks.com): "Non-fiction Submissions are currently closed." They "cannot accept submissions outside of call-out windows ... so please don't send unsolicited submissions." No simultaneous submissions ("must notify us if you submit your idea to other ... publishers"). No paper submissions (posted ones are not considered). UK-resident writers, 18+. No work generated in part or entirely by AI ("e.g. ChatGPT"). When open, non-fiction asks for a synopsis, a chapter outline and sample material (2024 call-out post).
  • Graffeg (graffeg.com): open submissions; explicitly welcomes "illustrated non-fiction about heritage, food, travel, arts and culture ...", serialisable books of special interest. Submit through the web form on that page (not email). Max 3 titles per calendar month. No individual replies: silence for 12 weeks = no. English preferred (publishes English and Welsh).
  • Gwasg Carreg Gwalch (Llanrwst): mainly Welsh-language, also English local history/heritage. No submissions page found on carreg-gwalch.cymru (contact page only; its terms treat unsolicited ideas as non-confidential). Not checked: whether they take English proposals by email; ask in one line first.

How a nonfiction proposal is built (Jane Friedman, janefriedman.com)

  1. Overview first in the document, written last: 2-3 pages, a "water-tight business case"; becomes the query letter.
  2. Comparable titles (5-10): "People who've read X will want this because..." Never claim there are no comps.
  3. Target audience, defined by real groups and numbers.
  4. Marketing plan: only what you will do, with numbers; "never discuss what you hope to do". Weak: "I plan to...". Strong: "Our list of N subscribers; the site has N visitors/month; talks booked at...".
  5. Author bio: why you, credentials, platform.
  6. Chapter outline + sample chapter(s). Typical length 10-25 pages double-spaced excluding samples. - For nonfiction, "the marketability of the premise, topic, or author" often matters more than the prose.

Rules for us

  • Respect each publisher's channel: Graffeg = form; Seren = wait for a call-out (do not email now); Carreg Gwalch = short enquiry first.
  • One publisher at a time where they forbid simultaneous submissions (Seren). Graffeg sets no such rule; track the 12-week clock.
  • AI disclosure: Seren bars AI-generated work. Our drafts are machine-assisted; a submission to Seren must be the project lead's own writing, or not go. Say so honestly if asked.
  • Sender details: a real name, contact email and postal town/postcode is normal courtesy. Not checked: a legal duty for an individual's one-to-one pitch email to carry a postal address (Companies Act letterhead rules and the E-Commerce Regulations bind companies / online services, not a private author's letter). Don't tell the project lead the law requires it without a source.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  1. Seren draft: hold. Mark the HubSpot task "HOLD: Seren non-fiction closed (read 27 Oct); re-check serenbooks.com monthly". Add a monthly check to the bizdev plan, not an email.
  2. Graffeg: convert the email draft into form answers (title, synopsis, audience, comps, sample link to a free v0.0.1 edition). Illustrated heritage fits their list exactly; lead with the archive imagery and the tithe map. the project lead submits the form himself (it's in his name). Log date; follow-up = none; 12-week lapse date in HubSpot.
  3. Carreg Gwalch: rewrite as a 4-line enquiry ("Do you consider English-language local-history proposals? If so, how would you like them sent?"), Welsh greeting + English body. the project lead sends.
  4. Build one reusable proposal pack (overview, 5 comps from Welsh local history - e.g. Graffeg/Seren heritage titles, audience with real numbers from the growth report, marketing plan with numbers only, chapter outline, one sample chapter) under the project workspace Every number in it comes from our own logs.
