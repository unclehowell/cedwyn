# Wiki Loves Monuments UK: heritage photos of public listed sites, with free exposure on Wikipedia

Harvested 30 Oct 2026. Research and planning only. Nothing has been uploaded and nobody has been contacted.

## What it is (primary sources, read today)
- Wiki Loves Monuments (WLM) is an annual photo contest. You upload your own photos of eligible heritage sites to Wikimedia Commons under a free licence during **September**. Wikimedia UK organises the UK round. (Commons project page "Wiki Loves Monuments 2026 in the United Kingdom", last edited 11 Aug 2026.)
- **Rules from that page:** a photo can have been taken at any time, but it must not have been on Commons before 1 September. Anyone can enter; you don't have to live in the UK. A new angle or detail is worth more than repeating a shot Commons already has.
- **Scale:** the 2025 international round had 3,789 people submitting 227,918 photos to national contests in Sep–Oct 2025 (Wikimedia Foundation, 29 Apr 2026).
- **UK numbers:** the Commons category "Images from WLM 2025 in the United Kingdom" holds 9,423 files today, which fits Wikimedia UK's Instagram line of "nearly 10,000 images". The Wales category holds 549 files for 2025 and 3,533 for 2024. These are category counts read today and may be incomplete (not checked against Wikimedia UK's own totals).
- **Eligibility:** you need a designated heritage ID. In Wales that means a Cadw listed building or scheduled monument. The UK rules page (wikilovesmonuments.org.uk) returned 403 today, so the exact eligibility wording is **not checked**.
- **A local example:** St Dochdwy's Church, Llandough (near Penarth) is Wikidata Q97403861. It is Cadw listed building **13432**, Grade II, and its Commons category held only 10 files today. Its main image is a Geograph photo.

## Why it matters for marketing
- Each accepted photo goes into the public Commons pool, and Wikipedia and Wikidata articles about the site can use it. That gives the place permanent, free visibility, and the photographer's name travels with every reuse (CC BY-SA credit).
- You can't link to a shop from Commons or Wikipedia, and you shouldn't try. Promotional captions break the rules. What you gain is credit, plus a high-quality image you can legally reuse with attribution.
- Nobody has published numbers on traffic or sales from WLM entries. Treat it as brand and heritage goodwill, not a sales channel.

## How to do it (Sep 2027 round)
1. In August, check the UK rules page and the Wales list tool for the year's dates and eligible IDs.
2. Pick only public-access sites that have an ID and only a few photos on Commons.
3. Shoot in good light, without people or private houses in frame, at full resolution with EXIF kept.
4. In September, upload with the WLM UK upload link, which pre-fills the heritage ID. Choose CC BY-SA 4.0. Write a plain, factual description.
5. Track how often each file is used and viewed with the free Commons tools (GLAMorous / Pageviews); no install is needed.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Subjects:** public places only. St Dochdwy's Church (Cadw 13432), plus other listed public structures in Llandough, Leckwith and Penarth that have IDs; check the Leckwith old bridge's ID. Never Cydfin Farm land, never private houses or family holdings.
- **Who uploads:** the uploader must be the photographer. That rules out me as the uploader. It would be the project lead or a tour volunteer with their own Commons account. I can draft the shot list and descriptions.
- **Reuse:** these photos can serve the Wales tour pages and the walk leaflet. The credit must follow the CC BY-SA rules (see geograph-cc-by-sa-then-and-now-commons-route.md).
- **Timing:** the 2026 round is closed. Put a reminder in the programme-office calendar for **Aug 2027**, as a proposal only.
- **Measure:** file usage (pages that use each image) and Commons views at 3 and 12 months. If no file has been used after 12 months, drop it.
