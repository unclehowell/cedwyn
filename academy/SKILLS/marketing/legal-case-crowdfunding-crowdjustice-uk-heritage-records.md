# Legal-case crowdfunding (UK): CrowdJustice, and when it fits a heritage-records case

## What the platform is (sources below; site pages answered 403 from this box, so terms come from search snippets and a law firm's page)
- CrowdJustice is the UK platform built only for funding legal action. Individuals, groups and communities can all raise funds, publicly or privately.
- **Gate:** "The only requirement to launch your page on the platform is to have a lawyer instructed or be working with a registered…" (how-it-works snippet). The law firm Brett Wilson adds: "Assuming they accept your case, they will help you create a profile page", so CrowdJustice also vets the case.
- **Fees:** creating the page is free. CrowdJustice takes 3%, plus payment processing of 3% + 20p per card (terms snippet). Brett Wilson puts it as "for every £1 donated… 94p will be transferred to our client account".
- **Money flow:** money goes to the **lawyer's client account**, not to the case owner, and is set against fee invoices. Money above the target moves weekly.
- **All or nothing:** "All CrowdJustice cases and projects have 30 days to hit their initial all-or-nothing target." If the target is missed, no card is charged. Once it is met, a **stretch target** keeps the page raising.
- **Unused funds:** at the end of a case, pledges under £1,000 go to charity (currently the Access to Justice Foundation). Larger pledges are handled separately (Brett Wilson).
- **Staged targets:** firms often set the first target at the first stage of work. Brett Wilson asks for "typically at least £2,500" on client account. Later stages follow (advice → pre-action → issue → trial).
- **Editorial:** CrowdJustice states "we don't exercise editorial control" over case pages. Defamation risk on the page therefore sits with the case owner and the lawyer.

## What the numbers say (academic study of CrowdJustice judicial reviews)
Sam Guy (University of York), reported in Legal Futures on 17 Nov 2022 (Modern Law Review article):
- 413 judicial reviews raised **£9.16m** from **287,882 pledges**.
- Average pledge **£31.65**. Average total per case **£22,200**.
- 2 cases raised **£0**. The top case raised £422,760 (a lockdown challenge).
- "Outlier cases" distort the picture. For the typical claimant it was "incredibly burdensome".
- Traction skews to **campaigns with a communal element**. Cases about "purely individual entitlement… gain less traction".
- Topics: environment/planning 35% of pages, health 14%, education 10%.
- Outcomes: of 184 cases reaching permission, 129 were granted. Of 108 full hearings, claimants won 29 and lost 75.
- One live example (category page snippet): a community trust's planning JR raised £22,003 from 409 pledges, an average of about £54.
- UK baseline for comparison (CAF, via UK Fundraising, 16 Mar 2026): the median charity donation was £26 in 2025.

Caveat: these figures cover **judicial reviews**. A private civil matter, such as setting aside a 1987 judgment, is a different category. No figures for that category were found.

## How to do it (once gated through)
1. **Lawyer first.** Get a written scope and costs estimate per stage. The first CrowdJustice target = stage 1 cost ÷ 0.94 (to cover the 6% fees), rounded up.
2. **Frame it communally.** Base the case on public records, heritage and the integrity of record-keeping, which is something many people have a stake in. Do not frame it as one family's entitlement. This follows Guy's finding on traction.
3. **Size the target from your own circle.** Expected pledges ≈ your warm reach × the pledge rate. At about £30 per pledge, a £3,000 first stage needs about 100 pledges in 30 days. With no list, do not launch. See first-ebook-sale-warm-circle-rings-before-strangers.md and the matched-crowdfunding file (1% of backers gave 24%; line up the big givers first).
4. **Page copy rule.** Describe the records and the procedural steps, quote documents verbatim, and have the lawyer approve every sentence about a named body. Banned words still apply: states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken.
5. **Updates at stage milestones.** Post an update at 25/50/75/100% (GoFundMe's own update guidance applies here too), and one after each legal step. Updates are also where you tell backers what happens to surplus funds.
6. **Private page option.** If the case must stay out of the public eye, a private CrowdJustice page lets known supporters fund it without publicity.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Current fit: none yet.** No lawyer is on record as instructed for a reopen action. The reopen-loop's court-readiness bar is not met. The owner's standing order is that press and new public channels need his GO.
- **When it could fit:** the reopen-loop reports a ground at court-readiness, the owner instructs a firm, and the firm agrees to crowdfund. At that point:
  - draft the page text into HubSpot as a NOT_STARTED task for the owner and the lawyer;
  - put the forensic-assessment webapp's verbatim record quotes on the page;
  - link from /forensic/ and /support/ (internal links only).
- **Never** put the page up before a lawyer accepts it, never name individuals, and never set out liability conclusions on a public page. Liability and damages assessment stays in the private forensic files.
- **Fee comparison for the owner's decision**, on a £30 pledge:
  - CrowdJustice: about £28.00 lands, in the lawyer's client account.
  - GoFundMe UK: £29.23.
  - JustGiving Crowdfunding: £28.78.
  - See uk-donation-platforms-gofundme-justgiving-non-charity-heritage.md.
  - CrowdJustice costs the most, but it is the only one of these where the money is ring-fenced for legal fees. Donors to a legal case tend to see that as a reason to trust it.
