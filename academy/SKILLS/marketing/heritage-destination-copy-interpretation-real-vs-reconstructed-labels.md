# Heritage destination pages: interpretation-led copy, and labelling what is real vs reconstructed
Skill for visitor/tour pages (Cedwyn /wales/ and /argentina/, the project lead's ask 27 Oct 2026 12:11: history, heritage and family lead; the dispute is one point among many; real things shown as real, lost things as augmented).
Harvested 2026-10-27.

## What the evidence states
- **Tilden's interpretation principles** (Freeman Tilden, *Interpreting Our Heritage*, 1957; the founding text of the field, still taught by AHI/Interpret Europe). Summarised by HDC International (read 27 Oct 2026): "The chief aim of Interpretation is not instruction, but provocation"; interpretation that does not relate to the visitor's own experience "will be sterile"; shorthand Provoke - Relate - Reveal - Address the whole (one theme) - Message unity. Practitioner canon, not a metric.
- **One theme, many points.** Tilden's "address the whole" and Interpret Europe's theme work: a page is organised around ONE theme sentence; every stop supports it. A single dominant issue (here: the court case) on a visitor page turns it into a brief rather than an invitation.
- **Say what a reconstruction is.** London Charter 2.1 (2009; the international standard for computer-based heritage visualisation, PDF read 27 Oct 2026), principle 4.4: "It should be made clear to users what a computer-based visualisation seeks to represent, for example the existing state, an evidence-based restoration or an hypothetical reconstruction ... and the extent and nature of any factual uncertainty"; 4.5 publish the sources; 4.6 publish the decisions (paradata). The Charter's aim: "distinctions between evidence and hypothesis, and between different levels of probability".
- **AR for lost buildings is a known, liked pattern.** Calvium (heritage app studio, practitioner page, read): Historic Royal Palaces' *The Lost Palace* app placed the lost Palace of Whitehall over today's streets, "making an invisible heritage visible". No visitor numbers on the page; no controlled uplift study found — measure our own.
- **Trust matters most for older / less digital visitors.** Frontiers in Education 2026 (10.3389/feduc.2026.1889292, read): a protocol paper (no participants yet) proposing that cultural authenticity and trust weigh more for older visitors; AR "should be positioned between the building and the visitor's meaning-making, not above the building as a replacement". A hypothesis, cited as such.

## How to do it (checklist)
1. **Write the theme sentence first** (one line, visitor-facing). Example shape: "Walk the hill where one family farmed for centuries, from Iron Age fields to the 1988 bulldozers, and see the lost farmhouse rise again on your phone."
2. **Order sections by visitor appetite:** what you will see and do -> the place's story (eras) -> the family -> the stops -> practicalities -> "Other things we talk about on the walk" (the dispute sits here as one item, 1-2 neutral lines + link to research). No section about the dispute is longer than any other stop.
3. **Each stop = Provoke / Relate / Reveal in ~60 words:** a question or striking fact, a link to the visitor's life (home, work, family), the reveal.
4. **Status badge on every stop and image** (London Charter 4.4), three words only:
   - **Real** — stands today, photographed as it is (Wales: St Dochdwy's church, the Celtic cross; Argentina: home, stables, barn).
   - **Augmented (AR)** — a reconstruction shown on your phone (Wales: the lost farmhouse and buildings; Argentina: church and cross).
   - **Archive** — a period record (plan, photo, map) with its reference.
   Never render something that physically stands as a hologram; that misstates its status.
5. **Under every AR image:** "Reconstruction from <source>, <date>; <what is estimated>" (4.5/4.6), e.g. "from the 1974 measured survey; roof height estimated". Link the method note.
6. **Keep the dispute neutral and short:** dated facts, no adjectives, link out to the research section. Banned words still apply.
7. **Measure:** scroll depth to the stops, clicks on "book/register interest", and time on page before/after the rebalance (search-console-ga4-change-annotations-log.md — annotate the change date).
