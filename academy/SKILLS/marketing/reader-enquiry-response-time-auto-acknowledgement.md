## What the practitioners found (with numbers)
- **Jeff Toister, email response time survey, April 2018** (1,200+ US consumers; repeated 2015 n>1,000 and 2020 n>3,200): the most common choice was "one day" (43%), but one day meets only 56% of people's expectations; 4 hours meets 70%; **1 hour meets 89%**; 15 minutes = world-class. 2020 repeat: 1 hour met 88%. No big difference by age. (US sample; treat as direction, not UK fact.)
- **Toister 2015**: 66% of companies (his 2014 survey) took a day or more; people expecting a reply within 15 minutes rose from 4% (2014) to 14.5% (2015).
- **SuperOffice Customer Service Benchmark (2018, 1,000 companies emailed)**: average reply 12 h 10 min; **62% never replied at all** (as reported by Toister 2018 and SuperOffice's own blog). Speed is the easiest way to stand out.
- **inContact 2017** (cited by Toister): only 43% highly satisfied with their last email service contact; the happy ones named speed.
- SuperOffice response-time guide: a good autoresponder = friendly acknowledgement + the real expected reply time + where to find answers meanwhile.

## The playbook
1. Set one public promise you can keep: "We reply within 24 hours, usually the same day" (honest beats fast-but-broken; tighten to 4 h only when the measured median is under 2 h).
2. Auto-acknowledge every inbound reader email at once: thank them, restate the promise, give 2-3 self-serve links (free first edition, FAQ/how to buy, evidence catalogue search), and say a person/the team reads every message. No marketing, no tracking pixels, plain text.
3. Triage by type: purchase/download problem first (lost sale or refund risk), then corrections/new evidence (research value), then general praise.
4. Measure first-reply time weekly (median and worst), and count unanswered after 48 h; zero is the target.
5. Turn repeated questions into the FAQ and the autoresponder links.
