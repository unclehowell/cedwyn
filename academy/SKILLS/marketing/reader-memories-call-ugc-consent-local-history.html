Reader memories call: collecting local recollections and photos with proper consent (community building)

Harvested 29 Oct 2026 (SKILLS HARVEST RUN). Topic: community building / user-generated content for a local-history site. Status: playbook. Launching the call is a public post on existing channels; anything sent to a named person stays a HubSpot draft.

What the evidence states

  • The newsletter drives replies, not the site banner. The Welsh Government's final evaluation of People's Collection Wales (ERS, 2016; PDF read) ran its user survey three ways: a homepage link, social posts (both bilingual) and the mailing list. There were about 3,000 registered users but only 385 newsletter subscribers. Even so, 13 responses landed on the day the newsletter went out, and the evaluators judged most respondents came from that list. Result: 70 started, 38 finished (54%); 33 in English, 5 in Welsh. Lesson: send the call to the list first. Treat site banners and posts as backups.
  • Contribution can scale once there is a home for it. The same report counts 25,909 user plus partner contributions in 2015-16, almost double the pre-refresh level, after the platform was stable again. The PCW programme found that digitisation and oral-history training, plus "Champions" (trained volunteers), kept communities uploading.
  • Facebook groups already act as memory archives, but fragile ones. Gibbons (Information Research, 2019) studied memory-sharing Facebook groups. The oldest in her sample (2008) had 3,624 members but only 6 posts in 30 days, and often the admin posted while members only commented. Facebook controls what stays visible. Lesson: let people reply where they are, but copy what they give into your own archive page with permission.
  • Consent rules (Oral History Society, read in full):
  • Get written permission to record and to use the recording.
  • Use two agreements: a participation agreement before, and a recording agreement after, once the person knows what they said.
  • Ask for copyright to be assigned, or you must ask again every time you reuse it.
  • Get a separate written agreement for any photos you copy.
  • Remind people just before they start to share only what they're happy to make public, and send a thank-you soon after.
  • Never publish large unedited recordings. Listen first for living, identifiable third parties and anything that could be defamatory or distressing, and publish only safe extracts with timecoded summaries.
  • Keep the forms securely; GDPR applies (see OHS GDPR guidance and the ICO).

Playbook (small site, tiny list)

  1. One question, one place, one month. Example: "Do you remember the farm lane, the school field or the old tithe barn before 1970? Photos, a few lines, or a 5-minute voice note." A narrow prompt beats "share your memories".
  2. Collection route: a short web form on our own domain (name optional, email, town, the memory as text, a photo upload, a voice-note link) plus a reply-to email address. The form has two tick boxes: "you may publish this (edited for length)" and "I own this photo, or have the owner's permission, and give you a licence to publish it". Voice or recorded interviews get the OHS participation and recording agreements.
  3. Launch order: the newsletter first (Welsh and English, with Welsh first per the bilingual layout skill). Same day: one post each on the Facebook page and Instagram, linking to the form. Day 7: one reminder in the newsletter. Day 21: a "last week" post.
  4. Where people answer on social, reply publicly with thanks and a link to the form ("could we keep this on the archive? the form takes 2 minutes"). No DMs to strangers.
  5. Edit before publishing: remove names of living third parties unless they consented, cut anything about property ownership or disputes, keep the contributor's chosen credit ("a Llandough resident", first name only, or full name). Add a date and place line.
  6. Publish a "Remembered" page with each memory as a card: photo, 50-150 words, credit, date received. Link it to the matching evidence and place pages. Each new card is a newsletter item and a social post, so the call feeds itself.
  7. Measure: form starts and completions (target completion 50% or more, PCW got 54%), submissions per send, and newsletter signups from the page.
  8. Drop rule: if two newsletter sends bring fewer than 3 usable submissions, park the call. Try again with a narrower prompt or at a local talk, where the U3A and history-society talk skills apply.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • First call: "Llandough and Leckwith before 1970: the lanes, the farm, the school field". This ties to Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr / Cydfin Farm without asking anyone about ownership. Send it in the next Mailchimp issue, with Welsh first.
  • Form: a page on cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate (for example /remembered/share/), storing to the existing Worker or Airtable route already connected. The two consent boxes are mandatory. Voice notes get the OHS agreement templates, adapted, before any audio is published.
  • Public text rules: never name the project lead; banned words (states, assertions, per, second-hand report, not checked, reported, deception, taken); no insignia; nothing about family holdings or addresses; no accusations about named people; internal links only, with purchases via /ebooks/. Any memory touching the 1987 case or a living person is held back from the page and noted privately instead.
  • Spin-offs: the best 3 memories become quote cards in the Tuesday quote pack, with the contributor's permission. A strong memory with a date can feed the online-gap-finder ledger as a lead, never as evidence.
  • Where it lives: a proposal on QUEUE-after-framing.md until the bizdev plan picks it up. Zero spend, contact nobody directly.
