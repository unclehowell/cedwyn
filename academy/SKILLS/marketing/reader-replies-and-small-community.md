# Reader replies and a small community: newsletter conversations, then Discord only if it earns it
Harvested 2026-10-26 (skills harvest). Topic: community building from the mailing list (FB Groups covered in facebook-group-community-and-link-limits.md).

## What the practitioner evidence shows
- **Replies are the cheapest community, and they help deliverability (Inbox Collective, "leave a flank open").** A Danish newsletter editor's method: remove defensive "rebuttal" phrases ("some might say...", "it depends...", "there is no universal answer...") so readers have something to answer, or ask outright for missing ideas or corrections. Pre-write replies to the three most obvious objections, and answer every reader reply personally; several threads became long conversations and the repliers became the most loyal readers and sharers. Inbox Collective states that reader replies help deliverability (Gmail and others weigh replies as engagement). That is their statement; we have not measured it on our list.
- **A private Discord is a second job (Inbox Collective, "Come for the newsletter, stay for the Discord").** Example: one writer's Discord has ~800 members, about a fifth of her paying subscribers. She first wanted "this huge thing" and settled for a small, tight group after watching others sprawl. Operators describe the moderation and conversation-starting load as real work and "potential headaches".
- **Discord's own playbook (Discord Community Team).** Turn on Community server features: rules screening (members must agree to the rules before they can talk, react or DM), a welcome screen pointing to the right channels, announcement channels others can follow, and Server Insights. Insights reports visitors, communicators (members who posted) and new-member retention (the share who come back after a week). Discord uses engagement and retention to judge Partner applications.

## How-to
1. **Make every email answerable.** One specific question per email with a one-line reply ("Do you have a family record from Llandough before 1950? Reply with what it is."). Keep it a question the archive wants answered.
2. **Remove rebuttals, keep accuracy.** Cut defensive hedges; state what the record shows and invite corrections and additions.
3. **Reply to every reply within 48 h**, from a monitored address, personally. Log useful leads (a record someone holds, a correction) as a task.
4. **Feature replies (with permission).** Quote a reader's addition in the next email (first name or initials only, with consent). This shows that replies are read.
5. **Gate any Discord on numbers.** Open one only when about 20+ people have replied in the past month and someone can moderate daily. Start with three channels: #announcements, #ask-the-archive, #share-a-record. Turn on rules screening and the welcome screen. Watch new-member retention and communicators weekly, and close or merge channels that stay silent.
6. **Measure.** Replies per send (target: trending up), leads per month, unsubscribes after question emails; Discord communicators/visitors and one-week retention.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Add one reply question to each welcome-sequence email (welcome-sequence-first-sale-small-ebook-list.md) and to the weekly email. The "leave a flank open" version for an archive is "what are we missing?": ask readers for records, photos, map editions and family recollections of Tŷ Mawr / Great House Farm and Llandough, in English, Welsh and Spanish (Welsh Patagonia readers may hold letters).
- Replies arrive at the list's reply-to address. A reader reply becomes a HubSpot task to review the lead (read-only; no automated answers). Any answer is drafted and waits for sign-off; nothing is sent automatically.
- No Discord yet: the list is small. Revisit when replies pass ~20/month. The FB Group and fediverse accounts are the public community until then.
- Rules: never name the project lead publicly; banned words apply; quote readers only with explicit consent; no outreach to private individuals beyond answering people who wrote to us first.
