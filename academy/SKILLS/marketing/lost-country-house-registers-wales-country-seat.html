Lost-country-house registers: get a demolished manorial house onto the national tallies

Checked 29 Oct 2026. This is a niche but high-trust route. Great House / Tŷ Mawr, Llandough is a former manorial house that has been demolished. The people who keep the UK's lost-house tallies are architectural historians with large, loyal audiences, and they ask the public for information.

Evidence

  • The Country Seat (Matthew Beckett), "The growing silence" (27 Nov 2024, read):
  • The 1974 V&A Destruction of the Country House gazetteer listed 1,099 lost houses, only 46 of them in Wales.
  • Personal research projects have since raised the totals: England 2,019, Scotland 545 (Dr Alastair Disley), Wales 390 (Dr Mark Baker), Northern Ireland 100 (Andrew Triggs). That makes 3,054 UK houses lost since 1800.
  • So the Welsh count grew about 8.5x through one researcher taking contributions. The post ends with an explicit "Request for help ... history, dates for losses, or family photos or recollections ... please do contact me".
  • The Country Seat "About" (read): "If you have news you'd like to share, please do feel free to get in contact: [address kept in the project workspace]". The blog has published "Rules for comments", and Beckett takes no sponsorship. The site was short-listed for the SAHGB Colvin Prize in 2019, per Lost Heritage.
  • Lost Heritage (read): England only. For Wales it points to Dr Mark Baker's "The Welsh Country House / Lost Houses", marked "[temporarily not available]" on 29 Oct 2026. The printed references are Mark Baker, The Welsh Country House (Graffeg, 2018), and Thomas Lloyd, The Lost Houses of Wales (SAVE Britain's Heritage), both cited in a 2024 UWP book (snippet).
  • Not checked: whether Great House / Tŷ Mawr is already on Baker's 390, and whether a manorial farmhouse meets the "country house" threshold these lists use. Many lists include gentry and manor houses, but some exclude farmhouses. Check before any approach.

How to do it

  1. Check before contacting anyone (read-only): - Is the house in Lloyd's Lost Houses of Wales or Baker's The Welsh Country House? Use library catalogue or Google Books snippet search. - Is it on Wikipedia's "Welsh country houses destroyed in the 20th century" category? - Is it in any Country Seat post? Search the site for Llandough / Penarth / Glamorgan.
  2. Build a one-page fact sheet (public-safe): - name and Welsh name; - parish; - earliest dated record; - manorial status with its source; - date range of the loss (last map showing it, first map without it); - one image we hold copyright in, or a reference to a public archive image (e.g. a Britain from Above frame), never a copied image. - Use no ownership dispute, no court case, no family names and no banned words.
  3. Draft, don't send: - a short note to The Country Seat contact address, answering its "request for help" with the fact sheet; - a separate note to Dr Baker, via Graffeg or his university page, once his site is back. - Both are HubSpot draft tasks. Outreach goes only on the project lead's GO.
  4. Comment route (lower stakes): if a Country Seat post covers Glamorgan losses, one factual comment following its comment rules, with a link only to a free Cedwyn record page. Never to /ebooks/.
  5. Wikipedia: the category page is one place a properly sourced article could later sit. COI rules apply. See wikimedia-commons-wikidata-heritage-coi.md, and don't edit it ourselves.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Why it matters for sales: being on an authoritative national tally gives a permanent, citable third-party mention. AI answers and Google lean on exactly this kind of source (see ai-answer-citations-geo-sources-quotes-stats.md). It also reaches the country-house audience that buys local history.
  • Measure: referrals from thecountryseat.org.uk or Baker's site, and whether Grok and Perplexity name the house when asked "lost houses of the Vale of Glamorgan" (fold into the Monday visibility check).
  • Kill: if the house isn't eligible (a farmhouse below the threshold), stop after step 1. Record that in the log so later runs don't retry it.
  • Parcel rule: the house sat on its own tithe parcel, near the church. Name that parcel's tithe number from the project workspace in the fact sheet, or write "parcel not identified".
  • Rules: public text never names private people. Banned words apply. Nothing about family holdings or addresses. Zero spend; don't buy the books, use library or snippet access. Contact nobody without the project lead's GO.
