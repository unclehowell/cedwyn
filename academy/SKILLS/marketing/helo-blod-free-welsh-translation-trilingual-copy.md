# Free human Welsh for trilingual marketing: Helo Blod + the Commissioner's proofreading

Harvested 2026-10-27 07:27Z. Practical, zero-cost route to put checked Welsh (not machine Welsh) on the copy that sells.

## The facts (primary, read 27 Oct 2026)
- **Helo Blod (Welsh Government, Business Wales):** "can translate up to **500 words into Welsh per calendar month** for your business, charity or community group completely free of charge." **Text checking:** you write the Welsh, they check it (WCVA's summary: **1,000 words a year**). **Advice:** up to **10 enquiries a month** (WCVA). Free materials too (bilingual signs, badges, lanyards). Helpline 03000 25 88 88, Mon-Fri 10:00-15:00, or the contact form. Eligibility limits apply ("See if your request is eligible"; the eligibility page answered 403 to a script, so not read).
- Business Wales' own post: in 2020-2022 it supported customers with only 5 Welsh translation projects; "this year alone" 12 (Facebook snippet, not read in full). So demand is low and turnaround is realistic.
- **Welsh Language Commissioner, Hybu team:** free proofreading of **up to 1,000 words a year** for charities/voluntary groups (WCVA, 26 May 2020; current Commissioner page lists businesses and charities under the Cynnig Cymraeg). Email [address kept in the project workspace] (do not send without the project lead's OK).
- **Cynnig Cymraeg (the Welsh Offer):** a Commissioner recognition for private and third-sector bodies with a Welsh-language plan; badge usable in marketing.

So in a year: about 6,000 words translated (Helo Blod) + 1,000 checked (Helo Blod) + 1,000 proofread (Commissioner) = ~8,000 words of human Welsh, free.

## How to use it well
1. **Spend the words where money or trust changes hands.** Ranked: buy box and price lines; email signup line + welcome email; the e-book description (first 150 words); YouTube/Instagram bio; one-line calls to action; the cy subtitle lines spoken in films (only short ones).
2. **Batch monthly.** Month cap resets on the calendar month: send the 500 words in the first week, so the next batch can follow on the 1st.
3. **Prepare the English for translation:** short sentences, no idioms, no puns, a glossary of fixed terms (ask the translator which Welsh place-name forms to use, e.g. for Llandough; do not guess), and a note on register (friendly, "ti" vs "chi": ask for "chi" for public copy).
4. **Use checking for long text.** Draft long Welsh with machine help, then spend the checking allowance on the most visible passages.
5. **Version the copy:** store returned Welsh with date, source ("Helo Blod, checked"), and the English it matches, so nobody re-machines it later.
6. **Show it:** human-checked Welsh lets you drop disclaimers. The Cynnig Cymraeg badge is a later step once a simple Welsh-language plan exists.
