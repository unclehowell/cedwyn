# Unmoderated remote mobile usability test, free: phone screen recordings with voice, no paid platform

Date: 2026-10-31. Type: CRO / UX research skill. Distinct from small-sample-usability-tests-buy-path-5-users.md (moderated: someone is present, in person or on a call). Here nobody is present: the participant follows a written task card on their own phone and sends back a screen recording with their voice. Use it when a live session cannot be arranged, or to add more sessions cheaply between moderated rounds.

## What the evidence states (read 31 Oct 2026)
- Comparable numbers to lab tests: MeasuringU summarising Tullis et al. (Fidelity, same 17 intranet tasks): completion rates correlated r = .70, mean 60% lab vs 64% unmoderated, mean absolute difference 15%; the same tasks came out as hard in both. Task time correlated r = .65 (147 s lab vs 164 s unmoderated). In CUE-8 (Budget.com, 300+ participants) completion differed by 1% on average and SUS by 1.6 points; task time agreed less well (28% mean difference).
- Cheaper per person, not cheaper to set up: in CUE-9 teams spent on average 37 hours on unmoderated studies vs 60 hours moderated. The fixed cost (tasks, questions, pilot) is the same; the saving is per participant (MeasuringU, "10 Things to Know About Unmoderated Usability Testing").
- Main weaknesses (NN/g, "Remote Usability Tests: Moderated and Unmoderated"): you do not see a problem until the session is over and some sessions are unusable; participants go quiet because no one reminds them to think aloud; no follow-up questions. "The written instructions need to stand on their own... anything that can be misunderstood will be misunderstood." Pilot every task first.
- Sample: five sessions find the main problems (see the 5-user skill); unmoderated sessions fail more often, so recruit 6-7 to end with 5 usable recordings (our planning margin, not a published figure).

## The free kit (no paid platform, nothing to install)
- iPhone: built-in Screen Recording in Control Centre; long-press the record button to switch the microphone on (Apple Support, "Take a screen recording on iPhone").
- Android 11+: built-in Screen recorder in Quick Settings with the "Record audio: microphone" option.
- One task card page (plain HTML or a PDF) with a start link carrying a UTM tag (utm_source=usertest) so the session can be separated from real traffic in our stats.
- Return route: participant shares the video file to a dropbox/upload form the project already owns. Ask them to trim nothing.
- Tip from practice: notifications pop up in screen recordings; ask participants to switch on Do Not Disturb first.

## Step by step
1. Pick 2-3 tasks only; mobile sessions longer than 15-20 minutes lose people.
2. Write each task as a scenario with an end state, no button names: "You heard a talk about a Welsh farm's lost records and want to read more tonight. Find an e-book about it and get as far as the payment page. Stop there." Never say "click Buy".
3. Add a think-aloud line on every task card, not just at the top: "Say out loud what you are looking for and what you expect to happen."
4. After each task, one question: "How easy or hard was that, 1-7?" (SEQ, covered in its own skill) and "What, if anything, nearly made you give up?"
5. Pilot: one person runs the card while you watch the recording; rewrite every line they misread. Repeat until a pilot runs clean.
6. Send the card with consent text: what is recorded, what it is for, that they can stop at any time, that recordings are deleted after analysis.
7. Analyse each recording the same day on one sheet: task, success (yes/partial/no), time, where they hesitated (timestamp), what they said, severity 1-4. A problem counts when 2+ people hit it, or 1 person fails because of it.
8. Fix the top 1-3 problems, then run another small round. Small repeated rounds beat one big round (Nielsen, in the 5-user skill).

## Watch-outs
- Do not pay or reward in a way that buys praise; if a thank-you is given, give it whatever they say.
- Participants who already know the project are biased; prefer people who have not seen the site.
- Quiet recordings: keep them, but code only what they did, not what they "probably thought".
- No real purchase is needed: tell them to stop at the payment page. Nothing is charged.
- Use the UTM tag so tests are not counted as reader traffic or conversions.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Use when the owner can find testers (talk audiences, society contacts, friends of friends who have not seen the site) but cannot sit with them. Agents never recruit or message people directly.
- Tasks v1 (keep fixed so rounds compare): (a) from a research article, find the related e-book and reach the payment page; (b) from the home page, find the bundle and say what is in it; (c) find the free sample and open it on the phone.
- Agents write the task card (390 px friendly, one task per screen), the consent text, the analysis sheet, and code returned videos into the sheet; severity 3-4 items are re-checked live at 390 px before anything is filed.
- Fixes go through the existing proposal queue; buy-path changes are compared with the PURE totals and 5-user rounds.
- Reach link: task (a) starts on an article, the route most outside readers take; a smoother article-to-shelf step turns the ~45 outside views a day into more readers.
