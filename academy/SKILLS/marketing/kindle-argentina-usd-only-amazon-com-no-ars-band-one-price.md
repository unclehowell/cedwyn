# Kindle for Argentine readers: USD only, no ARS price, and how that sits with the one-price law (Ley 25.542)

## What the primary pages show (read 31 Oct 2026)
- **KDP has no Argentine marketplace and no ARS price.** The KDP "eBook List Price Requirements" page (kdp.amazon.com) lists price tables for USD (Amazon.com), INR, CAD, BRL, GBP, EUR, JPY, MXN, AUD. The word "Argentina" does not appear. So an Argentine reader buys the Kindle edition from **Amazon.com, priced in US dollars** (our reading; the store an Argentine account lands on was not tested from an Argentine IP).
- **USD band:** 70% royalty option needs a list price of **$2.99-$12.99** on Amazon.com (band widened from $9.99 to $12.99). Below or above, 35%. Minimum list price with 35%: $0.99 (<3 MB), $1.99 (3-10 MB), $2.99 (10 MB+).
- **70% only "to customers in the 70% territories"** (eBook Royalties page G200644210, read): 70% of list price without VAT minus delivery cost (average $0.06/unit, by file size); 35% for each sale to a customer outside those territories. KDP's help pages do not name the territories country by country; **whether Argentina is a 70% territory is not checked**. Practitioner summaries (authorimprints.com) say 70% = "US, Canada, the UK, Australia and most of Europe" — treat Argentine Kindle sales as **35% until a KDP sales report shows otherwise**.
- **Brazil, Japan, Mexico, India need KDP Select (exclusivity) for 70%** (same page). Argentina is not on that list — consistent with it having no local store.
- **"We don't price-match your book"** is the KDP default; Amazon still sets the customer price at its discretion (KDP terms: Amazon may discount; royalty stays on list price). Any Amazon discount is outside our control.

## What this means for the one-price law
- Ley 25.542 binds "el editor, importador o representante" to one PVP for sales to the final buyer in Argentina. It never mentions e-books (statute read today; see the Ley 25.542 file) — **grey zone, not checked**.
- On Kindle we cannot set an ARS price at all. The Argentine reader sees a USD price, plus the 30% perception and possibly 21% IVA on the card (card-surcharges file). So the ARS amount moves daily with the exchange rate, and Amazon may discount. **Nothing we do on Kindle can make one fixed ARS price.**
- Practical reading (ours, not legal advice): keep Kindle as a **secondary, USD-priced shelf**. Our own ARS price (Google Play typed ARS, own checkout) is the one we treat as the PVP. Never advertise Kindle as "the price in Argentina" and never run an Amazon-only discount in Argentine channels outside the 10% fair windows.

## Worked numbers (arithmetic only)
- List $4.99 on Amazon.com, file 2 MB.
  - If Argentina is a 70% territory: 0.70 x (4.99 - 0.12 delivery est.) = about **$3.41** a sale (no VAT deducted; Argentine IVA collection by Amazon not checked).
  - If 35%: 0.35 x 4.99 = about **$1.75** a sale.
- Buyer's cost at the card: $4.99 + 30% perception (refundable advance) + up to 21% IVA = up to about $7.50 in pesos at the official card rate.
- Compare: Google Play ARS typed price keeps the share on list price (Google file). Own checkout keeps most of it but carries the same card surcharges.

## How to do it (checklist)
1. In KDP pricing, set the Amazon.com USD list price **inside $2.99-$12.99** so any 70% territory sale earns 70%.
2. Do **not** enrol in KDP Select for Argentina's sake (no Argentine benefit; Select would block Google Play/Kobo/own shop).
3. After the first Argentine sale, read the KDP sales report row for royalty type (35% vs 70%) and note it here — this settles the territory question with our own data.
4. Keep one public Argentine price in ARS on our own pages; show Kindle only as "también en Kindle (precio en dólares)".
5. Re-check the KDP price-requirements page once a quarter for an ARS table or an Amazon.com.ar launch.
