Honourable Society of Cymmrodorion: Transactions articles (peer-reviewed), lecture programme and the open archive

Harvested 2026-10-30 (skills harvest run). Topic: scholarly authority and backlinks for Cedwyn's history through Wales's oldest learned society (founded 1751), its peer-reviewed annual journal, and its London/online talks. It complements history-magazine-journal-articles-historian-history-today.md (popular magazines) and review-copies-welsh-local-history-journals.md (county journals): this is the national, peer-reviewed tier.

What it is (read 30 Oct 2026 via a reader proxy; the site blocks this box's IP with a Cloudflare 403)

  • A London-based Welsh learned society, open to all. Corporate members include the British Library, the National Libraries of Wales, Scotland and Ireland, the Centre for Advanced Welsh and Celtic Studies, and Oxford, Cambridge and Harvard (join page, read). Its readers are librarians and academics, which is the audience that buys or cites.
  • Transactions (about page, read):
  • an annual scholarly journal of lectures given to the Society plus "articles commissioned by the Editor or submitted by contributors";
  • covers "all areas of Welsh history and culture from any period", in Welsh or English;
  • calls itself "the only one to specialise in interdisciplinary Welsh studies".
  • Submission rules (read):
  • max 7,000 words including notes, MHRA style;
  • submit a Word file via the site's submit-an-article form, or by email or post to the Editor (role address [address kept in the project workspace]);
  • blind peer review by one Editorial Board member plus a second reader, with comments returned to the author.
  • Open access: volumes 1900–2004 are free on the National Library of Wales journals site (journals.library.wales), and 2005 onwards is online on the Society's site. Published articles therefore become permanent, citable, crawlable pages.
  • Talks: 6:30–8 pm at the Medical Society of London (11 Chandos St, W1) or the London Welsh Centre, and live online; also on the Eisteddfod field. The 2026–27 programme runs Nov 2026 to Aug 2027 (Gwen John, 25 Nov 2026; Sir Simon Jenkins "Wales: Churches, Houses, Castles", 16 Mar 2027; Prof Patrick Sims-Williams, 18 May 2027; Goronwy Owen, Aug 2027). Lectures often become Transactions papers.
  • Membership: £35 a year (PAID: not for us under zero spend). It gives the printed Transactions, MP3s of talks and newsletters. There is also a free email newsletter sign-up and an essay prize (the Angharad Dodds Essay Prize 2026; terms not read, page empty via proxy).

Why it works (practitioner logic, with numbers)

  • Peer review plus one of Britain's oldest journals means a citation that librarians and Wikipedia editors accept. A 7,000-word limit is far below a monograph, so one well-sourced paper is achievable from material we already catalogue.
  • The NLW open archive shows the Society lets its back issues sit permanently free online. An accepted paper is a durable, authoritative page that AI answer engines and search can cite (pairs with the AI-citations skill).
  • Lectures are hybrid (London + online), so a free online seat lets us study the format and audience at zero cost before pitching.

Step-by-step

  1. Join the free email newsletter (no membership fee) to get talk notices and calls.
  2. Attend one online lecture (e.g. 16 Mar 2027, Churches, Houses, Castles: on topic for a manorial house). Note the length, the Q&A style and the audience.
  3. Draft a ≤7,000-word article (MHRA notes) on a narrow, documentary question. For example: "Leases, tithes and rate books: reconstructing occupation of one Vale of Glamorgan farm, 1840–1990". Use primary records only, shown neutrally with both readings where a record is contested.
  4. Pre-check it against blind peer review: every claim footnoted to an archive reference, no argument about living people or current litigation, and nothing a reviewer would read as advocacy.
  5. Submit through the website form (Word file). Expect a long review cycle (timing not checked; assume 6–12 months to print).
  6. Once published, link the OA article from the evidence pages and the e-book's further-reading list.

Rules

  • Zero spend: no membership unless the owner approves; free newsletter and online talks only.
  • Submissions and any contact go out only as a HubSpot draft for the owner to send. One article at a time; never a pitch email to Board members personally.
  • Scholarly tone: no banned words; write "not checked" for unconfirmed items. Never "tenant farmers": a Bute paper is "a Bute estate tenancy document". No family holdings or addresses; never name the owner as author unless he chooses to (author line is his decision).
