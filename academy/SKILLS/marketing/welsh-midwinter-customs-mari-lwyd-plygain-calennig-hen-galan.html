Welsh midwinter customs as a content calendar: Mari Lwyd, Plygain, Calennig, Hen Galan

Harvested 2026-10-29 (skills harvest). Companion to calan-gaeaf-seasonal-hook-welsh-halloween-folklore.md (31 Oct) and st-dwynwens-day-25-jan-welsh-gift-free-book.md (25 Jan). This file fills the gap between them: Christmas Eve to 13 January.

What the primary source states (Museum Wales / Amgueddfa Cymru, read 29 Oct 2026)

  • Mari Lwyd ('Grey Mare'): a horse figure carried door to door by wassail singers, from Christmas Day to Twelfth Night and Hen Galan (13 Jan). A real horse skull with ribbons, rosettes and glass bottle eyes, the jaw on a spring, the carrier under a white cloth. At the door comes the pwnco, a contest of improvised verses sung in Welsh, often with the door shut; letting the Mari in was thought to bring luck. Characters: Leader, Sergeant, Merryman, Punch and Judy.
  • Earliest account 1798; boom years 1850-1920; almost entirely south Wales, "especially popular in Glamorgan", Monmouthshire and Carmarthenshire. Declined in the 20th century (fewer Welsh speakers to answer the verses; chapel disapproval of drunkenness); a revival is now running across Wales. St Fagans stages it each winter, and the figure is in its 'Wales: Told by Us' exhibition.
  • Plygain: a dawn carol service on Christmas morning, held between 3 a.m. and 6 a.m.; young people waited up at farmhouses making cyflaith (treacle toffee) and decorating with holly and mistletoe (Marford, 1830s); candles brought from even the remotest farmsteads; torch processions in Tenby, Laugharne and Llanfyllin.
  • Calennig (New Year gift): children go door to door early on New Year's Day carrying a decorated apple on three sticks with box and hazelnuts, singing a short verse for bread, cheese or coins. A Carmarthenshire resident in the 1860s called New Year's Day "the biggest day of the year". First-footing beliefs vary by place (Pencoed near Bridgend: a red-haired man first is bad luck). New Year water ritual: a sprig dipped in well water sprinkled on faces (Tenby photo, 1928).
  • Hen Galan (13 Jan, Old New Year under the Julian calendar): still kept in Cwm Gwaun, Pembrokeshire, with children singing calennig verses (Bluestone, RCAHMW and BBC Cymru Wales posts seen in search snippets only, not read).

Why it fits a Glamorgan farm archive

The farm is in the Vale of Glamorgan, the county Museum Wales names as the Mari Lwyd heartland. The 1850-1920 boom years match the period of the farm records. Farmhouses were the gathering places for plygain nights. Every post can be "the custom, as it would have been round here", without claiming the farm itself kept it.

How to run it (steps)

  1. Pick 4 dated posts, one per custom. Fixed dates: Plygain 24/25 Dec; Mari Lwyd from 26 Dec (best week 27 Dec-6 Jan); Calennig 1 Jan; Hen Galan 13 Jan.
  2. Every post = one record + one custom. Use a period image we hold the rights to, or a plain quote card (bilingual layout skill), plus 2-3 lines: what happened, where in Glamorgan, and one Welsh verse line with its English.
