An op-ed is OUR argument, written by us, published under a named author, usually unpaid. The outlet's readers see the case in full, and the author bio carries one link.

## What the sources say
- **Nation.Cymru contributor guide** (read; PDF from 2017, linked from the live Contact page, read 30 Oct 2026):
  - A volunteer-run national English-language news site for Wales. The Contact page states "over one million readers a month" (their figure, not checked) and opinion "from across the political spectrum".
  - Subjects: anything touching Wales as a nation. History and culture are named explicitly.
  - Length: "somewhere over 500 and under 800 words is the ideal". The guide notes that most people stop reading on screen after ~800 words, so split longer ideas into 2-3 pieces.
  - Find a **timely hook** in the news.
  - Put the point and argument at the start, backed by evidence, then the conclusion. Bullets and subheads are fine.
  - Write in accessible language, with no jargon.
  - "Punch up, not down": criticising governments, organisations and authorities is fine; blaming minority groups is not.
  - If the piece must run by a certain date, say so in the email. Expect a slow reply ("large volume of emails").
- **The OpEd Project, "Pitching"** (read). The email pitch answers:
  - Why now (the hook)?
  - Why this topic?
  - Why me?
  - It also carries the idea in a few lines, relevant credentials only, **the finished piece pasted below**, and contact details.
  - Pitch **one outlet at a time**. Most national papers refuse a piece sent to more than one paper at once.
  - Follow-up: set a deadline matched to shelf life (a day for news-pegged pieces, 1-2 weeks for evergreen). Then send one line saying you'll assume a pass and offer it elsewhere.
  - Thank the editor even after a no.
- **OpEd Project "Resources" FAQ** (read): a Washington Post 5-month count (2008) found 90% of op-ed submissions came from men, and 88% of bylines were male. Editors report 80-90% male submissions, so new and under-heard voices stand out.
- **Washington Post "Submit an op-ed"** (read, help centre):
  - At most 800 words, completed articles only (no proposals), exclusive to them.
  - **Full name required: anonymous or pseudonymous op-eds are not considered.** Home address, email and phone are also required, and any personal or financial interest must be disclosed.
  - They receive "hundreds of submissions a day" and run "a handful".
  - They select for timeliness, resonance and freshness. A title or special expertise isn't required, but explaining how your background informs your view helps.
  - This is a US national paper; UK selectivity is similar in kind (letters-page skill: NYT ~1-1.5%). No UK op-ed acceptance rate was found (not checked).

## Recipe
1. **Pick one outlet** that fits the argument. In Wales: Nation.Cymru (history and culture welcome), WalesOnline/Western Mail opinion (route not checked), the Welsh-language outlets in welsh-language-media-papur-bro-golwg360-heno.md, or the Western Mail Saturday heritage pages (not checked).
2. **Hook first:** a dated news event, anniversary, committee inquiry or report this week. Without one, hold the piece in a drafts folder until a hook appears.
3. **Write the whole piece (500-800 words):**
   - argument in the first two sentences;
   - 3 pieces of evidence, each one specific (a record, a date, a number);
   - what should change, and who can change it;
   - plain words.
4. **Pitch email:**
   - subject = headline idea plus the hook;
   - 3-line pitch (why now / why this / why this author);
   - 2-line bio with ONE link;
   - piece pasted below (not attached);
   - "needed by <date>" if news-pegged.
5. **Exclusivity:** one outlet at a time. Follow up once after 2-3 days (news) or 10 days (evergreen), then move to the next outlet.
6. **After publication:** add the outlet to the press/"as seen in" page. Share the outlet's link (not a copy) on owned channels. Never republish the full text without permission; a canonical-linked excerpt is fine.
7. **Measure:** referral sessions from the outlet domain (Cloudflare/GA4) and sign-ups in the 7 days after, using a UTM on the bio link if the outlet allows it (tools/utm.py).

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **GATE: needs GO** (press only on GO, per cedwyn-growth RULES.md). This is a PROPOSAL for the QUEUE-after-framing list, not an action.
- **Byline problem:** public text never names the project lead, and outlets need a real full name (WaPo explicitly; Nation.Cymru's policy on anonymity was not checked).
  - So an op-ed needs a different named author who is willing: a family member who agrees to be named, or a named researcher speaking for the project.
  - Choosing the author is a decision for the project lead. Aunts are first-hand witnesses, but no aunt names go into anything public without that decision.
- **Candidate hooks and angles** (neutral, no banned words, never "tenant farmers"):
  1. **Missing place-names.** The national List of Historic Place Names holds 1 tithe-era field name for Llandough parish vs 16 for Michaelston-le-Pit (place-names skill, read 30 Oct 2026). The argument: Wales's local records have gaps that families can help fill. Hook: any Welsh place-names news, or the Senedd Culture Committee after 2 Nov.
  2. **"Records that should exist."** The public missing-records register (MR IDs on /research/missing-records/) as a case for better archive retention and disclosure. Punch up at institutions and processes, never at named individuals.
  3. **Calan Gaeaf / seasonal heritage.** Too late for 31 Oct 2026; hold for 2027. See calan-gaeaf-seasonal-hook-welsh-halloween-folklore.md.
- **Bio line:** "<Author> writes for the Cedwyn heritage project" plus one link to cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate (or /ebooks/ only if the outlet allows commercial links).
- **Draft route:** a HubSpot task in the hive format (pitch plus pasted piece). Never sent by us. the project lead or the named author sends it.
- **Editorial scan before any draft:** no "states / assertions / per / second-hand report / not checked / reported / deception / taken"; no family holdings or addresses; no accusations about named people.
