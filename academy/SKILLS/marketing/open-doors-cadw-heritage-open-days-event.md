# Open Doors (Cadw) and Heritage Open Days: a free September heritage event
Harvested 26 Oct 2026 (skills harvest 15:52). Internal skill file.

## What they are
- **Open Doors** is Wales' part of European Heritage Days, funded and run by Cadw, all through September. Cadw's 2026 page: more than 200 historic sites, landmarks and hidden gems offered free entry, events or guided tours; it invites "heritage organisations, private owners, local authorities and others". Contact [address kept in the project workspace]
- Cadw's organiser guide (read in full; Crown copyright 2022, file dated 2023): in 2018, **1,300 events at 287 venues across all 22 local authorities**. Events must be FREE. Eligible activities include "a free event (a walking tour, lecture, exhibition, concert, memories, historical re-enactment) that in some way engages with the local heritage", and special events at routinely open places such as a library or church ("a talk, exhibition, access to records and treasures").
- What Cadw does: registers and publishes the whole programme on its website, runs national PR, marketing and social media, and supplies free posters, logos and a press-release template. What the organiser does: register online, run the event, put up posters, write a local press piece from the template, count visitors, fill in a feedback form.
- Dates in that guide: registration opens **1 March**, deadline **1 July** (check the 2027 dates with Cadw; the guide is older).
- **Heritage Open Days** is England's equivalent (11–20 Sept 2026; 10–19 Sept 2027). Its registration guide (read in full): submit by **13 August**; online events are accepted but every online event is checked by the national team, which can take weeks if submitted in July; entries can be edited until the event starts.

## Why it works (practitioner points from the organiser guide)
- Free national listing plus the national campaign: the event rides Cadw's PR rather than ours.
- Clusters: Cadw finds visitor numbers are higher when nearby sites run events on the same weekend, so pair with another Vale of Glamorgan event.
- Past organisers gained members and raised money from catering, retail or donations, so a free talk can still point people to the free e-books and the newsletter.

## How to do it
1. February: pick a format: a free talk or records display at a public venue (a library or community hall) that agrees to host, or a guided walk on public rights of way only.
2. Get the venue's written OK; check Cadw's Open Doors public liability page (not read yet) for insurance needs.
3. 1 March to 1 July: register on the Cadw form with the date, venue, accessibility, booking method and a photo you have the rights to (Cadw publishes an image agreement).
4. Use the Open Doors logo on every poster and post; schedule the anniversary-calendar posts (heritage-anniversary-hashtag-calendar.md) for the September weekend.
5. On the day: a printed QR card to the free e-books on /ebooks/ and the newsletter signup, with a UTM tag (utm_source=opendoors).
6. Count visitors, send Cadw's feedback form, log results in the project workspace

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Fit: medium-high for September 2027, zero spend.** A free talk, "Tŷ Mawr, Llandough: what the records show", built from the evidence catalogue and the e-books.
- Wording follows the editorial rules: the 1824 David Stewart survey is the first record mention of Tŷ Mawr found; 1667 is the family's acquisition, cited to the 1974 newspaper reports (Mary Williams' statement) and ITV News 1988; no Bute dates before 1824; none of the banned public words; never name the owner; nothing about family holdings or named people's conduct.
- No event on or near the old farm site without the landowner's written consent. Prefer a library venue.
- **Owner steps:** decide whether to run it, who presents, and which venue to approach. Tab drafts the Cadw registration text and a venue enquiry as HubSpot drafts only, never sent by us. No approach to the Llandough Community Council while its holds apply.
- Pairs with heritage-talks-circuit-aatp-branches.md (the same talk reused) and family-history-society-journals-forums.md (listing the event in society newsletters).
- Heritage Open Days (England) is a fallback for an online talk, but first check its organiser agreement for organisers based outside England.
