Consignment ("sale or return") for a print copy in local bookshops, museum and heritage gift shops (UK)

Harvested 28 Oct 2026 (skills harvest). Index had 0 hits for "consignment", "gift shop", "museum shop". Complements print-on-demand-paperback-kdp-talk-table.md (the paperback itself) and ffolio-cymru-books-council-wales-listing.md (Books Council distribution centre route).

What the practitioners report (with numbers)

  • Booksellers Association guide for indie authors (6-page PDF, read in full). Most usual trade practice for independently published titles is consignment terms: the shop pays once a copy sells and returns unsold stock when it chooses. Alternative: sale or return (shop pays on your invoice terms, may return unsold copies for a full refund). Agree a period; uncollected stock after 3 months may be disposed of by the shop. Nielsen: average bookshop discount is just over 40% off RRP; in the UK the supplier pays carriage. Email first, no unannounced visits. Wants: 2-3 line synopsis, a line on you, RRP, a professional invoice with your terms, format, sample pages, why it sells in that shop, comparable titles, shelf section, jacket jpg, local ties, local publicity lined up. Physical musts: a spine with the title on it and a 13-digit EAN barcode. "A standard paperback is around GBP 8.99." Launch Feb/March, not near Christmas (lost on shelves). No Amazon links in the pitch. Being on Gardners/Bertrams at standard discount with returns raises the chance of stocking "tenfold"; wholesaler cut about 15%. Warning: if you sell to all your friends directly, the shop sells none and returns the books within a month.
  • Karin Wiberg, Clear Sight Books (updated 24 Jun 2026). Most common split 60% author / 40% shop; seen shops take 50-55%; one non-profit museum bookshop kept only 30-35%. Shops ask for 3-5 copies, reconcile monthly or quarterly, and ask you to collect slow sellers. Many shops refuse KDP-printed books. You have already paid print and postage, so do the arithmetic.
  • Muirae Kenney, former bookshop owner (Aug 2025). About 500 author requests, fewer than 50 yeses (<10%). Consignment ("spec") made yes much easier. A memoir whose author had already given copies to friends, employees, neighbours: friends traded them in, shop stopped at five, three still unsold at closing. Pitch the audience you will send ("I'd love to send my readers here") rather than asking for shelf space. Don't drop and disappear; don't set up displays uninvited.
  • Beth Martin (15 Jun 2026), red flags. Per-title placement fees of $20-35 are spreading; she treats them as pay-to-play. Consignment bookkeeping is weak: authors report lost copies and forgotten payments, so check stock in person periodically.
  • Bookspace guide (vendor, secondary). Many Waterstones branches run informal local-author programmes on consignment, typically 60/40 (GBP 9.99 RRP -> about GBP 6.00 to the author); some WHSmith shops in tourist areas take local authors at 60/40 or 65/35. Treat as not checked per branch.

Why consignment, not the wholesaler, for us

KDP Expanded Distribution pays 40% minus print and allows no returns, so a shop ordering via Gardners cannot send unsold copies back and usually won't order. Consignment with author copies (print cost + postage) keeps the margin and removes the shop's risk.

The how-to

  1. Gate: a print edition exists with spine, EAN barcode and printed RRP (KDP free ISBN is fine for consignment). Pick one title, RRP GBP 8.99-9.99.
  2. Margin check per copy (Ty Mawr Deaths, 69 pp, print GBP 1.93 + postage, not checked, say GBP 0.80): at GBP 8.99 and 60% -> GBP 5.39 - 2.73 = about GBP 2.66; museum shop at 70% -> GBP 6.29 - 2.73 = about GBP 3.56; a GBP 25 placement fee wipes out the first 7-10 sales, so decline fees.
  3. Target list (10 shops max, all businesses): independent bookshops, museum and heritage-site shops, garden-centre/farm-shop book corners within ~20 miles; read each shop's "local authors"/"consignment" page first and note its split, fee, and whether it refuses KDP prints.
  4. Pitch email (HubSpot draft, never sent by us): the BA checklist above in 150 words, local tie first, the talk or event that will send buyers to them, invoice/consignment note attached (title, ISBN, RRP, split, copies left, review date, collection rule, payment monthly). Offer signed copies and a short talk or signing.
  5. Drop 3-5 copies, both sign the consignment note, photo of copies on shelf.
  6. Ledger: one row per shop: date, copies left, sold, paid, next check (every 4-6 weeks). Collect after 3 months if nil sold.
  7. Send buyers there: list "Also on the shelf at ..." on the website and in the newsletter; never also give free copies to the same local circle.
  8. Kill rule: under 2 sold per shop per quarter -> collect and move the copies to the talk table.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • PROPOSAL only, appended to the queue after framing; depends on the paperback proposal (the project lead's KDP account + GO) and costs author copies (spend needs the project lead's GO).
  • Companies only; HubSpot drafts only; the project lead sends. A shelf card ("Read the history") may carry a QR to Cedwyn /ebooks/ via our own redirect (qr-codes-print-placement-own-redirects-honest-counts.md), UTM src=shop-, med=print.
  • Editorial: the shelf card and any public listing never name the project lead; the pitch is a HubSpot draft for the project lead to review and send himself. No banned words, nothing about family holdings or addresses.
  • Pair with the Books Council of Wales distribution centre route for Welsh shops once an ISBN + printed price exist (35% shop + 20% centre).
  • Best timing: first drop Feb/March 2027 (BA advice), talk-season bookings as the "publicity lined up".
