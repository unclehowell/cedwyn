# Nextdoor Business Page for a local-history e-book shop (UK)

Read 26 Oct 2026. Status for Cedwyn: PROPOSAL (new channel; account needs the owner's own name and address check, so owner GO and owner signup).

## What the platform offers (primary pages read)
- UK reach: "Over 10 million neighbours on Nextdoor in the UK" and "1 in 4 UK households are on Nextdoor" (Nextdoor's own UK business page, business.nextdoor.com). Nextdoor's own figures; not checked independently.
- A Business Page is free. Nextdoor's current getting-started page offers "unlimited free posts". An older Nextdoor blog (2019, updated Jul 2021) and a 2026 agency guide both describe 2 free Business Posts a month. The two disagree; read the live limit in the account before planning a cadence.
- Eligibility (Nextdoor help article title and snippet only; page is script-rendered and did not load): real name and a valid address. Whether an online-only shop qualifies, and whether the address can be hidden on the public page, is not checked.
- Recommendations ("Faves"/hearts) are the social proof. Neighbours can recommend a Page; Nextdoor's own help steers businesses to ask existing customers.

## Nextdoor's own posting data (business.nextdoor.com blog "5 steps to success with Business Posts")
1. Pages with 5+ recommendations get 30% higher engagement (views, reactions, replies).
2. Posting 5-7 pm local time sees higher engagement.
3. Thursday and Friday posts beat earlier weekdays and weekends.
4. Engagement on a post declines after about two weeks, so post every other week.
5. Pages with a profile photo and photos in the Photos section are the most visited; posts with a photo do better.
These are Nextdoor's aggregate figures, not a small-seller case study. No UK heritage/author case with numbers was found this run.

## How to run it (zero spend)
1. Owner sets up the Page (real name, address check). Category: bookshop/publisher or local history; service area = the local neighbourhoods, not a pin on a home.
2. Fill every field: one-line description ("Archive-based local history of <place>, free first edition"), website link to the e-book page with UTM (utm_source=nextdoor&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=page), 4-6 photos (old maps, archive scans, cover).
3. First post: the story of the place, one striking archive image, a question neighbours can answer ("Who remembers the farmhouse before the estate?"). Ask people who already know the work to recommend the Page; target 5 recommendations before judging reach.
4. Cadence: one post every two weeks, Thu or Fri, 5-7 pm. Rotate: archive photo + question; free chapter offer; local talk or library date; "then and now" image.
5. No ads, no Local Deals that cost money, no sponsored posts. No DMs to strangers. Reply to every comment within a day.
6. Measure: Page views, recommendations, post reactions/replies in the account; sessions from utm_source=nextdoor in analytics; signups from that source. Judge after 4 posts (8 weeks): keep if it beats the median of our other social channels on sessions per post.

## Risks
- Nextdoor threads can turn into disputes about local people. Keep posts on records and places; no named-person accusations; do not argue in comments.
- Address privacy: never show the family's home address publicly. If the Page cannot hide it, do not proceed.
