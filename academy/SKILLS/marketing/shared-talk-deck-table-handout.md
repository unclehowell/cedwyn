# One shared talk deck + table handout for every talk circuit


## What practitioners do (read at source)
- Jodee Blanco (IngramSpark blog, updated 1 Apr 2026, 20+ years speaking): bring (1) QR codes in the slides or printed collateral linking straight to a mailing list or landing page, (2) "a printed hand-out with four or five highlights from your talk, the title of your book, and your contact info and author website", (3) a notebook to record leads. After: thank-you notes to everyone who organised it, then "catalytic outreach": ask the contact which other organisations might want the talk.
- Jackie Karneth (Books Forward, 31 Jan 2025): libraries want education, not an "author talk"; pitch research findings. "A history author may create a handout with useful terminology or timelines." Put a QR code "on your handout or slideshow that links to an online ordering page". Tell the venue how the talk was received last time; be flexible; consider waiving the fee.
- Sharyn (Author Training Programs, "Back of the Room Sales"): when a venue bans selling, Tom Antion put a book on every chair "and told the event planner that he needed to refer to his book throughout his presentation"; more than half of 400 then asked to buy (one practitioner anecdote, no date, not independently checked). Lesson for us: refer to the book as the source during the talk, rather than pitching it at the end.
- Our own circuit files: Rotary "must avoid overt sales" and no soliciting purchases, handouts welcome; WI excludes pure business pitches; Probus avoids politics/religion and wants 40-60 min; u3a members cannot charge. So the deck must work with ZERO sales pitch; the handout carries the offer quietly.

## The deck (one master, three lengths)
- Master 45 min (societies, Insole Court, libraries); cut to 20 min (Rotary) and 30 min (WI/Probus) by hiding sections, not rebuilding.
- 12-16 slides, one image + one line each; big type (older audiences, church-hall projectors).
- Structure: 1 title + QR; 2 the place today (map); 3-8 the record trail in date order (each slide = one document, its archive and date on the slide); 9-10 what changed and what is lost; 11 how to look up your own house/farm in the same records (the useful-skill part libraries and u3a want); 12 sources; 13 last slide = free edition QR + "questions".
- Every record slide names its source; that IS the book reference (the Antion move without selling).
- Export PDF too; bring on USB + phone; a 16:9 and a 4:3 version for old projectors.

## The handout (one A5 card, both sides)
- Front: title, 4-5 highlights as a dated timeline (Books Forward's "timeline" idea), one image.
- Back: "Find the records yourself" 3 tips (archives, maps, newspapers); the free edition QR; the shop QR; a short sign-up line; website.
- Print 1.5x the expected audience (societies ~20-40). Print cost needs the owner's GO (zero spend by default); a free PDF the organiser can print or email is the fallback.
- A sign-up sheet (name + email, tick-box consent wording) only where the venue allows it; otherwise QR only.

## After every talk (10 minutes)
1. Thank-you email draft to the organiser (HubSpot draft, owner's GO to send) with the PDF handout attached for their newsletter.
2. Catalytic outreach line in that email: "Is there another group you think would enjoy this?"
3. Log in HubSpot: venue, attendees, QR scans (Cloudflare/GA by utm_source), sign-ups, sales within 7 days.
4. Change one slide per talk based on questions asked.

## Numbers (honest)
- No practitioner gave a scan or sign-up rate per talk. Antion's "more than half of 400" is one anecdote, not checked. Earlier files: WI author "yet to sell a book after a talk". So measure, do not assume.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Build the deck once (presentation craft) and the handout once (document craft) as the next bizdev task; every circuit file then points here.
- Repeat bar per venue: 20 attendees or 5 sign-ups or 1 sale.
