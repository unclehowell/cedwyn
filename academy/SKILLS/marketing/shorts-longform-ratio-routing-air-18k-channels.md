# Shorts-to-long-form ratio and routing (AIR 18,000-channel study, education niche)

Harvested 2026-10-30. Question it answers: the project lead wants a steady drip of HyperFrames Shorts on YouTube (13:28, 13:42) — does a Shorts-heavy channel help or hurt the long-form documentary scenes and archive films, and how many Shorts per long video?

## What the evidence states
- **AIR Media-Tech, "Do YouTube Shorts help your long-form videos?" (Olha Kapinos, updated 28 May 2026, read):** 18,000 English-language channels tracked Mar 2025 - Mar 2026, 11 niches, each ≥40 uploads and ≥10 long-form in the period; median subscribers compared for Long-Only (<10% Shorts), Long+Short Mix (10-70%) and Transitional.
  - **Education:** Mix channels have **37% more subscribers** at 100K-1M monthly views (113,000 vs 82,200) and 12% more at 1M-10M (395,000 vs 354,000). Advice: Shorts as discovery ("a concept introduced, a question posed, a single fact demonstrated in 60 seconds"), "use the Related Video field to link directly to the full lesson", and do not overhaul identity in one quarter.
  - **Ratio:** best results at **25-40% Shorts** of total uploads (education median ~38%); **above 55%** Shorts crowd out long-form; below 10% = effectively Long-Only. Rule across niches: gradual integration beats abrupt pivots.
  - Case: a Canadian entertainment channel at 11,000 subs built Shorts as "traffic gateways into the long-form catalog" — Shorts views 169,724 → 615,683 in one quarter, **total watch time +624%**.
  - Limits stated by AIR: correlational, and the smallest segment is 100K monthly views. A channel with single-digit subscribers is outside the sample. AIR sells audits (vendor source) — treat numbers as directional.

## How to do it
1. **Count the mix monthly:** uploads in the last 30 days split Short (vertical, ≤3 min) vs long. Target band 25-40% Shorts once the long-form cadence exists; if long-form is near zero, the fix is more long-form anchors, not fewer Shorts.
2. **Every Short routes to one long video** (Related Video). One long-form anchor per story arc; Shorts are the trailer for a chapter, not standalone facts.
3. **Ramp, don't pivot:** add Shorts at a steady rate (e.g. 1/day) rather than bursts of 10 in a day; keep long-form on its own cadence.
4. **Measure the routing, not the Shorts views:** YouTube Analytics > Content > Shorts > "Related video" clicks (where shown) and long-form views with traffic source "Shorts feed"/"Related video"; subscribers gained per 1,000 Shorts views.
5. Review after 4 weeks; if long-form views per upload fall while Shorts rise past ~55% of uploads, slow Shorts.
