Academic lists: H-Net networks and JiscMail for a heritage archive (UK institutions)

Harvested 2026-10-27 06:40Z. Read today: H-Net's posting guide, network and Book Channel pages; Jisc's JiscMail page; GEM's list page. JiscMail's acceptable-use page answered 403 to a scripted fetch, so its exact wording is NOT read here; read it in a browser before any post.

Why this channel

  • H-Net (Michigan State University): about 200 free networks edited by scholars; "over 230,000 subscribers in more than 200 countries"; each network publishes 15-60 messages a week; subscribers are screened by editors and include researchers, teachers, librarians, archivists and journalists (h-net.org, read).
  • JiscMail (Jisc, UK education and research): "more than 9,000 lists and over a million unique users"; announcement lists exist for "publications, conferences and events" (jisc.ac.uk, snippet + page).
  • Example UK list size: GEM (Group for Education in Museums) JiscMail list, free to use, "2000+ subscribers" (gem.org.uk, read).
  • These are the inboxes of the exact UK institutional people a cold email cannot reach at scale, and posting to a list is opt-in for the reader (no PECR cold-email question).

The rules that decide what can be posted (H-Net, read)

  1. You need an H-Net profile with your FULL PERSONAL NAME (not an organisation's) and a professional affiliation; nothing is published until it is there.
  2. Announcements may NOT contain "publicity for new publications", "for-profit activities", duplicates/reminders, or solicitations of legal support for specific causes.
  3. New publications go to the separate H-Net Book Channel (logged-in submission form; it imports publisher catalogues; contact [address kept in the project workspace]).
  4. Reviews: H-Net "commissions reviews through network review editors, and not in response to direct solicitations from book authors". Do not ask for a review.
  5. An announcement passes central review, then each network editor decides whether to re-post to their subscribers.
  6. Non-English announcements need an English translation.

What fits (allowed shapes)

  • A free public event: an online talk, a heritage open day walk, a call for contributions (photos, documents, memories), a volunteer transcription call. Include date, time, place/link and the organising body.
  • A free research resource: a dataset, a catalogue of records, a timeline - posted as a resource/discussion where the network accepts them.
  • A genuine discussion question to a subject network (e.g. what records a 1950s estate transfer should have generated) - the best way to earn replies from archivists.
  • NOT allowed: "buy our e-book", a price, a sale, a campaign for a legal outcome.

How to do it (steps)

  1. Pick networks by fit: browse networks.h-net.org (e.g. H-Albion for British and Irish history, plus local/rural/environmental history networks). Read each network's own posting guidelines and the last 20 posts.
  2. Create the profile (person's real name + affiliation; an independent researcher affiliation is a judgement for the person).
  3. Draft one announcement per event/resource: title, 120-200 words, what it is, who it is for, date/time/link, organiser, English (plus Welsh/Spanish if useful, English required).
  4. Submit; log date, network, status, re-posts.
  5. JiscMail: find lists at jiscmail.ac.uk (archives, local history, museum education, Welsh studies, digital humanities). Read the list's own description and the JiscMail acceptable-use policy first; some lists are announcement-only, some discussion-only, most ban commercial posts. Join, read for a week, then post once.
  6. One post per event, no reminders (H-Net bans duplicates). Measure with a UTM on the link (utm_source=h-net or jiscmail-, utm_medium=list).

Testimony

  • Steven Mintz (University of Texas at Austin) on the Book Channel: with newspaper review sections gone "scholars have few timely ways to keep up with the latest books" (H-Net Book Channel page, read).
  • No published click or sales figure for list announcements was found today; treat this as reach into a screened professional audience, not a sales channel.

How we apply it to Cedwyn (PROPOSED; drafts only, nothing posted)

  • Conflict to settle first: H-Net requires a real personal name on the posting profile, and Cedwyn public text never names the person behind it. Only the project lead can decide whether a named person posts (his own name, or someone else with an affiliation). Until then, this channel is blocked; record it as a decision for him.
  • Shapes that fit Cedwyn without selling: (a) a call for public contributions (photos, documents, memories of the farm and the Vale), linked to a /contribute/ page if it exists; (b) the evidence catalogue / missing-records register as a free research resource; (c) a free online talk if one is booked; (d) one discussion question to an archives list about what records a mid-century land transfer should have produced.
  • Never: a post about buying e-books, prices, the court case as a cause, or anything soliciting legal help (H-Net bans it).
  • Book Channel: only if an e-book gets an ISBN and a publisher-style listing; it is aimed at academic titles. Low priority.
  • Draft each announcement as a HubSpot task for the project lead's review; press/outreach only on his GO. Log in the project workspace with the UTM.
