Social posting times: Buffer 2026 priors, then test on our own posts

Use when: choosing when Instagram / Facebook posts go out, or when reach dropped and timing is a suspect. Harvested 27 Oct 2026.

What the evidence states (read in full, 27 Oct 2026)

Instagram (Buffer, "Best Time to Post on Instagram in 2026", 23 Sep 2026): 9.6M posts, 200,000+ accounts, Jan 2024-Dec 2025. Metric = median engagement rate ((likes + comments + shares + saves) / followers x 100). Times normalised to the poster's own local time zone. - Top slots: Thu 09:00, Wed 12:00, Wed 18:00. - Best days: Wed, then Thu, Tue. Worst: Fri and Sat (lower in every slot). - Evenings (18:00-23:00) win every weekday except Thursday. On Thursday, 07:00-09:00 beats the evening. - Mornings (06:00-11:00) are the weakest, except Thu 09:00. - By day (top 3): Wed 12:00/18:00/08:00 · Thu 09:00/08:00/07:00 · Tue 19:00/15:00/17:00 · Mon 19:00/18:00/20:00 · Sun 21:00/22:00/20:00 · Fri 22:00/21:00/06:00 · Sat 21:00/22:00/20:00. - Buffer's own caveat: reels may peak differently. Test early-morning and midday slots for reels.

Facebook (Buffer, "Best Time to Post on Facebook - New Data from 14M Posts", 19 Oct 2026): 14M posts, same method and local-time normalisation. - Single best slot: Thu 09:00 (Thursday 08:00 and 10:00 also strong). Wed and Sun mornings are also strong. - Best day: Wed, then Thu, Tue. Worst day: Sat. Sun is a little better but still trails weekdays. - Facebook is the opposite of Instagram: mornings (06:00-11:00) peak on most weekdays, and afternoons (12:00-17:00) are lowest. - Monday is the exception: best at 21:00, 22:00, 19:00. Tue: 08:00, 19:00, 07:00. Wed: 08:00, 09:00, 18:00.

Limits of these numbers (our read): - The figures are engagement per follower, not reach, and come from Buffer's own customers. - They are medians across niches. Buffer itself states retail/media skew to mornings and finance/hospitality to afternoons. - They give you a starting slot. They don't predict how one account performs. - Timing matters through early momentum: Buffer: "recency still dictates your initial reach". It is not the main ranking signal; content and originality come first (see instagram-reach-collapse-account-status-originality-triage.md and facebook-page-reach-limited-originality-triage.md). - Hootsuite publishes different "best times" (e.g. IG 15:00-21:00 Mon). Sources disagree in detail. The two things they agree on are midweek beating Fri/Sat, and Instagram evenings beating Instagram mornings.

How to do it

  1. Start from the prior, per channel. Instagram goes in the evening (Thu 09:00 is the one morning exception). Facebook goes in the morning (Monday evening is the exception). Never post the same minute to both "because it is convenient": their peaks are opposite.
  2. Use the audience's clock, not the server's. Buffer's times are local. For a UK-first audience, read them as Europe/London. Check the country split in Insights. If a second country is >25% of followers, test one slot that suits both.
  3. Keep it to one variable. Same format, similar post quality, alternate only the slot. Run two slots for 4 weeks: at least 8 posts per slot, or the difference is noise.
  4. Measure reach from non-followers and saves/shares at 48 h. Likes alone mostly measure followers who were already online.
  5. Decide on medians. A slot wins if its median 48 h reach is at least 1.5x the other's across 8+ posts. Otherwise keep the prior.
  6. Don't do these: bursts of 3-4 posts within 10 minutes (they compete with each other); Friday or Saturday mornings; deleting and reposting a post to "retime" it.

Tool

tools/post_time_slots.py (own stdlib script, ~4 KB, no install). Give it a CSV with columns timestamp,channel,reach (UTC ISO times; optional format). - It buckets each post by Europe/London weekday x hour and prints median reach per bucket with n. - It flags buckets with n<8 as "too few". - It marks where each post sat against the Buffer prior: IG evening/Thu-am, FB morning/Mon-pm. - Run: python3 tools/post_time_slots.py posts.csv
