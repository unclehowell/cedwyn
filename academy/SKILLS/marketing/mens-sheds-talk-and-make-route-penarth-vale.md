# Men's Sheds talk-and-make route for a local-history project (Penarth / Vale)

Harvested 2026-10-31 ~23:45Z. Topic: Men's Sheds as an older-men audience for a local-history talk, a shared make-project and word-of-mouth. Before this, the index had no Men's Sheds file. U3A, WI and Rotary are covered elsewhere (u3a-speaker-circuit-local-history.md, rotary-club-talks-civic-audience.md).

## What the sources show (read unless marked)
- **Scale.** UK Men's Sheds Association (UKMSA) CEO, reported by Prof. Barry Golding's international round-up (barrygoanna.com, read): "over 1,180" sheds across the UK, up from 127 in 2015 and 810 in 2021. "Virtually every Shed we visit being at capacity." "'Men's Sheds Cymru' is now part of the UKMSA framework." UK ShedFest had "over 500 visitors". About 3,000 sheds worldwide in 12 countries (Australian Men's Shed Association, read).
- **Why sheds work.** UKMSA homepage (read): 88% of regular attendees feel connected with the wider community (UKMSA Health and Wellbeing Survey 2023; method not checked). The movement's motto is "Shoulder to Shoulder": men talk side by side, over a task, not face to face (Wikipedia, citing the 2008 AMSA conference). So an activity beats a lecture.
- **Wales.** Men's Sheds Cymru has development officers. It held the "EiShedfod" at Builth Wells in May 2019 and a Trebuchet Challenge (UKMSA "2020 with Men's Sheds Cymru", read). Swansea Council put more than £100k into its network, where 22 sheds got support (Facebook snippet, not checked).
- **On our doorstep.** The Penarth Times reported "Penarth's Men's Shed", a new initiative. Its organisers are "committed to integrating with other local communities and groups" (read; article date not shown on the page). A brand-new shed is looking for activities and partners, so a good offer has a better chance of a yes now than once it is established.
- **History already happens in sheds.** Snippets, not checked: Nettlestone and Seaview Men's Shed (Isle of Wight) restored an old bench as a memorial for 27 villagers at the request of their Community Partnership. Lancing and Sompting shed posts about local-history talks. A Facebook post states "Not an old photo but a little bit of local history" alongside a talk by the leader of Men's Sheds Cymru. Shed visits to sites ("we went to the Priory ruins") show up in shed Facebook groups.
- **Governance.** UKMSA is a CIO (charity 1162409). Each shed is independent, and its committee or coordinator decides on visitors. UKMSA "has never supported" sheds run by outside organisations for people, so come as a guest, never as an organiser.

## How to do it (practitioner pattern)
1. **Find the shed.** Use UKMSA's "Find a local Shed" map (menssheds.org.uk) and the Men's Sheds Cymru Facebook page. Note opening days. Most sheds are weekday daytime, for example "Mon-Weds 10am-4pm".
2. **Offer a make-and-talk, not a lecture.** 20 minutes of story with large prints passed round by hand, then a task the men can do with their hands. For example: build a scale model of the lost farmhouse from the plans; make a wooden finger-post or a small interpretation board frame for a heritage walk stop; mount and frame period map prints. The task is the reason to come back.
3. **Bring objects, not slides.** A1 prints of the tithe map and OS sheets, photocopies of sale particulars. Plenty of men in sheds are ex-trades or ex-engineering, and they read plans and maps easily.
4. **Ask, don't sell.** Shed rules are like Rotary's: no selling. Leave one A5 card with the free-read link, plus a "do you remember...?" prompt that asks for memories and photos of the area.
5. **Make the shed the hero afterwards.** Post a photo of the finished object with the shed credited, but only with their OK and no faces unless they agree. A shed's own Facebook page has local reach that we lack.
6. **Follow the money they already have.** Sheds win small community grants. A joint heritage make-project gives them a story for their next application, and costs us nothing.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- **Target.** Penarth Men's Shed first (new, wants partners, a mile or two from Llandough). Then sheds in Barry, Dinas Powys and Cardiff from the UKMSA map, all addresses still to be checked.
- **The offer (draft only; the project lead's GO before any approach).** "The farm that vanished: a 20-minute talk with big maps, then help us build a 1:50 model of Tŷ Mawr (demolished 1988) from the surviving plans." Make the model from the evidence catalogue's measured drawings. Each finished model can also be filmed for the /movie/ project and the Academy.
- **Second make.** Wooden waymark posts for the Llandough heritage walk stops. This depends on permissions and landowners and is noted as a gate, not a plan.
- **Memory harvest.** One card asking "Did you work at Llandough Hospital, Penarth Docks or the farms by the Ely? We'd love your stories." Answers go to the oral-history and reminiscence routes. Store nothing personal without consent.
- **Channel rules.** First contact is a HubSpot draft task in the hive format: "[OTHER AGENT'S TURN v0.1.0] Penarth Men's Shed: talk-and-make offer". Never send. A shed is a community group, so use the published contact only and follow the charity-register-society-sourcing-pecr-class.md rule if it is unincorporated (no cold email; post or opt-in).
- **Public text.** No family names and no case accusations: the shed sees heritage, not the dispute. Banned words apply. Talk about the farm as "the former manorial house / Great House Farm".
- **Measure.** Talks held, makes started, memory cards returned, and free-read signups with ?utm_source=mensshed&utm_campaign=penarth-shed.
