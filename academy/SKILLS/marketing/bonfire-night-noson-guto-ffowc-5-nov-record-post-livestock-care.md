## What the sources show
- **The record behind the night (TNA teaching resource, read 29 Oct):** Fawkes was found with 36 barrels of gunpowder around midnight on Mon 4 Nov 1605. Parliament then passed the Observance of the 5th of November Act (the "Thanksgiving Act"), which ordered prayers and church bells. The first Gunpowder Treason Day was Nov 1606. Archives lead with **the document, not the fireworks**. TNA posts the Monteagle Letter with a transcript, and its Facebook "Remember, remember… on this day in 1605" post is built around the 36 barrels.
- **The Welsh names are real and in use:** *Noson Guto Ffowc* (Wiktionary), *Noson tân gwyllt* (the caption NLW gave its own photo; also used by the Welsh shop Babipur on its 5 Nov posts). A Wrexham guide gives *Guto Ffowc*, *roced* (rocket) and *draenog* (hedgehog). Bilingual naming is free reach with Welsh-speaking accounts.
- **A free, licensed period image exists:** Wikimedia Commons "Bonfire night (1555977).jpg", Geoff Charles, **5 Nov 1983**, National Library of Wales collection, **CC BY-SA 4.0** (licence read on the file page, 29 Oct). It can be used with a credit line ("Geoff Charles / National Library of Wales, CC BY-SA 4.0"), and any crop or edit must be shared under the same licence. Do not write to NLW about it (they refuse AI agents); the open licence is all we need.
- **The farm angle has numbers:** RSPCA Assured press release (29 Oct 2025, read) cites RSPCA polling: about **6.16 million households (22%) in England and Wales** planned to set off fireworks. It warns that cattle and sheep panic, hit fences or bolt onto roads, and that poultry are affected too. Its advice is to tell neighbours where livestock are kept and to go to organised displays. The poll is the charity's own figure; we haven't checked the method.
