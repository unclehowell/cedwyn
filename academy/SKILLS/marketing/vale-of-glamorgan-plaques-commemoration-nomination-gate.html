Vale of Glamorgan plaques: what exists (GATE note)

Harvested 29 Oct 2026. This closes the "Vale scheme not checked" gap left in blue-plaque-schemes-penarth-civic-society-further-reading-gate.md. Result: the Vale has no council heritage blue-plaque scheme. Three adjacent routes exist, and none is a sales channel for the farm.

Facts (council and tourism pages, read 29 Oct 2026)

  1. Recognition and Commemoration review (valeofglamorgan.gov.uk). Started after 2020 under the Strategic Equality Plan. It covers statues, monuments, buildings and street names, and future celebration of "the diverse communities within the Vale". - There are two online forms: Submit a Commemoration for Review (location + reason) and Suggest a Person/Event to be Recognised or Commemorated (name + reason). - No criteria, timetable, panel or count of awards is published on the page. Not checked further.
  2. Memorial plaques on benches (Dedications and Memorials page). These are bereavement dedications, not heritage plaques. - Price: £322.50 per plaque on a shared bench, or £800 for an exclusive bench. A new bench costs from £1,475. - Not allowed at Porthkerry or Cosmeston Country Parks or on Penarth Pier, which have their own routes. - The council may alter or refuse inscriptions, and the plaque becomes council property.
  3. Blue Plaque Town Trails in Llantwit Major and Cowbridge, listed by Visit the Vale (visitthevale.com). These are town-led trails. Who runs them and how to nominate has not been checked: the page is behind a cookie wall and gave no detail from the box. - Penarth: Penarth Civic Society Heritage Trail (see the Penarth gate skill). Llandough sits outside every town trail.

Judgement

  • A plaque for the farm, the Williams family or the case = NO. It is private, contested, a non-town site with no family holdings, and the bench plaques are memorial and paid.
  • The Suggest a Person/Event form is open to anyone. A defensible use is a public, long-dead figure or documented event that touches Llandough/Penarth and has its own sourced Cedwyn page. That lets the council's research find the Cedwyn page as further reading.
  • Candidates to test against sources: the May 1897 Lavernock–Flat Holm wireless trials. These belong to Lavernock, not Llandough, unless the parcel-B worker finds a primary source for lodging at Great House.
  • Never put a hypothesis on a council form.
  • Any nomination is an outward step in the project lead's name or Tab's mailbox = PROPOSAL, the project lead's GO, and a HubSpot draft first.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Don't spend: bench plaques are a zero-spend breach and not heritage.
  • Add the two forms and the Llantwit/Cowbridge trails to the change-watch list as "absence watch" pages. If the council publishes a list of commemorations, a Llandough entry would show there.
  • Content angle at no risk: a short Cedwyn page or post, "Where are the Vale's plaques?", listing the Llantwit Major and Cowbridge trails and Penarth's trail, with Llandough noted as having none. This is local-search content that links to /ebooks/ internally only.
  • Kill rule: if no sourced public figure or event with a Llandough link exists by the next growth report, drop the nomination idea and keep the watch only.
