# Google Ad Grants ($10k/month of free search ads): GATE = NO for a commercial shop

Checked 30 Oct 2026 ('ad grants' appeared only in one harvest note before, never as a skill; related: givingtuesday-1-dec-non-charity-gate-commercial-participator-s60.md).

## What it is
- Eligible nonprofits get **up to US$10,000 a month** in Google Search ads (google.com, Google Ads Help 57772; search previews).
- **Compliance rules** (Google Ad Grants Help 117827, read):
  - The account must keep a **5% click-through rate each month**, measured across the account, not per keyword. Two months in a row below 5% means temporary deactivation.
  - Each campaign must use Maximise conversions, Maximise conversion value, target CPA or target ROAS bidding. Maximise clicks is allowed only at the start.
  - "Meaningful" conversion tracking is required. Google lists purchases, ticket sales, membership registrations and email sign-ups as examples.
  - Location targeting must be specific.
- **Practitioner guidance** (Big Sea 2026 guide, read):
  - The **website can't contain for-profit sales**, and ads can't land on pages that promote them.
  - Avoid single-word and generic keywords.
  - The most common reasons for suspension: CTR below 5%, missing sitelinks or wrong location targeting, broken conversion tracking, banned bidding strategies, and not answering the annual programme survey.
- **Case with numbers** (Digital Tabby, museum nonprofit, read, agency self-report):
  - In 2023 the account spent only $1,051 and recorded 0 conversions.
  - After a rebuild it used **$129K of grant in 12 months** and produced **$5,746 in ticket revenue**, reaching the maximum daily spend 2 weeks after launch.
  - Lesson: the grant buys plenty of clicks but little revenue (about 4.5 cents of revenue per grant dollar), so it suits awareness and sign-ups more than sales.
- **UK eligibility:** organisations must be validated as charities through Google for Nonprofits. Search previews (Elite Digital, 'Google Grants for UK Charities') say "valid charity status / registered charity". I did not read Google's UK eligibility page in full, so treat that as not checked.

## Verdict for Cedwyn: NO (gate)
- cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate is a **commercial shop**: e-books at £4.99, a £19.96 bundle and a £49.99/mo academy. It is not a registered charity. Two hard gates fail: charity status, and no for-profit sales on the site.
- Do **not** apply, and do not create a Google Ads account, because zero spend applies and a paid account would be a new channel.
- **Reopens only if** a separate registered charity or CIO is ever set up for the heritage walk or museum objectives (Wales tour, Argentina museum in the programme office). Its own non-commercial site would need to send donations, volunteer and walk sign-ups to its own pages, never to /ebooks/ or /learn/. That decision is the project lead's: log it as a PROPOSAL in the project workspace, not an action.

## If it ever reopens: the 5-step setup that keeps an account alive
1. Track one real conversion first, such as a walk booking or newsletter sign-up form submit in GA4. Don't count pageviews.
2. Build 3-5 tightly themed ad groups around long-tail intent, for example "Llandough history walk", "Welsh farm history Penarth", "Welsh Patagonia museum". Use no single-word keywords.
3. Use Maximise clicks for about 2 weeks to collect data, then switch to Maximise conversions.
4. Add at least 2 sitelinks and set exact locations (Cardiff/Vale for the walk; UK and Chubut for heritage).
5. Check CTR weekly. Pause any keyword under 5% CTR after about 100 impressions so the account average stays above the line. Answer the annual programme survey.
