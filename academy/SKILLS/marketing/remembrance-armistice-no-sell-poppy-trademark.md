# Remembrance and Armistice (Sun 8 Nov / Wed 11 Nov 2026): commemorate, don't sell. Poppy trade-mark rules

Harvested 2026-10-28's skills harvest. The index had nothing on this before ("remembrance" and "armistice": 0 hits).

## What the sources show
- **Dates.** The National Service of Remembrance is Sunday 8 November 2026, 11:00, at the Cenotaph. GOV.UK (DCMS/MoD guidance, published 8 Oct 2026) was read. Armistice Day is always 11 November, which this year is a Wednesday. Remembrance Sunday 2027 is 14 Nov (Wikipedia date table).
- **"Poppy" is a registered trade mark.** The Royal British Legion's own page footer (read) states "RBL, Poppy, Poppy Appeal, Poppy Shop, are all registered trade marks of the Royal British Legion". Its brand guidelines (2021 PDF, snippet only, not read) say do not "recreate or adapt the master artwork, or separate the poppy from our logo, without permission".
- **Only partners sell poppy goods.** IPO/GOV.UK press release (read): "Only corporate partners are authorised by the Royal British Legion to sell poppy merchandise". The RBL registered its rights to the two-petal poppy, and Poppy Scotland's four-petal poppy is covered in Scotland. So a poppy beside a "buy" button is a legal risk as well as a tone risk.
- **What backlash looks like.** A small UK business publicly apologised for a poppy-mask product ad ("We made a mistake, an error in our advertising and we're sorry"; Facebook post, snippet only). Most of the risk is in tone, not law.
- **What heritage practitioners actually do: tell the stories behind the names.** Frances Moreton of the War Memorials Trust, writing in The Gazette (read): "The names on a war memorial may not be found anywhere else". About two-thirds of the UK's estimated 100,000 war memorials were made after WWI. After WWII, communities often added names to the old memorial or chose "utilitarian forms" such as halls. BALH publishes WWI local-history research guides (Kate Tiller, "Remembrance and Community: War Memorials and Local History", 2013).
- **IWM War Memorials Register (read).** Anyone can send details of a local memorial or upload a photograph. Uploads are licensed for IWM's **non-commercial** reuse. Remote "digital volunteer" roles exist, about 3 h a week (2026 role PDF, snippet).
- **Local hook (Wikipedia, read; check against a primary source before any public use).** The Llandough and Leckwith War Memorial Institute was opened in 1924 and built with donations from families in both villages. The hall was replaced about 50 years later. In WWII the building was used by the RAF, and an anti-aircraft battery and barrage-balloon unit were stationed in the village.
- **Numbers:** I found **no** sales or engagement figures for heritage Remembrance posts. Treat this as reputation and community work, not a sales lever.

## How to do it (rules)
1. **No commercial use of the poppy.** No poppy emoji or image on any post, email or page that carries a price, a buy link or a shop CTA. Never adapt the RBL poppy artwork. If an image is needed, use a real archive photo (a memorial, the hall, a period document) with its source line.
2. **The Remembrance post carries no CTA.** On 8 Nov and 11 Nov: no buy link, no discount, no "free book" offer, no countdown. Link to an internal evidence or research page only, if at all.
3. **Use the 2-minute silence.** Schedule nothing at 11:00–11:05 on 8 Nov or 11 Nov, and pause every automated social post on 11 Nov from 10:30 to 11:30.
4. **Content that works: one name or one place, sourced.** A local memorial or hall, with what the records show (date, who paid, what it replaced). Name individuals only when they are on a public memorial or in CWGC records, never living family. Where something is not checked, write "not checked".
5. **Give back to the record.** If we hold a photo of a local memorial the Register lacks, offer it to IWM's free upload form. This is a proposal (it is outward, in the company's name), and note IWM's non-commercial licence.
6. **Shop emails.** Skip the newsletter that week, or send a plain note with no offer. Data Axle/Yes Lifecycle (2018, read) found holiday-themed emails convert better than ordinary ones. That covers shopping holidays only; Remembrance is not one, so do not apply it.
