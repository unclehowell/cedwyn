Then-and-now rephotography posts (same spot, old photo vs today) for heritage reach

Harvested 2026-10-31 ~23:45Z. Topic: the "then and now" format (also called rephotography, mirror images or before/after sliders) as a repeatable social and on-site content format. Before this, the index had nothing for "then and now", "rephotography" or "Dear Photograph". Historypin, Google Images and Geograph are covered elsewhere and only overlap.

What the sources show (read unless marked)

  • Dear Photograph (Taylor Jones, 2011). Jones held an old family photo up in the exact spot it was taken, photographed it and posted it with six others on a blog. "Within days... gone viral." Eight months later: 10m hits, 20,000 hits a day, named one of Time's 10 best sites, a HarperCollins book deal, and he left his job (Guardian, 4 Jan 2012, read). A year in: about 200 reader-submitted photos in the same format, 23,000 followers, around 20 new submissions a day (Photoworks, read). The lesson: a format simple enough for readers to copy turns the audience into contributors.
  • Interactive toggle time on page. At the Guardian, the American Civil War "Then and Now" piece used a toggle between old and new photos. Readers "stayed on the site for an average of three minutes, a noteworthy amount of time for an interactive". That is from executive editor Aron Pilhofer, as reported in MIT Open Documentary Lab's Guardian case study (read; secondhand, not checked against Ophan data).
  • Welsh precedent. WalesOnline's Welsh Affairs Editor ran "The mirror images that show how Cardiff has changed over the last 120 years" (17 Nov 2019, read): archive 1900 shots paired with "those exact same spots... today". WalesOnline's Facebook keeps reposting the "50 years ago... feels like another world" style (snippet). So the format is proven with a Cardiff audience. We have no engagement numbers for it.
  • Groups exist and police selling. The Facebook group "CARDIFF NOW & THEN" (old and new Cardiff memories) states "PLEASE NO SALES ADVERTS" (snippet). Contribute photos; don't promote.
  • Teaching method. Berson & Berson, "A Slippage of Time: Using Rephotography to Promote Community-Based Historical Inquiry", Social Education 80(2) 2016 (read): rephotography works as place-based inquiry because the viewer has to explain the change.
  • Tools. GhostViewer (UK app, Feb 2026 post, read) aligns old and new photos for heritage and conservation groups. It pitches the format as "a direct visual comparison that anyone can understand in seconds", useful for documenting what you are trying to protect. It is a vendor page. A free alternative is a plain HTML/CSS before/after slider, under 2 KB, with no service needed.

How to do it

  1. Pick the photo with a fixed anchor: a church tower, a bridge, a road junction, a hill line. Without an anchor the viewer can't "lock" the match.
  2. Stand where the photographer stood. Match the lens (old cameras are often around 50 mm equivalent), height and season. Shoot a burst and choose the best alignment. Same-time-of-day light helps.
  3. Choose the format by channel. Instagram/Facebook: 2-slide carousel (then, now), or a 4-6 s crossfade Reel. Website: a drag slider with the date on each side. Facebook groups: a single side-by-side image with a question.
  4. Caption with a question, not a claim. "Who remembers the farm track here?" draws memories in the comments, and those memories are the content for the next post.
  5. Credit and licence. Source line on the old image: archive, reference, date. Use only public-domain, licensed or own-family images cleared by the owner.
  6. Make it a series with a fixed slot, for example "Then & Now Thursday". A serial format builds the habit.
  7. Invite copies. The Dear Photograph lesson: ask readers to send their own same-spot pairs of the area, credited and with permission.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Signature absence shots. Tŷ Mawr was demolished in 1988, so "now" is the empty site or the road over it. The absence is the story. Pair OS/tithe map overlays and any public-archive photos of the farm with a same-spot photo today, with grid ref ST 167 733 in the caption. Use evidence-catalogue images only: source line plus EV-ID, linking to the evidence page and /ebooks/.
  • Where the slider lives. A then-and-now strip on the parcel modal of each webapp ("NN of 59"). Each parcel gets its own pair, which also feeds the parcel's job of gathering everything known about it. The slider is pure CSS/JS, no outbound links, a dark background, and works at 390 px.
  • Channels. A weekly carousel on Instagram @cedwynestate and the Great House Farm II Facebook page through the existing auto-social pipeline. Groups such as Cardiff Now & Then get a photo and a memory question only, with no links or sales unless the group rules allow them. Draft the posts; the project lead's GO is needed for any group post.
  • Film tie-in. Each pair becomes a 5 s crossfade insert for YouTube Shorts, with the documentary line linking /movie/.
  • Field work. "Now" photos need someone on site. Log a task for the project lead or a volunteer with the exact standpoint (lat/long and bearing). We never fake a "now" with generated imagery: the format's power is that it is real.
  • Measure. Saves and shares per post compared with the account median, comments with memories, slider interactions (an event in analytics), and free-read signups with ?utm_campaign=then-now.
