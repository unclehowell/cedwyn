Tourist leaflet racks in Wales for the guided walk: local-only distribution, bilingual layout, coded tracking

Harvested 28 Oct 2026 (skills harvest). Index had 0 hits for "leaflet", "tourist information", "rack". Complements wales-travel-trade-gto-coach-dmc-net-rates.md (trade buyers) and qr-codes-print-placement-own-redirects-honest-counts.md (QR honesty).

Evidence

  • Visit Wales "Visitor Information Needs" (Welsh Government research, 2013, 39 pp, read). Offline sources matter most during the trip: leaflets and word of mouth from locals are the main influences on where visitors go and what they do. "If a visitor to an attraction found out about it from a leaflet, it is very likely they picked up that leaflet not very far away"; pre-trip discovery comes from websites. "Leaflet distribution is much more cost-effective when focussed locally." Visitors pick up leaflets during one trip to plan the next. About a third (32%) of offline users used a national or local-authority brochure pre-trip. Layout asks: enough snippets to entice, no more; English and Welsh on opposite sides, not jumbled; multi-attraction leaflets preferred to single-attraction. Suggestions of dropping print were "met with denial". QR codes were called "a non-starter" (2013; see our QR skill for 2020s numbers, still low).
  • North Wales Tourism (live page, read). Distribution to Tourist Information Centres and points is included in its membership fee for attractions/events; 6.5 million pieces a year to 2,500+ outlets in North/Mid Wales; South Wales covered via partner distribution companies.
  • Pear Communications (Cardiff/Carmarthen), via a Visit Wales member-benefits page last updated 3 Sep 2018. Member rate GBP 21.50 per 1,000 leaflets (list GBP 34.50); posters GBP 2-4 per site (min 5 sites, 4 weeks); free display stands serviced for outlets; clients include Visit Wales and Cadw. Prices are 2018, not checked current.
  • Rezgo (tour-booking vendor, read). Of operators the author spoke to over the years, none tracked rack-card returns. Fix: a discount code printed only on the rack card (e.g. "5OFFRACK"), honoured online and by phone; on cards already printed, add "Brochure/leaflet" to the "how did you hear" question.
  • Rejected: door-to-door leaflet drops (Cardiff from GBP 59 per 1,000, dor2dor) reach residents, not visitors; wrong audience for a walk.

The how-to

  1. Gate: product bookable (date, price, booking page) and insured. No leaflet before that.
  2. Design (DL, 99x210 mm): Welsh side and English side, never mixed; one image, 3 snippets (what, where it starts, how long), price, dates, booking URL in plain text (short, typed easily), a code only this leaflet carries; QR optional, to our own redirect.
  3. Print small: 250-500 copies first run.
  4. Distribute locally only: places visitors already stand within ~10 miles: nearest TIC or information point, B&Bs/holiday lets/campsites, cafes, pubs, the start point; ask each owner first, self-deliver (free), note outlet + count.
  5. Shared beats single: offer a nearby attraction a swap (their leaflet at ours, ours at theirs) or a joint "things to do near X" sheet; visitors prefer multi-attraction.
  6. Track: per-outlet code variant if more than 3 outlets (e.g. TOUR-CAFE, TOUR-BNB); booking form "how did you hear" with "leaflet"; weekly: leaflets left per outlet (restock rate = interest proxy), coded bookings.
  7. Paid distributor only after proof: consider Pear-type networks only when self-delivered leaflets produce bookings; get a current quote first.
  8. Kill rule: under 2 coded bookings per 500 leaflets over a season -> stop print, keep the PDF for trade packs.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • For the Llandough guided walk only, after the trial walk and insurance are settled (same gate as the travel-trade skill). PROPOSAL appended to the queue after framing: printing is spend, so it needs the project lead's GO; distribution is self-delivered, zero spend.
  • Outlets and partner attractions are businesses: approach by HubSpot draft or in person by the project lead; never private individuals.
  • Leaflet text follows the editorial rules: never names the project lead, no banned words, no family addresses or holdings; route shown only via public viewpoints, no promise of private-land access.
  • A single line on the back: "Read the history: cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate/ebooks" (typed URL, UTM via our redirect src=leaflet-) so the leaflet also feeds e-book sales.
  • Log outlets and counts in the project workspace; report coded bookings in the Sunday growth report.
