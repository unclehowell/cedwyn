Selling a small heritage walk through Airbnb Experiences, Viator and GetYourGuide (and when not to)

Harvested 27 Oct 2026 (skills harvest run). Purpose: the programme-office objective "Launch the Llandough guided tour (Wales)" plans a free booking path first and listings later. This file has the 2026 marketplace terms and operator testimony to decide which marketplace, when, and on what terms. It updates the fee rows marked "not checked" in the project workspace (lines ~109–112).

2. What operators report (testimony, PhocusWire, 21 Oct 2026)

  • Andrew Lamb, Wales Outdoors, a Welsh operator:
  • GetYourGuide sales dipped in summer after a strong April–May, then "September was our best month we've ever had."
  • He refused Viator's offer to raise his commission from 25% to 30% for extra visibility, because he had no evidence it would pay: "I'm not going to give them 30% when I can get away with 25."
  • Peter Fischer Flórez, a Málaga operator: Viator bookings fell almost 35% year on year while total sales rose about 20%, with "a lot more direct bookings." He had invested in SEO and generative-engine (AI answer) optimisation and saw more enquiries over WhatsApp.
  • Mitch Bach (TripSchool/Tourpreneur): small operators see sudden visibility swings. He calls the paid-visibility programme "a disaster" from what he has heard, but the article itself flags that as anecdotal.
  • Viator's spokesperson: organic ranking is "not based on revenue"; what helps is accurate descriptions, strong photos and positive reviews.
  • GetYourGuide's VP of supply: about half of its customers now start trip research in a chatbot.
  • Phocuswright: 35–40% of UK travellers use AI for travel planning.

Lesson: marketplaces are rented reach that can swing 35% with no change on your side. The durable asset is a page that search and AI answers can find, plus your own list of guests.

3. Playbook: from pilot walk to listings

  1. Pilot direct first. Run free or donation walks booked through the Cedwyn page or a free Eventbrite ticket. Collect each walker's email (with consent) and ask them for a short written review. You need ~5–10 real reviews before any marketplace listing has a chance.
  2. Fix the listing basics before applying anywhere: - one-line promise, 90-minute duration and meeting point; - 6–10 real photos of the route (no people without consent); - accessibility notes (terrain, stiles); - cancellation policy; - liability insurance in place, since Airbnb requires it and Viator/GYG expect it.
  3. Then one marketplace, not three. - Airbnb Experiences is the first pick: published flat 20%, human review, fast payout, and no paid-visibility upsell. - Viator only if Tripadvisor exposure is worth the $29 fee and 25%. - Never opt into Accelerate or higher-commission visibility without a measured test.
  4. Price for the commission and keep parity. List the same customer price everywhere and absorb the 20–25% in the margin. A £20 walk nets £16 on Airbnb and £15 on Viator. Undercutting the marketplaces on your own page can break their parity terms; give direct bookers value (free e-book, audio guide link) rather than a lower price.
  5. Move marketplace guests into your own channels. After the walk, follow up within the platform's rules: a thank-you that points to the free e-book and the audio stops on the Cedwyn page. Don't email marketplace guests outside the platform unless they gave you their address themselves.
  6. Watch the share. Track bookings by channel monthly. If one marketplace is more than ~50% of bookings, put effort back into direct search/AI visibility and the list (the Málaga pattern).

  • Stage now (Oct 2026): the pilot Llandough walking loop (stops 1–3, 8, 9) has no guide or insurance decided. Don't list on any marketplace yet; the programme office keeps "who guides the pilot and the insurance cover" as an owner decision.
