Freedom of Information Act 2000 Request

Sir/Madam,

I am making this request under the Freedom of Information Act 2000.

I seek all recorded information held concerning furniture, furnishings, antiques, fixtures, heirlooms and other movable property originating from Great House Farm (Ty Mawr), Llandough, including any and all items we received from Bute Estate, Cardiff Castle, Cardiff Council. Weather it be museums, archives or any other public body. I know museums have artifacts from our land in Llandough.

Specifically, I request copies of all:

1. Inventories, catalogues and asset registers.
2. Acquisition, disposal, loan and transfer records.
3. Provenance files and ownership records.
4. Photographs of items identified as originating from Great House Farm.
5. Correspondence concerning the identification, recovery, ownership or location of such items.
6. Internal reports, legal advice and memoranda relating to Great House Farm furniture or household effects.
7. Correspondence mentioning the Buckler or Williams families in connection with these items.
8. Records concerning any efforts by Cardiff Council or Cardiff Castle to trace, recover or secure items believed to have originated from Great House Farm.
9. Correspondence with the Bute Estate, museums, the Department for Culture, Media and Sport, the Reviewing Committee on the Export of Works of Art and Objects of Cultural Interest (RCEWA), Arts Council England or any other public authority regarding objects from Great House Farm.

For context, I am allegeing that my family was unlawfully dispossessed of Great House Farm through fraud, and that furniture and other historic possessions belonging to the family were consequently dispersed. It’s possible the bailiffs from BP were also SAS soldiers. We are still gathering actionable intelligence.

I appreciate that the Freedom of Information Act does not require the authority to determine the merits of that allegation. However, it is directly relevant to understanding why I seek the recorded information.

I am currently developing a full-scale replica of Great House Farm in Patagonia, Argentina as a museum dedicated to preserving the history of the house, its occupants and its contents. Should records identify surviving items from the farm, whether held by Cardiff Council, Cardiff Castle, museums or other public institutions, I intend to pursue their lawful return or transfer so they may ultimately be reunited with the reconstructed Great House Farm museum. This statement is provided solely as context for my request and does not seek a determination under the Freedom of Information Act.

If any information is withheld, please identify each exemption relied upon and explain why it applies, including the outcome of any public interest test where required.

If another public authority is more likely to hold the requested information, I would be grateful if you would transfer the request or advise me accordingly.

I look forward to your response within the statutory time limit.

Yours faithfully,

Sion Buckler (Ret’d Sergeant)

FOI Team,

Thank you for your detailed response and for undertaking the additional enquiries.

I appreciate the further information you have provided concerning the searches undertaken. However, I would like to request an internal review under the Freedom of Information Act 2000.

My concern is not simply that no objects have been identified. It is whether the searches undertaken have fully addressed the scope of my original request, particularly the documentary evidence concerning the provenance, acquisition, transfer, ownership, identification and dispersal of material originating from Great House Farm (Ty Mawr).

I would be grateful if the internal review could consider the following points.

1. Information identified concerning Great House Farm

Your response states that the additional enquiries identified “some information relating generally to Great House Farm and the surrounding area”.

Please could you identify what categories of information this refers to and, where it is not exempt, provide the relevant recorded information or explain where it can be accessed?

This appears potentially relevant to the original request even if it does not itself identify furniture or household objects.

2. Scope of the archived electronic search

You state that the Information Technology team reviewed “archived electronic systems and records retained by the Museum”.

Please could the review clarify:

– which archived systems were searched;
– the approximate date ranges covered;
– whether historical email or correspondence archives were searched;
– whether legacy collections-management databases were searched;
– whether databases or systems preceding the current CMS were searched; and
– whether any archived systems were unavailable, incomplete or incapable of being searched.

3. Correspondence and administrative records

My original request expressly sought correspondence concerning the identification, recovery, ownership or location of objects, as well as correspondence with the Bute Estate, Cardiff Council, Cardiff Castle and other public authorities.

Your response refers generally to “associated collection documentation” and “administrative records”, but does not make clear whether historical correspondence files were specifically searched.

Please could the internal review establish whether searches were made of:

– curator correspondence;
– departmental correspondence files;
– donor correspondence;
– acquisition correspondence;
– disposal correspondence;
– loan correspondence;
– provenance correspondence;
– correspondence with Cardiff Council;
– correspondence with Cardiff Castle;
– correspondence with the Bute Estate;
– correspondence with other museums or public authorities; and
– any relevant institutional or external correspondence indexes.

4. Search terms

Thank you for identifying many of the search terms used.

Please could the internal review confirm whether the search also included combinations and variants involving: “Bute Estate”, “Bute”, “Cardiff Castle”, “Cardiff Council”, “Great House Farm”, “Ty Mawr”, “Great House”, “Llandough”, “Buckler”, “Williams”, “Mary Williams”, “Mary Doreen Williams”, “household effects”, “contents”, “furniture”, “furnishings”, “heirlooms”, “fixtures”, “estate”, “dispersal”, “recovery”, “provenance”, “ownership”, “transfer”, “demolition”.

I appreciate that historical records may not have been consistently indexed, which is why I would be grateful for confirmation of the methodology used.

5. Transfer, disposal and destruction

Your response states:

“We are also not aware of any relevant collection records relating to such material having been transferred to another institution or destroyed.”

Please could the internal review clarify whether this conclusion is based upon:

a. a search of transfer/accession-out records;
b. a review of retention and disposal records;
c. enquiries with relevant collections staff;
d. review of archival transfer documentation; or
e. institutional knowledge rather than a documentary search.

I am particularly interested in distinguishing between “no record exists” and “no record was identified in the systems searched”.

6. The original request concerning external institutions

The original request was deliberately framed broadly because the question concerns possible dispersal of the contents of a demolished historic property.

It expressly included records concerning Cardiff Council, Cardiff Castle, the Bute Estate, museums, Arts Council England, DCMS, RCEWA and other public authorities.

I would therefore be grateful if the review could confirm whether records of communications or transactions involving those bodies were searched within Amgueddfa Cymru’s records, rather than simply searching the Museum’s object catalogue for Great House Farm objects.

7. Advice and assistance

You have stated that Cardiff Council and Cardiff Castle “would appear to be the organisations most likely to hold records relating to furniture, furnishings or other movable property associated with Great House Farm”.

I appreciate that you cannot transfer another authority’s records to me. However, could you please provide any further information available to the Museum as to which particular department, collection, archive or record series at Cardiff Council or Cardiff Castle is most likely to contain such material?

That would materially assist me in directing subsequent FOI and archival enquiries.

I am grateful for the additional work already undertaken. I am requesting the internal review because I would like the record to be clear as to precisely what was searched, what information was identified, and what the negative result does and does not establish.

I am not asking Amgueddfa Cymru to determine any allegation concerning the historical ownership of Great House Farm. The purpose of the review is simply to ensure that the documentary search corresponds fully with the scope of the original FOI request.

Please treat this email as my formal request for an internal review of FOI 2026-020.

Regards,

Sion Buckler (Ret’d Sergeant)

Dear Freedom of Information Team,

I am writing to request information under the Freedom of Information Act 2000 and, where applicable, the Environmental Information Regulations 2004.

A published local history account states that, during works to replace the stone floor of the dining room at Great House Farm (also known as Ty Mawr), Llandough, Cardiff, the remains of a soldier and his horse were discovered beneath the floor.

The remains were subsequently re-buried in the churchyard, and the soldier’s visor and lance were given to the National Museum of Wales (Amgueddfa Cymru).

I therefore request the following recorded information held by Amgueddfa Cymru:

Any records relating to the acquisition, accession, donation, transfer or deposit of a medieval visor, lance, helmet, or other items of armour associated with Great House Farm / Ty Mawr / Llandough.

The accession number(s), date of acquisition or transfer, name of the donor or transferring body, and any provenance or discovery notes linked to these objects.

Any catalogue entries, object records, or internal correspondence concerning the above items.

Any records of associated human or animal remains from the same discovery, including notes on reburial.

Any related excavation, watching-brief, or discovery reports held by the Museum.

If any of the requested information is already publicly available (for example via the Museum’s online collections database), I would be grateful if you could simply provide the relevant accession numbers or catalogue links.

Please treat this as a request for recorded information only. I am not seeking advice or opinion.

I look forward to your response within the statutory timescale.

Yours faithfully,

Sion Buckler (Ret’d Sergeant)

26 August 2026, 06:24 UTC · Email — sent by the family · Re: FOI – 2026-024 – Great House Farm, Llandough — internal review request

From: Sion Buckler
To: Amgueddfa Cymru FOI Team
Date: 26 August 2026, 06:24 UTC

Dear FOI Team,

Thank you for your response.

I am requesting an internal review of the handling of my request. I do not consider the response sufficient because it appears that the searches were conducted too narrowly around the later description of a “medieval visor, lance, helmet or other item of armour”, rather than around the underlying historical discovery at Great House Farm itself.

The issue I am investigating is a reported nineteenth-century discovery at Great House Farm (Ty Mawr), Llandough. My understanding from contemporary or near-contemporary newspaper and historical accounts is that the discovery was described as involving the remains of a Roman soldier, with associated remains and objects. The dating and description of the individual objects as “medieval” may therefore itself be inaccurate, or may represent a later conflation or retelling of the original discovery.

Your response is particularly significant because it states:

“Our searches identified records relating to … other archaeological discoveries recorded at Great House Farm (Ty Mawr), Llandough. These records do not relate to, nor are they otherwise associated with, the discovery described in your request. We have therefore not considered them to fall within the scope of this request.”

I respectfully challenge that approach.

The central question is precisely whether the discovery described in later historical accounts can be identified, corroborated, corrected or explained through the Museum’s records concerning archaeological discoveries at Great House Farm. If the Museum has identified records relating to other discoveries at the same historically significant site, it is not sufficient simply to conclude internally that they are unrelated without disclosing what those discoveries are, when they were recorded, and the basis upon which that conclusion was reached.

I therefore request that the internal review reconsider the scope of my request and undertake searches using, at minimum, the following terms and concepts: Great House Farm; Ty Mawr; Llandough; Llandough, Cardiff; Roman soldier; Roman burial; Roman grave; Roman remains; human remains; skeleton; horse remains; equine remains; soldier and horse; cavalry burial; military burial; grave goods; Roman military equipment; visor; helmet; lance; spear; armour; archaeological discovery; nineteenth-century discovery; 1870 and adjacent years.

Please also search for relevant records under alternative historical place-name indexing, donor names, findspot descriptions, parish names and catalogue terminology where the precise modern name “Great House Farm” may not have been used.

Most importantly, I ask that you disclose sufficient information about the “other archaeological discoveries” which your searches identified at Great House Farm to enable me to understand and assess the Museum’s conclusion that they are unrelated. This should include, so far as held:

1. A description or title of each relevant discovery or record.
2. The approximate date of the discovery or record.
3. The nature of the material or remains involved.
4. Any accession number, catalogue number or archival reference.
5. The recorded provenance or findspot.
6. Any dating attributed to the discovery.
7. The basis upon which Amgueddfa Cymru concluded that each record was unrelated to the nineteenth-century discovery described in my request.

I am not asking the Museum to create new archaeological opinions or undertake speculative research. I am asking for recorded information which has already been identified during the searches and which directly concerns archaeological discoveries at the specific site named in my request.

If the Museum maintains that any of those records fall outside the original scope, then please treat this email additionally as a refined request for the recorded information identified above.

I would also ask the reviewer to consider whether the original response gave insufficient weight to the possibility that the historical account may have inaccurately dated or described the find. A search which excludes Roman, nineteenth-century, burial and equine terminology risks missing the very records capable of establishing what was actually discovered beneath Great House Farm.

Given that your searches have already identified records concerning archaeological discoveries at Great House Farm itself, I would be grateful for a full reconsideration and disclosure of the recorded information requested above.

Yours faithfully,

Sion Buckler (Ret’d Sergeant)

– the request was interpreted correctly;

– relevant information held by the Museum was identified and considered; and
– the response complied with the requirements of the Freedom of Information Act 2000.
