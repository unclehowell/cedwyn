B. What the letter actually states, and what it does not 
 
The operative paragraph is this: 
 
“We have carried out a search of our digital records and have determined that the Wales 
Office does not hold the information requested. Any paper files previously held by the former 
Welsh Office relating to this matter would have been transferred to the National Assembly for 
Wales in 1999, and subsequently to the Welsh Government in 2007.” 
 
That paragraph does not answer the request. The gaps are these. 
 
1. Digital records only. The letter states a search of digital records. It does not say that paper, 
microfilm, card indexes, transfer lists, accession registers, or destruction schedules were 
searched. A 1988 Welsh Office file is a paper file. A search confined to current digital 
holdings cannot find it, and cannot find the transfer or destruction docket that the fourth bullet 
asked for. 
 
2. “Would have been transferred” is not a search result. It is an assumption about devolution. 
It is not a statement that a transfer list was consulted, that a file for this house appears on 
that list, or that the file was received in Cardiff. “Would have been” covers three different 
outcomes: transferred, destroyed, or still here under another description. The letter does not 
distinguish them. 
 
3. The fourth bullet asked for the transfer, disposal and accession records themselves. 
Those records, if they exist, are Wales Office records of what left the building. They are not 
the assessment file. Pointing me to the Welsh Government does not answer a request for 
the docket, the list, the database entry or the disposal authority. Those may still be held here 
even if the file went to Cardiff. The letter does not say they were searched for. 
 

4 
 
4. The five bullets are not answered one by one. There is no finding on communications with 
Cadw; no finding on any briefing or appeal to the Secretary of State; no finding on 
communications with occupiers and owners before 5 December 1988; no finding on the 
evidence said to have supported a 1988 decision. A single sentence that digital records 
do not hold “the information requested” does not show that each limb was searched. 
 
5. The letter uses “the refusal decision of 5 December 1988” only because I used it. It does 
not say whether that decision is recognised, whether a file for it was identified, or whether 
the 
decision is one of the papers “which would have been transferred”. Silence on a dated 
decision is not a not-held finding. 
 
6. “You may therefore wish” is not section 16 advice. Section 16 requires advice and 
assistance so far as reasonable. A reasonable step, where the department asserts a 
transfer, is to give the receiving body's file reference, the date of transfer, and the series. 
None is given. If the intention was section 21 (information reasonably accessible elsewhere), 
that exemption is not named, and the conditions are not addressed. A suggestion that I write 
to [email redacted] is not a section 21 notice. 
 
7. “Does not hold” is stated as a conclusion. The search described cannot support it. A not-
held finding under section 1 requires a search capable of finding the information. A search of 
digital records, plus an assumption about 1999, is not that search. 
 
8. The request was answered from a Northern Ireland Office mailbox. The letter is in the 
name of the Wales Office. The review must be of the Wales Office search. Please confirm it 
will not be taken by the person who signed the 22 September letter.

The public index is https://greathousefarmwiki.wordpress.com/ 
 
The investigation distinguishes Parcel A (farmhouse, garden, buildings and yard — the 
family's maintained freehold interest out of the Bute estate) from Parcel B (eastern fields). 
Later papers use “whole farm”. BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2 decided

Our integration

The review upholds "not held" and confirms the search was of digital records only. A Welsh Office file from 1988 would have been paper, so a digital-only search could not have found it, or any record of its transfer or destruction. The search terms quoted do not include "Tŷ Mawr" or "Ty Mawr", the name the 1988 listing papers use.

Concern to test: the letter states pre-1999 paper files "would have been" transferred to the National Assembly (1999) and then the Welsh Government (2007). That is how devolution transfers normally ran, not the result of checking a transfer list. The Welsh Government's own answers (ATISN 27021, ATISN 26-0010) are where that trail continues; see GHF-E106-R56-A1 and GHF-E106-R59-A1.

What cuts against the case: the reply was on time, the review was done within 20 working days, and a department that inherited no Welsh Office paper may genuinely hold nothing. An empty search here is not, by itself, a sign that anything was withheld.

Next dated step: an ICO complaint is open to the Requester; nothing is due from the Wales Office.
