A. Paper title and the registration

T01 An unbroken paper title to the farmhouse plot (Parcel A) from the Bute estate, through the 25 Dec 1938 conveyance to Western Ground Rents (WGR), the 31 Dec 1969 conveyance to BP Pension Trust and the 23 May 1975 conveyance to BP Properties.

Record Missing BP must show

The law. Possession is presumed to sit with the paper owner (Powell v McFarlane (1977) 38 P & CR 452, 470, adopted in JA Pye (Oxford) Ltd v Graham [2002] UKHL 30, paras 31, 40). LRA 1925 s.5: first registration vests the fee simple subject to overriding interests (s.70(1)).

The record. The Court of Appeal (CA) took BP's 'paper title to the farm' as its starting point ([1987] EWCA Civ 2). The 1938 conveyance and plan are not held (BP's archive: nothing 'prior to 1955'); the 1969 conveyance is absent from HM Land Registry's 30 Jul 2026 first-registration schedule (EV-0019, EV-0043, EV-0052).

What would settle it. The 1938 conveyance plan and schedules; the 1969 conveyance with plan; the 1971 abstract of title lodged at first registration. Catalogue: MR-0005, MR-0015, MR-0018, MR-0041.

T02 The court was told the registration history accurately.

Not Met BP must show

The law. Takhar v Gracefield [2019] UKSC 13, para 56 (adopting RBS v Highland [2013] EWCA Civ 328, para 106): a statement relevant to the judgment, if knowingly untrue and material, can found an action to set the judgment aside.

The record. The CA recorded BP Properties as registered for 'the whole of the farm' in November 1982. HM Land Registry's file shows the farmhouse on a separate title, WA240304, applied for on 23 Feb 1983 by Form 1C (EV-0063, EV-0066). The recital and the register do not match.

What would settle it. The WA240304 and WA231076 application files. Weak on materiality: the CA's result turned on the 1974 letters, not on the registration date. Catalogue: MR-0020, MR-0043.

T03 First registration in 1982-83 was made subject to the rights of the person in actual occupation (Mary (Nancy) Williams was living in the house on 23 Feb 1983).

Record Missing BP must show

The law. LRA 1925 s.70(1)(f) (rights acquired or being acquired under the Limitation Acts) and s.70(1)(g) (rights of every person in actual occupation) bind a first registered proprietor; s.82 rectification; s.83 indemnity.

The record. She was in occupation; she died 26 Mar 1983, 31 days after the application. Whether the examiner raised a requisition or inspected is not held. No HMLR notice to the occupier has been found (EV-0060 is family testimony).

What would settle it. The examiner's requisitions and inspection note (Form A13 list) and any notice sent to the occupier. Whether the 1925 Rules required notice to an occupier on voluntary first registration: not checked. Catalogue: MR-0020, MR-0021.

T04 The registration was voluntary or compulsory, and the right forms were used.

Not Proven BP must show

The law. Compulsory registration on sale began in the Vale of Glamorgan on 1 Dec 1975 (SI 1975/160; EV-0265). The 23 May 1975 conveyance predates it, so the 1982-83 applications were voluntary (LRA 1925 s.4).

The record. Form 1C application with a solicitor's certificate of title (EV-0063). Contents not held.

What would settle it. The Form 1C and certificate of title on WA240304. Catalogue: MR-0020.

B. The 1955 → 1962 → 1974 chain

T05 The 1955 High Court possession order covered the land later fought over (Parcel A, the farmhouse and garden).

Disputed BP must show

The law. A possession order binds only the land it describes. Order 14 (summary judgment) in 1955.

The record. The CA records the 1955 Order 14 order 'in respect of the whole of the farm', enforced on 4 Jul 1955 on everything except the farmhouse and garden. Whether the order's own wording or plan reached the house, or only the Parcel B fields, is not checked. The order, writ and officer's return are not held.

What would settle it. The 1955 writ, order and officer's return of 4 Jul 1955, with any plan (Queen's Bench records, TNA J series): not checked. Catalogue: MR-0008.

T06 The 1962 Cardiff County Court order was a valid order for the farmhouse and garden.

Record Missing BP must show

The law. Limitation Act 1939 s.4(3) (12 years). The CA held an action begun inside 12 years stops extinction of title while it runs (St Marylebone v Fairweather [1963] AC 510, applied).

The record. The CA: a 'fresh action' for possession of the farmhouse and garden and mesne profits since 1955; order of 11 Dec 1962 (Judge Temple Morris QC). The order, plaint and plan are not held. In law the 1962 order stands on its own action, not on the wording of the 1955 order; but the right it enforced is said to have accrued when the tenancy ended on 2 Feb 1955, which assumes the house was inside that tenancy.

What would settle it. The 1962 plaint, particulars and plan; the county court registers (Glamorgan Archives ACA series, EV-0030). Catalogue: MR-0011, MR-0014.

T07 The 1962 order could still be enforced in September 1974.

Met BP must show

The law. LA 1939 s.2(4) (12 years on a judgment); the CA relied on Lougher v Donovan [1948] 2 All ER 11. The House of Lords later held that 'action' in LA 1980 s.24(1) 'does not include proceedings by way of execution' (Lowsley v Forbes [1998] UKHL 34).

The record. Leave to enforce was given on 19 Sep 1974, 11 years 9 months after the order (EV-0049). The CA held it was within time.

What would settle it. Nothing on the record; Lowsley v Forbes makes the time point weaker for the family, not stronger. Catalogue: MR-0012.

T08 Leave to enforce was given on proper notice to the occupier.

Not Proven BP must show

The law. County court rules on leave to issue a warrant on an old judgment (exact 1974 rule not checked).

The record. BP Pension Trust gave notice on 17 Sep 1974 and leave was given on 19 Sep 1974. Mrs Buckler's solicitors appealed on 30 Oct 1974 for 'insufficient notice'. The appeal was never heard because the warrant was withdrawn after the letters (CA).

What would settle it. The September 1974 application, sworn statement and notice of appeal (Cardiff County Court holds nothing; no retention schedule). Catalogue: MR-0012, MR-0014.

T09 BP Pension Trust was entitled to enforce WGR's 1962 order 'as successors in title'.

Record Missing BP must show

The law. A successor must show its title to the land to take the benefit of a possession judgment (rule not checked for 1974 county court practice).

The record. Leave was sought by BP Pension Trust as WGR's successor (CA). The 31 Dec 1969 conveyance it relied on is not on HMLR's first-registration schedule (R4).

What would settle it. The 1969 conveyance and the leave application's exhibits. Catalogue: MR-0012, MR-0015.

T10 The owner's own 1974 action against Mary (Nancy) Williams was decided.

Not Met BP must show

The law. A pleaded limitation defence stands until the action is decided or discontinued.

The record. The CA: the 1974 action, in which her defence pleaded that BP's case was barred under the Limitation Act 1939, 'has never been finally heard' (EV-0049).

What would settle it. The 1974 pleadings and any notice of discontinuance. Catalogue: MR-0013.

C. The 1974 letters: did they have it to give?

T11 The person granting the licence on 31 Oct 1974 held the legal title.

Not Met BP must show

The law. Time cannot run for a person who occupies 'by licence of the owner with the paper title' (Powell v McFarlane at 469, quoted by the CA). Pye para 37: consent 'of the paper title owner'.

The record. On 31 Oct 1974 the legal owner was BP Pension Trust. BP Properties, which granted the life licence, took its conveyance only in May 1975 (CA). The CA called the point 'rather technical'.

What would settle it. Already on the face of the judgment. The live question is the next row. Catalogue: MR-0041.

T12 If not the legal owner, BP Properties held an equitable interest under a binding contract to buy on 31 Oct 1974.

Not Proven BP must show

The law. LPA 1925 s.40(1): no action on a contract for the sale of land unless it, or a memorandum of it, is in writing and signed (part performance aside). The CA treated the letters' words 'have agreed to buy' as enough to make BP Properties 'prima facie in a position to grant a licence'.

The record. No contract or memorandum is held. HMLR's WA240304 file adds a plan to the sale dated 23 Feb 1975, almost four months after the letters.

What would settle it. The BP Pension Trust → BP Properties contract or board minute of October 1974; the 1975 conveyance and plan. Catalogue: MR-0041; new MR candidate.

T13 The legal owner's own letter did not itself give permission.

Not Met Family must show

The law. The licence question is decided from the paper owner's standpoint (CA, final paragraphs).

The record. BP Pension Trust's own letter of 31 Oct 1974 withdrew the bailiff and said the sale was 'the only way' to 'safeguard your future occupation'. A court could read the two letters together as the owner's consent.

What would settle it. Cuts against us; counsel to assess. See section H.

T14 The occupier accepted the licence, or gave something for it.

Not Met BP must show

The law. Contract formation needs acceptance and consideration. The CA held neither was needed for a licence to stop time.

The record. The CA: 'artificial to say that Mrs Buckler accepted' the letters, and 'equally artificial to say there was any consideration'.

What would settle it. Already found by the CA, but it held that this did not matter. Only a higher court could change that rule.

T15 She repudiated the letters: she told BP plainly she did not accept them and still asserted ownership.

Record Missing Family must show

The law. The CA: 'It may be that the result would have been different if Mrs Buckler had... plainly told BP Properties Ltd that she did not accept the letters... she did not, however, do that.'

The record. The CA found she followed advice to do nothing until 11 Dec 1974. No written rejection sent to BP between Nov 1974 and Mar 1983 is held.

What would settle it. Her solicitors' file for Nov 1974 onward; any letter to BP or its solicitors rejecting the letters; press statements after Nov 1974. Catalogue: new MR candidate.

T16 The occupation after 31 Oct 1974 was a tenancy rather than a licence, so a different clock applied.

Not Met Family must show

The law. Street v Mountford [1985] AC 809 and Ramnarace v Lutchman [2001] UKPC 25, paras 13-16: exclusive possession points to a tenancy, unless it is referable to charity or another relationship. LA 1939 s.9(1) (tenancy at will deemed ended after one year) was repealed in 1980.

The record. The letters gave rent-free occupation of the house and garden for life, framed as a 'gesture'. Even on a tenancy-at-will reading, 12 years would run only from about Nov 1975, after BP sued.

What would settle it. Considered and set aside: it does not help on the dates.

D. The family's possession (12 years, contests)

T17 Factual possession of the farmhouse and garden, with the intention to possess.

Met Family must show

The law. Powell v McFarlane at 470-471; Pye paras 40-43 (intention to possess, not to own).

The record. The CA: Mr and Mrs Frederick Buckler were 'jointly in adverse possession of the farmhouse and garden from... February 2 1955'; Mrs Buckler's 'obvious animus possidendi'. BP admitted adverse possession from her husband's death to 31 Oct 1974.

What would settle it. Already found in the family's favour by the CA.

T18 Offers of a tenancy that she refused did not stop time.

Met Family must show

The law. LA 1939 s.13 (no formal entry or continual claim preserves a right of action), quoted by the CA: the landlord's 'notion' that asserting title at the farmhouse stopped time 'was erroneous'. Pye para 46: willingness to pay does not defeat possession.

The record. She refused the 1959 and 1965 tenancy offers and later alternative accommodation (CA; EV-0039). The CA still held her possession adverse until 31 Oct 1974.

What would settle it. Already in the judgment. The 1959/1965 offer letters themselves are not held. Catalogue: MR-0039.

T19 The family objected to and contested the owner's assertions before 31 Oct 1974.

Met Family must show

The law. Relevant to intention to possess (Pye paras 40-46) and to any later repudiation; objection by itself does not stop or start time.

The record. Public objection, 19-20 Nov 1958 (EV-0037); belief in title by 1959 (CA); refusals 1959 and 1965 (EV-0039); house opened to the public with a claim of title, 15 Apr 1974 (EV-0048); defence pleading the Limitation Act, 3 Jul 1974 (EV-0049); notice of appeal, 30 Oct 1974 (CA).

What would settle it. Already established; it helps intention but the CA's decision turned on conduct after 31 Oct 1974 (next rows).

T20 12 unbroken years of adverse possession before the owner sued (Feb 1955 to Feb 1967).

Not Met Family must show

The law. LA 1939 s.4(3), s.16; LA 1980 s.15(1), s.17; Sch.1 para 1 (accrual on dispossession), para 8(1)-(2) (time stops if possession ceases to be adverse).

The record. The CA: the 1962 action was begun 'well before there had, on any view, been 12 years' adverse possession', so title was not extinguished while the order could be enforced.

What would settle it. Only by setting the 1987 judgment aside and showing the 1962 order or its enforcement was invalid. Catalogue: MR-0011.

T21 12 unbroken years ending after 1974 (e.g. from her husband's death, 1965-67, to 1979).

Not Met Family must show

The law. LA 1939 s.10(2) (= LA 1980 Sch.1 para 8(2)): once possession stops being adverse, the clock resets.

The record. The CA held possession stopped being adverse on receipt of the letters (31 Oct / 4 Nov 1974).

What would settle it. Depends on rows T11-T12 (licensor's title) and T15 (repudiation).

T22 Parcel A was already the family's own before 1955: the last rent was paid in 1928, so the owner's right was barred by 1940.

Not Proven Family must show

The law. Real Property Limitation Acts 1833 s.8 and 1874 s.1 (texts not available on legislation.gov.uk; not checked), carried into LA 1939 s.9(2) and LA 1980 Sch.1 para 5: an unwritten yearly tenancy is treated as ended at the last receipt of rent. LA 1980 s.29(7): a barred right cannot be revived by a later acknowledgment.

The record. 1928 last rent and Parcel A being regarded as the family's own is family testimony (EV-0016). Against it: the CA records rent arrears in the mid-1930s and late 1940s and a 1949 surrender and re-grant to Frederick Buckler (EV-0025). This route was not argued in 1986-87.

What would settle it. The 1916 tenancy (written or not, and its plan); the 1928 receipt; WGR rental books 1938-44 (Glamorgan Archives D696, EV-0020/EV-0257). Catalogue: MR-0003, MR-0004.

E. Setting the 1987 judgment aside

T23 Conscious and deliberate dishonesty in evidence given, an act, a statement or a concealment relevant to the judgment.

Not Proven Family must show

The law. Takhar v Gracefield [2019] UKSC 13, para 56, adopting RBS v Highland Financial Partners [2013] EWCA Civ 328, para 106.

The record. No document yet shows anyone knew a statement put to the court was untrue (no ground tested so far scores above 2 of 5 on this limb).

What would settle it. BP's 1984-87 litigation files; the October 1974 sale contract; HMLR examination papers. Catalogue: MR-0024, MR-0020, MR-0012.

T24 Materiality: the dishonest matter was an operative cause of the decision, judged against the evidence the court relied on.

Not Proven Family must show

The law. Takhar para 56 (RBS v Highland para 106, second and third principles).

The record. The decisive finding was the 1974 licence and the absence of repudiation. A false 'agreed to buy' (R1) or a hidden written rejection (R2) would go to that point; the registration recital (T02) would not.

What would settle it. Proof that no contract existed on 31 Oct 1974, or that a written rejection reached BP.

T25 No reasonable-diligence bar: the dishonesty was not raised at the 1986-87 trial.

Met Family must show

The law. Takhar para 54 (Lord Kerr): no diligence requirement where no allegation of dishonesty was raised at trial; para 55 and para 66 (Lord Sumption): discretion if it was raised, or a deliberate decision was taken not to investigate.

The record. 0 hits for dishonesty or concealment in [1987] EWCA Civ 2. The 1989 Commission decision (Application 14464/88) records complaints of bias and disregard of evidence, not dishonesty by BP.

What would settle it. The House of Lords petition record (Parliamentary Archives) is the one unread record that could change this.

T26 The right route and standing: a fresh action to set aside, brought by the right person.

Record Missing Family must show

The law. Takhar para 60: setting a judgment aside on this ground is a cause of action. CPR 52.30 reopening only where there is 'no alternative effective remedy'. The CA noted the defendant had to rely on his mother's estate, and no grant had been taken out.

The record. No grant of representation to Mary (Nancy) Williams's estate is held; her death registration has not been found.

What would settle it. Death certificate, then a grant of letters of administration. Catalogue: MR-0022, MR-0023.

F. The 1939 WGR tenancy and John Williams's signature

T27 What land the May 1939 agreement covered.

Record Missing BP must show

The law. An acknowledgment of title only covers the land it describes (LA 1980 ss.29-30; LA 1939 ss.23-24, not checked directly).

The record. Glamorgan Archives DBDT123/7, catalogued 'tenancy of piece or parcel of land' (duplicate), monthly from 1 Mar 1939 at 8s a month, meadow or pasture only. The plan photo shows pink land east of the buildings, with the buildings outside it (EV-0023; reading in ). Page 1 (parties, parcels, acreage) is not in the photo.

What would settle it. Page 1 of DBDT123/7 and a clean scan of the plan. Catalogue: new MR candidate.

T28 The signature on the 1939 agreement is John Williams's own.

Not Proven BP must show

The law. A forged signature makes the document of no effect against the person whose name is used; a written acknowledgment must be 'signed by the person making it' (LA 1980 s.30(1)). Forgery and Counterfeiting Act 1981 s.1 (criminal). Civil standard: more probable than not (Re B (Children) [2008] UKHL 35); seriousness affects how strong the evidence must be, not the standard.

The record. Attestation reads 'WITNESS to the signature of... JOHN WILLIAMS', witness read as 'H R Howe(?)', 'Cashier', 'Lloyds [Bank]' (uncertain). He was about 72 (born c.1867: note by Mrs Nancy Buckler, Glamorgan Archives D/D XJF 64/30). No comparison has been made.

What would settle it. A forensic document examiner's comparison of the original with known signatures of the same period (see the method in section F of the PDF). Catalogue: new MR candidate.

T29 If he did sign: he lacked capacity, or did not know what he signed (non est factum), or was pressed (undue influence).

Not Proven Family must show

The law. Capacity is presumed; age alone is not incapacity (common law; the same rule now in Mental Capacity Act 2005 s.2(3)). Non est factum needs a document 'fundamentally different' from what he thought, and no carelessness; the burden is on the signer (Saunders v Anglia Building Society [1970] UKHL 5). Undue influence must be proved by the person wronged (RBS v Etridge (No 2) [2001] UKHL 44).

The record. No evidence on any of these. Age 72 by itself is not enough.

What would settle it. Medical or family evidence of his condition in 1939; evidence of how the agreement was presented.

T30 Does the 1939 signature matter to Parcel A?

Not Proven Either side

The law. Only as an acknowledgment of WGR's title within the 12 years from the 1928 last rent (LA 1980 s.29(2), s.30(1)); a barred right cannot be revived (s.29(7)).

The record. If page 1 limits it to the Parcel B pasture, it acknowledges nothing about the house. If page 1 names the house, a genuine signature would cut against the 1928-1940 route (T22); a forged one would not.

What would settle it. Page 1 of DBDT123/7, then the handwriting comparison.

G. Correcting the register today

T31 A 'mistake' in the register, so it can be altered or rectified.

Not Met Family must show

The law. LRA 2002 Sch.4 paras 1-3 (court) and 5-6 (registrar): rectification corrects a mistake; against a proprietor in possession only with consent, or for dishonesty or lack of proper care, or if it would be unjust not to. Sch.12 para 18: a squatter whose title was complete under LRA 1925 s.75(1) before 13 Oct 2003 can be registered.

The record. While the 1987 judgment stands, the family's possession is held not to have matured, so no s.75 trust arose and the register shows no mistake on that ground.

What would settle it. First set the judgment aside (section E); then Sch.4 rectification and Sch.12 para 18.

T32 Indemnity if the register is changed or a loss flows from a mistake.

Not Proven Either side

The law. LRA 2002 Sch.8 para 1 (indemnity), para 5 (none for a claimant's own dishonesty; reduced for lack of care), para 8 (time runs from when the claimant knew or ought to have known). LRA 2002 s.58: the register is conclusive as to the legal estate, subject to alteration.

The record. Not yet engaged.

What would settle it. Depends on the outcome of section E and G.

H. Where the 1987 Court of Appeal decided against the family, and why

The judge (Hollis J, 24 Jul 1986) held the family's acts on the rest of the farm 'too tenuous and equivocal'; that part was not appealed.

Title was not extinguished by Feb 1967 because WGR sued in 1962 within the 12 years, and a possession order obtained in time stays enforceable (St Marylebone v Fairweather [1963] AC 510; Lougher v Donovan).

Leave to enforce on 19 Sep 1974 was within 12 years of the 1962 order, so the court had jurisdiction.

The 'wrong company' point was 'rather technical': the letters recorded that BP Properties 'had agreed to buy', so it was 'prima facie in a position to grant a licence'.

A unilateral licence, never accepted and given for nothing, still stopped time, because BP would have had to treat her as a licensee and could not evict her without ending it. Possession 'referable to a lawful title' is not adverse (Hughes v Griffin).

She did not repudiate the letters; she followed advice to do nothing until 11 Dec 1974, and asserted no ownership after that.

Leave to appeal to the House of Lords was refused. The European Commission of Human Rights declared Application 14464/88 inadmissible on 14 Apr 1989.

I. Points against this reading

BP v Buckler is still good law. It was applied after Pye in Colin Dawson Windows v King's Lynn [2005] EWCA Civ 9 (para 40, as cited in Clowes Developments v Walters [2005] EWHC 669 (Ch)); Colin Dawson itself: not checked.

BP Pension Trust, the legal owner, also wrote on 31 Oct 1974 and withdrew the bailiff. The two letters together may count as the owner's consent even if BP Properties had nothing to give (T13).

Lowsley v Forbes [1998] UKHL 34 held that the time limit on actions on a judgment does not apply to execution. The '12-year cliff' on the 1962 order is therefore weaker in law than a simple timeline suggests (T07).

The Court of Appeal's own recital records the 1955 order covered 'the whole of the farm' (T05).

The 1939 agreement, if genuine and limited to Parcel B, is consistent with the court's tenancy history; earlier research in this library withdrew the idea that it narrowed the farm tenancy.

The 1928 last-rent route rests on family testimony (EV-0016), and the court records rent arrears in the 1930s and 1940s and a 1949 surrender and re-grant (T22).

Every ground tested so far scores 0-2 of 5 on the dishonesty limb of Takhar; none meets the court-ready bar (every limb 4 or more).

Res judicata: the 1987 judgment binds the family's side. New legal arguments (for example the 1928 route or the tenancy-at-will point) cannot by themselves reopen it; only proof of dishonesty can (Takhar paras 60-63).

WGR's 1968-69 change of control was public and cleared by the Board of Trade.
