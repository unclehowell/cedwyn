      About this diagram, the parcel numbers and what is still unsettled

      Tŷ Mawr, Llandough. Into the circle comes one farm, A + B, on the root title chain. Out come the later titles, A3 and B, plus two more lines, SA1 and SA2. The family’s reading calls them synthetic and ties each to a step that treated the occupation as permissive; follow each line to its records. Press ↻ to watch every line grow and hear it told; tap anything for its record.

      Numbers are the parcel figures on the 1824 Stewart plan of Cedfin (Tŷ Mawr). The clearest figures run 1 to 45. Several figures towards the bottom of the plan read two ways — 30 to 39, or 50 to 59 if the leading digit is a 5 rather than an old-style 3; the hand cannot be settled from the photograph we hold, so both readings are listed and no number is dropped. The alternate readings cover 50, 52 and 53; no figure clearly reads 46 to 49 or 51, and that too stays open until the original is re-read at higher resolution. Pick a number for everything the records tie to it.

      The list shows every number something is held for; numbers with nothing held are left out. On the 1824 plan the bracket is the name in the plan’s own reference table where one is read (a family photograph of the archive screen, so digits are not certain). The map choice changes which records show beneath a number. The colour by each number marks what is held for it — green, concrete info for that number (its figure clearly read in the site’s 1824 reading record, outside the two-ways band, and the parcel placed on the plan, with its record); amber, something held even if ambiguous; red, nothing held for that number yet — and the bracket gives its core identifying trait: an 1841 field name or land use where one is entered, or the note on why nothing is held. A tithe match in a bracket is the site’s own reading, not a source statement. The tithe map’s own plot numbers appear inside each record, never in the list.

      ▶ Watch the title chain step by step — an animated chronology, 1841–1987: who held the paper, who held the land, the twelve-year pairs and the parcels taken, each step tied to its catalogue ID.

      🗺️ Map opens the chosen map cropped to the picked parcel (crops are set by eye and are approximate). Tenants: shown adds the tenancies and leases held in the records beside the freehold lines. Where the family reads a parcel as part of A or B, that line plays when the parcel is picked; the reading is the family position, not a finding. The data sits in a workbook with one tab each for maps, parcels and title events; an edit there reaches this page on its next load.
