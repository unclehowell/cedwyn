Later judgments citing BP Properties Ltd v Buckler [1987] EWCA Civ 2

Eleven later judicial decisions have been checked against their primary judgment texts: four in the Court of Appeal, four in the High Court, one in the Privy Council, one in the Upper Tribunal (Lands Chamber) and one in the First-tier Tribunal. Each entry gives the paragraphs where the 1987 case appears and how it is treated (cites, follows, applies, distinguishes, limits or mentions). This curated register was compiled using case-name, neutral-citation and reported-citation searches — not just references to Great House Farm or Tŷ Mawr. The static page is not yet an automatic, comprehensive legal citator.

  1. 2003 · Good Challenger Navegante SA v Metalexportimport SA

    Court of Appeal (Civil Division) · [2003] EWCA Civ 1668 · 24 November 2003 · paragraph 106 · treatment: mentions

    At paragraph 106 the Court of Appeal quotes a passage from Duer v Frazer setting out principles that Evans-Lombe J derived from Dillon LJ's judgment in BP Properties Ltd v Buckler (cited there as [1987] EGLR 168) on when the court will extend time beyond six years to issue execution of a judgment. The point is enforcement of judgments, not adverse possession.

    Read primary judgment on Find Case Law

  2. 2005 · Colin Dawson Windows Ltd v Borough Council of King's Lynn & West Norfolk & Anor

    Court of Appeal (Civil Division) · [2005] EWCA Civ 9 · 20 January 2005 · paragraphs 33, 38–40 · treatment: distinguishes

    Quotes the Buckler facts and Dillon LJ's reasoning. At paragraph 39 it distinguishes Buckler as an express-licence case while considering the related permission principle in the context of negotiations. At paragraph 40 it records that the submission that Pye v Graham had implicitly overruled Buckler was withdrawn.

    Read primary judgment on BAILII

  3. 2005 · Clowes Developments (UK) Ltd v Walters & Ors

    High Court, Chancery Division · [2005] EWHC 669 (Ch) · 20 April 2005 · paragraphs 42 and 44–45 · treatment: applies

    At paragraph 42 the judge notes that Buckler survives Pye (citing Colin Dawson Windows at paragraph 40) and that any implied permission would have been effective to make later possession permissive. At paragraphs 44–45 he sets out the Court of Appeal's holding and Dillon LJ's statement that possession is not adverse if it can be referred to a lawful title, and applies that reasoning to the facts before him.

    Read primary judgment on Find Case Law

  4. 2005 · Batsford Estates (1983) Co Ltd v Taylor & Anor

    Court of Appeal (Civil Division) · [2005] EWCA Civ 489 · 29 April 2005 · paragraph 22 · treatment: mentions

    At paragraph 22 the Court of Appeal records that, in Colin Dawson Windows, Rix LJ read extensively from Dillon LJ's judgment in BP Properties Limited v Buckler before turning to the test for inferring permission from an overt act or demonstrable circumstances.

    Read primary judgment on Find Case Law

  5. 2006 · Odey & Ors v Barber

    High Court, Chancery Division · [2006] EWHC 3109 (Ch) · 29 November 2006 · paragraphs 72–73 (conclusion at 74–75) · treatment: cites

    At paragraphs 72–73 the judge sets out how O'Mara and Gray and Gray derive support from BP Properties v Buckler (1988) 55 P & CR 337 for the proposition that an unsolicited grant of permission prevents an easement arising. At paragraphs 74–75 he finds that reasoning compelling and holds that an unsought permission prevented time running for a right of way by prescription.

    Read primary judgment on Find Case Law

  6. 2007 · Roberts v Swangrove Estates Ltd & Anor

    High Court, Chancery Division · [2007] EWHC 513 (Ch) · 14 March 2007 · paragraph 83 · treatment: applies

    At paragraph 83 a party relies on Hughes v Griffin and BP Properties v Buckler [1987] 2 EGLR 168. The judge records Dillon LJ's statement that it does not matter that the person in possession did not know the possession was lawful, and accepts the argument.

    Read primary judgment on Find Case Law

  7. 2008 · Totton and Eling Town Council v Caunter & Anor

    High Court, Chancery Division · [2008] EWHC 3630 (Ch) · 11 June 2008 · paragraphs 17–20 · treatment: cites

    Identifies BP Properties v Buckler at paragraph 17 and sets out the letter, the absence of express acceptance or rejection, and Dillon LJ's reasoning in paragraphs 17–20.

    Read primary judgment on BAILII

  8. 2013 · Smart v London Borough of Lambeth

    Court of Appeal (Civil Division) · [2013] EWCA Civ 1375 · 7 November 2013 · paragraphs 34–36 · treatment: applies

    Expressly cites BP Properties v Buckler at paragraph 34, explaining that permission need not be contractually binding. Paragraph 35 sets out the overt-act or demonstrable-circumstance test for implied permission; paragraph 36 discusses permission pending negotiations and cites Colin Dawson Windows.

    Read primary judgment on BAILII · Case text and citator

  9. 2016 · Smith (Personal Representative of Hugh Smith (Deceased)) and others v Molyneaux

    Judicial Committee of the Privy Council · [2016] UKPC 35 · 21 November 2016 · paragraphs 28–30 · British Virgin Islands · treatment: applies

    The Board itself drew the parties' attention to Buckler, discussed unilateral permission without acceptance, and recorded the parties' acceptance that the principle applied under section 135(1) of the BVI Registered Land Act. This is an overseas Privy Council decision, not an English Court of Appeal ruling.

    Read official Privy Council judgment (PDF)

  10. 2024 · Brown v Ridley & Anor

    Upper Tribunal (Lands Chamber) · [2024] UKUT 14 (LC) · 23 January 2024 · table of authorities; paragraphs 154–155 · treatment: distinguishes / limits

    Lists the case among its authorities and discusses it at paragraphs 154–155. The Tribunal found it too far removed from the issue before it to help, even by analogy, and held that “BP Properties is not authority for the proposition that a registered proprietor can, in any case, put an end to adverse possession by the unilateral grant of a permission or licence to occupy the relevant land”, noting that Dillon LJ left open the position where the occupier rejects the permission. A limit on the principle, not a holding that Buckler was wrongly decided.

    Read primary judgment on Find Case Law

  11. 2025 · Kerridge v Hook

    First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) · [2025] UKFTT 1076 (PC) · 4 August 2025 · paragraph 33 · treatment: cites

    At paragraph 33 the Tribunal quotes Simon Brown LJ in Markfield Investments v Evans [2001] 1 WLR 1321, listing the ways adverse possession may cease, including “the true owner's grant of a tenancy or licence to the occupier (even a unilateral licence: see BP Properties Ltd v Buckler (1987) 55 P & CR 337)”.

    Read primary judgment on Find Case Law
