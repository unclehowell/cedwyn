PRINCE2 7 Compliance & Control Manual

Method baseline: PRINCE2 Project Management (Version 7), tailored to the Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr recovery programme. This page defines the management method and control architecture. It does not claim external certification or assurance.

1. Architecture

The umbrella programme coordinates four separately governed projects: P1 Evidence & Archive; P2 Title, Justice & Accountability with P2-T, P2-F and P2-C streams; P3 Artefacts; and P4 Tŷ Mawr Replica Museum. PRINCE2 governance is applied at project level. Each project requires its own project definition, business case, PID, tolerances, plans, work packages, products, registers, reports and closure decision.

8. Tolerance and exception control

Learn more

Each project and stage has tolerances for benefits, costs, time, quality, scope, sustainability and risk. The Project Manager manages within those limits. A forecast breach or matter outside delegated authority becomes an exception. The exception record states the deviation, cause, effect, options, recommendation, business-case impact and decision required. Legal limitation, court/tribunal deadlines and evidence-preservation actions are treated as zero-tolerance controls.

9. Product-based planning rule

Every controlled product has a product ID, purpose, composition, inputs, quality criteria, quality method, owner, approver, target, cost basis, evidence location and acceptance test. Activities are subordinate to the product they create or verify.

10. Evidence-quality rule

Evidence is managed separately from interpretation. Every evidence unit receives an identifier, provenance, date/source, preservation status, truth classification and proposition links where applicable. “Not found”, “not held”, “not searched”, “search incomplete” and “destroyed” are distinct states. 🕒

Recovery flywheel — operational control, 19 October 2026

The flywheel is an event-triggered control loop, not a claim of unattended background monitoring. Each run must leave a dated record stating what was checked, the inputs, changes, blockers and next review. Do not report a six-hour cycle as completed unless a run log proves it.

Learn more
  1. Trigger: new incoming correspondence, attachment, catalogue entry, archive lead, new deadline, material contradiction, scheduled review or risk of disposal/loss.
  2. Preserve: save the original file/message and its metadata; retain exact source reference, date, sender/custodian, attachments and access limitations. Use a hash when available. Never overwrite the source with a summary.
  3. Catalogue: assign/confirm the stable Evidence Library ID; record provenance, location, preservation status and what the item does and does not establish. “Not found”, “not held”, “not searched”, “search incomplete”, “withheld/partial” and “destroyed” are different results.
  4. Control the work: update the authoritative HubSpot case ticket; associate the task to the relevant work-package/project where those records exist; set one owner, priority, next action and a date justified by the actual trigger or response deadline.
  5. Test acceptance: distinguish acknowledgement from substantive response, artifact received from artifact verified, and search result from complete search. A task closes only when its acceptance criterion is satisfied or a recorded decision says no further action is required.
  6. Exception and learning: same-day action if evidence may be lost or a statutory/preservation clock is threatened; raise an Exception Report for a forecast tolerance breach; update the risk/issue/decision/lessons record and the next review date.
Learn more

Idempotence and safety: use source IDs, message IDs, case references and hashes to detect repeat inputs; update existing records instead of creating another task for the same next action. Similar wording alone is not a duplicate test. Never merge organisations, delete records, send correspondence or mark a task complete merely to improve dashboard figures. The original and the audit trail survive every housekeeping step.

19 October operational baseline: 305 HubSpot tasks (201 NOT_STARTED, 48 WAITING, 21 IN_PROGRESS, 35 COMPLETED); 111 open tasks flagged overdue; 72 open tasks have priority NONE; 70 tickets with 27 direct task-to-ticket associations. These are snapshot figures and must be recalculated on the next recorded run.
