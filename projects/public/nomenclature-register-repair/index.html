PRINCE2 mythology · what the controls mean in practice

The story of control is not the evidence itself

In this programme, “PRINCE2 mythology” is used deliberately for the governing story that turns work into roles, stages, products, tolerances and decisions. The model is useful only when the statements it makes can be checked against the record.

  • Roles are appointments, not magic words. Writing “Executive”, “Senior User”, “Project Board” or “Independent Project Assurance” does not appoint a person. Where the programme has not recorded an appointment, this work package does not invent one.
  • A green report is not a verified fact. The source merge can be proven from Git; publication can be proven from a successful build/deployment; acceptance requires observing the live URL. Each is a different claim with its own evidence.
  • Tolerance is a boundary, not permission to overrun. A count mismatch, route failure, external cost or evidential overclaim triggers escalation and a recorded decision rather than a quiet scope change.
  • A product description is a test, not decoration. The accepted product is the usable register with the agreed fields, coverage, navigation and limits, not simply an HTML file that exists.
  • Lessons change the next stage. The practical lesson is that an outbound citation saying “all nomenclature” must lead to a register whose coverage and limitations are visible on arrival. A dictionary-style stub must not pass as a complete catalogue.

PRINCE2 status does not decide ownership, historical identity, legal cause of action or whether a court should reopen a case. Those remain evidence and legal-gate questions under the separate programme controls.
