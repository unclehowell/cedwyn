The nine-stop tour already exists as words and pictures on the Wales page. This plan turns it into tours people can book and walk: a 90-minute Llandough walking loop first, needing no vehicles or partners, then the wider stops by minibus or car.

The route

Walking core and vehicle extension, timed by map router

Part 1 · The Llandough walking loop (about 90 minutes with stories): stop 1 Tŷ Mawr site, Church View Close → stop 2 St Dochdwy's church and the Irbic Cross (0.18 km, 2-3 min) → stop 3 Roman villa site, south of the church (about 90 m in a straight line; the mapped path is longer) → stop 8 the village green → stop 9 back to Church View Close. Under 2 km in all.

Part 2 · The wider stops (by car or minibus, about 52 minutes of driving): Llandough → stop 4 Brynwell, Leckwith (2.9 km, 6 min) → stop 5 the Driscoll statue, Bute Street (about 10.7 km by the mapped route, an upper bound) → stop 6 Cardiff Bay waterfront (2.5 km, 4 min; or a 27-minute walk) → stop 7 Lavernock Point (10.2 km, 16 min) → back to Llandough (6.6 km, 13 min). About 33 km.

Method: OpenStreetMap places and the OSRM foot and car routers, free-flow times with no traffic or stop time (a minimum). The villa point is the national monuments record's 100 m grid square, so the exact spot is not checked. Timings will be replaced by the pilot walk's own.

Getting there, parking and access

Buses, free car parks, what is public and what is private
  • Buses to Llandough: the Llandough Hospital stops, about 0.5 km from the church, are served by routes 7, 95, 304 and 305; the Llandough Institute stop is about 180 m from the church (routes not checked).
  • Buses to Lavernock: routes 88 and 94 stop on Fort Road, about 800 m from the nature reserve.
  • Parking: two free public car parks in Llandough and one with 18 spaces; the church car park is for church users, so ask first. At Lavernock, limited parking by the gate and at the end of Fort Road.
  • Access: Church View Close is a public street of private homes, viewed from the pavement. The churchyard needs the church's permission for groups. Nothing of the villa shows above ground; view from public land. Brynwell is private: view from the road. The Driscoll statue and the Bay are public spaces. The Marconi plaque at Lavernock is on the church by the car park, reachable without the cliff paths; the reserve has stiles and eroding cliff paths.
  • Step-free: the Llandough core is short but on a hill; gradients and pavements are not checked and need a site walk.

Pilot plan

Small, free or low-risk, and reviewed
  1. Walk the Llandough loop with phone GPS; photograph signage; confirm step-free sections and where the green is.
  2. Written permission for group visits from the church and the green's owner (an Executive decision; nobody has been contacted).
  3. A guide: a family guide or a qualified tour guide (the guides' association's 2026 half-day fee is £208), and public liability cover (Executive decision).
  4. Audio for all nine stops in English and Welsh, so the tour also works self-guided.
  5. Pilot walk with 8-12 guests; ask each for a written review; time every leg.
  6. Then a fixed public schedule: at least two walks a month in season. Benchmark: £15-18 per person for a two-hour public walk, about 14 guests per walk.
