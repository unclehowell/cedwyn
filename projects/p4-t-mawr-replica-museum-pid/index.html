Visitor proposition and scope

Core museum first; later amenities optional

The house and landscape are the central offer: a guided tour, researched rooms, farm and family interpretation, a garden/orchard concept, education resources and a public source catalogue. The virtual Wales tour should be able to deliver value before any building opens.

Books, maps, prints and locally made products may support a shop. Weddings, accommodation, wax figures and equestrian facilities remain optional; each needs a separate permissions, demand, staffing and lifecycle-cost test.

The museum is a cultural and educational project. It is separate from UK legal arguments and can be planned independently of litigation or records-request outcomes.

Acreage and location control

106 and 166 acres remain alternatives

The historical research file currently retains 106 acres and 166 acres as competing readings. As unit conversions, these are approximately 42.9 hectares and 67.2 hectares. The figures must be reconciled to their source documents; neither is an established Argentine boundary or a purchase requirement.

The site-search area is south of Puerto Madryn. The parallel between Tŷ Mawr, Cardiff and the sea, and a future site, Puerto Madryn and the Atlantic, is a geographical design hypothesis. GIS work must define points and measurement methods before stating that the distances match.

See the Land Reconnaissance and GIS Plan.

Philanthropy, partners and grants

No third-party award assumed

The working funding assumption is founder-funded philanthropy for early work across four ring-fenced streams: UK legal work, Argentina feasibility, the Wales virtual tour and the digital/online presence. Each stream needs its own cost centre, budget cap, approvals, supplier evidence and report of actuals. This does not imply that a charity exists or that expenditure qualifies for tax relief.

Partner categories include local Chubut architecture, survey, legal, environmental and planning professionals; museums and schools; Welsh-Patagonian cultural groups; universities; local makers and visitor operators.

No grant, sponsor, public subsidy, partner or land agreement should enter the baseline before written confirmation. Current leads and eligibility restrictions are in the Funding and Partners Plan.

Stages and acceptance gates

No construction baseline yet
  1. Research setup: source-graded building brief and reconciled acreage question.
  2. Land reconnaissance: GIS, cadastral/title checks, environment, access, water and service constraints.
  3. Concept: climate-adapted house and landscape, visitor journey and community feedback.
  4. Business case: local capital, operating, staffing, maintenance and contingency estimates; lawful route to site and permissions.
  5. Delivery: detailed design, approvals, funding, procurement, construction and operational-readiness tests.

See the Implementation Roadmap. A site or construction decision cannot pass until legal access, permitted use, ecological suitability and total cost have been reviewed independently.
