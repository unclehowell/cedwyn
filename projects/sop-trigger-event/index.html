7. The four-month figure

It belongs to a new Strasbourg application, counted from the last domestic decision

Four months is the Strasbourg time limit: an application must be lodged “within a period of four months from the date on which the final decision was taken” (Art 35(1) ECHR, as amended by Protocol 15; four months since 1 February 2022, six months before).

It does not run from the discovery of new evidence. The final decision in the 1987 case was the House of Lords’ refusal of leave on 3 March 1988, and the 1988 application was lodged inside the limit then in force, on 3 May 1988. A new four-month clock needs a new final domestic decision first: in practice the end of a set-aside action (route B), or of a challenge to a new public-body decision (route F).

The clocks that start at discovery are the Limitation Act, s.32 (route D) and, for any future Strasbourg judgment, Rule 80’s six months (route C3).

8. The 1989 Strasbourg application

Application 14464/88, William Buckler v the United Kingdom
  • Introduced 3 May 1988; registered 12 December 1988; declared inadmissible by the European Commission of Human Rights on 14 April 1989.
  • Complaints: Article 6 (fair trial), Article 8 (home), Article 1 of Protocol 1 (possessions), Article 13 (effective remedy).
  • Reasons: Article 6 not substantiated; the possession order “in accordance with law” and “necessary in a democratic society” to protect the owner held by the courts; no interference with possessions because the courts held the property belonged to another; appeals that fail are still an effective remedy.
  • The decision was not published on HUDOC. The four-page decision is held: scan (wayback EV-0122).
  • Not the same case: Buckley v the United Kingdom (application 20348/92, judgment 25 September 1996) concerns a Gypsy caravan site in Willingham, Cambridgeshire (HUDOC).
  • Open: what the Strasbourg registry still holds of file 14464/88 (I-076).
