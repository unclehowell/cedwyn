Medieval Llandough fee (1440): Barry or Cowbridge

A medieval inquisition transcription gives an alternative place-name wording. It is treated as a separate holding reference, not as a name of the Penarth farmhouse or the later Cowbridge parish.

1 historical occurrence · ⓘ Mapping the Medieval Countryside, E-CIPM 25-330

  1. “Llandough-juxta-Barry or Llandough-juxta-Cowbridge (Landogh)”

    3 Nov 1440 (writ 29 Dec 1439) secondary

    Isabel, Countess of Warwick; inquisition at Gloucester; E-CIPM 25-330. The National Archives references: C 139/96/3, membranes 45–46; E 149/169/6, membrane 9. Read the transcription.

    The transcription describes a knight’s fee formerly held by the heirs of John de Wynton, valued at £20. Its wording explicitly gives “Barry or Cowbridge”. The original TNA membranes have not been inspected; this page records the published transcription, not a direct reading of the medieval document.

Not established as the same place or property

  • Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr beside St Dochdwy’s Church — No source links the 1440 fee to the farmhouse or its parcels. See the Penarth parish record.
  • Llandough Juxta Penarth Parish — Vision of Britain unit 10484613 is a later parish-unit database record. No link between it and this medieval fee has been established. Unit record.
  • Llandough Parish / Llandough Juxta Cowbridge — Vision of Britain unit 10484870 is a separate later parish-unit record. The medieval phrase does not prove identity with it. Unit record.
