# Storytime v0.1.0 — scene scripts

One script per scene. Museum narration is the long text; the Wales tour reads the short text (translated, machine translated, 18 languages); Patagonia is organised by location. Every scene lists its evidence-catalogue IDs; lines marked “Our reading” are the project's working interpretation.

## s01 · Beneath the ground (before 1824)

Catalogue: EV-0055, EV-0056, EV-0054, EV-0206, EV-0018, EV-0068, EV-0293, EV-0126, EV-0134

Museum: Long before any estate paper names the farm, the ground at Llandough was lived on. A 1980 newspaper report describes the substantial remains of a Roman villa at Llandough. A 1931 letter records a spearhead, a spur and the skeletons of six big men found at the church farm about 1858. The 1994 excavation beside St Dochdwy's church recorded more than a thousand burials. In 1983 a warehouse fire destroyed the finds from the villa site. Our reading: this was a place of standing for many centuries before the paper record begins.

Wales tour: Long before any estate paper, Llandough was lived on: a Roman villa, an early Christian cemetery of over a thousand burials beside the church, and finds later lost in a 1983 fire.

## s02 · The name Cedwyn (1392 to 1902)

Catalogue: EV-0001, EV-0195, EV-0198, EV-0200, EV-0210, EV-0219, EV-0218

Museum: The estate's name runs through the landscape. Research notes gather Cwm-Cedwyn, the wooded dell on the Ely, Nant-Cedwyn, the brook, and Ynys-Cedwyn. A 1392 reference to Cwm-Cedwyn in Llandough boundary records is noted, with the original not yet held. In 1902 a sale catalogue lists Cwm Cydfin Wood in Leckwith and Llandough, and the same name survives today in the Cwm Cydfin nature site. Our reading: the name Cedwyn is older than the estate papers that later describe the land.

Wales tour: The name Cedwyn runs through the land: Cwm-Cedwyn on the Ely, Nant-Cedwyn, Cwm Cydfin Wood. Our reading: the name is older than the estate papers.

## s03 · Tŷ Mawr on the map (1824)

Catalogue: EV-0004, EV-0211

Museum: In 1824 a survey of the district draws the holding as plan 19: Cedfin, or Tŷ Mawr Farm, with a table of its fields. An 1824 map of Tŷ Mawr survives as an image. This is the first mention found so far in the estate surveys. Our reading: the survey records a house and lands already established, not something new.

Wales tour: 1824: a survey draws the holding as plan 19, Cedfin or Tŷ Mawr Farm, with a table of its fields. The first mention found so far in the estate surveys.

## s04 · The tithe map (about 1841)

Catalogue: EV-0215, EV-0005

Museum: Around 1841 the tithe apportionment for Llandough, Cogan and Leckwith lists John Williams as occupier of thirty-six parcels: thirty-three entered against the Marquess of Bute, three against the church glebe. A tithe apportionment was a list for a church charge; it did not test who held title. Our reading: these entries are the earliest paper statement of Bute ownership here, and they are the statement the project scrutinises.

Wales tour: About 1841: the tithe list puts John Williams on 36 parcels, 33 entered against Bute. A tithe list did not test title; our reading is that this Bute entry is the one to scrutinise.

## s05 · Rates and the quarry (1872 to 1877)

Catalogue: EV-0212, EV-0006, EV-0007, EV-0008

Museum: Parish rate books from 1872 to 1876 name John Williams at the farm; the identification is still provisional. In 1877 an agreement between Bute and Daniel Thomas and Son touches the house parcel, either a sale or a quarry lease; the original is not yet located. A paper of 6 November 1877 in John Williams's name is reported on later files, its original also unlocated. Our reading, under the project's working paradigm: the quarry is where the carving of the estate begins, the line that leads to the limeworks acres. The catalogue does not yet hold the acreage.

Wales tour: 1872 to 1877: rate books name John Williams; an 1877 Bute agreement with a quarry firm touches the house parcel. Our reading: the start of the limeworks carve-up.

Testimony / not yet catalogued: The 33 acres taken for the limeworks: a line the project traces; the acreage is not yet in the catalogue

## s06 · A household of standing (1881 to 1910)

Catalogue: EV-0224, EV-0010, EV-0011, EV-0012, EV-0283

Museum: The 1881 census finds John Williams, farmer, and his household. In 1891 a newspaper reports three geese taken from Mr John Williams of Great House Farm. In 1897 the Marconi wireless trials run from Lavernock Point nearby. In 1900 the late Marquess's will passes his estates in Leckwith with Llandough to a younger son. In 1910 John Williams sits on the grand jury of the court leet at Llandough, which records no encroachments to present. Our reading: the Williams appear in the record as a family of standing in the parish.

Wales tour: 1881 to 1910: John Williams, farmer, in the census; on the court leet grand jury in 1910. Our reading: a family of standing in the parish.

## s07 · Woods, drains and estate papers (1902 to 1931)

Catalogue: EV-0219, EV-0013, EV-0014, EV-0015, EV-0016, EV-0009, EV-0199

Museum: In 1902 Bute's coppice woods, Cwm Cydfin Wood among them, go to auction. A Bute conveyance of 1905 is cited on a later registration file. In February 1916 a Bute estate tenancy document with a plan refers to the whole of the farm: it is the estate's own paper, and exactly what is contested. A drain agreement follows in 1924, and quarrying ends by 1928. Across these years the family keep planting trees on the land, the native custom of asserting ownership, recorded in a family photograph.

Wales tour: 1902 to 1931: estate auctions, conveyances and a 1916 estate paper the family contest. Meanwhile the family plant trees, the native custom of asserting ownership.

## s08 · Transferred over their heads (1938 to 1942)

Catalogue: EV-0022, EV-0019, EV-0020, EV-0216, EV-0023, EV-0220, EV-0024

Museum: In December 1938 the Bute interest in Cardiff ground rents passes to Western Ground Rents. A conveyance of the reversion carries a plan, and a 1965 contract later names a conveyance of 25 December 1938 as the root of title. In May 1939 Western Ground Rents draws up an agreement in John Williams's name, with a plan. From 1940 the company gives about 109 acres of Leckwith Woods to Cardiff council. Testimony, not yet in the catalogue, places wartime smoke-screen vehicles on parcel B from 1939. Our reading: the family's ground changed hands on paper without them.

Wales tour: 1938 to 1942: the Bute interest passes to Western Ground Rents; a 1939 paper is drawn up in John Williams's name. Our reading: the land changed hands on paper without the family.

Testimony / not yet catalogued: Wartime smoke-screen vehicles on parcel B from 1939: testimony (1 Nov 2026); no catalogue item yet

## s09 · The courts begin (1949 to 1957)

Catalogue: EV-0025, EV-0026, EV-0027, EV-0029, EV-0030, EV-0031, EV-0032, EV-0034

Museum: In 1949 an unwritten surrender and re-grant is recorded against Frederick Buckler; no written record of it is held. Around 1950 the family's title papers go missing from the blanket box in the farmhouse. Bute manor disposals run from 1950 to 1957. County court registers open in 1953, a further attempt follows in 1954, and a possession order of 1955 is still to be located. Our reading: each step frames the family's long occupation as permission, the twelve-year pattern the project traces.

Wales tour: 1949 to 1957: an unwritten re-grant, title papers missing from the farmhouse, then the county court. Our reading: occupation recast as permission.

## s10 · A Welsh Crichel Down (1958)

Catalogue: EV-0035, EV-0036, EV-0037, EV-0038

Museum: In May 1958, at the Leckwith Common inquiry, Mrs Mary Doreen Buckler of Great House Farm objects to anything beyond playing fields. In November the Western Mail reports her objection to industrial development near her farm, Tŷ Mawr, and records that land farmed by her family since 1667 had been wrongfully appropriated. She calls it a Crichel Down, after the 1954 national scandal over land taken for public use and not offered back to its owners. Our reading: she named the pattern the project now traces.

Wales tour: 1958: Mrs Mary Buckler of Tŷ Mawr calls it a Welsh Crichel Down: land farmed by her family since 1667, wrongfully appropriated.

## s11 · Orders, a school and a green (1959 to 1970)

Catalogue: EV-0039, EV-0040, EV-0041, EV-0042, EV-0043, EV-0044

Museum: In 1959 and 1965 Western Ground Rents sends offers concerning the house and garden. A 1961 deed with a plan involves a brick and tile company and Cardiff City. A possession order of December 1962 is recorded but not yet held. In June 1965 three and a third acres go to the county council for the Llandough school. In 1969 a conveyance passes land to the BP Pension Trust, and in June 1970 parcel C is registered as village green VG41. Our reading: the estate is taken a parcel at a time.

Wales tour: 1959 to 1970: offers, a 1962 order, the school site in 1965, BP in 1969, and parcel C registered as village green in 1970. Our reading: taken a parcel at a time.

## s12 · The survey and the open day (1971 to 1975)

Catalogue: EV-0045, EV-0046, EV-0630, EV-0631, EV-0047, EV-0048, EV-0049, EV-0050, EV-0051, EV-0052

Museum: On 26 April 1974 an architect of the Royal Commission measures and draws the house. That April the Daily Telegraph reports an open day to save the ancient Welsh house, quoting the family: four hundred years at Tŷ Mawr. In July Mary Williams pleads ownership in the county court action. A letter writer calls the threatened demolition a grievous loss. In May 1975 the farmhouse and garden pass between two BP companies on paper. Our reading: the house was recognised as historic while its title was moved around it.

Wales tour: 1974: the Royal Commission measures the house; an open day to save it; Mary Williams pleads ownership. 1975: the house moves between BP companies on paper.

## s13 · Registered (1980 to 1985)

Catalogue: EV-0057, EV-0058, EV-0059, EV-0066, EV-0063, EV-0065, EV-0069, EV-0070, EV-0071, EV-0067, EV-0061, EV-0072

Museum: In 1980 a Member of Parliament calls Western Ground Rents the most rapacious ground landlord, now owned by the BP pension fund. In 1982 the archaeological trust warns of the site's sensitivity. In 1982, and then in February 1983, the land and the farmhouse are first registered, as titles WA231076 and WA240304. Mary Doreen Williams dies in March 1983. Our reading: first registration, months after the warning and weeks before her death, is the clandestine registration step in the pattern the project traces.

Wales tour: 1982 to 1983: the land, then the farmhouse, first registered as titles WA231076 and WA240304. Our reading: the registration step in the pattern.

## s14 · Judgment (1986 to 1987)

Catalogue: EV-0073, EV-0074, EV-0075, EV-0077, EV-0078, EV-0079, EV-0080, EV-0076, EV-0214, EV-0632

Museum: In July 1986 the High Court at Cardiff gives judgment for BP. In February 1987 the East Vale local plan marks the buildings on the site to be retained, and the two titles are joined. On 31 July 1987 the Court of Appeal decides BP Properties against Buckler, and a petition to the House of Lords follows. Our reading: the judgment turned on paper title and on letters, never on how the family came to the land.

Wales tour: 1986 to 1987: High Court, then Court of Appeal, for BP. The local plan still marked the buildings to be retained.

## s15 · The house comes down (1988)

Catalogue: EV-0086, EV-0088, EV-0089, EV-0090, EV-0092, EV-0093, EV-0094, EV-0095, EV-0096, EV-0098, EV-0099, EV-0103, EV-0104, EV-0105, EV-0106, EV-0107, EV-0084, EV-0629, EV-0083

Museum: In April 1988 police wait outside the farmhouse. In July BP's Welsh sales are reported, and Cadw photographs the farmhouse and the barn. At the end of November entry is forced and an outbuilding roof is taken off. In early December Cadw is reported to be considering listing the house. On 7 December a team with bulldozers moves in just after four in the morning; by breakfast no building is left standing. Our reading: the house came down while its listing was still under consideration.

Wales tour: 1988: eviction, then on 7 December bulldozers just after 4 am. By breakfast nothing stood, while listing was still being considered.

## s16 · After (1989 to 1996)

Catalogue: EV-0108, EV-0112, EV-0117, EV-0118, EV-0119, EV-0122, EV-0113, EV-0124, EV-0126, EV-0127, EV-0129, EV-0131, EV-0133, EV-0135, EV-0134, EV-0136

Museum: In 1989 the monuments record notes the building destroyed by its owners, and Vale councillors authorise legal action against BP Properties over the demolition. The family's application to the European Commission of Human Rights, lodged in May 1988, is decided. Outline planning permission for housing follows, then twenty detached houses in 1992, with a village green planting plan. In 1994 the excavation north of the church records the early Christian cemetery. Our reading: the record kept growing after the house was gone.

Wales tour: 1989 to 1996: building destroyed by owners, the record notes. Houses and a green follow; the 1994 dig finds the early Christian cemetery.

## s17 · In the law books (2002 to 2017)

Catalogue: EV-0637, EV-0633, EV-0638, EV-0644, EV-0641, EV-0639, EV-0636, EV-0217, EV-0139

Museum: The 1987 judgment enters the land-law textbooks as a leading case on adverse possession, taught and cited as far as Hong Kong. Meanwhile parts of the land pass on: woodland to the Forest of Cardiff, then a transfer to a house builder. Our reading: the case became a lesson about permission while the questions about title stayed unasked.

Wales tour: 2002 onward: the case becomes a textbook lesson on adverse possession while the land passes on.

# Patagonia walk-round (by location)

## Location 1 · The barn

Catalogue: EV-0094, EV-0055, EV-0056, EV-0054, EV-0206, EV-0068, EV-0293

Location one: the barn. At Tŷ Mawr the barn stood across the yard from the house, and it was the ground for parish life. A photograph in the People's Collection Wales shows a chapel fete at Great House Farm, with the barn behind the crowd, a slide for the children and a refreshment tent. Testimony holds that Peerless Jim Driscoll, the Cardiff boxing champion, fought one of his early bouts in this barn, and that a Welsh roundhouse was found beneath it. Neither is yet in the evidence catalogue. What the catalogue does hold: Cadw photographed this barn on 29 July 1988, months before it was pulled down, and the ground around it carried a Roman villa and an early Christian cemetery.

Testimony / not yet catalogued: Chapel fete at the barn: photographs in the People's Collection Wales (items 871431 and 871446), not yet catalogued; Peerless Jim Driscoll fought in the barn: testimony, no catalogue item yet; A Welsh roundhouse found under the barn: testimony, no catalogue item yet

## Location 2 · The main house

Catalogue: EV-0004, EV-0211, EV-0215, EV-0224, EV-0283, EV-0035, EV-0037, EV-0047, EV-0048, EV-0049, EV-0066, EV-0080, EV-0086, EV-0103, EV-0106, EV-0118

Location two: the main house, Tŷ Mawr, the former manorial house of Llandough. In 1824 a survey draws it as Cedfin or Tŷ Mawr Farm, plan 19. In 1881 the census finds John Williams and his household here, and in 1910 he sits on the grand jury of the court leet. In 1958 Mary Doreen Buckler, speaking from this house, calls the loss of the family's land since 1667 a Welsh Crichel Down. In April 1974 the Royal Commission measures every room, the drawing this replica is built from, and the house holds an open day to save it. In 1982 and 1983 the land and the house are first registered to others. In December 1988 bulldozers arrive just after four in the morning, while listing is still under consideration. Our reading: the house is the centre of the whole chronology, and it stands again here.

## Location 3 · The church

Catalogue: EV-0293, EV-0018, EV-0272, EV-0084, EV-0134

Location three: the church. St Dochdwy's stood a few steps from the house. The 1994 excavation north of the church recorded more than a thousand early Christian burials, and Cadw listed the church in 1989. The church of the seventeenth century will not be rebuilt in Patagonia, so it appears here as a hologram. No image of that medieval church has been found yet: this hologram is traced from the oldest photograph found, of the church that stood here before 1866.

## Location 4 · The yard and the stables

Catalogue: EV-0010, EV-0212, EV-0009, EV-0199, EV-0014, EV-0023, EV-0025, EV-0026, EV-0030

Location four: the yard and the stables, the working heart of the farm. Parish books of the 1870s name John Williams here, and in 1891 a newspaper reports three geese taken from his yard. The family planted trees on the land, the native custom of asserting ownership, and a family photograph records it. From 1916 the estate side drew up papers describing the family's occupation on its own terms; the family contest every one of them. Around 1950 the family's title papers went missing from the blanket box in the house, and the county court registers open in 1953. Our reading: each paper recast a long occupation as permission.

## Location 5 · The gate and the green

Catalogue: EV-0042, EV-0044, EV-0133, EV-0135, EV-0122, EV-0147, EV-0161, EV-0156

Location five: the entrance gate, looking out to what was parcel C. In 1965 land of the estate went for the Llandough school, and in June 1970 parcel C was registered as village green VG41. After 1988 the site became a housing plan, with a village green planting scheme. In 2026 the record holders were asked directly: the Land Registry, Cadw, the archives. Every answer is a catalogue entry; every gap is a missing record. Our reading: the gate is where the estate went out, parcel by parcel, and where the record now comes back in.
