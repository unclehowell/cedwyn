Language Forensics

Trace how a word enters the record, who uses it, what it changes, and whether the document actually supports the meaning later attached to it.

Method · not verdictGreat House Farm / Tŷ MawrLocal browser analysis
Central rule
Do not let a label become a fact by repetition. A letter offering a licence, a person accepting a licence, an admission of another party’s title, and a court treating occupation as permissive are four different propositions.
01 · Provenance
Who?

Identify the speaker, author, recipient, institution and source. Do not treat an institution’s description of a person as that person’s own description.

02 · Exact language
What?

Preserve the original word, punctuation, date and surrounding sentence. Distinguish a quotation from a paraphrase and a judgment from a later summary.

03 · Scope
Where?

Map every phrase such as “farm”, “whole farm”, “house and garden” or “curtilage” to the contemporaneous plan and parcel—not to a later label.

04 · Consent
Did?

Separate offer, receipt, acceptance, signature, conduct, refusal, silence and legal effect. They are not synonyms.

05 · Time
When?

Track when a term first appears, when it changes, and whether a later document silently applies it to an earlier period.

06 · Consequence
So?

State what the source proves, what it does not prove, and what further evidence is needed before inferring fraud, concealment, procedural unfairness or error.

Working discipline: this tool identifies wording shifts and questions. It does not determine that a person committed fraud, that a court was corrupt, or that a register is wrong. Those conclusions require independently verified evidence and legal analysis.
Case study · 1974

The “licence” problem

BP’s 31 October 1974 letters were later treated by the Court of Appeal as making Mary’s occupation permissive, whether or not she accepted them. That legal conclusion must not be rewritten as proof that she signed a licence, consented to BP’s ownership, or abandoned her own title claim.

The addressee’s identity is also an open documentary question: family copies are described as addressed to “Mary Buckler”; the judgment describes “Mrs Buckler (described again as Mrs Williams)”. See the case file for the current source boundary.

Use it on any document

From wording to a testable question

  1. Paste the exact passage into Phrase audit.
  2. Compare it with the earlier or later document.
  3. Check the term against the Term register.
  4. Write a Claim test with “proves” and “does not prove”.
  5. Export the analysis with sources and unresolved questions.
What the method is designed to expose

Category substitution

A family member’s ownership claim becomes “tenant”, “licensee” or simply “occupier” in a later institutional record.

Scope substitution

A tenancy of identified fields or one parcel is retold as a tenancy of “the whole farm”.

Consent substitution

An offer or unilateral letter is retold as acceptance, consent or acknowledgement of title.

Workbench 01

Phrase audit

Paste a quotation, letter, judgment passage, register entry or institutional summary. The browser flags wording that deserves close reading; it does not decide the legal effect.

Keep the source reference and page or paragraph number beside the passage. A summary is not a substitute for the document.

Workbench 02

Compare wording across documents

Place the earlier or family wording on the left and the later institutional or judicial wording on the right. The comparison shows lexical substitutions, additions and omissions. Confirm every result against the actual originals.

Waiting for comparison

Run the comparison to see words present only in A or only in B.

A difference is not automatically deliberate. It becomes significant when it changes the apparent actor, legal status, parcel, time period, consent or evidential certainty—and when the source history supports that inference.

Parcel language · current working model

Keep the map attached to the word

The current working parcel model treats A as the wider revised holding, with A1 for the north woodland and A2 for Mary’s house and garden; B for eastern fields including the cottage/meadow; and the green south of the house as a provisional C rather than assuming it belongs to A. This is a research crosswalk, not a substitute for dated plans and conveyances.

Older records and webapps may use A/B/C differently. Never overwrite a historical label with the current model: record the source’s own nomenclature first, then add a separately labelled crosswalk.

Case file · 1974–1987

How an offer became “permissive occupation”

A worked example of the difference between the text of a letter, the recipient’s response, the parties’ competing positions, and the Court of Appeal’s later legal treatment.

The central distinction: The Court of Appeal treated the 1974 letters as making occupation permissive for the adverse-possession issue, whether or not Mary accepted them. That holding is not itself proof that she signed a licence, accepted BP’s title, abandoned ownership, or that BP had a valid root of title to every parcel. Each proposition requires its own source and analysis.
15 April 1974 · Public account

Mary opens the house to the public

The Daily Telegraph report records Mary publicly asserting that the house was her home and that her family had lived there for more than 400 years; it reports about 1,000 petition signatures. This is a press report of her position, not a judgment or title instrument.

Wiki: 1974 Licence Letters · EV-0048 / press transcript
3 July 1974 · Pleaded position

Mary pleads ownership

The current Wiki account says Mary pleaded that she owned the house parcel and relied on the Limitation Act 1939. The action was part-heard and adjourned; the research note says it was never relisted and no court decided who owned the house parcel in that action. The pleadings themselves remain the controlling record to obtain and read.

19 September 1974 · Enforcement procedure

Leave to enforce the 1962 order

The current chronology says leave was given without notice to Mary, with enforcement postponed until 31 October. Her solicitors appealed on notice grounds. Do not merge this enforcement application with the separate 1974 action in which she pleaded ownership.

Wiki: 1962 Possession Order · Court of Appeal judgment, paras 11, 13, 16
31 October 1974 · Letter

BP sends the licence letters

The letters are described as offering Mary a licence to remain in the farmhouse. The actual copies and complete correspondence must be examined for exact wording, recipient, service, signature, conditions, duration and any acceptance mechanism.

EV-0050 · BP Licence Letters
4 November 1974 · Hearing and withdrawal

Solicitors seek instructions; warrant withdrawn

The Wiki chronology says the hearing took place after the letters reached Mary’s solicitor, and BP’s solicitors withdrew the warrant before 25 November. The exact hearing note, appeal papers and withdrawal record are essential to test what was said, what was agreed, and whether any consent was actually given.

Wiki: 1974 enforcement sequence
1986–1987 · Judicial interpretation

The letters are treated as making occupation permissive

The Court of Appeal later held that the letters made Mary’s possession permissive, whether or not she accepted them, defeating the adverse-possession argument on the issue before it. Audit the exact judgment, the pleadings and the lower-court reasons before describing its scope or claiming that it decided a different root-of-title question.

Wiki: BP Properties Ltd v Buckler · EV-0080 · judgment
Identity is not settled

“Mrs Buckler (Mrs Williams)”

Family copies are described as addressed to “Mary Buckler” (image source pending); the judgment uses “Mrs Buckler (described again as Mrs Williams)”. The Wiki flags more than one Mary Buckler in the family. Do not silently resolve the addressee without examining the letter image, envelope, address, solicitor’s file and the full family identity chronology.

Scope is not settled by a noun

“Farmhouse”, “farm” and “whole farm”

A letter about remaining in the farmhouse does not, without more, identify all land in a historic farm holding. Link each term to the plan attached to the relevant deed, tenancy or order. Keep the historic parcel label and the present working crosswalk in separate fields.

  • Mary’s complete 1974 defence, the hearing note, the notice appeal and warrant withdrawal record.
  • The 1962 order, 1955 order and plans; the 1916 and 1939 tenancy instruments and their plans.
