TH-06 Tested · the records so far do not match it added 3 November 2026

Bounced-notice theory

Some recipients of the family's record-preservation notices might be able to read emails that show as bounced on the sender's side, so that a bounce would leave room to write later that no notice arrived, and to date any disposal of records before the first notice they accept.

Records it rests on

  • No record yet supports it.

What cuts against it

  • All four bounces to these recipients (February, June, August and September 2026) were refused by the recipient's own mail server at the address check, before the message text is sent, so the text never reached them.
  • Each bounce went to a closed or wrong address; each recipient had a working address that accepted the notices.
  • The preservation notice of 28 September 2026 did not bounce for any of these recipients.
  • Bounces are common in the family's mailbox overall, across many unrelated recipients.
  • No recipient has dated a disposal of records to a time after a bounce.

What would test it: A subject access request for each recipient's mail-gateway logs (accepted, refused, quarantined) for the family's emails; a message trace for the 28 September notice.

First “not held” replies can understate what a body holds

Two bodies first replied that they held nothing in scope, then produced a record after a challenge or a fresh look.

Records it rests on

  • TOC-F01 · Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales reply
  • TOC-F02 · Historic England reply

What cuts against it

  • In both cases the holder corrected the reply itself; an ordinary search error explains it.
  • Two cases do not make a pattern.

What would test it: Track every “not held” reply for a later find, and ask each body for the search terms it used.

TH-05 Open · not checked added 2 November 2026

A 1928 end to rent would have barred any action to recover the house about 1940

The family's c.1974 statement records that the last rent was paid in 1928. If the house parcel stopped being let then, any right to recover it accrued in 1928 and was barred about twelve years later, before the 1949 re-grant and the 1955 date the 1987 court used.

Records it rests on

  • GHF-E196-01 · the family's c.1974 statement

What cuts against it

  • The 1987 judgment records rent arrears in the mid-1930s and late 1940s, which implies a tenancy continuing after 1928.
  • The 1928 rent receipt has not been found; only a later diary reference is held.

What would test it: The estate rental books and plan book for c.1938–44, and any 1928 estate rental that drops the house parcel.

TH-03 Open · our coding, not checked added 27 October 2026

Bodies with records at stake answer on process, not substance

In the request correspondence (913 emails, 57 bodies), the bodies that hold registration, demolition and burial records reply about process and rarely address the events asked about, while archives and museums with nothing at stake explain their searches in detail.

Records it rests on

  • TOC-F03 · Vale of Glamorgan Council reply
  • TOC-F04 · Heneb (HER) reply
  • TOC-L10 · HM Land Registry reply

What cuts against it

  • Narrow searches, old files and routine destruction explain many short replies without any intent.
  • The counts come from our own coding of the emails, which no one else has checked.

What would test it: Internal reviews and regulator decisions that require a body to show the search it made.

That Old Chestnut

TH-02 Open · not checked added 26 October 2026

The 1974 letters did the work of an enforcement that was running out of time

Leave to enforce the 1962 possession order was given in September 1974, about 12 weeks before the order's 12-year anniversary. The warrant was withdrawn on 31 Oct 1974 for lack of notice, and the licence letters are dated the same day. The Court of Appeal later held that those letters turned the occupation into a licence.

Records it rests on

  • EV-0049 · withdrawal of the 1974 warrant
  • EV-0050 · 31 Oct 1974 licence letters

What cuts against it

  • The House of Lords held in 1999 (Lowsley v Forbes) that the 12-year limit on actions on a judgment does not stop execution itself, so the clock may not have bound anyone in law.

What would test it: The 1974 leave application and its sworn statement (MR-0012). The county court replied that it holds nothing because of age (MR-0014). MR-0012 MR-0014

TH-01 Open · partly matches the records added 26 October 2026

Twelve-year gaps between a refused arrangement and a registration

On three lines of the land’s history (woodland, green, house), a permissive arrangement the family refused was followed by a deed or registration in another name roughly 12 years later: 1949 to 1961, 1958/9 to 1970, 1974 to 1983. Twelve years is the limitation period for recovering land.

Records it rests on

  • EV-0040 · 1961 deed recited on the woodland title
  • EV-0039 · 1958/9 tenancy offer
  • EV-0044 · 1970 green registration
  • EV-0050 · 31 Oct 1974 licence letters
  • EV-0066 · 1983 application to register the house

What cuts against it

  • Across all landholder moves from 1928 to 1988 there is no regular 12-year rhythm; the gap holds per line, not as a cycle.
  • A green registration rests on 20 years' use as of right, not 12 years' possession.
  • Only three lines, and the start dates were picked after the pattern was noticed.

What would test it: Any record showing whether the occupier was told at each step (missing-records MR-0007, MR-0010, MR-0017, MR-0012, MR-0020, MR-0021). MR-0007 MR-0010 MR-0017 MR-0012 MR-0020 MR-0021

How this page is kept

The page has its own small database, theories.json. A sync reads the theories and checks each evidence ID against the central evidence catalogue, keeping only the title, summary, status, date added, the evidence IDs for and against, and what would test it. IDs the central catalogue does not carry yet point to the register that holds them. Agents can read the file, or use the page's WebMCP tools (theories_list, theories_get).

Status words: Open means no record yet settles it. Tested means the records read so far were checked against it; the result is shown in the entry.
