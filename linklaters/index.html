Forensic Assessment — Linklaters

Scope: Linklaters’ historic professional conduct as BP-related solicitors, especially registration and litigation work. No dishonesty is inferred.

Established role

The HMLR file schedule records multiple Linklaters & Paines letters, including 12 May 1983 correspondence, an undated letter, and documents connected with WA240304 and WA231076. The project also treats Linklaters as part of the BP legal team in the 1984–87 litigation history.

Forensic control test

The key question is documentary: what instructions did Linklaters receive, what title material did they hold, what did they tell HMLR, what did they disclose to the court, and what did they know about the family ownership dispute? A professional firm’s involvement alone proves none of these matters.

Current finding

As at 10-10-2026, the material reviewed does not establish dishonesty or professional misconduct by the firm. This is not a timeless finding that no relevant record or act exists. But the record trail makes the firm’s archival file potentially critical to the Evidence-Before-Court and registration controls.

Appeal-file lead and preservation enquiry — 16 October 2026

The 31 July 1987 Court of Appeal report identifies P. Morgan, instructed by Linklaters & Paines, as appearing for respondent BP Properties Ltd. That supports a targeted enquiry to Linklaters LLP about the surviving historic appeal file and any connected title/registration instructions; it does not establish that the firm still holds the file or that any solicitor acted improperly.

Request preservation and confirmation of custody for the 1986 trial, 1987 appeal and any connected 1969/1975 conveyancing or 1982–83 first-registration work, including instructions, counsel briefs, pleadings, bundle indexes, expert/plan material, correspondence with BP and HMLR, file-transfer records, retention schedules and any destruction/disposal log. Ask the firm to identify what it can disclose voluntarily and what authority or consent it requires, without demanding disclosure of privileged material where privilege lawfully applies.

The appeal report names Linklaters & Paines—not Masons—as BP’s firm for the reported appeal. Any enquiry to Pinsent Masons should therefore be framed separately: ask whether Masons or a predecessor ever acted for BP in another Great House Farm title, conveyancing, registration or enforcement matter, and ask that relevant files be preserved if located. Treat any asserted role as unconfirmed unless supported by a separate document.
