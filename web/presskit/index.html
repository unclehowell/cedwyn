01 · The baseline

What can be stated plainly

These are deliberately narrow statements. None of them decides the historic freehold or the extent of any disputed parcel.

The building was demolished in 1988

Contemporary reporting and later heritage records establish the demolition year. Sources differ on the precise day, so this summary does not assert one.

EV-0098 · EV-0106 · EV-0126

The family lived there

The Williams–Buckler household occupied and made its home at Great House Farm before the 1988 demolition. Residence is not, by itself, proof of freehold ownership.

EV-0080 · EV-0098 · EV-0100

The church stood beside it

St Dochdwy’s Church and churchyard are directly beside the former Great House site. Adjacency does not define the legal boundary around either property.

EV-0211 · EV-0047 · EV-0084
Nomenclature: name certainty is not title certainty. The 1824 survey names “Cedfin or Ty Mawr Farm”. Later sources use forms including Tŷ Mawr, Great House and Great House Farm. These names can identify the place when the source context matches Llandough-juxta-Penarth; they do not make every similarly named holding or historic parcel identical. The 1667 date is an account attributed to Mary Williams in 1974 reporting and 1988 broadcast material; no 1667 instrument is currently in this register.

03 · The method

How the grades are made

Source strength, legal reasoning and drafting defects are different questions. The app keeps them separate.

Evidence score · 100 points

Every factual statement is assessed against six factors. The score applies to the exact wording shown, not to a whole theory or a person’s credibility.

Legal formulation · 100 points

Only used where a statement makes a legal proposition. The published combined score is the lower of the evidence score and the legal-formulation score.

04 · Legal grammar

CSSCPSG · S1–S6

These are diagnostic flags, not evidence grades. A flag identifies a drafting or document-structure risk; it does not prove deception, illegality or falsity.

05 · Standards and limits

Forensic questions, not a claim of certification

The register adapts established evidence-handling questions for public scrutiny. It is not an expert report or a court assessment.

Important: editorial triage only. Scores are not probabilities, findings of fact, admissibility rulings, expert opinions or predictions of litigation outcomes. An authentic document may be irrelevant to a particular proposition. A green rating is confined to the precise statement that was assessed. A red rating quarantines the present wording; it does not prove that the underlying event did not happen.
