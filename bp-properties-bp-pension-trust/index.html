BP-side solicitor records — 16 October 2026 update

The reported 1987 appeal identifies Linklaters & Paines as the solicitors instructing counsel for BP Properties Ltd in the Court of Appeal. A preservation and voluntary disclosure enquiry to Linklaters LLP is therefore the direct appeal-file route shown by that source. It should seek records of the 1986–87 proceedings and ask separately whether related conveyancing, root-title or first-registration files survive.

The reference to Masons/Pinsent Masons remains a separate lead, not a confirmed attribution in the reported appeal. Ask that firm whether it or a predecessor acted for BP on another relevant matter, and request preservation only if a responsive file can be identified. The existence of an adverse title question or a missing document is not itself proof of dishonesty or professional misconduct.

Current finding

As at 14 October 2026, the material reviewed does not establish dishonesty or misconduct by either BP entity. The family’s case is that Parcel A passed to the Williams family in 1928 and was later swept into the “whole farm” descriptions and registered extents. The missing 1969 root deed and 1971 abstract are a gap in the chain description; they do not by themselves prove a Parcel A defect. No individual has been identified as a decision-maker from the records reviewed.

Forensic control test and next step

Identify who at each entity instructed the 1974 letters, the 1982–83 applications and the 1988 enforcement, and what they held. Preservation requests have gone to related archives (see the BP Archive review). Acceptance: the instructing files are located, or a documented result states they are not held.
