How the reports compare

27 October 2026

Farmland

Four reports put rural land in the sale: Newsweek (“some farm lands”), Life (“several surrounding villages”), the Belfast Telegraph (“very large fertile areas around the city and neighbouring villages”) and the Canadian Press (“extensive agricultural lands near the city”). None names a farm, a village or a parish.

The Associated Press wire, The Economist (“all ground rents”), The Times index (“Bute freeholds in Cardiff and Penarth”) and both Commons debates describe urban ground rents only.

Manorial rights

Only the New Statesman addresses them: excluded from the 1938 sale, with the mineral rights and five castles. No free press source found covers Mountjoy Ltd’s later disposal of the Glamorgan manors (1950–57).

Acreage

The Associated Press: not disclosed. The Hong Kong Daily Press: 10044, unit unclear in the scan, for “practically the whole of the Mountjoy estate”. No other report gives a figure.

What stayed with Bute

Mineral rights, manorial rights and five castles (New Statesman); Cardiff Castle and Caerphilly Castle (Associated Press); and in October 1940 the chief interest in the Cardiff–Penarth toll road (PR-0013, PR-0014).

How exact the reports are

Four prices appear (£4m, £5m, £20m and $100m). The 1938 count of 20,000 houses was corrected to 11,000 in the Commons in 1955.

Concerns that cut against the case for Tŷ Mawr

  • Four reports place agricultural land and villages near Cardiff in the sale. That makes it more plausible that Llandough farmland passed to Western Ground Rents in 1938.
  • “Practically the whole of the Mountjoy estate” (Hong Kong Daily Press) reads wider than the New Statesman’s list of exclusions.
  • “They take over the property on Christmas Day” (Belfast Telegraph): 25 December was the agreed takeover day, so the date of the conveyance carries no weight on its own.

Points that sit with the case

  • No report names Llandough, Leckwith, Cogan or any farm there.
  • The financial press and The Times index frame the purchase as ground rents and freeholds in Cardiff and Penarth.
  • Bute interests near Penarth (the manorial rights; the toll road in 1940) remained after 1938, so the Bute estate did not pass whole.

What decides it

Press reports are not the deed. What passed is set by the conveyance of 25 December 1938, its schedule of parcels and its plan (EV-0019). These have not been inspected.
